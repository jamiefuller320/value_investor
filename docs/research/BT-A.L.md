# BT Group plc (BT-A.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-09T06:06:46.466006+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
BT Group is the UK’s incumbent fixed and mobile operator, mid-way through a once-in-a-generation full-fibre build via Openreach, with management guiding to a normalised free cash flow inflection to c.£2bn in FY27 and c.£3bn by FY30. The quantitative screen flags the name as a strong buy on cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk-adjusted metrics (P/E 18.1, P/B 1.6, yield 4.2%, Piotroski 7/9), though eleven models fail on leverage, liquidity and quality thresholds. The investment case rests on sustained adjusted EBITDA near £8.2bn, a progressive dividend (8.32p proposed for FY26), and peak capex rolling off as fibre penetration rises — set against declining revenue, heavy net debt, and intense UK broadband competition. Key debate: whether the forthcoming cash-flow step-up is sufficiently de-risked to justify owning a highly levered telecom, or whether revenue erosion and regulatory/competitive pressure cap re-rating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, BT offers a classic “capex cycle turning” setup at a modest absolute valuation. The screen passes five metric families — cheapness, quality (partial), dividend, GARP and risk — with full data coverage (20/20 metrics) and filing-aligned FCF of $1.86bn versus a lower TTM screen figure of $1.20bn, suggesting the market may under-appreciate operating cash generation. Filings show adjusted EBITDA stabilising at £8.2bn (FY25–FY26) after rising from £7.6bn in FY22, while normalised free cash flow recovered from £1.3bn (FY23–FY24) to £1.6bn in FY25 before a modest FY26 dip to £1.5bn as capex ticked up to £5.1bn. Management reiterates FY27 FCF of c.£2bn and c.£3bn by FY30, underpinning dividend growth (8.16p FY25 → 8.32p proposed FY26). Strategic progress is tangible: Openreach full fibre passed 23m premises in FY26 (>two-thirds of UK homes and businesses), Consumer customer counts grew for the first time in eight years, and c.£900m of annualised cost savings were delivered one year into a £3bn five-year programme. The screen’s quality pass is selective — ROE 8.5% clears the 8% hurdle but fails Buffett/Economic Moat tests — while failed Financial Health and Graham Defensive models correctly flag debt/equity of 181 and a current ratio of 0.85. Net-net, the strong-buy signal is supported by cash-flow trajectory and income, but requires tolerance for balance-sheet risk and revenue headwinds.

**Observations from filings:**

- **Revenue** has drifted lower for three consecutive years to £19.7bn in FY26 (−3% YoY), which FY25 management attributed partly to international disposals and handset mix; FY26 revenue decline warrants monitoring as fibre migration completes.
- **Adjusted EBITDA** has been resilient, rising from £7.6bn (FY22) to a plateau of £8.2bn (FY25–FY26), with FY25 management noting EBITDA and normalised FCF grew despite lower revenue, helped by c.£900m annualised savings under a £3bn cost programme.
- **Normalised free cash flow** improved materially in FY25 (+25% to £1.6bn) before easing 6% to £1.5bn in FY26 as capex rose to £5.1bn (+6%). Management reiterates an inflection to c.£2bn in FY27 and c.£3bn by FY30.
- **Profit before tax** recovered from a FY24 trough of £1.2bn (−31% YoY, when EPS fell 55% to 8.7p) to £1.4bn in FY26 (+8% YoY).
- **Dividend:** Progressive policy maintained — FY25 full-year dividend raised 2% to 8.16p; FY26 proposed dividend 8.32p (annual report FY26).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and liquidity:** The screen fails Financial Health, Graham Defensive, Buffett Quality and Economic Moat on high debt/equity (181) and weak current ratio (0.85). Yahoo shows net debt of c.$18.3bn against equity of c.$12.6bn. FY26 filings reference maintaining pension contributions and a “strong balance sheet” but do not provide covenant detail in the extracted text; full financial-statement notes were not available in body extracts.

**Pension:** FY26 chairman’s letter cites pension fund obligations alongside dividend and investment priorities. Non-current pension liabilities were c.$4.4bn per Yahoo FY26 — material but not fully stress-tested in this pack.

**Regulatory:** FY25–FY26 reports emphasise Ofcom engagement and a “fair bet” on full-fibre returns; wholesale access pricing and USO obligations remain structural risks to Openreach economics. Regulatory Affairs director appointments noted in FY25.

**Competition:** FY26 CEO commentary acknowledges a “highly competitive environment”; yfinance (23 Jul 2026) reports Q1 profit down 1% with broadband losses and margin pressure — not corroborated by interim filings in this pack. Alt-net overbuild and Virgin Media O2 bundling pressure consumer ARPU.

**Execution and capex:** FY26 capex rose 6% to £5.1bn despite “passed peak capex” narrative in prior years; slippage on the FY27 £2bn FCF target would undermine the core thesis.

**Governance / insider activity:** “Insiders At BT Group Sold UK£634k In Stock” (simplywall.st, 23 May 2025) and “BT Group rally looks stretched, broker says” (Proactive Investors, 22 May 2025) are sentiment flags, not fundamental impairments.

**Strategic / M&A:** The 50:50 international enterprise JV with Verizon (~$4bn combined revenue, $625m equalisation payment, completion targeted 2027 subject to regulatory approvals) simplifies the portfolio but introduces execution and antitrust risk; 18 months seeking a full buyer before structuring the JV suggests limited standalone value for that unit.

**Going concern / viability:** FY25 and FY26 annual reports include viability statements (pages 72+) and principal risks sections, but extracted body text does not reproduce going-concern auditor language — **verify in full statutory accounts before sizing**.
