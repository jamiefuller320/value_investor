# Imperial Brands PLC (IMB.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-10T20:35:11.849465+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Imperial Brands offers a classic tobacco-sector value proposition: a stabilising combustibles franchise, accelerating next-generation product (NGP) growth, and a capital-return programme (progressive dividend plus multi-billion-pound buybacks) funded by strong free cash flow. The quantitative screen flags the name as a Strong Buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families, anchored by ~6% yield, ~15.6% FCF yield, and ROE above 37%. The valuation hook is cash yield and capital returns at ~12.5x P/E, not earnings growth — statutory/adjusted earnings have softened and the balance sheet screens poorly on leverage and liquidity. The central debate is whether Strategy 2030 (launched March 2025) can sustain mid-single-digit profit growth and £320m of annual savings by 2030 while navigating volume decline, NGP investment, and tightening regulation — without eroding the dividend or investment-grade profile.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Imperial passes 11 of 22 screen models, spanning FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality. Five metric families clear the hurdle (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk) with full data quality (20/20 metrics present). The business quality case rests on filing-backed operational improvement: tobacco and NGP net revenue has risen from £8.0bn (FY2023) to £8.2bn (FY2024, +4.6% constant currency) to £8.3bn (FY2025), the strongest like-for-like top-line trajectory cited in the FY2024 annual report in over a decade. Five priority combustible markets (US, Germany, UK, Spain, Australia) contribute c.70% of adjusted tobacco operating profit; aggregate share in those markets has stabilised after years of decline. NGP net revenue reached £329m in FY2024 (+26.4% CC) and grew 13.7% in FY2025 per the latest annual report body. Capital allocation is explicit and shareholder-friendly: progressive dividend (153.42p in FY2024, +4.5%), FY2025 buyback commitment of £1.25bn (expanded to a £1.45bn programme per recent news), and leverage targeted at 2.0–2.5x adjusted net debt/EBITDA at the lower end, supporting investment-grade status. FCF dividend coverage of ~2.0x (screen) and filing-cited FCF of £2.4bn (FY2024) underpin the income case. The screen fails deep-value and balance-sheet screens (P/B 5.2, debt/equity ~244%, current ratio 0.74, Piotroski 6/9), which is appropriate for a goodwill-heavy, leveraged tobacco name — the thesis is cash and returns, not net-net or Graham defensive.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and illicit trade.** FY2024 Chair's statement warns that prohibition-leaning policy creates unintended consequences, citing Australia where **~30% of tobacco products are illicit** due to restrictive NGP rules and high taxation — damaging consumers, government excise receipts, and legal operators. Imperial explicitly engages for balanced regulation while supporting adult smokers' freedoms; escalating bans (EU flavour restrictions, US nicotine limits, plain packaging expansion) remain a structural overhang not captured by valuation screens.

**Volume and category decline.** Combustible volumes fall structurally; the model depends on pricing power (+7.8% in FY2024). Failure to hold aggregate share in five priority markets — UK share fell 85bps and Spain 45bps in FY2025 filing KPIs — would pressure the profit bridge.

**NGP economics.** NGP is growing fast but historically loss-making at the segment level; FY2024 filing notes reduced NGP losses alongside +26% revenue growth. Black Buffalo acquisition (May 2026 news, **≥$150m**) adds US modern-oral exposure but integration and regulatory risk in nicotine pouches remain.

**Leverage and liquidity.** Debt/equity ~244%, current ratio 0.74, and negative tangible equity are red flags for Graham-style screens. Filing bodies reference a **2.0–2.5x net debt/EBITDA** target but do not provide covenant detail in available extracts. Net debt rose in FY2025 (Yahoo) despite strong FCF, partly reflecting buybacks and dividends.

**Pension.** **$801m** non-current pension/post-retirement liability (Yahoo FY2025) — material but not fully disclosed in filing bodies; interest-rate and longevity risk persists.

**Restructuring and execution.** August 2026 news reports **thousands of job cuts** in the US and Europe under a plan for **£320m annual savings by 2030**; execution risk and disruption to commercial capability. ERP migration and organisational simplification (Strategy 2030 enabler) add operational risk.

**Governance/sentiment.** News (June 2026) flags an insider reducing holding by 59% over the prior year. April 2026 share price weakness linked to Middle East conflict uncertainty despite reaffirmed FY2026 outlook.

**Filing limitations.** Principal risks and uncertainties are referenced in annual report tables of contents but **not extracted** in available bodies; going-concern and contingency language could not be verified. Treat legal/litigation exposure as unresolved in this pass.
