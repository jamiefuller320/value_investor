# Endeavour Mining plc (EDV.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-24T20:56:19.168006+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Endeavour Mining plc (EDV.L) is the largest gold producer in West Africa, with five operating mines across Senegal, Côte d’Ivoire and Burkina Faso, a net-cash balance sheet, and a visible path to ~1.5Moz production by 2030 via the Assafou development project. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy (13/22 models; composite 70%), driven by cheapness (FCF yield ~18%), quality (ROE ~31%), dividend/GARP metrics, and favourable timing (RSI ~43, price below the 200-day moving average). The valuation hook is levered free cash flow at elevated gold prices combined with a sector-leading shareholder returns programme ($1bn minimum dividends over 2026–2028, with management guiding >$2bn at prevailing prices). The central debate is whether West African jurisdiction, cost inflation and Assafou capex (~$1.1bn upfront) are adequately compensated by current cash generation — filings confirm operational momentum into Q1 2026, but FY2025 annual accounts in the index lack a downloadable body, leaving a verify-before-trade gap on the latest full-year IFRS set.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, EDV offers a rare combination: first-quartile operating costs, rising production, and capital return at a screen-implied discount to normalised cash earnings. The screen passes five factor families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk — with standout metrics including P/E 13.6, FCF yield 18.1%, PEG 0.13, dividend yield 3.0% and Piotroski F-Score pass. Business quality supports the signal: the 2024 annual report (Companies House, filed July 2025) records 1,103koz production, adjusted EBITDA of $1,325m (50% margin), AISC of $1,218/oz, free cash flow of $313m and leverage of 0.55x net debt/adjusted EBITDA — near the stated ≤0.5x target. Q1 2026 interim results (RNS, 30 April 2026) accelerate the story: record adjusted EBITDA of $880m, record free cash flow of $613m, net cash of $405m and liquidity of $1,704m, with FY-2026 production guidance of 1,090–1,265koz on track. The Assafou DFS (23 April 2026) adds organic growth optionality — 320kozpa at $1,026/oz AISC in years 1–8, after-tax NPV (5%) of $5.1bn at $4,000/oz gold — without requiring M&A. Management has returned $1.6bn to shareholders since 2021 (83% above minimum commitments), aligning the equity with a cash-return, low-leverage capital allocation framework that screens well on dividend growth and acquirer’s multiple models. Near-term technical weakness (below 50- and 200-day MAs) offers an entry point consistent with the screen’s “accumulate” timing label.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Jurisdiction and fiscal:** Operations concentrated in Burkina Faso, Côte d’Ivoire and Senegal. Filings reference 10% government free-carried interests at mine level and heightened political attention in Burkina Faso (2022 chair’s statement). Q1 2026 RNS notes Côte d’Ivoire royalty rates rising from 6% to 8%, retroactively applied from 2025 — a direct margin headwind at high gold prices. Assafou DFS flags that Côte d’Ivoire’s mining code is under review; fiscal terms may change before the mining convention is granted. Forward-looking language in RNS filings cites risks of expropriation, licence non-renewal and adverse regulatory change.

**Safety and ESG:** A fatal contractor accident at Mana, Burkina Faso, was reported on 6 March 2026 (RNS headline indexed; body not downloaded). TRIFR of 0.72 (LTM to 31 March 2026) remains low but the incident underscores persistent operational risk. Resettlement at Assafou is on the project critical path; delays would push FID and construction.

**Financial/cyclical:** AISC rose to $1,834/oz in Q1 2026 (vs $1,129/oz in Q1 2025), driven by royalties, grid-power outages at Houndé/Mana and mine sequencing — partially gold-price-linked but also operational. Assafou upfront capex of $1,061m (DFS) will absorb cash even with net-cash starting position; execution risk on a 24–30 month construction timeline. Q4-2025 included a $193m impairment of mining interests (Q1 2026 earnings bridge), indicating asset-level write-down risk if costs or reserves disappoint.

**Governance:** 2026 AGM (21 May 2026) passed all resolutions; director re-election support was strong though Naguib Sawiris received 91.9% for. No going-concern qualification identified in available extracts. The FY-2024 annual report includes a viability statement (body text not fully extracted via OCR), consistent with standard UK premium-listing practice.

**Screen blind spots:** Quantitative risk pass does not capture single-country tax upstreaming ($660–770m guided cash taxes in 2026), FX (group reports in USD; LSE quote in GBP), or gold-price mean reversion from levels underpinning current FCF.
