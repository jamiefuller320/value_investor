# Morgan Advanced Materials plc (MGAM.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:26:49.037326+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Morgan Advanced Materials is a global advanced ceramics and carbon business (~£1bn revenue, ~57 sites) undergoing a deliberate portfolio and operational reset under CEO Damien Caby. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cheapness and cash-generation metrics (FCF yield ~7.6%, P/B 2.1x, dividend yield 5.1%), but reported ROE is depressed by the 2025 earnings trough and heavy adjusting items. The valuation hook is a cyclically compressed industrial trading below medium-term margin ambitions (12% adjusted operating margin by 2028) while simplification savings (£27m run-rate), MMS disposal proceeds, and the August 2026 Foseco India share sale (~£53m net) should support de-leveraging toward ~1.7x net debt/EBITDA. The central debate is whether self-help and portfolio pruning can offset semiconductor weakness, European industrial softness, and Thermal Products operational drag—or whether margin recovery remains too execution-dependent for a value entry.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MGAM offers a cash-generative, asset-backed industrial at a screen-attractive valuation with identifiable catalysts: completed Molten Metal Systems disposal, ongoing Thermal Products strategic review (including potential sale), and a simplification programme nearing its £27m savings target. The screen passes FCF Yield, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality—consistent with a business whose statutory earnings are distorted by ERP spend, restructuring, and semiconductor impairments while underlying cash conversion remains respectable (FY2025 headline free cash flow £45.4m on adjusted operating profit of £93.8m from continuing operations).

Business quality is mixed but not distressed. Morgan holds niche positions in mission-critical materials (aerospace turbine ceramics, defence, energy, semiconductors) with high switching costs, and H1 2026 showed organic constant-currency revenue growth of 4.8% as end-markets stabilised. Technical Ceramics (13.0% H1 margin) and Performance Carbon (17.1% H1 margin, flattered by a one-off take-or-pay receipt) carry the portfolio; Thermal Products remains the weak link (6.4% H1 margin, strategic review underway). Management's medium-term framework—GDP-plus organic growth, 12–14% sustainable margins, ROIC 17–20%, leverage 1.0–1.5x—provides a credible roadmap if execution holds. The buy case aligns with the screen: cheap on cash and earnings quality, with balance-sheet repair and portfolio optionality as upside paths, tempered by cyclical exposure and thin dividend cover (1.3x adjusted EPS in FY2025).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and end-market:** Semiconductor demand weakness drove FY2025 revenue decline and a £15.6m UK asset impairment; management notes supply-chain shift toward China. European industrial markets remain soft (Thermal Products H1 US operational issues). Aerospace/defence and energy provide partial offset but do not eliminate cyclicality.

**Execution and portfolio:** Thermal Products strategic review (potential disposal) creates uncertainty; division margin was 6.7% FY2025 and 6.4% H1 2026. Global ERP roll-out will incur £22–24m of adjusting costs in 2026 with implementation risk through 2027.

**Leverage and liquidity:** Net debt/EBITDA rose to 1.8x FY2025 and 2.0x H1 2026, above the stated 1.0–1.5x framework. October 2026 bond maturities ($97m, €25m) require refinancing/repayment—manageable given £347.7m undrawn headroom at H1, but worth monitoring. Buyback paused after £20m repurchased to preserve balance-sheet resilience.

**Governance and key person:** CFO Richard Armitage intends to retire in H1 2027; successor search underway. CEO Caby joined July 2025—strategy reset is early-stage.

**Pension:** IAS 19 net pension obligation remains (defined benefit assets £12.4m, obligations £34.4m at FY2025 year-end); net financing cost on pensions £0.4m FY2025. Not a primary concern but adds to fixed charges.

**Regulatory, environmental, safety:** Filing language references environmental remediation provisions requiring judgement on contingent liabilities. Lost-time accident rate rose to 0.18 (2024: 0.13). Going concern assessment (18-month horizon) confirms adequate resources, covenant headroom (max net debt/EBITDA 3.0x, min interest cover 4.0x), and reverse stress-test capacity—no going-concern qualification.

**Accounting:** Heavy reliance on adjusted metrics; £8.9m H1 take-or-pay and £5.2m FY2025 Performance Carbon trading receipts distort comparability. Dividend cover below policy target.
