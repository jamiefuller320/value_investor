## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ME Group International plc operates a global estate of self-service photobooths (Photo.ME) and unattended laundry (Wash.ME), with FY2025 record profit before tax of £78.2 million on revenue of £315.4 million (RNS annual results, 23 March 2026). The quantitative screen rates MEGP.L as a strong buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk (15/22 models, composite 82%, c.6.8% yield, P/E 8.2, ROE 28.2%), but overlays flag weak adjusted EPS growth, interim EPS decline, and statutory free-cash-flow dividend coverage below 1×. The valuation hook is a cash-generative, net-cash balance sheet funding laundry rollout and buybacks, against a debate over cyclical consumer softness (April 2026 France/trading update), photobooth regulation and equipment cycles, and whether FY2026 PBT guidance of £69–74 million marks a genuine step-down from FY2025.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, ME Group combines a visible asset base (49,000+ vending units, long-dated site partnerships) with improving mix: laundry already contributed 46% of Group EBITDA in FY2025 and over half at H1 2026, supporting margin resilience even when Photo.ME vending revenue falls. The screen’s strong buy is grounded in rare breadth—five factor families passing, including Economic Moat (sole pass among screened UK Industrials peers), High Dividend Yield, FCF Yield, Buffett/Quality Value, and Financial Health—with modest leverage (debt/equity c.28%) and persistent signal (14 weeks, conviction 72%). Business quality in filings aligns with the quality bucket: FY2025 EBITDA margin 38.2%, net cash £26.5 million (excluding lease liabilities), and dividend growth (+9.5% to 8.64p) alongside an £18 million buyback. The tension is growth: adjusted/core EPS growth in the screen is near flat (~0.4%), Lynch PEG fails, Piotroski scores 6/9, and Earnings Quality fails on weak free-cash conversion—so the strong buy is a “quality income at a discount” story more than a growth compounder. Capital deployment (laundry capex, buybacks, >55% payout policy) should be weighed against H1 2026 net cash falling to £7.5 million and reduced interim dividend (3.60p, −6.5%).
