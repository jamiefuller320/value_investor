# ME Group International plc (MEGP.L) — Research memo

_Version 2 · Updated 2026-09-13T08:01:11.400367+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ME Group International plc operates a large, geographically diversified estate of photobooths and fast-growing unattended laundry machines, with FY2025 record profit before tax of £78.2m on revenue of £315.4m (RNS annual results, 23 March 2026). The quantitative screen rates the stock a strong buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk buckets (15/22 models; composite ~81%; P/E ~8.2, yield ~6.8%, ROE ~28%, debt/equity ~28%), but overlays flag interim dividend reduction, weak statutory free-cash-flow dividend cover and earnings-growth definition gaps. The valuation hook is a cash-generative, asset-heavy vending franchise trading at a low multiple while laundry mix rises; the key debate is whether photobooth/regulatory and consumer cyclicality (France, Germany) plus higher capex and accounting/audit noise justify the discount—or signal structural pressure on earnings and distributions.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MEGP combines enterprising Graham-style metrics (low P/E, high yield, moderate leverage, positive operating cash flow) with unusually strong reported returns on equity and a rare “economic moat” pass among UK industrial peers in the screen snapshot. Primary filings support a quality narrative: FY2025 cash generated from operations of £115.5m, net cash of £26.5m (excluding lease liabilities), and laundry revenue up 17.3% to £112.4m while the estate expanded (7,607 Wash.ME units). The screen’s quality and dividend families align with management’s capital-return posture (FY2025 total dividend 8.64p, +9.5%; £18m buyback launched post-results), but the same filing set shows growth is increasingly capex-led (FY2025 capex £65.6m) and photobooth revenue fell 4.0% on German ID rules and supply issues. H1 FY2026 interim results (13 July 2026) show laundry still accelerating (Wash.ME revenue +16.3%) yet group PBT down 3.8% to £32.7m and full-year PBT guidance cut to £69m–£74m—so the strong-buy signal rests on trailing full-year economics and multiples more than on near-term earnings momentum. Failed screen models (Lynch PEG on ~0.4% adjusted EPS growth, Piotroski 6/9, earnings quality) are consistent with that tension: cheap on headline numbers, less compelling on forward growth and cash conversion consistency.
