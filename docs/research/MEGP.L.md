# ME Group International plc (MEGP.L) — Research memo

_Version 3 · Updated 2026-07-24T21:26:49.089141+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ME Group operates ~49,000 self-service vending units (photobooths, laundry, printing) across 16 countries, with a strategic pivot toward high-margin Wash.ME laundry now contributing 38% of H1 vending revenue and 54% of EBITDA. The quantitative screen flags a compelling value-income profile (P/E ~7.1, yield ~8.0%, ROE ~28%, debt/equity ~28%, 14/22 models passed across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families), but near-term earnings momentum has turned negative and cash conversion is under pressure. FY2025 delivered record profit before tax of £78.2m on revenue of £315.4m; H1 2026 showed flat revenue and PBT down 3.8% to £32.7m, with management guiding FY2026 PBT to a revised £69–74m range. The central debate is whether laundry-led diversification and contract renewals (ASDA, SNCF, RATP) justify accumulating a statistically cheap, dividend-paying name, or whether April’s France/photobooth wobble, regulatory headwinds in Germany, and tightening free-cash-flow cover signal a peak-earnings trap.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MEGP offers a rare combination of low multiple, high reported return on equity, and an explicit payout policy targeting >55% of post-tax profits, backed by a net-cash balance sheet and a long-established vending estate. The screen’s Strong Buy rating (composite 81%, sector-relative 88%, conviction 53%, new signal) is supported by passes on Graham Enterprising, Low P/E + High Yield, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, High Dividend Yield, Magic Formula and Financial Health — metrics consistent with a cash-generative, asset-heavy operator trading below intrinsic value on trailing earnings.

Business quality is better than the “photo booth” label suggests. FY2025 (annual results, 31 October 2025) saw revenue rise 2.4% to £315.4m, EBITDA up 5.4% to £120.4m (margin 38.2%), and record PBT of £78.2m (+6.5%). Wash.ME installed a net 1,145 laundry machines; laundry EBITDA of £55.5m now represents 46% of group EBITDA. The board returned £32.6m via dividends (+9.5% to 8.64p) and launched an £18m share buyback in March 2026. H1 2026 interim results (30 April 2026) confirm strategic progress — Wash.ME vending revenue +16.3%, ASDA partnership secured, SNCF/RATP contracts renewed (>£9m revenue combined) — even as photobooth revenue fell 6.2% and group PBT declined to £32.7m.

The screen fails FCF yield (~3.6%), Lynch PEG (negative earnings growth ~−3.9%) and Piotroski F-Score (4/9), which aligns with the qualitative tension: statutory FY2025 net income rose +4.6% (Yahoo fallback: £56.6m vs £54.1m), but trailing/forward momentum and H1 cash generation (−18.9% on cash from operations to £38.7m) argue the cheapness partly reflects an earnings inflection, not just market neglect. For a patient value allocator, the case rests on laundry scale-up converting recurring vending revenue into restored FCF, while the ~8% yield and sub-8x P/E provide downside cushion if FY2026 guidance (£69–74m PBT) is met.
