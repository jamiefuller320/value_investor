# ME Group International plc (MEGP.L) — Research memo

_Version 3 · Updated 2026-09-02T11:09:55.304229+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ME Group International plc operates a global estate of ~49,000 self-service vending units, chiefly photobooths (Photo.ME) and unattended laundry (Wash.ME), across 16 countries. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy on cheapness (P/E ~8.1, yield ~6.5%), quality (ROE ~28%), dividend, GARP and risk metrics, with filing-aligned FY25 free cash flow of $25.2m supporting the valuation hook. FY25 delivered record profit before tax of £78.2m and a strategic pivot toward higher-margin laundry, but H1 FY26 shows softer momentum: profit before tax fell 3.8% to £32.7m, FY26 guidance was cut to £69–74m, and the interim dividend was reduced. The central debate is whether laundry rollout, the ASDA partnership and capital returns offset photobooth regulatory drag in Germany, cyclical weakness in France, FX headwinds and governance noise from audit delays and cash-classification restatements.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal rests on a credible value stack: 15 of 22 models pass, composite score 82%, debt/equity ~28%, and a business generating strong operating cash flow (£115.5m in FY25 per the 2025 Annual Results RNS) against a market capitalisation that implies mid-single-digit earnings and high-single-digit FCF yields. Business quality supports the cheapness case: ME Group holds leading photobooth positions, long-dated site contracts (no single customer above 2% of turnover), and a fast-growing laundry division that contributed 46% of group EBITDA in FY25 and exceeded photobooth EBITDA contribution in H1 FY26 (53.9% of group EBITDA). Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly: FY25 total dividend of 8.64p (+9.5%), an £18m buyback launched in March 2026, and net cash of £26.5m at 31 October 2025 (£7.5m at 30 April 2026 after capex, dividends and buybacks). For a value investor, the thesis is a cash-generative, asset-heavy franchise trading at a discount because near-term earnings are decelerating, the market prices in Germany ID-photo regulation and April 2026 consumer softness, and headline growth is negligible (statutory EPS growth ~0.4% on the screen). The screen’s interim-quality overlay (interim EPS decline, net FCF/dividend coverage below 1.0×) already moderated the signal to Buy; deep research largely confirms the valuation case but does not fully dismiss the quality concerns behind that overlay.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclicality (evidenced):** Principal risks in `f40be706e75a10ae.txt` / `ac9d0c965a93c3fd.txt` cite recession, inflation and (interim) geopolitical conflict affecting discretionary consumer spend. April 2026 French softness and revised FY26 guidance (£69–74m PBT) demonstrate cyclical sensitivity despite “needs-driven” laundry mix. **Open:** durability of May/June recovery while travel uncertainty persists (interim RNS language).

**Technology / regulatory disruption (evidenced, not payment terminals):** Filings frame disruption as ID-photo centralisation and technology obsolescence in photobooth systems (`f40be706e75a10ae.txt` — “Centralisation of the production of ID photos”; “Failure to keep up with advances in technology”). German passport-photo rules continue to weigh on Photo.ME; next-generation compliant booths from H2 FY26. No filing reference to payment-terminal exposure. **Open:** smartphone/home-capture adoption beyond disclosed regulatory channels.

**Geographic concentration (evidenced):** CE 68.3% of FY25 revenue; France >50% of photobooth revenue only (`ir_9ac08315f9388f7e.txt`). Principal risks note dependence on trading conditions in France and the UK. **Open:** exact France share of group revenue.

**Customer concentration (evidenced, low):** No customer ≥10% (`ir_a006e8bd9b660a9c.txt`); no account >2% of turnover (`f40be706e75a10ae.txt`). ASDA partnership strategically large for laundry but not a disclosed concentration breach.

**Dividend / liquidity (evidenced):** Net statutory FCF/dividend 0.84× at FY25; interim dividend cut H1 FY26; competing £18m buyback and elevated capex (~£57–59m FY26 guide). Net cash positive (£7.5m H1) but compressed. Covenants comfortably met FY25 (`ir_55af4b8f27e3dd6f.txt`). **Open:** full-year dividend if PBT at low end of guidance.

**Screen-relative context (evidenced):** Among top strong-buy peers, MEGP uniquely passes economic_moat; ITV scores higher on high_dividend model. FCF TTM vs filing gaps affect all top names — verify before sizing (`screening_snapshot.json` cross-ticker).

**Accounting / governance (evidenced):** FY25 audit delay, cash-in-transit restatement, unqualified audit. **Open:** control remediation detail in full audit committee report.

**New CH refetch:** Historical 2019 accounts do not change current risk assessment.
