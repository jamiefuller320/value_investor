# Keller Group plc (KLR.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-19T10:41:31.644672+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Keller Group is the world’s largest geotechnical specialist contractor, reporting record momentum into 2026 on North American infrastructure and data-centre demand, a record £1.9bn order book, and a balance sheet with covenant net debt/EBITDA of 0.1x at H1 2026. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy (12/22 models; composite 73%), supported by ~9.5% FCF yield, PEG ~0.56, Piotroski 8/9, and robust dividend/ buyback capacity. Valuation is not deep Graham-style cheap (P/B ~3.1; fails net-net and low P/B screens), and the stock sits above its 200-day moving average with neutral timing. The central debate is whether megatrend-driven US volumes and capital returns justify a mid-teens multiple, or whether construction cyclicality, working-capital absorption, and geopolitical cost pressure will compress margins when the order book normalises.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Keller offers a rare combination of **cheap cash generation on a quality franchise**: the screen passes five factor families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk) with full data completeness (20/20 metrics). Reported FY2025 revenue of £3,087.3m and free cash flow of £175.9m (Companies House 2025 accounts) underpin a filing-aligned FCF of ~£168m and FCF yield near 9.5%, with net dividend coverage above 4.5x. Return on equity (~25%) and underlying ROCE (30.7% in FY2025 per Companies House) reflect disciplined project execution and a capital-light covenant balance sheet that moved to net cash of £59.7m at FY2025 year-end.

Business quality aligns with the screen’s quality and financial-health passes: global scale (~10,000 staff; ~5,500 projects annually), no customer above 4% of group revenue (annual report risk disclosure), and North America (~60% of revenue) levered to structurally supported end-markets (highways, power, data centres). Management is actively shrinking the equity base (£100m buyback tranche launched March 2026; £50m completed in 2025) and rebased dividends under a 2.5x–3.5x cover policy (interim 28.7p in H1 2026, +57% year on year).

The strong-buy signal is not a deep-value net-net: Graham defensive/enterprising, Schloss, and economic-moat models fail on P/B, current ratio, and ROA/margin thresholds. That makes Keller better framed as **quality compounder at a reasonable price** than classic asset bargain—exactly where Lynch PEG, FCF yield, Buffett Quality, and Piotroski screens converge.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / demand:** Filings explicitly warn construction is “grows more … during expansion and falls harder … when the economy contracts.” H1 2026 notes renewed inflation pressure from Middle East conflict, US steel tariffs, and potential customer deferrals if rates re-rise—despite a record order book.

**Project execution:** Principal risk of ineffective project management (cost overruns, disputes, remedy obligations). Management cites improved 2025 execution and PPM rollout from Q1 2026, but APAC margin compression and EME weather disruption show division-level volatility persists.

**Safety:** TRIR rose to 0.60 (H1 2025: 0.48) alongside higher activity; serious injury/fatality risk remains a viability-linked principal risk.

**Regulatory / compliance:** Multi-jurisdiction exposure (bribery, modern slavery, GDPR, UK “failure to prevent fraud” offence). Tax risk across 40+ countries; uncertain positions provisioned on judgement.

**Liquidity / leverage:** Not stressed near-term—going-concern review (interim) models 10% revenue decline, margin slippage, and combined downside scenarios without covenant breach through December 2027; auditor found no inappropriate going-concern adoption. Medium-term risk is **working-capital intensity** on large US projects (H1 receivables +£76.8m, inventory +£36.7m) compressing interim cash conversion.

**Pension:** Net defined benefit liabilities **£15.2m** at 30 June 2026; UK Keller Group Pension Scheme fully funded on IAS 19 with buy-in completed—limited balance-sheet threat.

**Competitive / concentration:** No single customer >4% revenue (annual report); top 10 customers 19% (H1 2026). Market-share loss and technology obsolescence flagged as strategic risks.

**Governance / capital allocation:** Enhanced dividends plus buybacks reduce balance-sheet optionality if cycle turns; ERP implementation costs (£4.7m non-underlying in H1 2026) indicate ongoing systems spend.
