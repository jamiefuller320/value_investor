# Keller Group plc (KLR.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-23T06:47:34.375177+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Keller Group is the world’s largest geotechnical specialist contractor (~£3bn revenue, ~10,000 staff, five continents), and the quantitative screen flags it as a strong buy on a blend of cash-flow cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and financial health. FY2025 delivered record revenue of £3,087.3m and net cash of £59.7m (covenant basis), with H1 2026 accelerating: revenue +11% constant currency to £1,608.0m and underlying operating profit +17% cc to £117.9m, supported by a record £1.9bn order book. The valuation hook is high free cash flow yield (~9.7% screen) and a PEG of 0.55 against mid-teens earnings growth, offset by P/B of 3.0x that fails classic deep-value screens. The central debate is whether North American infrastructure and data-centre demand can sustain margins through a construction cycle, or whether recent share-price strength and working-capital seasonality already embed much of the upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy rating rests on five passing factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with a Piotroski F-Score of 8/9 and full data quality (20/20 metrics). Keller passes FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Composite Value, Earnings Quality, and Financial Health; failures are concentrated in Graham-style deep value (P/B ≥3, current ratio 1.4) and moat screens (ROA below threshold), not in balance-sheet distress.

Business quality supports the quantitative signal. Keller is the global scale leader in ground engineering with geographic diversification (North America ~61% of H1 2026 revenue), no customer above 3–4% of revenue, and underlying operating margins sustained at 7.1–7.3%. FY2025 underlying ROCE reached 30.7% (Companies House annual accounts). Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly: total dividend rebased to 70.4p (+42%), a £100m buyback launched in March 2026 (c.£35m completed by H1), and covenant leverage at 0.1x versus a 0.5–1.5x target range. For a value investor, this is a cash-generative industrial compounder trading at a mid-teens P/E with ~9.7% FCF yield and 4.6x net FCF dividend cover—not a net-net, but a quality-at-a-reasonable-price candidate where the screen’s GARP and FCF metrics align with operational momentum in structurally supported end markets (US infrastructure, data centres, highway remediation).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical construction exposure.** The Annual Report states construction “grows more than many other industries during periods of economic expansion and falls harder… when the economy contracts.” Keller mitigates via geographic and sector diversification, but a rapid downturn remains a principal viability risk; management models up to 10% revenue decline in going-concern stress tests.

**Project execution and contract risk.** Fixed-price geotechnical work carries margin volatility from ground conditions, weather, and cost overruns. H1 2026 provisions for contract and legal disputes exceeded settlements. The new Project Performance Management standard is rolling out globally from Q1 2026—execution improvement is ongoing, not proven at scale.

**Geopolitical and supply-chain pressure.** Interim filings flag renewed inflation/supply-chain pressure from the Iran conflict and US steel tariffs, potentially delaying customer projects. Middle East operations have managed conflict-related productivity dips; this remains live.

**Regional softness.** South Florida residential, UK volumes, western European infrastructure spend, and Moretrench Industrial remediation deferrals partially offset NA strength. APAC order book fell 18.9% cc on fewer marine contracts.

**Non-underlying ERP costs.** Cloud ERP implementation cost £4.7m in H1 2026 (non-underlying); phased roll-out from 2027 carries further spend and integration risk.

**Governance/regulatory.** Multi-jurisdictional compliance (including UK “failure to prevent fraud” offence, September 2025), CSRD, and Pillar Two top-up tax (currently immaterial). No material going-concern qualification; covenants comfortably met.

**Pension.** Net defined benefit liabilities £15.2m; UK scheme fully funded on IAS 19 with buy-in completed in H1 2026—not a primary red flag.

**Valuation/screen mismatch.** P/B 3.0x and current ratio 1.4 fail defensive value screens; the stock is not statistically cheap on asset or net-net measures despite strong FCF metrics.

**Liquidity seasonality.** H1 2026 operating cash conversion of 36% and £87.5m working-capital outflow warrant monitoring, though undrawn facilities and net cash position provide ample headroom.
