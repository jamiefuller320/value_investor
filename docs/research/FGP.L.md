# FirstGroup plc (FGP.L) — Research memo

_Version 2 · Updated 2026-08-03T21:32:05.726789+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
FirstGroup is a UK-focused public transport operator (First Bus ~78% of adjusted revenue; First Rail ~22%) trading on low multiples with strong reported cash generation and an active capital-return programme. The quantitative screen rates the name a **strong buy** (11/22 models, 91% composite), driven by earnings yield, FCF yield, dividend growth, and Piotroski F-Score 8/9. Primary filings show adjusted operating profit rising from £204.3m (FY2024) to £222.8m (FY2025) and adjusted EPS from 16.7p to 19.4p, but adjusted net cash of £64.1m flipped to adjusted net debt of £86.9m as growth capex and the RATP London acquisition absorbed cash. The central debate is whether cheap headline metrics and contract wins (notably ~£3bn London Overground from May 2026) outweigh UK bus/rail policy transition, lease-adjusted leverage, and thin liquidity ratios that several quality screens fail.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, FirstGroup offers a rare combination of **statutory-scale cash businesses** (FY2025 statutory revenue £5.1bn per RNS) with **screen-level cheapness**: P/E 9.3, P/B 1.4, earnings yield 10.8%, FCF yield ~31% (screen TTM), and dividend yield 3.9%. Five factor families pass—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—supported by improving operational trends in filings: adjusted revenue up 17% to £1,370.0m and adjusted operating margin stable at ~16.3% (FY2025 annual report).

Business quality is better than pure cyclical transport: DfT rail contracts carry limited revenue risk; open access (Lumo, Hull Trains) and Additional services add higher-margin growth; First Bus is scaling adjacent services and decarbonisation with government co-funding. Management has returned capital consistently (~£92m buybacks in FY2025; ongoing programmes per news) while targeting at least flat adjusted EPS in FY2026 off a diversified base. The screen’s strong-buy signal aligns with a post-North-America-exit, UK-pure-play operator generating cash through operational improvement rather than financial engineering—though failed Graham, moat, and financial-health models flag leverage and liquidity as offsets to the value case.
