# FirstGroup plc (FGP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-04T08:45:33.506664+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
FirstGroup plc is a UK-focused public transport operator (First Bus and First Rail) that the quantitative screen rates as a strong buy: 11 of 22 models pass, composite score 91%, with cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families all clearing. Filings show a sustained earnings trajectory — adjusted EPS rose from 10.6p (FY 2023) to 16.7p (FY 2024) and 19.4p (FY 2025), with H1 2026 at 9.9p (+16% year-on-year) — supported by bus yield improvement, rail diversification and aggressive capital return. The valuation hook is low headline multiples (screen: P/E 9.2, FCF yield 37.3%, dividend 3.9%) against a business generating filing-aligned free cash flow and returning surplus via buybacks and a progressive dividend. The central debate is whether adjusted earnings and cash conversion survive UK bus policy headwinds (£3 fare cap, franchising), DfT TOC transition under Great British Railways, and a capex-heavy electrification cycle that pushed H1 2026 to a £35.6m free cash outflow and adjusted net debt of £207.6m. London Overground preferred-operator status (~£3bn contract, revenue risk with TfL) is a material positive offset, but statutory balance-sheet leverage and weak liquidity (screen: current ratio 0.73, debt/equity 161%) limit deep-value comfort.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, FirstGroup offers a rare combination of sub-10x earnings, high cash yield and an improving adjusted earnings base after the North America exit and portfolio refocus. The screen’s strong buy signal rests on earnings yield, FCF yield, low P/E plus yield, Neff PEGY, dividend growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Piotroski F-Score (8/9) and composite value — five factor families with full data quality (20/20 metrics). Business quality is corroborated by filing evidence: FY 2025 adjusted operating profit £222.8m on adjusted revenue £1,370.0m; First Bus reached a 10% H2 2025 margin target; open access rail and Rail Services are scaling; and management targets modest FY 2026 adjusted EPS growth then at least maintenance in FY 2027. Capital discipline is explicit — leverage policy below 2.0x adjusted net debt to Rail adjusted EBITDA, progressive dividend (~3x cover), and £92m of buybacks in FY 2025 plus ongoing programmes — which aligns with the screen’s dividend and risk passes despite failing Graham-style balance-sheet screens. The stock screens cheap on normalised cash economics; filings suggest the discount partly reflects transition risk rather than structural impairment, though leverage and IFRS 16 lease intensity mean this is not a classic net-net or deep-value balance-sheet play.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Regulatory and policy: The FY 2025 annual report flags bus franchising expansion (Manchester and other mayoral authorities) and the Railways Bill / Great British Railways, with legislation passed November 2024 enabling nationalisation of DfT TOCs over coming years. SWR transferred to DfTO in May 2025; FY 2026 guides lower DfT TOC adjusted revenue and profit and ~£40m IFRS 16 EBIT impact. Open access remains outside GBR scope but depends on ORR track-access approvals. The £3 fare cap (from January 2025) has pressured commercial bus volumes (-7% in H1 2026).

Balance sheet and liquidity: Screen failures on Graham Defensive, Financial Health, Buffett Quality and Economic Moat cite high debt/equity (161%), current ratio below 2 (0.73) and thin margins. Statutory net debt ~£992m at H1 2026 is dominated by IFRS 16 lease liabilities (£1,017.2m); adjusted net debt is lower but rose from £86.9m (FY 2025) to £207.6m (H1 2026). H1 free cash outflow and elevated capex (~£180m net bus capex guided for FY 2026) are near-term pressures.

Pension and contingencies: LGPS termination created large non-cash FY 2024 volatility. Escrow balances (~£65m bus section; group section ~£23m per FY 2025 presentation) remain. London Overground requires £30m performance bond and £80m parent company guarantee (RNS, 10 December 2025).

Operational and cyclical: First Bus London contracts were loss-making initially (FY 2026 guided £10–12m cash outflow excluding vehicle capex). Electrification and Hitachi train orders (~£500m) tie up capital. Competition in open access and labour/driver availability persist. Earnings growth on the screen is slightly negative (-5.9%), reflecting statutory/normalisation effects rather than filing-adjusted trajectory.

Governance: No adverse going-concern or covenant breach language in available bodies. Leverage policy (<2.0x adjusted net debt to Rail adjusted EBITDA) provides an internal guardrail; covenant detail not extracted from filing bodies.
