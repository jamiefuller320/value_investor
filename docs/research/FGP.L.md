# FirstGroup plc (FGP.L) — Research memo

_Version 3 · Updated 2026-08-30T07:47:54.096133+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
FirstGroup is a UK-focused bus and rail operator emerging from a deliberate portfolio transition: legacy North American and contracted-rail exposure has been reduced, while First Bus (including London franchising) and a diversified First Rail platform (DfT management-fee TOCs, open access, and rail services) drive adjusted earnings. FY2026 adjusted EPS rose to 20.3p (from 19.4p) on adjusted revenue of £1,718.7m, yet adjusted operating profit was marginally lower at £219.4m as open-access mobilisation and the South Western Railway (SWR) handback weighed on margins. The quantitative screen flags compelling value (P/E ~8.9, yield ~4.0%, Piotroski 8/9), but free-cash-flow definitions diverge materially (£362.6m statutory vs £73.8m company-adjusted), and leverage remains elevated under IFRS 16. The central debate is whether contract wins (London Overground), bus consolidation, and capital returns can sustain earnings growth through UK rail reform and accelerated bus decarbonisation capex.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy rating rests on five passing factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with 20/20 data quality and standout metrics: P/E 8.9, P/B 1.37, earnings yield, FCF yield (on filing-aligned basis), Lynch/Neff PEG variants, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, and Piotroski F-Score 8/9. That profile fits a value investor seeking a cash-generative industrial at a discount to its own recent earnings trajectory: adjusted EPS has compounded from 16.7p (FY2024) to 19.4p (FY2025) to 20.3p (FY2026), with dividends rising from 5.5p to 6.5p to 7.2p.

Business quality is better than the screen’s leverage-sensitive failures (Graham Defensive, Buffett Quality, Financial Health) imply. Management’s UK-only strategy is delivering visible wins: First Bus London expansion, open-access passenger growth, Rail Services externalisation, and the preferred-operator status for the London Overground contract (RNS, 10 December 2025). Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly—£89m returned in FY2026 via dividends and buybacks, a £100m buyback launched June 2026, and progressive dividend policy tightening toward ~2.5x adjusted-EPS cover. The valuation hook is a mid-single-digit earnings multiple on a business reinvesting in electrification while de-risking the pension bus section (£20m cash returned on triennial valuation completion, H1 2026 report).

For a value investor, the mismatch between statutory and adjusted metrics is the opportunity *and* the diligence item: the screen passes on filing-aligned FCF (£362.6m) and earnings quality, but company-adjusted FCF of £73.8m (FY2026 annual accounts) reflects heavy growth capex and acquisition spend that may be transitional rather than structural.
