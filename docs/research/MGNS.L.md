_Version 3 · Updated 2026-09-12T12:36:40.951261+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Morgan Sindall Group plc is a UK-listed partnerships, fit-out and construction services group whose recent results show record revenue, strong adjusted profit growth and a net-cash balance sheet, while the quantitative screen flags a **strong buy** on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (16/22 models; composite ~80%). The value case rests on low double-digit earnings yield, ~4% dividend with ~2.4× cover on filing-based free cash flow, high ROE and a Piotroski F-score of 8/9, set against a cyclical sector that rarely passes “moat” or deep-value screens. The central debate is whether Fit Out and public-sector-heavy Construction/Infrastructure can keep carrying earnings while Partnership Housing and Mixed Use Partnerships absorb capital and margin pressure from a subdued private housing market. The share price sits below its 200-day moving average with neutral timing, which may help entry but does not remove cycle risk.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MGNS combines **enterprising Graham-style valuation** (P/E ~10.5, P/B ~2.4, debt/equity ~18%) with **quality and cash attributes** that are atypical for contractors: year-end net cash of **£531m** (FY2025 RNS), average daily net cash **£368m**, operating cash conversion cited at **87%** of adjusted operating profit, and filing-aligned FCF of **£170.7m** (screen policy basis) supporting dividend growth (total dividend **158p**, +20% y/y). The screen passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk—and leads its industrial peer set on model count, consistent with a compounder-like track record (management cites ~**18% CAGR** in adjusted PBT over ten years in the FY2025 results statement).

Business quality in filing terms is not uniform: **Fit Out** is the profit engine (FY2025 operating profit **£139.9m** on revenue **£1,784m**, margin **7.8%**), while **Partnership Housing** and **Mixed Use Partnerships** invest heavily in long-dated public-sector regeneration (secured plus preferred-bidder workload **£19.1bn** group-wide at FY2025). That mix explains why screens reward earnings yield and financial health yet fail Economic Moat (contracting ROA/margin thresholds) and Graham Defensive (current ratio ~1.23). The strong-buy signal is therefore best read as **quality-at-a-reasonable-price in a cyclical name**, not net-net or defensive compounder. The screen’s FCF TTM vs filing mismatch (£141.5m vs £170.7m) is noted in the snapshot; primary filing cash flow supports the yield case but warrants consistency checks before sizing.
