# Morgan Sindall Group plc (MGNS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-13T07:53:44.196706+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Morgan Sindall is a UK-listed partnerships, fit-out and construction services group that has delivered a sustained run of record profits, net cash balance sheet strength, and a visible workload (secured order book plus preferred-bidder pipeline). The quantitative screen rates the name as a strong buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families (16/22 models, composite ~81%), anchored by roughly 10.5× earnings, ~4.1% yield, ~26% return on equity and filing-aligned free cash flow of about £171m for FY 2025. The valuation hook is a cyclical industrial compounder trading at a modest multiple despite adj. EPS growth and cash returns, not a net-net or deep-value special situation. The key debate is whether Fit Out and public-sector-backed Construction/Infrastructure can keep offsetting housing-related capital drag and margin normalisation, and whether the market is already discounting a housing recovery that remains slow in filings.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MGNS combines screen-friendly metrics with operating evidence of discipline: low leverage (debt/equity ~18% on the screen), Piotroski F-Score 8/9, dividend growth (158p total FY 2025, cover ~2.4× on an adjusted basis per the FY RNS), and earnings/FCF yield screens that pass alongside quality and financial health models. Business quality in filings is stronger in Fit Out (adj. operating profit £139.9m on £1,784m revenue, 7.8% margin in FY 2025) and improving Construction, while Partnership Housing and Mixed Use Partnerships absorb rising capital employed (£452m and £152m year-end capital employed respectively at FY 2025) with ROCE below medium-term ambitions. The decentralised five-division model and stated contract selectivity (frameworks, two-stage procurement) support the screen’s quality and risk passes, even though “economic moat” and Graham defensive tests fail (P/B ~2.4, current ratio ~1.2). The strong-buy signal has been persistent (~15 weeks) with neutral timing (RSI ~32, price below 200-day MA), which fits a accumulate-on-weakness stance rather than momentum chasing. Screen note: unverified TTM FCF (~£142m) diverges from filing FY 2025 FCF (£161.5m per RNS; Yahoo cash flow also shows £170.7m FCF for 2025)—yield screens should be traced to the filing-aligned £171m policy figure.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Filings emphasise cyclical and sector-specific risks: economic uncertainty, UK private housing exposure (slow open-market sales, planning, viability on regeneration), building safety obligations under the Developers Pledge and Building Safety Act (provisions ~£51.8m building safety at 30 June 2026; reimbursements to the Building Safety Fund potentially from H2 2026), partner insolvency and supply-chain strain, skills and culture in a decentralised model, working capital and partnership capital intensity (inventories £693.5m at HY 2026, up £134m vs prior HY), and elevated cyber risk (ISO 27001 / Cyber Essentials Plus cited). Contract and contingent liabilities on bonds, guarantees and claims are normal for the sector but material in aggregate. Governance: long-tenured CEO, committee restructuring (Responsible Business Committee dissolved February 2026), and routine employee share plan dilution—not flagged as governance failure in sources. Pension risk is not prominent in extracted bodies (minimal pension line in Yahoo 2023 balance sheet). Leverage is low in screen terms but partnership ROCE (Partnership Housing ROCE 8% LTM at HY 2026 vs 10% target path) is a return risk, not a solvency red flag given net cash. Screen fails on deep value and moat metrics remind that margins are good but not “wide moat” by ROA/margin rules.
