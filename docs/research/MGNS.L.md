# Morgan Sindall Group plc (MGNS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-30T06:54:10.312819+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Morgan Sindall is a UK-focused construction and regeneration group spanning partnership housing, mixed-use placemaking, market-leading fit-out, construction, and infrastructure. FY2025 delivered a record adjusted profit before tax of £232.6m on revenue of £5,019m, with year-end net cash of £531m and a secured order book of £12.0bn plus £7.1bn at preferred-bidder stage. The quantitative screen rates the stock a Strong Buy on cheapness (P/E 11.5, yield 3.7%), quality (ROE 26.1%, Piotroski 8/9), cash generation, and financial health (D/E 18%), though it fails deep-value and Graham Defensive thresholds on P/B and current ratio. The central debate is whether Fit Out and public-sector-backed construction can sustain earnings momentum while partnership housing absorbs capital in a subdued private market — a tension visible in H1 2026, where group profit rose 21% but Partnership Housing full-year profit is guided slightly below 2025.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy signal aligns with a business that combines cyclical construction exposure with structurally supported end markets (affordable housing, education, healthcare, energy infrastructure, defence) and an unusually strong balance sheet for the sector. Sixteen of twenty-two models pass across five families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with full data quality (20/20 metrics) and filing-aligned free cash flow of £170.7m covering dividends roughly 2.6× (net basis). Adjusted EPS grew 33% to 370.0p in FY2025 and the dividend rose 20% to 158p, with cover of 2.4× within the stated 2.0–2.5× policy; management cites a ten-year adjusted PBT CAGR of 18%. Fit Out (operating profit £139.9m, margin 7.8%) and Construction/Infrastructure provide cash and margin discipline, funding long-dated partnership pipelines (£19.1bn secured plus preferred-bidder work at year-end 2025). At 11.5× earnings and 2.7× book, the market prices MGNS as a mid-cycle contractor rather than a compounder with net cash and recurring public-sector frameworks — the value case the screen captures. Timing is neutral (RSI ~42, below 200-day MA), which suits phased accumulation rather than momentum chasing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / housing.** Filings repeatedly flag subdued private housing demand, affordability, and planning delays. Partnership Housing open-market completions fell (785 units FY2025, -10%); H1 2026 open-market sales -23%. Capital employed in partnerships is rising (£566m period-end HY2026) while ROCE has slipped to 8% (12-month basis). Mixed Use Partnerships remains loss-making near term despite a £4.6bn order book.

**Regulatory / building safety.** Exceptional building safety charge was modest (£0.4m FY2025; nil H1 2026), but provisions remain material — building safety provision £51.8m at 30 June 2026 per interim notes, with Building Safety Fund reimbursements potentially commencing H2 2026. Contingent liabilities on cladding, defects, and JV guarantees are disclosed; insurance recoveries recognised when virtually certain.

**Working capital / liquidity.** Risk status in the annual report marks *mismanagement of working capital* as increased: WIP on open-market developments is elevated though net cash buffers are large (£270m lowest daily balance FY2025). H1 free cash outflow of £27.5m is seasonal but bears monitoring.

**Operational.** Principal risks per FY2025 annual report and results RNS include partner/subcontractor insolvency (partially mitigated by public-sector skew), contract selectivity, project delivery, skills shortages, and climate transition execution. Cyber risk is flagged as *increasing* despite ISO 27001 and MOD certification.

**Governance.** Board changes (dissolution of Responsible Business Committee; expanded CFO/commercial remit for Kelly Gangotra from April 2026) are administrative rather than red flags. No going-concern qualification.

**Screen blind spots.** Current ratio 1.23 fails Graham Defensive; P/B 2.7 fails deep-value screens; Economic Moat model fails on ROA and margins — consistent with low-margin construction mix despite high ROE from equity efficiency and net cash.
