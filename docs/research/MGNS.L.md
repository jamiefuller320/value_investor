# Morgan Sindall Group plc (MGNS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-10T16:20:39.943489+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Morgan Sindall is a UK partnerships, fit-out and construction services group trading at a modest earnings multiple despite a multi-year record of profit growth, net-cash balance sheet strength, and a secured order book of roughly £19bn plus preferred-bidder work. FY2025 (year ended 31 December 2025) delivered adjusted profit before tax of £232.6m on revenue of £5,019m (+35% and +10% year-on-year respectively), with year-end net cash of £531m; H1 2026 continued the momentum with adjusted PBT of £116.1m (+21%) on revenue of £2,562m. The quantitative screen rates the name a **strong buy** (16/22 models; composite 78%), consistent with cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk factors passing together. The central debate is whether Fit Out and Construction can sustain elevated margins while Partnership Housing and Mixed Use Partnerships absorb rising capital in a subdued private housing market — a tension the screen’s quality metrics may under-weight.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MGNS offers a rare combination: **statutory-level cheapness on earnings and cash** (screen P/E 11.6, FCF yield supportive, debt/equity 18%, Piotroski F-Score 8/9) layered on **demonstrated business quality** (ROE 26.1%, ten-year adjusted PBT CAGR of 18% per management, dividend raised 20% to 158p in FY2025 with 2.4x cover). The screen passes Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Lynch/Neff PEG variants, Buffett Quality, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, Earnings Quality and Financial Health — five of five factor families.

The economic moat is not classical (screen fails Economic Moat on ROA/margins), but operational moat-like characteristics exist: market leadership in Fit Out (operating profit £139.9m in FY2025, margin 7.8%), framework-heavy Construction/Infrastructure order books (~98% framework-derived in Infrastructure), and long-dated public-sector partnership pipelines. Net cash and £180m of undrawn committed facilities (per H1 2026 interim accounts) support contract selectivity and counter-cyclical bidding — a tangible advantage in UK construction.

What the screen does **not** fully capture is **capital intensity in housing**: Partnership Housing average capital employed rose to £446m in FY2025 (from £338m in FY2024) with ROCE at 10% versus an 8% margin / ~25% ROCE medium-term target. That is value-accretive only if public-sector partnership volumes offset weak open-market sales — management guides FY2026 Partnership Housing profit slightly below FY2025. The strong-buy signal is therefore best read as **quality compounder at a cyclical discount**, not deep asset value (P/B 2.7; screen fails Graham Defensive, Net-Net, Schloss, Deep Value).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**UK residential / capital cycle (elevated).** Management repeatedly cites subdued private housing demand, affordability, planning delays, and rising partnership capital employed (FY2025 average £446m; H1 2026 period-end £566m). ROCE fell to 8% (12-month) in H1 2026. Open-market unit completions down; London apartment sales slow. Annual report rates residential exposure **Stable** but working-capital/investment mismanagement risk **Increased**.

**Mixed Use Partnerships losses.** FY2025 operating loss £5.3m; H1 2026 loss £1.1m; full-year 2026 expected **small loss** as schemes start on site. Long-dated £4.6bn order book supports optionality but near-term earnings drag.

**Building safety / cladding.** Exceptional building safety charge £0.4m in FY2025; provisions ~£51.8m at 30 June 2026 (building safety line). Group signed Developers Pledge; reimbursements to Building Safety Fund may commence H2 2026. Insurance receivables partially offset. Contingent liabilities noted for cladding/legacy fire matters (H1 note 12).

**Fit Out normalisation.** FY2025 was exceptional (+41% profit); H1 2026 strong but management guides FY2026 profit **below 2025** yet above raised medium-term target (£100m–£130m). Order book £1.3bn (−9% YoY at FY2025 ye) — monitor for mean reversion.

**Cyber security.** Annual report flags cyber risk as **Increased**; ISO 27001 and MOD certification cited as mitigants.

**Governance / concentration.** Founder-CEO John Morgan remains central; decentralised model is a strength but key-person dependent. February 2026 board changes dissolved Responsible Business Committee; CFO Kelly Gangotra becomes Chief Financial and Commercial Officer from April 2026.

**Cyclical / macro.** Filings cite geopolitical uncertainty, tariff impacts, and client budget pressure lengthening preconstruction. No going-concern qualification; covenant and liquidity language is supportive.

**Screen blind spots.** Fails Graham Defensive (current ratio 1.23 < 2; P/B not low enough), High Dividend Yield quartile, Economic Moat — consistent with a **quality GARP** not deep-value profile.
