# Morgan Sindall Group plc (MGNS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-04T09:01:39.691255+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Morgan Sindall is a UK partnerships, fit-out, and construction services group delivering a sustained run of record profits, rising dividends, and a net-cash balance sheet — a profile that aligns well with a quantitative **Strong Buy** (16/22 models; composite 77%). At roughly 11.6× earnings, 2.7× book, and a 3.7% yield, the market prices in cyclicality more than the decade-long 18% adjusted PBT CAGR cited in the 2025 annual report. The investment hook is quality compounder trading at mid-cycle multiples: Fit Out and Infrastructure offset housing weakness, while a record £11.97bn secured workload and £19.1bn at preferred-bidder stage underpin forward visibility. The key debate is whether public-sector and regeneration tailwinds (Affordable Homes Programme, National Housing Bank) outweigh private-housing softness, margin pressure in Property Services, and construction-cycle working-capital risk. Price sits below the 200-day moving average with neutral timing (RSI ~39), suggesting entry patience rather than a broken thesis.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags MGNS across five factor families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with full data coverage (20/20 metrics) and a Piotroski F-Score of 8/9. That is unusual for a mid-cap UK contractor and reflects genuine business quality rather than a distressed deep-value setup.

**Valuation vs quality.** P/E 11.6 and earnings yield screen passes sit alongside ROE of 26.1%, debt/equity of 18%, and positive, growing free cash flow — a GARP profile, not a net-net. Failed screens (Graham Defensive, Net-Net, Deep Value, Economic Moat) are consistent with a capital-light-but-working-capital-intensive contractor: current ratio 1.23 and ROA below moat thresholds are sector norms, not distress signals.

**Earnings momentum and capital returns.** Primary filings show revenue rising from £3,213m (2021) to £5,018.6m (2025) and adjusted operating profit from £131.3m to £225.7m over the same period. Adjusted PBT reached £233m in 2025 (+35% YoY per the CEO statement), with the total dividend up 20% to 158p — extending a compounding dividend track (92p → 101p → 114p → 131.5p → 158p). The group self-funds growth, repurchases shares, and maintains substantial net cash; Yahoo (fallback) shows FY2025 cash of £590.5m against total debt of £132.9m.

**Order book and mix.** Secured workload grew from £8,614m (2021) to £11,972.2m (2025). Fit Out (£1,784m revenue) and Construction/Infrastructure (£1,159m and £935m) drive scale; Partnership Housing and Mixed Use Partnerships provide medium-term regeneration optionality. Management upgraded medium-term targets for Mixed Use Partnerships and Infrastructure in 2025, signalling confidence beyond the current cycle.

**Why Strong Buy for a value investor.** The name combines Graham enterprising-style valuation (low P/E, moderate P/B, yield support) with Buffett/Magic Formula quality metrics and financial health — a “quality at a reasonable price” contractor, not a balance-sheet bargain. Cyclical headline risk is partially mitigated by net cash, public-sector mix (~77% of Construction/Infrastructure in public/regulated sectors per 2023 annual report), and a decentralised, contract-selective culture emphasised consistently across filings.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and end-market exposure.** 2025 and prior annual reports flag subdued private housing and apartment sales, with gradual recovery expected as rates ease. Partnership Housing experienced slower private-home sales on mixed-tenure sites (2023 filing: Help to Buy expiry, mortgage rates, planning delays). Urban regeneration schemes face build-cost inflation affecting viability on selected projects — “not material to the overall portfolio” per 2023 language, but tail risk on individual developments.

**Contracting and inflation.** Construction and Infrastructure rely on contractual inflation protection on live jobs; new tenders face budget pressure and delayed client decisions (2023 filing). Margin preservation is an explicit management focus (2024 CEO statement).

**Supply chain and subcontractor insolvency.** 2025 filing describes Partnership Housing stepping in where clients’ original contractors entered administration — operational capability, but also sector stress signal. Board oversight of supply-chain payment practices and insolvency mitigation is documented.

**Property Services turnaround.** Division loss in 2023; small revenue base (£212m in 2025) but reputational and margin risk if inflation re-accelerates on fixed-price maintenance contracts.

**Working capital and liquidity.** Current ratio 1.23 (screen) sits below Graham Defensive thresholds. Inventory and receivable build on Yahoo FY2025 data increases balance-sheet intensity if revenue growth slows.

**Governance and pension.** Filings reference principal risks (pages cited in extracts) and going-concern/viability statements, but full going-concern text is not legible in the OCR extracts reviewed — **cannot confirm covenant or going-concern language from body text.** Yahoo shows negligible pension liabilities on 2023 balance sheet (£0.2m non-current); no detailed 2025 pension disclosure extracted.

**Screen blind spots.** Economic Moat failure (ROA, margins) reflects contractor economics, not fraud risk. Failed High Dividend Yield screen indicates yield is supportive but not top-quartile — dividend sustainability relies on continued FCF, not excessive payout.

**Data quality.** Reliance on OCR-degraded Companies House PDFs for some 2025 headline numbers; absence of interim RNS limits timeliness of financial review.
