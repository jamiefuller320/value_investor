# Morgan Sindall Group plc (MGNS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T10:52:03.849579+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Morgan Sindall Group plc (MGNS.L) is a UK-listed partnerships, fit-out and construction services group trading at roughly 11× earnings with a net-cash balance sheet, a 3.8% dividend yield and a record £19bn+ secured order book. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy (16/22 models; composite 78%), supported by cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families passing together. Filings confirm accelerating earnings: FY2025 adjusted profit before tax rose 35% to £232.6m and H1 2026 adjusted PBT rose 21% to £116.1m, with management reiterating full-year expectations. The central debate is whether Fit Out and public-sector construction momentum sufficiently offset housing-related capital absorption, inventory build and cyclical construction risk — not whether the franchise is weak.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal aligns with a business that combines cyclical construction exposure with unusually strong balance-sheet quality and cash conversion. Morgan Sindall passes 16 of 22 value models, including Graham Enterprising, earnings yield, FCF yield, Lynch PEG, Piotroski F-Score (8/9), Buffett Quality and financial health, with debt/equity near 18% and ROE above 26% on screen inputs. That is unusual for a contractor.

Operationally, the group is not a commodity builder. It is a decentralised portfolio of five divisions — Partnership Housing, Mixed Use Partnerships, Fit Out, Construction and Infrastructure — with roughly 87% of Fit Out revenue from commercial offices and a large share of workload secured via frameworks and negotiated two-stage procurement with public and regulated clients. FY2025 revenue of £5,019m (+10%) and adjusted operating margin of 4.5% (+90bps) reflect Fit Out’s market-leading £139.9m operating profit (+41%) and improving Construction margins, while net cash of £531m and free cash flow of £161.5m (per FY2025 RNS) underpin dividend growth (158p total, +20%; cover 2.4×).

For a value investor, the hook is quality compounder characteristics — ten-year adjusted PBT CAGR cited at 18% — priced like a mid-cycle industrial: P/E 11.0, P/B 2.5, FCF yield screen ~2.6× net dividend cover. Failures on Graham Defensive (current ratio 1.23) and deep-value/net-net screens reflect contractor working-capital mechanics, not distress. Timing is neutral (RSI ~33; price below 200-day MA), which suits phased accumulation rather than momentum chasing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and housing exposure.** Filings repeatedly flag subdued private housing demand, affordability constraints and planning delays. Partnership Housing H1 2026 revenue fell 14% to £347m; full-year 2026 operating profit is now expected slightly below FY2025 despite a £2.5bn+ order book. Mixed Use Partnerships reported a £5.3m FY2025 loss and a small H1 2026 loss, with FY2026 now expected to show a small full-year loss as schemes start on site. Capital employed in Partnership Housing rose to £566m at H1 2026 (+30% vs prior period-end), compressing ROCE to 8% (from 10%).

**Working capital and investment intensity.** Inventories/work-in-progress rose £127m in FY2025 and a further £90m in H1 2026, driven by partnership development activity and slower open-market sales (206 exchanged-but-not-completed units at H1 vs 73 prior year). The annual report flags “mismanagement of working capital” risk status as *increasing*, though average daily net cash remains robust (£423m H1 2026).

**Regulatory and legacy building safety.** Building Safety Act obligations remain live: H1 2026 building safety provisions £51.8m; filings note potential Building Safety Fund reimbursements from H2 2026. Contingent liabilities on surety bonds, guarantees and cladding-related claims are disclosed; insurance mitigants recognised where virtually certain. Not a balance-sheet crisis, but a persistent overhang.

**Operational and competitive.** Construction remains competitively bid despite framework selectivity. Partner insolvency risk is flagged as contained but present; supply-chain stress from NI/tax changes noted. Health and safety, contract selectivity and project delivery risks are standard but material — LTIR improved to 0.18 in FY2025.

**Governance and cyber.** Cyber-attack risk is rated *increasing* in the 2025 annual report appendix; ISO 27001 and MOD certification cited as mitigants. Responsible Business Committee dissolved (February 2026); responsibilities reallocated to Board/Audit Committee — minor governance streamlining, not a red flag on its own.

**Liquidity and leverage.** Low concern: net cash £418–531m across reporting dates, undrawn £180m RCF to 2028, going concern to July 2027. No pension deficit flagged in available extracts. Screen current ratio 1.23 fails Graham Defensive threshold — typical for negative working-capital construction models.
