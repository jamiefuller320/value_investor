# Morgan Sindall Group plc (MGNS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-10T20:29:09.302659+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Morgan Sindall is a UK partnerships, fit-out and construction services group delivering a sustained run of record profits, underpinned by a net-cash balance sheet and a secured order book of £12.2bn plus £7.3bn at preferred-bidder stage (H1 2026). FY 2025 adjusted profit before tax rose 35% to £232.6m on revenue of £5,019m; H1 2026 adjusted PBT rose 21% to £116.1m, with management reiterating full-year expectations. The quantitative screen flags the stock as cheap for its quality profile (P/E 11.6, ROE 26.1%, Piotroski 8/9, dividend yield 3.6%), though it fails deep-value and Graham Defensive thresholds on P/B and liquidity. The central debate is whether Fit Out and Construction momentum can offset housing-related capital lock-up and cyclical exposure in Partnerships, and whether the market is under-pricing a durable compounder or correctly discounting UK construction and residential risk.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy signal rests on a rare combination for UK industrials: **cheapness plus quality plus cash returns**. Sixteen of twenty-two models pass, spanning cheapness (Graham Enterprising, earnings yield, FCF yield, low P/E + high yield), quality (Buffett Quality, Magic Formula, Piotroski F-Score 8/9), dividend growth, GARP (Lynch PEG), and risk (Dreman, financial health). Composite score is 0.77 (sector-relative 0.80) with full data quality (20/20 metrics).

Business quality supports the screen rather than contradicting it. Management reports an **18% CAGR in adjusted PBT over ten years** (FY 2025 RNS). FY 2025 adjusted operating margin expanded 90bps to 4.5%; Fit Out operating profit grew 41% to £139.9m at a 7.8% margin. The group ended FY 2025 with **net cash of £531m** and FY 2025 free cash flow of **£161.5m**, funding a **20% dividend increase to 158p** (cover 2.4x, within the 2.0–2.5x policy). H1 2026 net cash was £418m with average daily net cash of **£423m**, above the >£400m guidance.

For a value investor, the hook is a **cash-rich, order-book-backed earnings compounder trading at ~11x earnings** with a visible public-sector and infrastructure pipeline (education, healthcare, nuclear, energy transmission, affordable housing) and a decentralised model that has repeatedly upgraded medium-term divisional targets. The screen’s neutral timing (RSI 42, price marginally below 200-day MA) aligns with accumulating on weakness rather than chasing momentum. Failures on Graham Defensive, deep value, and economic moat are instructive: this is **quality-at-a-reasonable-price**, not net-net or ultra-defensive.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**UK residential and capital intensity.** Filings repeatedly flag subdued private housing demand, affordability, and planning delays. Partnership Housing average capital employed rose to **£445.7m** (FY 2025) and **£506.9m** (H1 2026); FY 2026 guidance expects **£500m–£580m** average capital employed with **FY 2026 operating profit slightly below FY 2025**. ROCE fell to **8%** (12-month, H1 2026). Mixed Use Partnerships expected a **small FY 2026 loss** as schemes start on site. Annual Report rates **working capital and investments** risk as **increased**, noting WIP build on open-market developments.

**Building safety.** Exceptional building safety charge **£0.4m** in FY 2025; provisions of **£51.8m** at 30 June 2026 (plus insurance receivables). Repayments to the Building Safety Fund may commence H2 2026. Legacy cladding/fire obligations remain a contingent tail.

**Cyclical and macro.** Principal risks include economic uncertainty, tariff/geopolitical disruption, and post-inflationary client budgets stretching preconstruction timelines. Fit Out is **75% London / 87% commercial office** — concentration if office demand normalises after the current surge (FY 2025 Fit Out order book −9% YoY; normalisation flagged).

**Operational.** Health & safety, skills shortages, partner insolvency (supply-chain stress post-NI increases), and **cyber attack** (risk rated **increased**; ISO 27001 and MoD certification noted as mitigants).

**Governance / structure.** Feb 2026 board changes: dissolution of Responsible Business Committee; Kelly Gangotra becomes **Chief Financial and Commercial Officer** from April 2026. Property Services integrated into Construction from 1 January 2026 (remediation complete; modest £2m FY 2025 profit).

**Financial structure.** Net cash is a strength, but **current ratio ~1.23** (screen) fails Graham Defensive liquidity tests. Screen fails **economic moat** on ROA and margins — returns are good but not wide-moat by quantitative definition.

**Pension.** No material pension deficit highlighted in available filing extracts; not a flagged principal risk in the 2025 Annual Report appendix.
