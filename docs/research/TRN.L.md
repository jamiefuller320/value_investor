# Trainline plc (TRN.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T07:31:24.105243+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Trainline is Europe’s leading independent rail ticketing platform, with FY2026 net ticket sales of £6.3 billion and a scaled app franchise across the UK and continental Europe. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cheapness, quality, GARP and risk metrics (P/E ~10.1, FCF yield ~9.6%, ROE ~32.8%, PEG ~0.27), with timing favouring accumulation (RSI ~32, trading below the 200-day moving average). Filings show a multi-year earnings inflection: revenue rose from £327 million (FY2023) to £453 million (FY2026), with operating profit reaching £122 million and basic EPS of 19.4p. The central debate is whether structural growth in European rail liberalisation and AI-led distribution outweigh UK regulatory overhangs—Great British Railways (GBR) market design, operator self-preferencing, and a live CMA drip-pricing probe—plus rising leverage from aggressive buybacks (£294 million repurchased since September 2023). Valuation looks attractive on screen inputs, but the equity is not a clean regulatory pass.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Trainline offers a rare combination: a capital-light, high-ROE aggregator (screen ROE 32.8%) trading at a low-teens P/E despite double-digit adjusted EBITDA growth and improving free cash conversion. The screen passes 11 of 22 models, including Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Lynch PEG (0.27), Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Piotroski F-Score and Composite Value—suggesting earnings quality and cash generation are not purely cyclical artefacts.

Business quality supports the screen. FY2026 filings show 27 million active customers, #1 European rail app downloads, and Trainline Solutions net ticket sales up 15% (international B2B +58%). International Consumer adjusted EBITDA losses narrowed from £20 million to £11 million, with breakeven guided for FY2027. UK ancillary revenue (hotels, insurance) and digital railcards (2.7 million users, +16%) deepen monetisation beyond headline commission rates.

Capital return amplifies per-share value: basic EPS rose 48% to 19.4p as diluted average shares fell from ~461 million (FY2025) to ~417 million (FY2026). At ~£191.50 (screen reference price), earnings yield and FCF yield screen among the best in FTSE mid-caps. The buy case is a profitable, oligopolistic distribution layer on growing European rail markets, mispriced after regulatory headlines and UK reform uncertainty.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory (UK).** GBR Online Retail procurement (contract award targeted January 2027) creates structural uncertainty over third-party retailer economics. Filings flag TOC self-preferencing (automated Delay Repay, loyalty schemes locked to operator channels) and **Project Oval** (~£150m UK Consumer net ticket sales at risk). March 2026 government commitment to open Delay Repay to third-party retailers is positive but implementation is years away. **CMA investigation** (19 August 2026 RNS) into drip-pricing/fee presentation under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 triggered a ~14% share fall in press coverage; outcome and potential remediation costs are unresolved.

**Regulatory (Europe).** EU Mobility Package and French Senate amendments on FRAND distribution could help intermediaries long term, but timing and scope are uncertain.

**Commercial / competitive.** UK headline commission reduction (MOU, effective April 2025) structurally caps UK Consumer revenue growth despite volume gains. **ScotRail white-label contract loss** expected FY2027; Cross-Country white label already lost. Refund-fee tightening from April 2026 and regulated fare freeze until March 2027 add near-term revenue pressure per management guidance.

**Leverage and capital allocation.** Net debt/EBITDA doubled to 1.0x as buybacks (£294m cumulative, ~23% of issued capital) were partly debt-funded. Covenants complied with headroom, but continued repurchases under the £150m programme ( £94m executed to 30 April 2026) limit balance-sheet flexibility if volumes disappoint.

**Accounting / intangible-heavy balance sheet.** Goodwill £420m; **tangible book value negative** (~£297m per Yahoo balance sheet, consistent with filing structure). Goodwill impairment testing flagged as key estimation uncertainty; reasonably possible adverse changes could erode headroom.

**Governance / key person.** CEO transition: **Ian Brown** (ex-Flutter UK & Ireland) succeeds **Jody Ford** from 28 September 2026. Execution risk around European expansion and UK regulatory engagement during handover.

**Cyclical / operational.** Spain rail accidents moderated growth; Middle East geopolitics cited as weighing on European inbound travel. Working-capital volatility (weekend year-end receivables) affects reported FCF.

**Pension.** Defined-benefit re-measurement gain £13k in FY2026 comprehensive income—immaterial; not a material red flag in filings.

**Going concern:** Not flagged; directors explicitly affirm 12-month viability with liquidity and covenant tests passed.
