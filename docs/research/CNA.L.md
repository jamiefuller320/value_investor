# Centrica plc (CNA.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:21:04.044494+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Centrica is a UK-integrated energy group pivoting from volatile optimisation earnings toward regulated and contracted infrastructure (nuclear, LNG, flexible generation, meter assets), with British Gas retail providing a large customer base. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy on cheapness (P/E 10.4, yield 3.7%, P/B 2.1), quality (ROE 19.9%) and financial health, though an FCF basis mismatch (filing £141m vs company-adjusted £989m) warrants scrutiny. FY2025 adjusted EBITDA fell to £1.4bn from £2.3bn as markets normalised, while heavy investment (Sizewell C, Grain LNG, MAP, Severn CCGT) consumed cash and prompted a buyback pause. The central debate is whether infrastructure build-out and a £2.0bn adjusted EBITDA target by 2030 justify today's valuation, or whether regulatory intervention, customer-debt pressures and earnings cyclicality cap upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Centrica offers a rare combination in UK utilities: statistically cheap equity (13/22 screen models passed; composite score 71%), a net-cash balance sheet (adjusted net cash £709m at H1 2026 per filings), and a dividend that management continues to grow (interim raised 9% to 2.0p in H1 2026). The screen passes span cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families — consistent with a business that generated adjusted operating profit of £814m in FY2025 and retains ROE near 20% despite the earnings step-down.

Business quality is improving structurally, not just cyclically. Management is recycling capital from non-core disposals (Spirit Energy producing assets, European energy solutions — proceeds exceeding £80m in February 2026) into long-duration assets: a capped £1.3bn Sizewell C stake (10.8% real ROE, 12%+ IRR), Grain LNG (~14% equity IRR), the fastest-growing UK meter asset provider (2.3m meters under management at H1 2026), and the £367m Severn CCGT acquisition (expected £30–60m annual EBITDA from 2027). The Sizewell B life extension to 2055 under a £70.50/MWh regulated CfD further de-risks the nuclear portfolio.

The screen's buy signal is directionally supported: Centrica trades below historical earnings power on normalised metrics, carries manageable leverage (debt/equity ~74% per screen), and management is explicitly targeting £1.7bn adjusted EBITDA by end-2028 and £2.0bn by 2030. Timing is neutral (RSI ~41, below 200-day MA), suggesting no urgency but no reason to avoid initiation for a patient value allocator.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory.** British Gas agreed a £20m Ofgem Voluntary Redress Fund payment (May 2026) over legacy prepayment meter practices, plus up to £70m in vulnerable-customer debt write-offs. Management states no impact on 2026 guidance, but reputational and further enforcement risk persists. UK government proposals to "rewire" electricity pricing away from gas linkage (April 2026 news flow) could compress generation margins for Centrica's flexible and nuclear assets, though Citi argued Centrica's short nuclear lifespan limits delink exposure. Consumer energy debt across the sector is projected at ~£7bn (British Gas RNS, May 2026).

**Cyclical / earnings quality.** Adjusted EBITDA has fallen 38% from FY2024 to FY2025; Optimisation is guided to ~£250m in 2026, below the £300–400m medium-term range. Statutory earnings are heavily distorted by derivative mark-to-market (£0.7bn net loss in FY2025), making reported P/E unreliable — the screen's 10.4x likely uses adjusted or blended earnings.

**Pension.** IAS 19 deficit of £295m (FY2025) with ongoing deficit contributions (£111m paid in H1 2026 per half-year cash flow notes). Funding plan unchanged but rising discount-rate sensitivity remains.

**Investment / capital allocation.** Buyback paused; capex rising to ~£1.1bn in 2026. Sizewell C (£1.3bn capped), Severn (£367m) and MAP (£133m in H1 alone) consume cash before returns resume. Transformation programme costs estimated at ~£0.6bn (£0.4bn operating, £0.2bn capital).

**Operational.** British Gas plans ~1,300 job cuts over two years (~14% of customer operations workforce) per July 2026 news, reflecting cost pressure in retail.

**Governance.** Board refresh: Jonathan Howell appointed NED and future Audit & Risk Committee chair (May 2026); director share purchases in August 2026 (Frank Mastiaux, Sue Whalley). Mixed insider activity (some sales reported March–May 2026).

**Going concern.** H1 2026 auditor review found no material going-concern uncertainties; however, adjusted net cash has halved in six months and heavy capex continues.
