## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
MONY Group plc operates UK consumer financial comparison brands (MoneySuperMarket, MoneySavingExpert, Quidco) and screens as a strong buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk factors (15/22 models; composite 70%). The investment case rests on a cash-generative, asset-light model trading at roughly 12x earnings with a ~6.5% yield, ~8% FCF yield and high reported ROE, whilst management returns capital via dividends and buybacks. The central debate is whether generative-AI disintermediation of price comparison (car insurance in particular) structurally impairs traffic and monetisation, or whether MONY’s membership, data and “everyday financial companion” pivot offset that risk. Primary UK RNS filing bodies were not retrieved in this research pack, so financial and risk conclusions lean on Yahoo fallback data and news; conviction is moderated accordingly.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags MONY as a strong buy with unusually broad model support: earnings yield, FCF yield, Neff PEGY (0.02), quality value, Buffett quality, economic moat, high dividend yield, dividend growth, Magic Formula, acquirer’s multiple, Piotroski F-Score (pass), composite value, earnings quality and financial health all pass, with 20/20 data quality on screen inputs. That profile fits a classic value-and-income compounder: mid-teens P/E on a business producing roughly £100m of annual free cash flow (Yahoo, 2025) against a market cap implied by screen metrics that embeds a double-digit earnings yield.

Business quality, as visible in Yahoo annual series (2022–2025), shows revenue rising from £387.6m to £446.3m, operating income from £89.0m to £117.4m, and net income from £68.3m to £81.2m—steady mid-single-digit top-line growth with margin expansion. FCF has been consistently strong (£93.0m–£101.5m across 2022–2024; £98.1m in 2025), supporting a dividend-plus-buyback return model (cash dividends paid ~£63–67m p.a.; repurchases stepped up to £30.2m in 2025). Net debt has fallen materially (total debt £72.6m in 2022 to £34.0m in 2025; cash £20.3m), and working capital turned positive (£17.5m in 2025 vs negative in prior years). For a value investor, the hook is not explosive growth but durable UK consumer reach, high cash conversion, and shareholder yield at a discount to perceived AI-disruption fears—consistent with Berenberg’s February 2026 view that the selloff was overdone, even as Morgan Stanley (December 2025) took the opposite side.

**Capital allocation:** Dividends plus buybacks near or above earnings/FCF compress reinvestment headroom and increase sensitivity to any FCF downturn.

**Cyclical / regulatory:** UK personal-lines insurance and credit markets drive lead volumes; FCA conduct rules and insurer commission structures can shift economics. No macro overlay applied per research rules.

**Governance / news:** Routine buyback cancellations and SIP purchases (Jul 2026); Emma Lloyd appointed independent NED (20 Jul 2026)—neutral to mildly positive. No major governance scandal in manifest.
