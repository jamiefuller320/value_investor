# MONY Group plc (MONY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T12:57:21.963765+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
MONY Group plc is a UK consumer finance comparison platform (MoneySuperMarket, MoneySavingExpert, Quidco and related brands) trading on a low-teens earnings multiple with a c.6.4% dividend yield and active buybacks. The quantitative screen flags strong value on earnings yield, FCF yield, quality and dividend metrics, consistent with a cash-generative, capital-light model that has delivered record revenue and adjusted EBITDA in FY2025 despite insurance and consumer headwinds. The central debate is whether AI-driven distribution (e.g. conversational insurance quotes) structurally impairs price-comparison economics, or whether MONY’s brand trust, member base (SuperSaveClub >2m) and data platform allow it to adapt. Valuation looks compelling on earnings and cash returns, but not on book value (P/B 4.9x), and organic growth remains modest at low single digits.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy signal rests on five passing factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk—and is well supported by the underlying business profile.

**Cheapness and cash returns.** Key screen metrics include P/E 12.3x (earnings yield 8.1%), FCF yield 7.9%, and Neff PEGY 0.02 despite reported earnings growth of only 4.7%. FY2025 operating cash flow of £108m (Companies House annual report, filed May 2026) represents cash conversion above 80%, aligning with the screen’s FCF Yield and Earnings Quality passes. The board returned £96m to shareholders in FY2025 (1% dividend growth plus a completed £30m buyback), and announced a further £25m repurchase programme in February 2026.

**Quality and financial health.** ROE of 37.1%, Piotroski F-Score 7/9, and debt/equity of c.32% (Yahoo fallback) indicate a robust balance sheet. The group is capital-light with low capex (~£10m annually per filings trend) and minimal net debt. Passes on Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Financial Health and Acquirer’s Multiple reflect sustained profitability rather than deep asset discount.

**Business quality supporting value.** MONY operates a diversified two-sided marketplace across Insurance (£233m), Money (£106m), Home Services (£48m), Cashback and Travel. Management cites an estimated £2.8bn in customer savings in 2025 and NPS of 73 for core brands—evidence of brand moat that screens cannot fully capture. The SuperSaveClub membership model and B2B/provider propositions (Market Boost, Tenancy) are shifting the mix from transactional to recurring engagement.

**Where the screen overstates conviction.** The signal is new (one week, conviction score 45%) and fails classic deep-value and Graham screens on P/B (4.9x), current ratio (1.18) and Lynch PEG (2.61x). For a pure asset or net-net investor this is not a Strong Buy; for an earnings-yield and income-oriented value investor, the case is coherent.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**AI and competitive disruption.** The most material near-term risk, reflected in a February 2026 sector sell-off and a Morgan Stanley downgrade (December 2025). Filings frame AI as an opportunity (OpenAI enterprise agreement, “Agent” concierge rollout), but the market has priced in disintermediation risk for price-comparison models. This is not captured by backward-looking screens.

**Cyclical and segment headwinds.** FY2025 filings cite sustained pressure in motor insurance, PPC costs, weak UK consumer confidence (Cashback), and Travel competition. Organic revenue growth was 2% in FY2025—below the Lynch screen’s 5% floor. Reliance on commercial deal availability in Money (credit cards, switching promotions) introduces partner-cycle risk.

**Capital structure and intangibles.** P/B of 4.9x and failure of Graham, Schloss and Deep Value screens reflect an intangible-heavy balance sheet (goodwill £203m per Yahoo). Tangible book value is negative; asset-based downside protection is limited.

**Payout intensity.** FY2025 combined dividends (~£67m) and buybacks (~£30m) against FCF of ~£98m imply a high payout ratio. Sustainable only if cash generation holds; any EBITDA disappointment would pressure the dividend/buyback stack before operational investment.

**Governance and regulatory.** FCA-regulated activities across insurance distribution; compliance cited as a competitive advantage in FY2025 chair’s statement. No going-concern qualification in available extracts. An older viability assessment (2022 accounts) modelled severe scenarios and concluded no threat to liquidity, solvency or debt covenants—current-year viability language was not fully extractable from OCR but no red flags surfaced.

**Corporate actions.** ITG deconsolidation adds modelling complexity for FY2026 comparables. Minority interest and segment restatements require care when cross-checking consensus.

**Pension.** No material pension deficit flagged in available filing extracts; not a primary concern from current sources.
