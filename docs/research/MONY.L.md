# MONY Group plc (MONY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-06T07:33:27.263084+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
MONY Group plc operates the UK’s leading price-comparison and savings ecosystem (MoneySuperMarket, MoneySavingExpert, Quidco), pivoting from transactional traffic toward a member-based “everyday financial companion” model via SuperSaveClub and new product launches. The quantitative screen flags a strong buy on cheapness (P/E 12.4, FCF yield ~9.7%), quality (ROE 37%, Piotroski 7/9), dividend (6.4% yield, progressive policy), and GARP (Neff PEGY 0.02), with all five factor families passing. The valuation hook is a cash-generative, capital-light aggregator trading below intrinsic estimates in sell-side and screener frameworks, returning >£90m to shareholders in 2026 via dividends and buybacks. The key debate is whether AI-driven search disruption and insurance/PPC cyclicality permanently compress margins, or whether MONY’s regulated moat, first-party data, and membership economics allow it to re-rate as growth re-accelerates.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MONY offers a rare combination: high current yield, strong free-cash-flow conversion, and a balance sheet that supports both organic investment and shareholder returns without distress. The screen passes 15 of 22 models across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families — notably Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, High Dividend Yield, Magic Formula, and Piotroski F-Score (7/9: positive earnings and OCF, OCF > net income, improving current ratio, no dilution, improving asset turnover). Failures are concentrated in deep-value and Graham criteria (P/B 4.9, current ratio 1.18, low statutory growth), which is consistent with an intangible-heavy, brand-driven business rather than a balance-sheet bargain.

Business quality supports the screen signal. FY2025 revenue rose 2% to £446.3m and adjusted EBITDA 2% to £145.1m (annual report); adjusted basic EPS grew 5% to 17.9p; operating cash flow was £107.7m per the annual report chart (Yahoo: £107.7m, used only where filing OCR is ambiguous on sub-lines). FCF of £98.1m (filing-aligned; screen notes TTM £80m divergence) covers the £66.9m dividend ~1.5x on a net basis. Management is actively shrinking the share count (£30m buyback completed Dec 2025; £25m programme ongoing in 2026) while maintaining progressive dividends (total FY25 12.63p, +1%).

The strategic shift toward SuperSaveClub (2.5m+ members, 19% of group revenue H1 2026) improves unit economics: members show ~77% incremental margin vs 63% group average and roughly double lifetime value by year three per management. New propositions (Investments, SuperSaveClub Insurance, Business Banking) diversify beyond click-out comparison into recurring and AUM-linked revenue. This aligns with screen quality passes (Economic Moat, Earnings Quality) and explains why deep-value screens fail despite attractive earnings-based multiples.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and tax:** Price comparison and insurance broking operate under FCA oversight; interim accounts disclose an ongoing HMRC dispute over the Partial Exemption Special Method for VAT recovery, with £2.3m charged H1 2026 (£7.5m provision at 30 Jun 2026) and resolution expected through 2026–2027. Amounts remain uncertain; assessments are being paid under HMRC’s view while MONY contests.

**Competitive / AI disruption:** Filings and news cite PPC cost inflation (~8% exiting H1 2026, down from >20%), Google AI overviews affecting acquisition economics, and market concern that generative AI could disintermediate comparison sites — Morgan Stanley downgraded on AI concerns (Dec 2025); Berenberg subsequently argued the selloff was overdone (Mar 2026). Active users fell 0.5m to 12.5m, partly from car-insurance enquiry mix-out.

**Cyclical exposure:** Insurance premiums (car −5% YoY, improving), cashback (−13% H1 revenue), and consumer spending softness create vertical-specific headwinds. Cashback and travel affiliate markets remain uneven.

**Balance sheet / accounting:** Goodwill and intangibles £223.6m at H1 2026 (57% of total assets); tangible book value negative. Heavy use of adjusted metrics (adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS) excludes VAT provisions and acquisition amortisation — statutory basic EPS (15.3p FY25) is materially below adjusted (17.9p). No impairment indicators flagged H1 2026, but intangible concentration is a screen failure on Graham/NCAV criteria.

**Leverage and liquidity:** Net debt £31.8m at H1 2026 after £48m RCF draw; still modest vs EBITDA but direction reversed from net cash. Piotroski leverage component failed (leverage not declining). Dividend plus buyback (£90m+ planned 2026 returns) competes with working-capital seasonality.

**Governance / other:** Going concern and covenant compliance confirmed in interim accounts; reverse stress testing performed. Minority interest in Podium Solutions (£2.0m NCI liability). Professional indemnity insurance covers operational/tech errors. No material pension deficit flagged in available extracts.
