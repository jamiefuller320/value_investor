# Rio Tinto Group (RIO.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-24T21:13:30.699408+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Rio Tinto is a globally diversified miner (iron ore, copper, aluminium/lithium) trading at modest multiples (P/E ~15, yield ~4.4%) with a decade-long record of paying at the top of its 40–60% underlying-earnings dividend policy. FY2025 results (Form 20-F / Companies House) show resilient cash generation—$16.8bn operating cash flow and $25.4bn underlying EBITDA—offset by a step-up in net debt to $14.4bn after the $7.6bn Arcadium Lithium acquisition and heavy growth capex ($12.3bn). The quantitative screen rates the name a **buy** across cheapness, quality, dividend and risk families, with timing flagged as **accumulate**. The central debate is whether copper/lithium diversification and Simandou/Oyu Tolgoi growth justify holding through iron-ore cyclicality, elevated leverage, and persistent safety and social-licence risks that screens underweight.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Rio Tinto offers a rare combination of **yield, balance-sheet capacity and portfolio optionality** in large-cap mining. The screen passes eight of 22 models (composite 60%, sector-relative 66%), including Graham Enterprising, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth and Financial Health, with full data quality (20/20 metrics). Key metrics—P/E 15.1, P/B 2.4, ROE 16.4%, debt/equity 35%—suggest the market prices cyclical normalisation rather than structural impairment.

Business quality supports the signal: FY2025 underlying EBITDA rose 9% to **$25.4bn** on 7% revenue growth and 5% unit-cost reduction (20-F), while copper EBITDA more than doubled to **$7.4bn** on Oyu Tolgoi ramp-up. Management targets 3% copper-equivalent CAGR to 2030, $650m annualised productivity gains already announced, and $5–10bn asset-base cash release—aligning with the screen’s quality and GARP passes. Dividend policy discipline (402 US cents, 60% payout, flat YoY) underpins the dividend-family pass. Near-term technicals (RSI ~45, price ~flat vs 200-day SMA) align with the screen’s accumulate timing without contradicting the fundamental case.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and operational:** Commodity-price exposure remains the dominant risk; FY2025 iron-ore index fell 6%, partially offset by copper, aluminium and bauxite (20-F). Pilbara cyclones cut Q1 volumes; climate-related disruption is explicitly flagged in the Chair’s statement. Kennecott refined copper production was 31% lower in 2025 on planned smelter shutdown and geotechnical constraints until 2027.

**Balance sheet:** Net debt rose **$8.9bn** in 2025 (to $14.4bn) following $9bn bond issuance for Arcadium and $6.1bn dividends, partially offset by $4.0bn FCF. Gearing doubled to 18%— manageable under stated single-A framework but **a material shift** screens may lag.

**Safety and governance:** One fatality and one permanent-injury case in 2025 (20-F KPIs); further fatalities at Simandou/SimFer (Feb 2026 noted in Chair/CEO letters). Falling objects, heights and vehicle incidents remain top fatal-risk categories. Heritage and Indigenous relations remain sensitive despite the 2025 PKKP Agreement—ongoing reputational overhang from prior Juukan Gorge legacy.

**Regulatory and geopolitical:** Operations span Guinea (Simandou), Mongolia (Oyu Tolgoi shareholder loan renegotiation reported Jun 2025), US tariffs on aluminium, and complex closure/provision estimates ($0.2bn after-tax closure charge in 2025). Effective tax rate on underlying earnings rose to **31.5%** (from 28.3%). Decarbonisation targets depend on timely Pacific aluminium renewable-power deals—management warns delays could affect 2030 goals.

**Impairments and portfolio:** $0.2bn after-tax impairment on Yarwun refinery tailings capacity (40% production curtailment from Oct 2026). Borates and Iron & Titanium businesses under strategic review; Jadar (Serbia) placed in care and maintenance.

**Pension / covenants:** Central pension costs appear in segment bridge (−$0.1bn net central line); no going-concern qualification identified in available extracts. Detailed covenant language not present in cached bodies—**verify in full 20-F notes before trade**.

**M&A:** Jan 2026 Glencore mega-merger talks collapsed over price (5 Feb 2026, multiple sources)—removes near-term integration risk but highlights strategic uncertainty.
