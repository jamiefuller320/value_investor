# Rio Tinto Group (RIO.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T12:58:54.869027+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Rio Tinto is a diversified, investment-grade miner trading on a value screen (P/E ~13, yield ~4.9%, ROE ~19%, modest leverage) while executing a portfolio shift toward copper, aluminium and lithium. Primary filings show FY2025 resilience (underlying EBITDA $25.4bn) and a stronger H1 2026 step-up (underlying EBITDA +28% to $14.8bn; interim dividend +43%), supported by commodity prices, Oyu Tolgoi ramp and a productivity programme. The valuation hook is cyclically depressed headline multiples on a business generating substantial cash and paying at the top of its 40–60% underlying-earnings dividend policy. The key debate is whether near-term earnings momentum and diversification offset cyclical commodity exposure, elevated closure liabilities (~$18.9bn provision at 30 June 2026), post-Arcadium leverage, and persistent safety and governance overhangs.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags **Strong Buy** (12/22 models; composite 68%; sector-relative 72%), passing cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families with full data quality (20/20 metrics). That aligns with filing evidence of a high-quality asset base: FY2025 underlying ROCE of 16%, net gearing of 18% at year-end (16% at H1 2026), and a stated policy to return 40–60% of underlying earnings (FY2025 payout at 60%; H1 2026 interim at 50%). Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Magic Formula and Acquirer's Multiple passes suggest the market prices Rio as a cyclical rather than a compounder, despite management's copper-equivalent growth target (~20% from 2024 to 2030) and diversification (copper, aluminium and lithium exceeded half of H1 2026 underlying EBITDA).

For a value investor, the case rests on: (1) **earnings power through the cycle** — diversified commodity mix limiting single-commodity dependence (iron ore EBITDA down in FY2025 but copper up 114%); (2) **cash return discipline** — $6.5bn FY2025 dividends and $3.4bn H1 2026 interim; (3) **balance sheet capacity** — net debt ~$14.1–14.4bn against $9bn+ cash/liquid investments and long-dated debt (weighted average maturity 11 years); and (4) **screen-validated quality at a discount** — ROE 19.3% and D/E 32% pass risk screens, while P/E 13.1 sits below many global peers despite tier-one assets (Pilbara, Oyu Tolgoi, Simandou). Screen failures (FCF yield 3.1%, Piotroski 5/9, no deep-value P/B) flag that free-cash conversion and balance-sheet trend scores lag earnings — a typical mining-cycle pattern during heavy growth capex (Simandou, lithium, Resolution).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and operational:** Mining earnings remain commodity-price sensitive; H1 2026 EBITDA included a **$3.6bn price benefit**. Pilbara cyclones in early 2025 cut production (~13 Mt in Q1); weather and diesel ($140/bbl in H1 2026) remain cost risks. US **50% aluminium tariffs** materially affected realised tariff costs (~$773m in H1 2026 for US-destined volumes); CEO indicated no US smelter investment for tariff relief (WSJ, 29 July 2026).

**Safety and governance:** H1 2026 RNS records **two colleague fatalities** (Simandou, Kennecott); AIFR 0.40. FY2025 Form 20-F reports one fatality at managed operations. Cultural heritage and Indigenous relations remain licence-to-operate risks despite PKKP agreement progress (2025).

**Legal and tax:** **$443m Oyu Tolgoi tax payment** in H1 2026 following Mongolian Tax Authority assessments for 2021–22; Rio Tinto reserves dispute rights, citing inconsistency with the Investment Agreement. Kennecott flash furnace breach (June 2026) may reduce H2 refined copper/gold output.

**Balance sheet and liabilities:** Net debt rose **$8.9bn in FY2025** (Arcadium, dividends, capex). **Closure cost provision $18.9bn** at 30 June 2026 (up from $17.8bn at Dec 2025), with ~**$1bn/year** closure cash spend guided. Long-term provisions on balance sheet are substantial (Yahoo: **$17.1bn** non-current provisions at FY2025 — secondary reference only). **Floating-rate debt (76%)** creates rate sensitivity. Impairment: **Yarwun alumina refinery** ($0.2bn after tax in FY2025); 40% production curtailment from October 2026.

**Screen-identified weaknesses:** Piotroski **5/9** (ROA, leverage, current ratio and asset turnover not improving); **FCF yield 3.1%** vs 5% threshold; earnings quality flag on weak free-cash conversion — consistent with FY2025 FCF of $4.0bn vs underlying earnings $10.9bn during peak growth capex.

**Strategic execution:** Simandou ramp, lithium build-out (negative FCF at lithium segment), Nemaska construction slowdown, and failed **Glencore merger talks** (February 2026) highlight M&A and capital-allocation complexity. Decarbonisation targets depend on third-party renewable projects "neither of which can be guaranteed."

No explicit **going concern** qualification appears in available body extracts; risk-factor and covenant detail from the full 20-F risk section is **not present** in downloaded bodies (index lists 20-F without body).
