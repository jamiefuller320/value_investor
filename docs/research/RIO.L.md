# Rio Tinto Group (RIO.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-24T18:36:21.993678+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Rio Tinto is a diversified, tier-one miner whose iron ore franchise funds a pivot towards copper, aluminium and lithium, with FY2025 results showing revenue growth and stable underlying earnings despite softer reported net profit. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend and risk metrics (P/E 15.1, yield 4.4%, ROE 16.4%, D/E 35%), with timing favouring accumulation near the 200-day moving average. Valuation embeds cyclical commodity risk and a step-up in net debt after the Arcadium acquisition, but filings confirm strong operating cash generation ($16.8bn) and a decade-long record of paying dividends at the top of the 40–60% underlying-earnings policy range. The central debate is whether copper/lithium growth and productivity gains can offset iron ore margin compression, elevated capex ($11.4bn) and governance/safety liabilities that screens underweight.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Rio Tinto offers a rare combination of balance-sheet capacity, visible cash returns and franchise-quality assets at a modest multiple. The screen passes eight models spanning Graham Enterprising, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple and Financial Health — five factor families in total — indicating the signal is not driven by a single cheapness metric but by cross-sectional quality and payout support.

Filings corroborate business quality: FY2025 consolidated sales revenue rose 7% to **$57.6bn** (2024: $53.7bn), underlying EBITDA increased 9% to **$25.4bn**, and underlying earnings were stable at **$10.9bn** while the ordinary dividend was maintained at **402 US cents** ($6.5bn total). Underlying ROCE of **16%** and net operating cash flow of **$16.8bn** support the screen's quality and dividend passes. Management targets ~20% copper-equivalent production growth from 2024 to 2030, $650m of annualised productivity benefits (with more targeted), and $5–10bn of asset-base cash release — optionality not fully priced at ~15x earnings.

The screen's low conviction score (39%, new signal) reflects cyclical uncertainty rather than data gaps (20/20 metrics present). Timing overlay (accumulate, RSI ~45) aligns with building exposure into operational recovery rather than chasing momentum.

**Revenue and earnings:** Revenue grew on higher volumes (copper equivalent sales +5%) and portfolio diversification, partially offset by a 6% lower iron ore index price. Underlying EBITDA rose $2.0bn, driven by volume/mix (+$2.4bn) and 5% operating unit-cost reduction (2024 real terms), partly offset by Pilbara cyclone impact (−$0.7bn EBITDA). Reported net earnings fell 14% to $10.0bn on higher depreciation (Oyu Tolgoi ramp, Arcadium), taxes (effective rate 31.5% vs 28.3%) and non-underlying items including Yarwun alumina refinery impairment.

**Segment mix (FY2025 underlying EBITDA):** Iron Ore $15.2bn (−11% YoY); Copper $7.4bn (+114%); Aluminium & Lithium $4.6bn (+29%). Iron ore remains the profit engine but copper is the growth swing factor.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** Operating cash conversion remained ~66% of underlying EBITDA. Capex on property, plant and equipment rose to **$12.3bn** (growth $4.1bn, replacement $3.6bn, sustaining $4.5bn; Simandou-heavy). Free cash flow fell to **$4.0bn** after **$6.1bn** dividends and **$7.6bn** Arcadium acquisition (including $9.0bn bond issuance in March 2025 per 6-K). Net debt increased **$8.9bn** to **$14.4bn**; management anchors the balance sheet around a single-A credit rating with no formal net-debt target.

**Balance sheet (Yahoo fallback where filing extract lacks detail):** Per Form 20-F strategic report, operating assets rose to **$77bn** (2024: $61bn) post-Arcadium. Yahoo annual data (fallback) shows total debt **$23.5bn**, net debt **$15.0bn**, long-term provisions **$17.1bn** (closure/rehabilitation), and pension-related liabilities ~**$1.1bn** non-current — material off-balance-sheet-style commitments screens may not fully capture.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and commodity:** FY2025 iron ore EBITDA fell 11% on lower prices; ~60%+ of EBITDA still iron-linked. Five-year TSR (66.4%) trailed mining and world indices, reflecting commodity and macro sensitivity the screen cannot eliminate.

**Leverage and capex:** Net gearing doubled to **18%**; **$11.4bn** capital investment in 2025 with Simandou, Oyu Tolgoi and lithium integration ongoing. Free cash flow cover of dividends narrowed (FCF $4.0bn vs dividends $6.5bn declared). Filing language: balance sheet anchored to **single-A credit rating** — no going-concern qualification in available extracts, but leverage headroom is reduced.

**Safety and governance:** One fatality at managed operations in 2025 (vs five in 2024); further fatalities at Simandou/SimFer referenced in chair/CEO statements. Indigenous heritage and social-licence risk remains material despite PKKP agreement (October 2025 per 6-K index). Jadar (Serbia) placed in care and maintenance.

**Operational:** Four Pilbara cyclones cut Q1 production by 13 Mt (−$0.7bn EBITDA). Kennecott smelter shutdown and geotechnical constraints limit refined copper until 2027. Yarwun refinery impairment; 40% production curtailment from October 2026.

**Tax and provisions:** Effective tax rate on underlying earnings rose to **31.5%**. Closure estimate movements and long-term provisions (Yahoo: **$17.1bn**) create earnings volatility. March 2025 **$9.0bn** debt issuance increases finance cost sensitivity.

**Strategic/M&A:** Glencore merger talks collapsed (February 2025, news manifest). Arcadium integration and lithium price cyclicality add execution risk. Borates and Iron & Titanium businesses under strategic review.

**Filing coverage gap:** Full "Risk factors" section (20-F page 91) is **not present** in the extracted body — only strategic-report and financial-review sections. Contingency and covenant detail from notes 1–40 not available in this pack.
