# Rio Tinto Group (RIO.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-03T20:42:00.998752+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Rio Tinto is a globally diversified miner transitioning from an iron-ore-heavy cash engine toward copper, aluminium and lithium, with H1 2026 results showing a marked step-up in earnings quality and cash generation. The quantitative screen flags the name as a Strong Buy on cheapness (P/E 12.9, P/B 2.4), quality (ROE 19.3%), dividend yield (4.9%) and balance-sheet metrics (D/E 32%), though FCF yield and earnings-quality models fail. The central debate is whether current commodity tailwinds and a productivity programme ($870m banked in H1, targeting $1.8bn annualised run-rate) justify a re-rating, or whether elevated growth capex, net debt of c.$14bn post-Arcadium, and cyclical price exposure cap upside once normalisation sets in.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Rio Tinto offers a rare combination: a FTSE 100 mega-cap trading below 13x earnings with a near-5% dividend yield, passing Graham Enterprising, Lynch PEG, Buffett Quality, Magic Formula and High Dividend Yield screens simultaneously. Business quality is underwritten by tier-one assets (Pilbara iron ore, Oyu Tolgoi copper, integrated aluminium, Arcadium lithium) and a stated returns policy of 40–60% of underlying earnings through the cycle—the company has paid at the top of that range for a decade.

The screen’s Strong Buy signal aligns with improving fundamentals visible in primary filings. H1 2026 underlying EBITDA rose 28% to $14.8bn and free cash flow 75% to $3.8bn, with underlying ROCE at 17% and an interim dividend raised 43% to 211 US cents (50% payout). Copper, aluminium and lithium together contributed over half of segment EBITDA, reducing pure iron-ore dependence. A multi-year productivity programme and project pipeline (Simandou first sales, lithium ramp toward ~200 ktpa LCE by 2028) support the screen’s quality and GARP passes.

Where the screen is less convincing: FCF Yield fails (3.9% vs 5% threshold), Piotroski F-Score is 5/9, and earnings-quality flags weak free-cash conversion—consistent with FY2025 free cash flow of $4.0bn against $11.4bn capital investment and the $7.6bn Arcadium acquisition. The dividend-yield overlay already adjusts the headline signal from Strong Buy to Buy. Net-net, the quantitative case is sound on valuation and franchise quality, but not on deep-value or cash-yield extremes.

FY2025 showed resilient underlying earnings despite a 6% lower iron-ore index price, offset by higher copper (+61% production at Oyu Tolgoi), bauxite and aluminium prices. Volume growth (+5% copper-equivalent sales) and a 5% reduction in operating unit costs supported EBITDA. Net earnings fell on higher depreciation ($0.6bn), tax ($1.0bn; effective rate 31.5% vs 28.3%), and non-underlying items including Yarwun refinery impairment. Free cash flow was compressed by Rio Tinto share of capital investment of $11.4bn (growth $3.2bn, replacement $3.6bn, sustaining $4.5bn) and the Arcadium acquisition ($7.6bn including net debt assumed). Net debt rose sharply but gearing at 18% remains moderate; management targets a single-A credit rating without a formal net-debt target.

Segment EBITDA mix shifted: copper $7.4bn (+114%), aluminium & lithium $4.6bn (+29%), iron ore $15.2bn (−11%), reflecting portfolio diversification.

H1 2026 represents a clear inflection: $3.6bn of the EBITDA uplift came from stronger commodity prices (copper benchmark +39% YoY average), with volumes (+3% CuEq production) and productivity ($870m benefits banked) contributing $1.5bn. Copper segment EBITDA doubled (+84% to $5.7bn). Cash conversion improved (62% EBITDA-to-cash vs prior year). Net debt edged lower despite $5.0bn capital investment and $4.2bn dividend paid (2025 final). Closure-cost provision stands at $18.9bn (up from $17.8bn at Dec-25), with ~$1bn annual cash spend on rehabilitation flagged for coming years.

**Trend synthesis:** Revenue and underlying EBITDA have accelerated from FY2025 (+7%/+9%) into H1 2026 (+15%/+28%), driven primarily by price rather than volume alone. Underlying earnings were flat in FY2025 but rose sharply in H1 2026. Free cash flow remains the weak point at the annual level ($4.0bn on $16.8bn OCF) due to growth capex, though H1 2026 FCF of $3.8bn annualises well above FY2025 if commodity prices hold. Balance sheet leverage increased post-Arcadium but is stabilising (gearing 16% at Jun-26). ## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and commodity:** H1 EBITDA sensitivity tables in the interim release show a 10% iron-ore price move affects 12-month EBITDA by ~$2.3bn; copper by ~$1.1bn. Current earnings embed favourable prices (copper LME avg 593 c/lb H1 vs 428 c/lb prior year). Normalisation would materially reduce earnings and dividend capacity.

**Safety and operational:** Two colleague fatalities in H1 2026 (Simandou, Kennecott). AIFR was 0.40 in H1. Kennecott flash furnace breach in late June will reduce H2 refined copper and gold output. Pilbara Q1 cyclone impact (~8 Mt) partially recovered. These are execution and safety risks not captured by valuation screens.

**Regulatory and tax:** Oyu Tolgoi made a $443m tax payment following Mongolian Tax Authority assessments for 2021–22; the company reserves its right to dispute, stating assessments are inconsistent with the Investment Agreement. Effective tax rate guidance lowered to ~25% for FY2026 (from ~30%) but expected to revert to ~30% from 2027.

**Balance sheet and liabilities:** Net debt rose from $5.5bn to $14.4bn in FY2025 (Arcadium, dividends, capex). Closure provisions of $18.9bn are substantial and growing (+$0.9bn in H1 from new/adjusted provisions). Maximum single-year debt maturity is $2.9bn (2028); 76% of financing liabilities at floating rates at Jun-26. Central pension costs appear in segment reconciliations but pension detail is not available in the body extracts reviewed.

**Project and capital:** Up to $11bn capital investment guided for FY2026; lithium projects (Rincon, Nemaska) remain cash-consuming (lithium FCF negative $0.6bn in H1). Simandou ramp carries execution and Guinea sovereign-partner risk. Yarwun alumina refinery production cut 40% from October 2026 following tailings impairment.

**Governance and social licence:** Heritage and Indigenous relations remain sensitive (PKKP agreement updated 2025; Pilbara approvals subject to heritage clearances). Decarbonisation pathway (50% Scope 1+2 reduction by 2030) depends on third-party renewable projects and commercial negotiations “neither of which can be guaranteed.”

**M&A:** Preliminary Glencore combination talks (Jan 2026) were abandoned (Feb 2026) after Rio determined no value-accretive agreement was achievable—removing near-term integration risk but highlighting strategic uncertainty.

No going-concern qualification or covenant breach language appears in the filing extracts available; this is expected for an investment-grade miner but the full 20-F risk-factor section was not downloadable.
