# Bellway p.l.c. (BWY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:53:21.345295+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Bellway is a quality UK private housebuilder trading below book (P/B ~0.7) with low leverage (D/E ~5%), a growing dividend, and active capital return via buybacks, yet the quantitative screen rates it **hold** with only 5/22 models passing and a **wait** timing signal. Filings show a recovery in volume from the FY24 trough (7,654 completions) through FY25 (8,749) to FY26 (~9,695), but underlying operating margins remain compressed at ~10% versus mid-teens pre-cycle, and returns on capital are modest (underlying RoCE ~8.9% at H1 FY26). The central debate is whether asset-turn improvement, net-cash generation, and shareholder returns justify accumulation at sub-book, or whether mortgage-rate volatility, legacy building-safety costs, and cyclical demand moderation cap re-rating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s **hold** is consistent with a business that looks **cheap on balance-sheet metrics** but **mediocre on earnings quality**. Bellway passes four metric families—cheapness (Schloss Low P/B), GARP (Lynch PEG ~0.78, Neff PEGY), dividend growth, and financial health (D/E ~5%)—yet fails Graham-style quality screens (ROE ~4.5% vs an 8% hurdle; P/E ~16.4 vs a 15 ceiling). Composite score 49% sits below the sector average (~55%), conviction is low at 23% (new signal, one week), and timing is **wait** (RSI ~60, momentum weakening).

For a value investor, this is a **hold rather than a conviction buy**: the franchise is defensible (five-star HBF rating, ~94k-plot land bank, net cash at FY26 year-end), and capital discipline is visible (£150m buyback completed; £50m FY27 tranche launched). However, earnings power has not normalised—FY26 underlying operating profit ~£320m implies only modest growth on FY25’s £303.5m despite ~11% volume growth, because bulk sales and incentives dilute margin. ROE remains structurally depressed by legacy remediation charges and cyclical margin compression. Until margins and cash conversion stabilise without one-off volume mix, the screen’s caution is warranted despite apparent cheapness.

**FCF gap:** Yahoo `financials_annual.json` (fallback) shows FY24 **free cash flow -£66.9m** (operating cashflow -£65.5m), consistent with the screen’s filing FCF of **-£66.9m**. ## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / demand:** Management flags moderation in reservations since April 2026 as mortgage rates rose; forward order book down ~21% YoY to 4,206 homes at Jul 2026. Incentives ~5% of ASP. Geopolitical cost inflation (Middle East) cited as a build-cost risk. H1 going-concern stress test assumes a GFC-style volume collapse—headroom exists but illustrates earnings sensitivity.

**Regulatory / legacy building safety:** £506.5m provision remaining at H1 FY26; £718.2m cumulative set-aside since 2017. SRT/Pact/Scottish Accord obligations; contingent liability language warns estimates may change as surveys progress. Residential Property Developer Tax (4%) adds to effective tax ~29%.

**Competitive / sector:** UK class-action news (Jul 2026 headlines) hit sector sentiment. CMA market investigation costs treated as exceptional (£0.3m H1 FY26; £15.4m in FY25 per interim note). Phasing out Ashberry brand over 3–4 years—operational, not material financially.

**Governance / M&A:** Bellway approached Crest Nicholson in 2024 (Crest response filing, Jun 2024); no completed deal—integration risk if revived.

**Liquidity / leverage:** Low at present (net cash £157.7m FY26), but H1 net debt £72m and May net debt £236m show **working-capital seasonality**; not a structural concern given facilities.

**Accounting:** Underlying metrics exclude legacy safety and exceptional items—material gap between underlying (£320m operating profit FY26E) and statutory earnings (legacy + RPDT drag). FY25 annual report body OCR quality limits direct verification of some annual-only disclosures.
