# Grafton Group (GFTU.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T06:33:12.857128+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Grafton is a European building-materials distributor and DIY retailer (Woodie's, Chadwicks, Selco, Salvador Escoda) that the quantitative screen rates **Strong Buy**, supported by modest valuation (P/E 12.6, P/B 1.2, 3.6% yield), solid balance-sheet metrics (current ratio 2.1, Piotroski 8/9), and nine passing value models spanning cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk. FY2025 filings show revenue up 10.4% to £2,520m and adjusted operating profit up 7.1% to £190.2m, with £274m net cash before lease liabilities and a disciplined capital-return programme that has cut the share count by over 20% since 2022. The central debate is whether geographic diversification (Iberia and Ireland now driving growth) and self-help margin management can offset a structurally weak Great Britain construction market (H1 2026 like-for-like revenue −5.1%) and integration risk from a stepped-up M&A pace. Near-term timing is neutral (RSI ~77); the value case rests on earnings compounding, buybacks, and cyclical recovery optionality rather than deep asset discount.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy signal aligns with a business that combines **cyclical resilience with shareholder-friendly capital allocation**. Grafton passes Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Dreman Contrarian, Piotroski F-Score (8/9), Composite Value, Earnings Quality and Financial Health — five metric families with full 20/20 data quality. Cheapness is genuine on headline multiples, whilst quality screens capture improving cash conversion, declining leverage and dividend cover of roughly 2.0× on adjusted EPS of 75.4p.

Business quality rests on a **federated distributor model** across four geographies (~490 branches, ~10,000 colleagues), industry-leading adjusted EBITA margins (~7.3%), and more than £700m of company-defined free cash flow over four years (per FY2025 results). Management has redeployed surplus capital aggressively: £428m returned via buybacks to end-2025 plus ongoing £25m programmes, alongside bolt-on acquisitions (Salvador Escoda, HSS Hire Ireland, Cygnum, Mercaluz) that tilt the portfolio toward faster-growing Iberia and Ireland — over 75% of 2025 operating profit was generated outside Great Britain.

For a value investor, the hook is **modest absolute valuation on normalised earnings with embedded optionality**: UK and Northern European markets are cyclically depressed, yet the group guides FY2026 adjusted operating profit to £190–200m and targets >10% adjusted EPS CAGR to 2030 (Capital Markets Event, June 2026). Share count reduction amplifies per-share metrics. The screen’s failures (deep value, FCF yield, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat) correctly flag that this is not a net-net or high-ROE compounder — it is a **well-run, cash-generative cyclical trading below mid-cycle perception**, with balance-sheet firepower to buy earnings growth.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / geographic:** Great Britain remains the principal weak spot — H1 2026 LFL revenue −5.1%, with independent commentators cited in the May 2026 update expecting UK construction to contract in 2026. Less than 25% of 2025 adjusted operating profit came from GB, but sentiment and index weighting still matter for the London-listed CDI. Finland and the Netherlands recovery timing remains uncertain; FY2025 outlook language explicitly deferred meaningful improvement.

**M&A and goodwill:** Rapid Iberia build-out (Salvador Escoda, Mercaluz) and Ireland (HSS Hire, Cygnum) increases integration and execution risk. Goodwill totalled **£659.1m** at 31 December 2025 (Annual Report). Auditors performed a VIU impairment test on the UK Distribution CGU group at 30 September 2025 following segment reorganisation; no impairment recorded, but **goodwill concentration and acquisition earn-outs** (contingent consideration per accounting policy) warrant monitoring.

**Inflation / supply chain / geopolitical:** 2026 trading updates cite Middle East conflict-related supply chain and inflation risks, supplier price increases and fuel costs. No material disruption reported to date, but sustained inflation could compress volumes.

**Leverage (lease-adjusted):** Headline net cash is misleading without IFRS 16: net debt including leases was £123.4m (0.39× EBITDA). Capital lease obligations ~£397m (Yahoo balance sheet, 2025). Financial leverage ceiling stated at ~2.0× net debt/EBITDA in the Annual Report — well within, but **lease-adjusted metrics matter**.

**Returns quality:** Screen failures on ROE (8.4%), FCF yield (0.1% on screen TTM basis), Lynch PEG (1.5) and Economic Moat flag that earnings quality is adequate rather than exceptional. Margins are good for distribution but not wide-moat.

**Governance / going concern:** Deloitte reported **no material uncertainties** on going concern (Annual Report). Pension obligations are modest relative to scale (defined benefit pension assets/liabilities ~£11.6m per Yahoo balance sheet, 2025); remeasurement gains of £2.3m in 2025 per cash flow statement.

**Market timing:** Quantitative timing signal is neutral with RSI ~77 and price above 200-day MA — strong momentum may limit near-term entry unless staged.
