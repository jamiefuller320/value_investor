# Grafton Group plc (GFTU.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-13T07:55:35.603067+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Grafton is a pan-European construction-products merchant (Ireland, Great Britain, Northern Europe, Iberia) trading on inexpensive headline multiples while delivering mid-single-digit adjusted earnings growth, strong cash conversion, and active capital returns. The quantitative screen’s strong buy rests mainly on cheapness (P/E ~12.6, P/B ~1.1), dividend/GARP overlays, Piotroski 8/9, and broad “risk/financial health” passes, with all five factor families clearing despite modest reported ROE (~8%). Filings show FY2025 revenue up 10.4% to £2,520m and adjusted operating profit up 7.1% to £190.2m, with H1 2026 momentum continuing but Great Britain still a drag. The central debate is whether geographic diversification and Iberia/M&A can compound through a prolonged UK RMI/construction slump without goodwill pressure in GB or erosion of returns on the acquisition trail. Free cash flow definitions differ materially between statutory cash flow (£205.6m per Yahoo, aligned with one filing bridge) and management “free cash flow” (£168.3m in FY2025 results); dividend coverage looks comfortable on management’s basis but warrants reconciliation before sizing yield trades.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, GFTU offers a familiar “quality cyclical at a discount” profile: a branch-based distribution platform with investment-grade balance sheet capacity, habitual buybacks, and a dividend policy targeting 2.0–3.0× cover on adjusted earnings. The screen’s strong buy (composite ~80%, 10/22 models, conviction ~68%, persistent ~15 weeks) is coherent with Graham enterprising, Schloss low P/B, Neff PEGY, dividend growth, Dreman contrarian, composite value, earnings quality, and financial health passes—while explicit “quality compounder” screens fail on ROE, margins, and Lynch PEG (~2.5). That split matches the business: operational resilience and cash (Piotroski 8/9, current ratio ~2.1, D/E ~44%) versus structurally lower through-cycle returns than top-tier industrials peers. FY2025 results (IR final results, 31 December 2025) show adjusted EPS 75.4p (+5.1%) and ROCE 10.9%, with net cash £274.0m before IFRS 16 leases and lease-inclusive net debt £123.4m (leverage ~0.39× on management’s lease-adjusted basis). H1 2026 (RNS half-year report, 3 September 2026) reaffirmed full-year adjusted operating profit guidance of £190m–£200m and grew adjusted EPS 10.8% to 39.4p, helped by Ireland/Iberia and buybacks—supporting the screen’s cheapness + cash-return story without proving a high-ROE franchise. The 2030 targets (EPS CAGR >10%, cumulative FCF >£850m 2026–2030) skew the narrative toward capital allocation and consolidation; the value case depends on execution at sensible prices, not moat-level economics.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
- **Cyclical / geographic:** Filings and H1 2026 outlook stress **challenging Great Britain** (weak RMI, housebuilding, consumer confidence) and **uncertain Northern Europe** recovery; H1 2026 GB like-for-like revenue −5.1%. Group performance increasingly relies on Ireland and Iberia—concentration of growth drivers, not revenue concentration in one customer.
- **Goodwill / accounting:** H1 2026 notes **impairment testing triggered for Great Britain CGUs**; value-in-use has **“more limited headroom”**—a ~440 bps higher discount rate or ~180 bps lower revenue CAGR would eliminate headroom (interim goodwill note). Acquisition amortisation and APM “adjusted” lines widen statutory vs adjusted EPS gaps.
- **Leverage and leases:** IFRS 16 lease liabilities are large; lease-inclusive net debt rose to **£315.2m** at June 2026 from **£123.4m** at Dec 2025. Management targets lease-adjusted net debt/EBITDA **1.0–2.0×** for investment grade—currently manageable with **£590.6m liquidity**, but acquisition-led debt draws bear watching.
- **Pension:** Four legacy defined benefit schemes (closed to accrual); H1 2026 reports **accounting surpluses** in UK/Ireland schemes and a **small Netherlands deficit (~£0.7m)**—not a dominant balance-sheet item but adds actuarial volatility.
- **Operational / geopolitical:** Interim and FY outlook cite **geopolitical uncertainty, inflation, and supply chain** risks; management reports proactive supply-chain management but margin defence depends on pricing pass-through in competitive merchant markets.
- **Governance / capital allocation:** Sustained **share buybacks** (£453.9m cumulative to June 2026 per H1 report; ongoing £25m programme) support EPS but reduce balance-sheet flexibility if cyclical downturn coincides with M&A spend (Mercaluz, Cygnum).
