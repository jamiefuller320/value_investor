# Grafton Group plc (GFTU.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-19T10:33:59.244972+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Grafton Group plc is a European building-materials distributor and manufacturer with a federated branch network across Ireland, Great Britain, Northern Europe and Iberia. The quantitative screen rates GFTU.L as a **strong buy** (11/22 models; composite 83%), supported by modest valuation (P/E 12.6, P/B 1.2, dividend yield 3.7%), solid balance-sheet metrics (current ratio 2.1; Piotroski 8/9) and strong cash conversion. FY2025 primary filings show revenue of **£2,520m** (+10.4%) and adjusted operating profit of **£190.2m** (+7.1%), ahead of consensus, with **£274.0m net cash** before IFRS 16 leases. The value case rests on geographic diversification, acquisition-led Iberian expansion, disciplined capital returns (buybacks and dividends), and operating leverage when cyclically weak UK RMI markets recover. The key debate is whether Iberia/Ireland can offset a structurally soft Great Britain segment — where H1 2026 like-for-like revenue fell **5.1%** — and whether goodwill on UK distribution (auditor-flagged limited headroom at September 2025) limits upside if the UK recovery is delayed.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal aligns with a business that passes multiple classic value frameworks — Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Schloss low P/B, FCF yield, Neff PEGY, Dreman contrarian, dividend growth, composite value, earnings quality and financial health — while failing only on deep quality/moat screens (ROE 8.4% vs higher thresholds). That profile fits a **quality-at-a-reasonable-price distributor** rather than a deep net-net or Buffett compounder.

Business quality is underwritten by filings, not just multiples. Management reports more than **£700m** of free cash flow over four years; FY2025 company-adjusted free cash flow was **£168.3m** (Annual Report 2025), with statutory operating cash flow of **£251.8m** (cash flow statement). Net cash before leases of **£274.0m** and lease-adjusted leverage of **0.39×** provide capacity for bolt-on M&A (Salvador Escoda, Mercaluz, Cygnum, HSS Hire Ireland) and ongoing buybacks — **£129.2m** returned in 2025 plus a new **£25.0m** programme. Dividend cover at **2.0×** adjusted EPS (37.75p dividend; 75.4p adjusted EPS) sits at the lower bound of the 2–3× target, but FCF dividend coverage from the screen (~2.8× net) adds comfort.

Geographic rebalancing strengthens the case: non-UK revenue exceeded **64%** at H1 2025 (Interim Results, 30 June 2025), with Iberia contributing **£212.9m** revenue and **£13.6m** adjusted operating profit in FY2025. At the June 2026 Capital Markets Event, management targeted **>10% CAGR adjusted EPS to 2030** and ~**13% ROCE**, framing Grafton as a consolidation platform in fragmented European markets. For a value investor, the hook is a cyclically depressed UK weighting (30.4% of FY2025 revenue) masking resilient group profitability, with the market pricing mid-teens earnings power on trough-like UK conditions.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / geographic:** Construction markets are inherently cyclical (Chair’s statement). Great Britain — heavily London/Southeast-exposed (c.60% of segment revenue) — saw subdued RMI and the weakest housing starts in London in 40+ years (Annual Report regional review). H1 2026 GB LFL **−5.1%** confirms near-term pressure persists despite manufacturing strength.

**Goodwill / accounting:** Goodwill of **£659.1m** (Note 12). Auditors flagged **limited headroom** in the former UK Distribution CGU group at **30 September 2025**, triggering an impairment assessment on resegmentation to Great Britain. No impairment was recorded in the extracts reviewed, but sensitivity to UK recovery assumptions is material.

**Acquisition / integration:** Revenue growth relies on Salvador Escoda, Mercaluz, Cygnum and HSS Hire. Contingent consideration receivable of **£12.3m** (Final Results notes) and earn-out mechanics add estimation risk. Segment reclassification (effective 1 October 2025) changes comparability.

**Leverage / leases:** Pre-lease net cash masks **£123.4m** net debt including IFRS 16 lease liabilities; lease payments of **£81.7m** in FY2025 cash flow. Board targets max ~**2×** lease-adjusted net debt/EBITDA through the cycle — currently well inside, but lease burden is structurally significant for a branch estate.

**Pension:** Defined benefit obligations present (remeasurement gains in equity; contributions **£2.4m** above IAS 19 charge in FY2025). Not large relative to market cap but warrants monitoring.

**Competitive / operational:** Fragmented European distribution invites price competition; supply-chain and geopolitical inflation risks noted in July 2026 trading update (actively managed, no material disruption to date).

**Governance / going concern:** Auditors reported **no material uncertainties** on going concern (Annual Report audit report). Tax uncertainties disclosed in note language.

**Screen blind spots:** Failed quality/moat models (ROE, margins), Lynch PEG 1.5, and EV/EBIT just above threshold — the name is cheap, not high-ROIC compounder.
