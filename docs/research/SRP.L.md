# Serco Group plc (SRP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T17:33:56.637498+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Serco is a government-services contractor pivoting toward defence and citizen services, with a record £14.6bn order book, decade-high £12.8bn pipeline, and margin progression toward ~6%. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on FCF yield (~8.8% on filing-adjusted FCF), dividend growth, earnings quality, and a Neff PEGY of 0.12, at 17.1x P/E and 1.8% yield. Filings support a value case built on surplus capital (0.75x leverage vs 1–2x target), £150m buybacks in 2026, and underlying profit guidance of ~£300m (+10% YoY). The central debate is whether defence-led growth and capital returns offset immigration/policy cyclicality, statutory–adjusted earnings divergence, and contract-rebid concentration (~40% of revenue faces rebid/extension before end-2028). Deep research broadly confirms the screen but stops short of an unqualified buy.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Serco offers a cash-generative, government-mission franchise trading below intrinsic momentum implied by its order book. The screen passes four model families with full data quality (20/20 metrics), led by FCF Yield and Earnings Quality — consistent with filing evidence: FY2025 free cash flow of £219m (112% trading cash conversion; seven-year average >100%), adjusted net debt of £206m (0.7x EBITDA), and a capital-allocation framework that returns surplus cash when leverage falls below 1.0x.

Business quality is improving, not merely cheap. Defence now represents the largest revenue sector (~£1.7bn in FY2025) and drove H1 2026 organic growth of 10%; two-thirds of £5.5bn FY2025 order intake was defence-weighted. MT&S (£242m acquisition, May 2025) deepened US defence/space capabilities. Contract retention exceeds 90% (95% H1 2026), average contract length ~7 years, and book-to-bill has exceeded 100% in recent periods. Underlying operating margin is guided to ~6.0% for 2026 (top of the 5–6% medium-term range), up from 5.6% in 2025 and 6.2% in H1 2026.

The valuation hook is cash yield plus visible capital returns, not heroic growth. At ~£5bn revenue and ~£300m underlying operating profit, the market prices modest execution risk despite a strengthening balance sheet (undrawn £400m RCF to June 2031; covenant leverage 0.75x vs 3.5x limit). Dividends rose 8% in FY2025 (4.50p total) and 10% at the interim; cumulative buybacks since 2021 reach ~£540m including the expanded 2026 programme. ROE of 17.6% (screen) aligns with underlying earnings power once non-underlying noise is stripped out.

The screen’s growth overlay (-41.8% headline growth) reflects statutory/adjusted divergence rather than underlying deterioration — a reason to prefer filing-adjusted figures and treat the buy signal as “accumulate on quality at reasonable price,” not deep cyclical distress.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and policy (immigration)** — Justice & Immigration remains ~£1.6bn of FY2025 revenue. Filings explicitly flag reduced UK immigration temporary-accommodation demand and the Australian onshore immigration contract exit. Immigration volumes are policy-sensitive; pre-close commentary notes Reform UK political claims (June 2025 news). A £25.4m provision on the European Homecare acquisition relates to contingent tax treatment (IFRS 3), requiring ongoing reassessment.

**Contract concentration and rebids** — ~78 contracts with aggregate annual revenue ~£1.8bn require rebid/extension before end-2028 (~40% of expected 2026 revenue). FY2025 rebid win rate by value was 92% over two years; going-concern stress testing assumes a 60% bid/extension failure before covenant breach — deemed implausible by directors but illustrates sensitivity.

**Statutory vs adjusted earnings** — Reported diluted EPS (14.07p FY2025; 9.50p H1 2026) trails underlying (16.93p; 10.17p) due to acquisition amortisation (£30m FY2025) and Australian deferred tax derecognition (£17.3m). FY2024 reported EPS of 4.10p was distorted by £114.5m Asia Pacific goodwill impairment. Value screens passing on “growth” should be read against this divergence.

**Leverage and leases** — Adjusted net debt is modest (0.75x), but reported net debt was £724m at H1 2026 including £496m lease liabilities, largely asylum-accommodation housing under contract. Lease interest rose with dispersed-property requirements. Yahoo D/E of 104% overstates economic leverage relative to covenant-adjusted measures but highlights gross balance-sheet scale.

**Liquidity and covenants** — Going-concern assessments (to March 2027 / August 2027) conclude adequate headroom: leverage 0.75x vs 3.5x covenant; £582m liquidity headroom at H1 2026. Net current liabilities (£80.5m) are disclosed and modelled. Contingent performance bonds totalled £211.3m (30 June 2026).

**Pension** — Accounting surplus moved to a £1.9m deficit at H1 2026; SPLAS triennial valuation requires £6.6m/year deficit recovery to March 2030. Not a near-term balance-sheet threat but a fixed cash call.

**Operational/geopolitical** — Middle East revenue fell 25% H1 2026 on contract novation to Mubadala JV and regional instability; filings cite operational resilience but limited H1 order intake (£11m standalone). US government procurement delays persisted into H1 2026. Asia Pacific turnaround remains incomplete (margin 2.2% H1 2026).

**Governance / key person** — CFO transition: Nigel Crossley retired March 2026; Mark Reid appointed Group CFO. North America CEO Michael LaRouche departing; successor search underway. No governance red flags in filings.

**Competitive** — Outsourcing market is bid-driven with peer pressure on margins; Serco’s CPAR scores >95% and >90% retention mitigate but do not eliminate pricing risk on rebids.
