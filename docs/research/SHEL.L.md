# Shell plc (SHEL.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T17:21:05.724341+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Shell plc screens as a buy on cheapness, dividend, quality, GARP, and risk metrics (P/E 10.3, P/B 1.4, yield 3.5%, debt/equity 40%), with sector-relative composite strength (74%). The investment case rests on a cash-generative integrated portfolio, progressive distributions, and active portfolio reshaping (UK upstream JV, chemicals exit, ARC acquisition, retail expansion). Valuation looks modest for a FTSE 100 energy anchor, but the screen flags earnings- and FCF-basis overlays that weaken conviction on cash-yield models. The central debate is whether elevated 2026 earnings from oil-price volatility and trading gains are sustainable, or whether Middle East disruption (Qatar LNG), rising capex ($24–26bn in 2026), and a ~$16bn ARC deal re-lever the story just as the cycle turns.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Shell offers a rare combination: headline multiples below the wider market, a 4% progressive dividend policy, and sustained buybacks ($3.0–3.5bn tranches), backed by one of the sector’s strongest balance sheets among European majors. The quantitative screen passes Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, Low P/E + High Yield, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, and Financial Health — consistent with a cyclical franchise trading below normalised mid-cycle earnings power.

Business quality is supported by filing evidence of structural cost reduction ($5.1bn pre-tax since 2022; $2.0bn in FY2025), integrated gas scale (LNG liquefaction ~28mt in FY2025), and capital discipline (40–50% of CFFO returned via dividends and buybacks). H1 2026 adjusted earnings of $16.8bn and free cash flow of $20.5bn (filings) demonstrate earnings leverage to higher realised prices, while ROACE improved to 12.4% in Q2 2026 from 9.4% at FY2025.

The screen’s buy signal is credible on price and balance-sheet metrics, but not unqualified: FCF Yield failed (company-adjusted FCF of £17.5m in the screen vs filing-aligned £23.9bn and Yahoo TTM ~£31.5bn), Piotroski F-Score is 5/9, and statutory vs adjusted EPS growth diverge. Deep research therefore supports the **valuation** buy case more than the **cash-yield** buy case — accumulate on weakness rather than treat the screen as a pure FCF compounder signal.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and commodity.** Earnings, CFFO, and FCF remain highly oil- and gas-price sensitive. FY2025 already showed adjusted earnings down 22% on softer prices; H1 2026 gains may partly reverse if geopolitical premia fade. Chemicals remained loss-making in FY2025 (adjusted earnings −$1.1bn for the segment).

**Geopolitical and operational.** Qatar LNG production has been shut since early March 2026 following an attack on Ras Laffan; Pearl GTL damage is still being assessed (RNS, 19 March 2026). Q2 integrated gas production fell 31% quarter-on-quarter on Qatari volumes. Norway’s Ormen Lange field is running ~40% below capacity until 2027 after a compressor failure (news, August 2026). These are material near-term volume risks not captured by static value screens.

**Leverage and capital allocation.** Net debt rose in FY2025 despite FCF; H1 2026 improvement is partly cyclical. The ARC Resources acquisition (~$16.4bn enterprise value, ~$13.6bn equity) adds production but increases balance-sheet complexity and pauses buybacks during regulatory windows. Cash capex guidance rises to $24–26bn in 2026. Raizen joint venture restructuring may require a BRL3.5bn ($685m) Shell capital injection and dilute Shell’s stake (H1 2026 notes).

**Regulatory and fiscal.** FY2025 upstream identified items included a $536m UK Energy Profits Levy charge. Pillar Two top-up tax was $299m in FY2025. Climate-related impairment and biofuels project write-offs (Rotterdam) continue in marketing/chemicals. Transition and emissions-compliance costs (e.g. Brennstoffemissionshandelsgesetz payments of $1.4bn in Q4 2025) affect cash timing.

**Pension and decommissioning.** At 31 December 2025 (annual filing balance sheet): retirement benefit asset $5.1bn vs non-current liability $7.1bn; decommissioning provisions $21.4bn non-current plus $5.9bn current — long-dated but real cash calls.

**Reserves and accounting.** SEC proved RRR was negative in 2025 (−40%); reserve quality bears monitoring. Adjusted vs statutory earnings diverge materially (identified items, fair-value commodity derivatives). Screen overlays flag earnings-basis and FCF-basis mismatches — do not treat screen FCF yield as reliable without filing reconciliation.

**Governance / transition.** Portfolio churn (renewables disposals to TotalEnergies, Sprng Energy sale, chemicals divestiture process) creates execution risk. Management’s 2050 net-zero ambition is explicitly outside the three-year planning horizon per filing boilerplate.
