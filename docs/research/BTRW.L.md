## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Barratt Redrow screens as a **buy** on deep asset discount, dividend yield, and balance-sheet strength, trading well below book after the Barratt–Redrow merger and a cyclical earnings trough. The quantitative screen passes seven of 22 models (composite 58%, sector-relative 61%), anchored by P/B 0.54, yield 5.9%, and D/E 3%, with cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk families all clearing. Operationally, FY26 is tracking solidly—consensus adjusted PBT ~£568m, completions 17,200–17,800, forward sales £3.5bn (+12.8%)—with net cash guided to £550–650m and Redrow synergies on plan. The central debate is whether sub-book valuation and accelerating capital returns (annual £100m+ buybacks; planned £400m FY27 return per recent news) outweigh cyclical housing risk, depressed reported ROE (2.8%), and legal/regulatory overhangs (building safety, CMA competition probe, £4bn+ class action). FY25 consolidated P&L from primary filing bodies is partially unreadable; figures below use Yahoo fallback where noted.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Barratt Redrow offers an uncommon combination in UK housebuilding: a net-cash balance sheet, sub-1.0x P/B, and a dividend-plus-buyback framework, set against a franchise with 17 consecutive HBF five-star ratings and the sector’s largest combined output platform post-merger.

The screen signal aligns with business quality on **cheapness** (Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value: P/B 0.54 vs tangible book ~£6.3bn per Yahoo FY2025 balance sheet), **dividend** (High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth: 5.9% yield; FY25 annual report confirms transition to 2.0x adjusted-earnings cover plus ≥£100m annual buybacks from FY26), **GARP** (Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY—valuation compresses faster than growth expectations have been cut), and **risk** (Financial Health: D/E 3%, net-cash history in filings). Data quality is high (20/20 metrics).

The Redrow merger (completed August 2024) enlarged scale—three brands, confirmed £100m cost synergies (£69m identified at FY25 year-end, £50m incremental in FY26 per Q3 trading update)—while near-term earnings remain well below pre-2022 peaks (Barratt standalone adjusted PBT fell from £1,054.8m in FY22 to £884.3m in FY23 per Companies House accounts). FY26 adjusted PBT consensus of £568m (Q3 trading update, 15 April 2026) implies recovery but not full normalisation. The screen’s negative FCF flag (-£77m) reflects working-capital absorption (inventory £8.3bn per Yahoo FY2025); operating cash generation is expected to strengthen as WIP converts through H2 FY26.

Timing is neutral (RSI 63, marginally below 200-day MA); screen conviction is low (30%) because the signal is new—deep research supports **accumulate** rather than an aggressive chase.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / macro:** UK mortgage affordability, interest rates, and consumer confidence remain principal demand drivers. Q3 FY26 update flags Middle East conflict contributing to rate/cost uncertainty beyond FY26. Reservation rates are resilient but not robustly above historical norms.

**Regulatory / legal:** FY2024 annual report (filings) notes CMA investigation (launched 26 February 2024) into suspected exchange of competitively sensitive information among eight housebuilders including Barratt and Redrow—investigation ongoing. News (30 June 2026): potential **£4bn+ class action** against major housebuilders including Barratt Redrow alleging price/incentive information sharing—requires Competition Appeal Tribunal approval; not captured in screen metrics.

**Building safety:** FY2022 annual report (filings): £396m additional provision for 30-year remediation pledge; Residential Property Developer Tax (RPDT) in effect; concern over expanded Building Safety Levy. Q3 FY26: legacy remediation payment timing affects net-cash guidance—liability persists.

**Merger / integration:** Redrow integration largely on track (IT completion April 2026 per Q3 update) but PPA adjustments distort statutory vs adjusted earnings; RBC downgrade (December 2025 news) cited integration issues. Revenue synergy outlets depend on planning—16 submitted at FY25 year-end.

**Governance / succession:** Group CEO David Thomas retiring; Dean Banks to succeed (final quarter 2026, RNS 4 March 2026). Rebecca Napier appointed CFO (June 2026 news). Transition risk moderate given Thomas remains until March 2027.

**Pension:** Defined benefit pension exposure referenced in historical filings; not quantified in available bodies for FY25.

**Competitive:** Five-star customer satisfaction unmatched, but sector consolidation and planning reform benefits are shared; land-buying discipline reduces near-term growth optionality (FY26 land approvals cut).

**Going concern / viability:** FY2025 and prior annual reports include viability statements; full going-concern wording not extractable from available body text—no explicit concern flagged in Chair/CEO narrative. Covenant detail not present in indexed bodies.

**Screen blind spots:** Reported ROE 2.8% and negative screen FCF reflect trough earnings and inventory cycle, not insolvency risk—but limit near-term “quality at a reasonable price” interpretation on earnings-based metrics.
