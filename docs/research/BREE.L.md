# Breedon Group plc (BREE.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-06T07:31:35.857298+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Breedon Group is a vertically integrated UK construction materials business (aggregates, cement, asphalt, ready-mixed concrete) with growing exposure to Ireland and the US Midwest. The quantitative screen rates it a **strong buy** on cheapness (P/B ~1.0, FCF yield ~11%), dividend yield (~4.5%), and cash conversion, despite failing quality/moat models on low ROE and elevated leverage. Filings show a business generating record post-Covid free cash flow (£133.2m in FY2025) while statutory earnings fall on amortisation, higher interest, and a fourth consecutive year of GB volume decline. The central debate is whether asset-backed cash generation and geographic diversification (Ireland NDP, US infrastructure) justify the valuation before a UK residential recovery, or whether cyclical pressure and acquisition-led leverage (covenant leverage 2.1x at H1 2026) cap upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal rests on four families passing: **cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk**. Breedon trades near book (P/B 0.96), at EV/EBITDA 7.2, with FCF yield ~11.3% and dividend yield ~4.5%; FCF covers the dividend ~2.6× on a net basis. Nine of 22 models pass, including Deep Value, FCF Yield, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, Earnings Quality, and Financial Health — a profile consistent with a cyclical value compounder rather than a quality compounder.

Business quality is mixed but not weak. Breedon holds ~1.7bn tonnes of mineral reserves, operates two GB cement plants, and has built a US platform via Lionmark (Mar 2025, US$238m EV) and Falling Springs (May 2026, US$120m EV). FY2025 underlying EBITDA rose 3% to £278.8m on revenue up 9% to £1,713.8m, with over £20m of self-help savings offsetting LFL revenue down 3%. Record FCF of £133.2m (48% conversion) and a progressive dividend (15.0p, +3%) support the income and cash-yield pillars of the screen.

The screen correctly flags weaknesses: statutory EPS growth is negative (−13.9% statutory; −18.7% filing-adjusted core), ROE is 6.9%, Piotroski F-Score is 5/9, and the quality family fails. For a value investor, the thesis is that **market pessimism on UK construction is partly priced in**, while Ireland/US growth and disciplined M&A provide optionality; the balance sheet remains within covenant limits (leverage threshold 3.0x; reported 1.8x at Dec 2025, 2.1x at Jun 2026).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / market.** GB construction volumes have declined for four consecutive years (management expects a fifth in 2026). Residential housebuilding is subdued; ready-mixed concrete volumes are under particular pressure. Pricing came under pressure after years of volume decline. Recovery timing in UK residential is uncertain despite planning reforms.

**Leverage and M&A.** Net debt rose to £690.5m at Jun 2026 from £527.3m at Dec 2025 following ~£110m H1 acquisitions. Covenant leverage 2.1x at H1 (threshold 3.0x; target 1–2x). Integration and amortisation burden from Lionmark, Booth, and Falling Springs; ROIC fell to 7.0% (H1 LTM) vs medium-term 10% target. Pro-forma leverage post-Falling Springs estimated at 2.0x.

**Regulatory / climate.** Emerging risks flagged include UK/EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (2026/2027), carbon capture/storage (Peak Cluster; >£20m investment over three years pre-FID), and cement decarbonisation costs (£5.8m non-underlying in FY2025). “Back British Cement” campaign reflects concern over import competition and uneven carbon regulation.

**Operational.** Principal risks per filings: acquisitions/capex execution, critical asset failure, land/mineral permitting, health & safety, supply-chain/input costs (Middle East conflict impact noted as minimal to date via hedging), IT/cyber. Kinnegad cement mill unscheduled shutdown (May 2026) temporarily compressed Ireland margins.

**Governance / going concern.** Going concern basis affirmed in both annual and interim filings with covenant headroom under stress scenarios. No material pension deficit flagged in available filing extracts (pension not listed among principal risks in RNS summaries).

**Screen-identified weaknesses.** Negative EPS growth, low ROE (6.9%), failed quality/moat/Buffett models, Piotroski 5/9, current ratio 1.35 (< Graham 2.0).
