# Breedon Group plc (BREE.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-24T20:44:27.097664+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Breedon Group plc is a vertically integrated construction materials group operating in Great Britain, Ireland and the United States, supplying aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete, asphalt and surfacing. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy (9/22 models; composite score 85%), anchored on cheapness (P/B 0.9, EV/EBITDA 6.0), a 4.9% dividend yield, a 10.2% FCF yield and broad passes across value, income and risk families. The valuation hook is an asset-backed consolidator — filings cite ~1.4bn tonnes of mineral reserves and a 19% revenue CAGR track record — trading below book after a material share-price drawdown noted in recent press. The key debate is whether US platform expansion and resilient underlying EBITDA offset a sharp UK cyclical downturn, rising absolute leverage after M&A, and four consecutive years of declining reported net income. Primary filing coverage in this pack is annual-only (Companies House); interim RNS bodies are absent and consolidated statement tables are not reliably extractable from OCR.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen's strong buy signal rests on four passed families — cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk — with full metric coverage (20/20) and sector-relative strength (83% composite vs 85% absolute). Breedon fits a classic asset-heavy value profile: locally essential materials, quarry consent barriers, and an aggregates-led model that pulls through into higher-margin downstream products. Filings describe a vertically integrated group with 100+ aggregate quarries, two cement plants, and a third geographic platform established in the US (BMC acquisition approved in 2024 per the annual report body).

For a value investor, the quantitative case is compelling on price versus cash and assets: passed models include Deep Value, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, Earnings Quality and Financial Health. The 2025 annual report OCR highlights a 47% dividend payout ratio, a 3% year-on-year dividend increase, and covenant leverage reduced by 0.4× since the half year — consistent with management's stated discipline of de-levering post-deal. Yahoo fallback shows revenue rising from £1,396m (2022) to £1,714m (2025) while FCF recovered to £105.8m in 2025 from £70.4m in 2024, supporting the FCF-yield screen pass.

Business quality is reasonable but not pristine: ROIC was 9.0% in 2024 and 9.9% in 2023 (filings), above many cyclicals but below best-in-class; screen ROE is 7.1%. Reported net income has fallen from £112.5m (2022) to £83.8m (2025) (Yahoo fallback), so the headline P/E of 13.0 may understate cyclical and normalised earnings risk. Screen conviction remains low (42%, new signal, one week at strong buy). The thesis is quality cyclical at a distressed multiple — not a clean growth compounder — where patient capital earns income and optionality on US consolidation while UK volumes recover.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Cyclical / end-market: UK construction materials demand weakened materially in 2025. News cites management describing a "testing year" with deferred infrastructure projects; Investors' Chronicle (11 Mar 2026) flagged difficulty escaping UK construction gloom. The 2024 annual report notes challenging markets and poor weather even as record revenue was delivered — volume and margin remain weather- and macro-sensitive.

Leverage and integration: Yahoo net debt of £481m (2025) follows BMC (2024) and further US expansion (~$120m Falling Springs quarry, TipRanks, 8 Jun 2026). Higher absolute debt and interest (£28.9m in 2025) increase sensitivity to a prolonged downturn. Filing OCR cites covenant leverage improvement, but RCF maturity in June 2026 (initial accounts) is a refinancing watchpoint not updated in legible later filings.

Earnings quality: Four-year decline in net income and EPS despite revenue growth; ROE 7.1% on screen is modest. Restructuring/M&A charges appear in each year (Yahoo). P/B discount must be weighed against £792m goodwill/intangibles.

Regulatory / reserves: Filings emphasise that new quarry consents are rarely granted; long reserve life (~47 years) depends on extensions, replenishment and M&A. Climate-related and principal-risk disclosures are indexed in the 2024 report table of contents but not legible in OCR bodies.

Governance / pension / contingencies: Initial accounts confirm going-concern basis and covenant headroom. Pension deficit, litigation quanta and contingency schedules are not surfaced in extractable filing text — material gaps for verify-before-trade work. No distressed financing or regulatory enforcement appears in the news manifest.
