# Breedon Group plc (BREE.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T18:34:24.585171+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Breedon Group plc is a vertically integrated construction materials business spanning Great Britain, Ireland and the United States, with long-life mineral reserves, two UK cement plants, and a progressive cash-return policy. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy on cheapness (P/B ~0.9, EV/EBITDA ~7.2), an ~11.5% FCF yield, and a ~4.5% dividend yield, with nine of 22 models passing across cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk families. Filings confirm resilient cash generation and covenant headroom, but earnings quality is cyclically constrained: Great Britain volumes are in a multi-year downturn, ROIC is 7.0–7.8% versus management’s 10% medium-term target, and leverage has risen with US M&A. The central debate is whether geographic diversification (Ireland, US infrastructure) and self-help can sustain cash returns through a prolonged UK construction trough, or whether further M&A and cyclical weakness will keep returns below cost of capital.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Breedon offers a rare combination in UK basic materials: tangible asset backing, statutory net assets of £1,197m at 31 December 2025, and filing-verified free cash flow of £133.2m in FY2025 (48% FCF conversion), supporting a 15.0p full-year dividend (47% payout). The screen’s strong-buy signal (composite 81%, conviction persistent for 12 weeks) is grounded in deep value and cash-return metrics rather than earnings momentum: statutory basic EPS fell to 24.2p in 2025 from 28.1p, yet the business passed Financial Health, Earnings Quality, FCF Yield, Dividend Growth and Composite Value models with full data quality (20/20 metrics).

Business quality supports the signal at the balance-sheet level. Breedon holds 1.5–1.7bn tonnes of mineral reserves, operates as the largest British-based domestic cement manufacturer, and has extended RCF maturity to July 2029 (subsequently July 2030 post-H1 2026) alongside long-dated USPP notes. Management delivered over £20m of operational savings in 2025, maintained underlying EBITDA margins near 16% group-wide despite LFL revenue decline, and reduced covenant leverage from a H1 2025 peak of 2.2x to 1.8x at year-end. The investment case is therefore “quality cyclical at a discount”: not a growth compounder today, but a cash-generative, asset-heavy platform priced for continued UK weakness, with Ireland and US bolt-ons providing partial offset and optionality when GB volumes recover.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Cyclical exposure dominates. Filings describe GB ready-mixed concrete volumes declining for a fifth consecutive year in 2026, with residential new-build particularly weak; pricing pressure intensified after four years of volume decline. Enquiry levels are elevated but conversion to orders remains delayed — a classic late-cycle pattern that screens may underweight.

Leverage and M&A integration. Lionmark (US$238m EV, March 2025), Falling Springs (US$120m, May/June 2026) and Booth (Ireland) have lifted net debt and amortisation of acquired intangibles (£10.9m in H1 2026 alone). Pro-forma leverage was guided at 2.0x post-Falling Springs. ROIC at 7.0–7.8% sits below management’s 10% medium-term target, reflecting acquisition dilution and subdued profitability — a red flag for capital allocation quality if M&A continues without organic recovery.

Regulatory and climate. Breedon flags UK/EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism implementation (2026/2027), uneven carbon regulation, high UK energy costs, and cement import competition. Peak Cluster CCS requires >£20m investment over three years pre-FID; cement decarbonisation costs were £5.8m in FY2025. These are structural, not transient.

Operational and financial covenant language is reassuring but not risk-free. Going concern statements (annual and interim) cite comfortable covenant compliance, liquidity, and a “severe but plausible” downside (5–10% revenue reduction) retaining headroom. Borrowing facilities (£400m RCF to 2030; ~£363m USPP) remain compliant; interest cover 13.5x. However, H1 2026 net debt of £690.5m at 2.1x leverage sits at the upper end of the 1–2x management target, with H1 FCF negative and £110m acquisition spend — deleveraging in H2 is assumed, not yet delivered.

Other filing-identified principal risks: acquisitions/capex execution, land and mineral permitting, health and safety, IT/cyber, supply-chain/input costs (Middle East conflict cited as monitored; minimal direct impact to date per H1 2026), and market/competition. Pension risk is not prominently quantified in the indexed filing extracts reviewed.
