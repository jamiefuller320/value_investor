# The Berkeley Group Holdings plc (BKG.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:28:22.391175+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Berkeley Group is a premium UK brownfield housebuilder focused on London, Birmingham and the South-East, with an asset-heavy model underpinned by c.52,800 plots and £6.4bn of risk-adjusted future gross margin per the FY26 final results (30 April 2026). The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness (P/E 10.8, P/B 0.9), dividend history, GARP and financial health, with the stock trading below reported NAV of £39.17 per share. Earnings have declined for four consecutive years (PBT £604m in FY23 to £451m in FY26 per filings), and forward sales have fallen to £1.0bn from £1.4bn, yet net cash rose to £363m and management is prioritising buybacks over new land. The central debate is whether sub-NAV capital returns and land-optionality compensate for a cyclical downturn, regulatory friction, and a April 2026 strategy reset that halted land buying and rephased profit targets.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
Berkeley fits a value screen because the market prices the equity below its balance sheet and below management’s stated NAV, despite a net-cash position and a capital-return framework targeting £2.0bn of shareholder returns under Berkeley 2035 (with £372m returned to date). The screen passes Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Dividend Growth, Dreman Contrarian and Financial Health — four of four model families (cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk). Business quality is differentiated versus volume housebuilders: 90% brownfield delivery, long-dated regeneration sites, and operating margins of 18.7% (FY26) versus a sector exposed to commodity land buying.

For a value investor, the hook is not peak earnings but **asset-backed downside protection plus optionality**. At P/B 0.9 and P/E 10.8 on depressed profits, the market appears to capitalise cyclical fear rather than £363m net cash, £1.0bn cash due on forward sales, and land holdings management values at £6.4bn future gross margin. Management explicitly favours buybacks when the share price sits below NAV — a policy-aligned catalyst. ROE of 12.5% and ROCE of 13.8% (FY26 filing) remain respectable for a de-risking phase, and the current ratio of 3.58 (screen) signals balance-sheet resilience screens cannot fully capture in a cyclical sector.

The screen’s FCF caveat is material: filing-aligned FCF is £192.1m (Yahoo annual cash flow, FY25 year-end) versus a higher TTM screen figure of £353.2m — working-capital swings in development inventory distort headline FCF. That gap does not invalidate cheapness on P/B and earnings yield, but it argues for patience on timing rather than aggressive sizing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / demand:** Forward sales fell to £1,006m (FY26) from £1,403m (FY25), with reservation values 15% below the prior two-year run-rate. Management describes buyer caution, SDLT drag, and delayed interest-rate relief. Completed stock spread across 29 developments; ASP on completions fell to £546k (from £593k) on mix. House price stagnation and higher-rate environment weigh on the sector (news flow, July 2026).

**Regulatory / planning:** FY26 RNS cites planning timelines extending to eight years (from five a decade ago), Peckham scheme blocked despite local-plan allocation, and Beckton/Motspur Park refusals. Building Safety Regulator approvals add delay. Management calls for SDLT reform and full implementation of the Homes for London package. April 2026 halt on new land buying explicitly cites inability to achieve required returns under current tax/regulatory burden — a structural headwind screens may underweight.

**Litigation / competition:** June 2026 news reports a potential £4bn class-action against major UK housebuilders including Berkeley, alleging anti-competitive information sharing on prices and incentives (Yahoo Finance, 30 June 2026). Outcome uncertain; reputational and financial exposure unquantified in filings reviewed.

**Building safety remediation:** Self-Remediation Contract with MHCLG covers 825 relevant buildings; 39 with works outstanding per FY26 RNS. Provisions of £226m include fire-safety and post-completion obligations. Residual liability and supply-chain recovery remain execution risks.

**Governance / strategy:** April 2026 profit warning and strategy reset (land-buy pause, rephased 2035 targets) prompted Deutsche Bank downgrade (24 April 2026, news manifest). Executive Chair Rob Perrins voluntarily reduced bonus ahead of September 2026 AGM (Kalkine Media, 1 September 2026) — signals alignment but also acknowledges shareholder pressure. Insider buying of c.£1.89m (May 2026, Simply Wall St) and ongoing buybacks support the capital-return story.

**Liquidity / leverage:** Net cash £363m and undrawn facilities provide headroom; going-concern basis confirmed in FY26 accounts. Liquidity risk is disclosed as the principal financial risk, mitigated by forward sales and low gearing — but £660m gross debt and land creditors of £486m require continued completions to unwind working capital.

**Accounting:** NAV at historic cost may understate realisable land value in an upturn but also embeds optimistic margin assumptions (24.4% risk-adjusted future gross margin on land holdings). Investment property on BTR platform (£297m) carries internal fair-value estimates with stabilisation discounts.
