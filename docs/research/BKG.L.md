# The Berkeley Group Holdings plc (BKG.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:04:12.595501+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Berkeley Group Holdings plc (BKG.L) screens as a buy on quantitative value metrics—P/E 10.7, P/B 0.9, and a current ratio of 3.58—supported by a net-cash balance sheet and a capital-return programme that prioritises buybacks when the share price trades below reported NAV. The investment case rests on asset backing (NAV per share £39.17 at 30 April 2026), brownfield land optionality (£6.4bn future gross margin in land holdings), and disciplined capital allocation under the Berkeley 2035 framework. The central debate is whether cyclical and regulatory headwinds—declining forward sales, halted land buying, and rephased profit targets—are already reflected in a sub-book valuation, or whether earnings compression has further to run. Forward sales have fallen from £1,403m to £1,006m in two years, which warrants caution even as FY2026 results were described as resilient and in line with guidance.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Berkeley offers a rare combination of deep-value multiples, balance-sheet strength, and a management team explicitly returning capital when the market prices the stock below NAV. The screen passes 11 of 22 models across all four families—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—including Graham Defensive, Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, and Financial Health, with composite score 62% (sector-relative 64%) and high data quality (18/20 metrics). Business quality is differentiated: London/South-East brownfield regeneration, 90% of completions on brownfield land, industry-leading customer metrics (NPS +77.9), and net cash of £363m after £233m of buybacks in FY2026. The screen’s FCF yield support (£192m FCF per Yahoo, aligned with filing cash generation) and P/B below 1.0 sit comfortably with a company that grew NAV per share 9% to £39.17 while shrinking the share count. Management’s stated preference for buybacks below NAV creates an explicit valuation floor mechanism that reinforces the quantitative cheapness signal, even as headline earnings decline.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Regulatory and planning: Berkeley cites lengthening London development timelines (eight years vs five a decade ago), Planning Inspectorate refusals (Peckham, 850+ homes), and pending Building Safety Levy. RPDT lifts the effective tax rate to 29.5%. Management argues SDLT surcharges and regulatory burden are suppressing London delivery to under 10% of MHCLG targets.

Cyclical exposure: Forward sales down 28% over two years (£1,403m → £1,006m); reservation run-rate 15% below prior two-year average. April 2026 strategy rephase—halted land buying, slower build rates—triggered a sharp share-price reaction (news: ~14–17% decline). PBT has declined three consecutive years (£557m → £529m → £451m).

Fire-safety remediation: Self-Remediation Terms with MHCLG cover 825 relevant buildings; 39 buildings still require works. Provisions of £226.3m include fire-safety and post-completion obligations. Remediation risk is partly mitigated by supply-chain and insurer recoveries, but cost and timing uncertainty remains.

Governance and key person: Executive Chair Rob Perrins leads strategy; estate of founder Tony Pidgley remains a 3%+ holder (RNS holding notification, April 2026). No material governance red flags in indexed filings, but concentration of strategic direction is notable.

Competitive and sector: Goldman Sachs initiated at Sell (November 2025, news); analyst fair-value estimates have edged down (Yahoo narrative articles, May–June 2026). Berkeley’s London premium positioning limits direct volume competition but increases policy sensitivity vs volume housebuilders.

Liquidity and leverage: Near-term liquidity appears strong (net cash, undrawn RCF, going-concern confirmation). Medium-term earnings visibility is weaker given rephased profit targets and declining forward sales—this is an earnings-quality concern rather than a balance-sheet liquidity crisis.

Pension: Actuarial gain on pension scheme £0.1m in FY2026 other comprehensive income; no material pension deficit flagged in indexed filing extracts.
