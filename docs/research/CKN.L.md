# Clarkson PLC (CKN.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:29:48.324682+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Clarkson PLC is the world’s largest integrated shipbroker and maritime investment bank, earning commission-led revenue across broking, capital markets, port support, and subscription research. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness (P/E 20.3, P/B 2.9), quality (ROE 15.3%, D/E ~11%), and 24 years of progressive dividends, against a franchise with net cash, a US$244m forward order book, and recurring research income (~91% of Research sales). The central debate is whether H1 2026’s record underlying PBT of £61.5m—boosted by Strait of Hormuz disruption and derivatives volatility—is sustainable, or whether FY2025’s 21% profit decline (to £90.6m) better reflects normalised earnings power. At ~20x reported earnings and with filing-aligned FCF of £57m (well below the screen’s suppressed TTM cashflow figure), the name screens as quality-at-a-reasonable-price rather than deep cyclical trough.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Clarksons offers a rare combination: a market-leading, asset-light intermediary with structural data and platform advantages (Sea, Clarksons Research, Zuma/Venetian), low balance-sheet risk, and a shareholder-friendly dividend policy that survived a down year in 2025. The screen passes seven models spanning Graham Enterprising, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Earnings Quality, and Financial Health—five factor families in total—with full data quality (20/20 metrics). That alignment is credible: reported leverage is modest (lease liabilities ~£56m; no material bank debt), pensions are in surplus (£14.4m at FY2025 year-end), and management returns excess cash while reinvesting in people, M&A, and technology. The 2025 dip in broking margins (geo-political caution, weaker USD) was partly offset by a record Financial division and resilient Research subscriptions, demonstrating diversification beyond pure freight-rate beta. The buy case rests on buying a compounding franchise near fair value, with upside if geopolitical volatility persists and downside capped by cash, order-book visibility, and management’s stress-tested going-concern framework (profit floor scenario: £30m PBT).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / earnings quality:** FY2025 underlying PBT fell 21% despite dividend growth; H1 2026’s surge is explicitly linked to Strait of Hormuz disruption and freight derivatives volatility—conditions that may normalise. The board warns full-year 2026 may not be second-half weighted, implying H1 strength could overstate run-rate earnings.

**FX:** Most revenue is USD-denominated; sterling reporting creates translation risk (FY2025 headwind from USD strength; H1 2025 unrealised FX losses elevated head-office costs).

**Credit / counterparty:** Net impairment losses on financial assets rose to £7.7m in H1 2026; filings flag client default as a principal risk. Bonus accruals and receivable swings distort half-year operating cashflow (H1 2026 operating cash outflow £29.4m).

**Structural weak spots:** Support division profits fell in FY2025 and H1 2026 on UK offshore/renewables delays, low Suez transits, and soft UK bulk volumes—partially offset by Northern Europe and Gibb Medical & Rescue.

**M&A / goodwill:** H1 2026 added ~£62m intangibles (Link £48.1m initial consideration plus employment-linked payments; Zuma £7.5m). Goodwill impairment is flagged if any market sees a “sustained downturn.”

**Governance / key person:** Long-serving CFO/COO Jeff Woyda retires September 2026; Niamh Staunton (ex-BP) appointed CFO. CEO Andi Case has led since 2008—succession concentration at the top.

**Tax / regulatory:** Pillar Two top-up tax of £1.3m recognised in H1 2026 (Greece, UAE, China, Switzerland). Sanctions complexity (~1,000 sanctioned tankers cited in FY2025 narrative) increases compliance and conduct risk. Litigation contingencies: ordinary-course claims covered by professional indemnity; none deemed material.

**Pension:** Combined defined-benefit surplus £12.0m at 30 Jun 2025 with asset-ceiling adjustment on Plowrights scheme—manageable but requires monitoring.
