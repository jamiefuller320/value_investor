# Vistry Group PLC (VTY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-04T09:07:50.609278+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Vistry Group PLC is a UK partnerships-led housebuilder trading at distressed multiples — the quantitative screen flags a **Strong Buy** (12/22 models; composite 91%) on P/B 0.29, P/E 7.2, FCF yield and a Piotroski score of 7/9, with leverage modest at c.18% D/E. Primary filings show FY2025 adjusted profit stabilised at £268.8m after the 2024 South Division cost-forecasting shock, but reported profitability remains well below 2023 restated peaks and ROCE has compressed to 13.9%. The central debate is whether the stock is a classic deep-value recovery — partnerships exposure to government affordable housing programmes, net debt reduction, and tangible book near £2.2bn — or a value trap given open-market weakness, governance/control failures, elevated short interest, and a guided H1 2026 statutory loss. Conviction is tempered by thin consolidated interim disclosure in the filing pack and deteriorating near-term earnings momentum in news flow.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Vistry offers textbook cheapness against asset backing and normalised earnings power. The screen passes all four metric families (cheapness, quality, GARP, risk): deep value and Schloss low P/B on 0.29× book; earnings and FCF yield screens on low single-digit P/E; Lynch PEG and Magic Formula on depressed price versus earnings growth (Yahoo: 21% earnings growth); and financial-health screens on sub-20% gearing and a current ratio above 2.5×. Business quality is mixed rather than absent: the Group is the UK’s largest builder by volume (15,658 completions in FY2025 per the 2025 annual report), 74% partner-funded, with a 71,501-plot owned/controlled landbank and 5-star HBF customer satisfaction — structural alignment with affordable housing policy (SAHP 2026–2036, Homes England PlacePoint JV).

The screen’s quality failures are informative rather than disqualifying for a deep-value sleeve: ROE 4.2% and thin margins reflect the 2024 earnings collapse and amortisation-heavy Countryside integration, not necessarily permanent impairment; Graham/Buffett quality screens fail on ROE and lack of dividend. Piotroski 7/9 confirms improving operating cash flow, declining leverage, and no dilution — consistent with management’s stated priority of inventory run-down and deleveraging (net debt £144.2m at 31 December 2025, down from £180.7m). At roughly 0.3× tangible book (Yahoo fallback: tangible book value £2.17bn at FY2025 year-end), the market prices permanent destruction of franchise value; if adjusted PBT can hold near £250–270m and ROCE rebuilds toward mid-teens, the risk/reward favours accumulation rather than avoidance — but this is a recovery thesis, not a quality compounder.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Operational and governance:** The 2024 South Division cost-forecasting failure (£91.5m adjusted PBT impact; 18 sites adjusted) exposed weak regional controls and triggered restated comparatives, leadership changes, and a flatter divisional structure. FY2025 AR states controls are “embedded,” but employee engagement scores were weak (9% favourable in July 2025 survey, recovering to 62% by November) and voluntary turnover rose to 18.6% — execution risk persists.

**Governance:** Greg Fitzgerald holds combined Executive Chair and CEO roles (acknowledged Code deviation); CEO succession planning cited but news reports Adam Daniels appointment (Jul 2026) amid ongoing leadership uncertainty (“Vistry held back by leadership uncertainty,” Investors’ Chronicle, 4 Mar 2026).

**Cyclical / market:** Open-market sales constrained by mortgage affordability; incentives ~4.5% of ASP. PRS volumes −23% in FY2025. Partner-funded demand delayed around Autumn Budget 2025. Inventory £3.2bn+ ties capital; pace of reduction is critical to net cash target.

**Legal / regulatory:** Voluntary CMA commitments (Jul 2025) following sector investigation. **Class-action lawsuit** alleging price/incentive information sharing among seven major housebuilders, potentially **£4bn+** (Yahoo Finance, 30 Jun 2026) — requires Competition Appeal Tribunal approval; contingent liability not quantified in available filing extracts.

**Financial structure:** Average daily debt £734m vs year-end net debt £144m implies intra-year working-capital swings; land acquisitions (c.9,500 plots H2 2025) consume cash even as management targets deleveraging.

**Screen blind spots:** No NCAV (Graham net-net fails); no dividend (multiple income screens fail); ROE and margins too low for quality-value models — consistent with cyclical trough, not necessarily balance-sheet distress (D/E 18%, current ratio 2.5×).

**Short interest:** Investegate short-position register (Feb 2026) shows multiple hedge funds with >0.5% disclosed shorts (e.g. GLG Partners 2.24%, Schonfeld UK 2.44%) — market scepticism is material.
