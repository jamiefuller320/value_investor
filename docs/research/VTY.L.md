# Vistry Group PLC (VTY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-23T06:55:59.535912+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Vistry Group is a UK mixed-tenure housebuilder operating almost entirely through its Partnerships model, pre-selling affordable and partner-funded homes to registered providers, local authorities and PRS operators, with a smaller Open Market channel via Bovis, Linden and Countryside brands. The quantitative screen flags a deep value opportunity: P/B ~0.3, P/E ~6.3, low leverage (D/E ~18%), and filing-aligned FCF of ~£175m, with 12 of 22 models passing across cheapness, quality, GARP and risk families.

The investment case rests on extreme book discount, improving FY2025 cash generation (net debt reduced to £144m at year-end), and structural UK housing undersupply aligned with the £37bn SAHP programme — but earnings quality is weak (ROCE 13.9% vs a 40% medium-term target; ROE 4.2%), and the share price reflects a bruising 2024 South Division cost-control failure (£165m), leadership upheaval, and elevated short interest. The central debate is whether 2026’s transition year — H1 loss, aggressive discounting, paused buybacks, and a pending CEO strategic review — marks a trough that unlocks net-cash balance-sheet optionality, or a value trap in a cyclical sector where margins and inventory remain under pressure.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
**Why the screen rates Strong Buy.** Vistry passes Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Piotroski F-Score (7/9), Composite Value, Earnings Quality and Financial Health. Key metrics: P/B 0.25, P/E 6.3, D/E 18%, current ratio 2.5, FCF ~£147–175m (screen TTM vs filing-aligned FY2025 £174.6m). Cheapness and financial-health signals dominate; the stock trades below tangible book (~£2.17bn per Yahoo balance sheet 2025) despite positive operating cash flow and manageable nominal leverage.

**Business quality — mixed.** Filings confirm a differentiated, capital-light Partnerships model: 74% of FY2025 completions were partner-funded; the Group delivered one in seven UK affordable homes and maintains a 5-star HBF satisfaction score. Land bank of 71,501 owned/controlled plots and a £4.5bn forward order book (May 2026 trading update) provide revenue visibility. However, the screen correctly fails quality/moat models: ROE 4.2% (below Graham/Buffett thresholds), thin margins, no dividend (failed High Dividend Yield and Dividend Growth), and ROCE of 13.9% is far below management’s 40% medium-term ambition. Business quality is adequate for a cyclical builder, not compounder-grade — which is precisely why it screens cheap.

**Value investor angle.** At ~0.25× book and ~6× earnings, the market prices permanent impairment from the 2024 South Division accounting/control failure, Open Market weakness, and scepticism over FY2026 guidance (consensus adjusted PBT ~£200m vs peak adjusted PBT of £419m in 2023). If new CEO Adam Daniels delivers net cash >£100m by end-2026, overhead savings of ~£25m, and SAHP-driven partner volume recovery in H2, normalised earnings power could re-rate the equity without requiring a housing boom. The screen’s neutral timing (RSI ~46, below 200-day MA) is consistent with a patient accumulation rather than momentum entry.

**FY2025 (Annual Report 2025, published March 2026):** Adjusted PBT was broadly flat at £268.8m (+2% vs £263.5m), but completions fell 9% to 15,658 and adjusted revenue eased to £4.2bn. A strong H2 offset Budget-related delays to partner deals in Q3/Q4. Land sales contributed £180m of revenue (FY2024: £91m) as the Group monetised non-core parcels. Net debt improved £36.5m to £144.2m at 31 December 2025, though average daily debt rose to £734m — inventory run-off and delayed partner completions limited deleveraging pace. Partner-funded mix held at 74%; Open Market units fell 11% to 4,065 with incentives averaging 4.5% of ASP.

**FY2024 (Annual Report 2024):** A step-change deterioration. South Division cost-forecasting failures produced £165m of cost adjustments, including £91.5m impact on adjusted PBT and £53m in future years; aggregate adjustments across other regions reduced adjusted PBT by a further £8m. Adjusted PBT fell 35% (restated) despite 7% volume growth. Net debt roughly doubled to £180.7m (per March 2025 market commentary in filing corpus). The Board cancelled the final ordinary dividend and prioritised cash generation over distributions; the £130m buyback programme announced in September 2024 proceeded but no further distributions were proposed for FY2024 earnings.

**FY2023 (Annual Report 2023):** Pre-crisis peak: adjusted PBT £419m, ROCE 21.3%, net debt £88.8m (from net cash £118m in 2022). Successful Countryside integration; Partnerships-only strategy implemented. Fire safety provision £289m; RPDT contributions ~£20m cumulative.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Governance and accounting (2024 South Division).** Filings describe insufficient management capability, non-compliant commercial forecasting, and poor divisional culture confined to the South Division, with £165m cost adjustments across 18 sites. Independent forensic review and enhanced controls followed, but this undermines confidence in historical cost-to-complete estimates — a critical metric for housebuilders. Combined Executive Chair/CEO role (Greg Fitzgerald, pending succession to Adam Daniels April 2026) remains a Code deviation; Rob Woodward as SID carries enhanced responsibilities.

**Leadership churn.** CEO transition: Greg Fitzgerald to retire by March 2027; Adam Daniels appointed April 2026. **CFO Tim Lawlor resigned 8 July 2026** (RNS headline in index; body not downloaded) — a red flag during an active balance-sheet restructuring. CEO Review (due 24 September 2026) may produce further one-off charges of undetermined scale.

**Cyclical and operational.** Open Market exposure (~26% of completions) faces mortgage affordability headwinds; H1 2026 discounting at 7.1% vs 1.4% prior year erodes margins. Build cost inflation stabilising at 3–4% (July 2026 update); Middle East-related material/labour pressure flagged. Partner demand constrained by SAHP transition hiatus between funding programmes.

**Leverage and liquidity.** Nominal year-end net debt is low (£144m FY2025), but **average daily debt** (£734m FY2025; £799m H1 2026) and inventory (£3.2bn+) represent substantial embedded leverage. H1 2026 net debt £470m shows seasonal/working-capital intensity. Going-concern and viability statement text is referenced in annual reports (pages 83 and 76 respectively) but **full going-concern wording is not present in downloaded body extracts** — cannot quote covenant headroom from primary sources; treat as unverified.

**Regulatory.** CMA housebuilding investigation concluded with voluntary commitments (July 2025); fire safety provision £289m (2023) plus RPDT; second-staircase requirements added £19.3m expense in 2023. Planning reform dependency for volume growth.

**Competitive / market sentiment.** Vistry is among the most shorted UK equities (AskTraders, 5 August 2026; Investegate short-register shows multiple holders >2%, including GLG Partners ~2.24%, Schonfeld ~2.44%). High short interest is consistent with scepticism on FY2026 H2 recovery assumptions.

**Distributions.** No final dividend for FY2024; buyback paused mid-2026. Screen fails all dividend models — income investors are excluded.
