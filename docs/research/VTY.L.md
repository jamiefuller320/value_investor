# Vistry Group PLC (VTY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-10T20:43:36.095285+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Vistry is the UK’s largest housebuilder by volume, operating a differentiated mixed-tenure Partnerships model (~74% partner-funded completions in FY2025) alongside Open Market brands (Bovis, Linden, Countryside). The quantitative screen flags a deep-value setup—P/B ~0.24, P/E ~5.9, low leverage (D/E ~18%), positive FCF, and a Piotroski score of 7/9—yet quality screens fail on thin margins and ROE (~4.2%). FY2025 statutory results stabilised after the 2024 South Division cost crisis, but 2026 is explicitly a transition year: H1 is guided to a ~£30m statutory loss after aggressive cash-generation actions, with full-year adjusted PBT still targeted at ~£200m consensus and net cash >£100m by year-end. The central debate is whether extreme book discount and a SAHP-funded partnerships pipeline outweigh execution risk under new leadership, elevated discounting, high working-capital intensity, and persistent short interest.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Vistry offers a rare combination of sub-book valuation, improving cash generation, and a business model aligned with structurally undersupplied affordable housing. The screen passes all four metric families—cheapness, quality (at the threshold), GARP, and risk—with 93% composite score and 19/20 data quality. Schloss/Deep Value, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Acquirer’s Multiple, Magic Formula, Piotroski (7/9), and Financial Health all pass; failures cluster in dividend and high-ROE quality models (no dividend, ROE ≤8%, thin margins), which is consistent with a cyclical, capital-intensive builder in recovery rather than a compounder.

Business quality is mixed but defensible. The Partnerships model pre-sells a large share of output to registered providers and PRS partners, providing forward visibility (£3.9bn forward book; 80% forward sold for FY2026 per the July 2026 trading update). FY2025 adjusted PBT of £268.8m (Companies House 2025 annual report) modestly exceeded FY2024’s £263.5m despite lower completions (15,658 vs 17,225), reflecting land-sale optimisation and margin mix. Management’s 2026 pivot—cutting unsold private WIP from ~£600m to under £300m, pausing buybacks, exiting part-exchange, targeting £25m overhead savings, and forecasting net cash >£100m—directly addresses the balance-sheet risk that screens partially capture via leverage and FCF metrics but not inventory cyclicality.

The valuation hook is tangible: at P/B 0.24, the market prices permanent impairment of a £3.3bn equity base (Yahoo FY2025 balance sheet; filing-confirmed net debt only £144.2m at 31 Dec 2025). If adjusted PBT normalises toward £200m+ and net cash is achieved, the equity re-rates on earnings recovery alone without requiring premium multiples. Timing is neutral (RSI ~40, below 200-day MA)—appropriate for staged accumulation rather than momentum chasing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Operational / cost control:** The 2024 South Division crisis (£91.5m adjusted PBT hit; confined to former housebuilding regions per independent review) exposed weak commercial forecasting culture. FY2024 annual report details root-and-branch commercial assurance reforms; risk of recurrence is explicitly flagged by management but not yet fully tested through a complete cycle.

**Cyclical and market:** Open Market units fell 11% in FY2025; H1 2026 private discounting at 7.1% vs 1.4% prior year signals pricing pressure. July update cites Middle East-driven material cost pressure (build cost inflation stabilising at 3–4%). Partner demand subdued during SAHP transition; grant allocation timing remains a revenue swing factor.

**Balance sheet / working capital:** ~£3.2bn inventory (Yahoo FY2025) and ~£600m unsold private WIP at start-2026 (July RNS) create earnings and liquidity sensitivity. H1 2026 net debt £470m vs £144m year-end 2025 shows intra-year volatility; covenant language not present in available filing extracts—**covenant headroom not verified in this pack**.

**Governance and leadership:** Combined Executive Chair/CEO role (Greg Fitzgerald) acknowledged as Code deviation in FY2025 accounts; CEO succession to Adam Daniels (appointed April 2026 per RNS index) during active strategy review. **CFO Tim Lawlor resignation announced 8 July 2026**—material near-term governance gap. July trading update warns CEO Review may produce additional exceptional charges.

**Going concern:** FY2025 accounts reference viability and going concern statements on page 83 of the full report; the extracted body does not include the auditor’s going-concern wording. No going-concern qualification is evident from available extracts, but the full statement was not downloaded.

**Pension:** Defined pension benefit £32.2m (Yahoo FY2025)— immaterial relative to equity.

**Short interest:** Investegate short-positions filing (`47907269a7c0f90e`) shows multiple holders >0.5%, including GLG Partners (2.24%) and Schonfeld (2.44%) as at July 2026—consistent with elevated bearish positioning.

**Screen blind spots:** NCAV unavailable (Graham Net-Net fails); no dividend (multiple yield models fail); ROE/margin thresholds fail Buffett/Economic Moat screens—appropriate given cyclical, low-margin profile.
