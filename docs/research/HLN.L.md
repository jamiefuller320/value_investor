# Haleon plc (HLN.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T17:31:33.766722+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Haleon is a global consumer healthcare pure-play (Sensodyne, Panadol, Advil, Centrum, Voltaren) that the quantitative screen rates as a **buy**, supported by cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk factor passes at P/E ~20x, P/B ~2.0x, ~2% yield, and debt/equity ~50%. Filings show a credible cash-compounding story: adjusted operating margin expanded to 22.9% in FY 2025 and 24.3% in H1 2026, with net debt/adjusted EBITDA at 2.6x (FY 2025) and 2.5x (H1 2026), underpinned by £1.9bn+ annual free cash flow, a £500m buyback, and rising dividends. The valuation hook is a defensive franchise returning capital while margins improve; the central debate is **growth quality**—organic revenue of 3.0% (FY 2025) and 2.6% (H1 2026) consistently trails management’s 4–6% medium-term ambition, with Respiratory Health and North America as persistent drags. Deep research supports measured accumulation rather than full conviction on the raw screen signal.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy rating rests on six passing models (Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Piotroski F-Score, Earnings Quality) across all five factor families, with composite score 53% (sector-relative 62%), full data quality (20/20 metrics), and a stable 12-week buy signal. That aligns with a business that screens as **quality-at-a-reasonable-price** rather than deep cyclical value.

**Business quality.** Haleon owns category-leading oral health brands (~32–33% of revenue; organic growth +7.9% FY 2025, +7.3% H1 2026), delivers high adjusted gross margins (65.2% FY 2025; 66.5% H1 2026), and converts earnings to cash reliably (FY 2025 FCF £1,913m per preliminary results; H1 2026 FCF £769m). Productivity initiatives—£800m gross supply-chain savings targeted by 2030 and operating-model simplification—are delivering measurable margin expansion even as top-line growth runs mid-single-digit at best.

**Capital returns.** FY 2025 total dividend rose 7.6% (final proposed 4.9p); H1 2026 interim dividend 2.4p (+9%); £457m of the £500m 2026 buyback was completed by end-H1; leverage is at management’s ~2.5x net debt/adjusted EBITDA target after £1.1bn shareholder returns in FY 2025. These attributes directly support the screen’s dividend, FCF yield, and risk passes.

**Value hook.** Investors are paid via FCF yield (~£1.8bn+ screen FCF vs market cap), buybacks, and dividends while awaiting organic growth to re-accelerate toward guidance (3–5% FY 2026; 4–6% medium-term). The adjusted earnings path (+5.0% FY 2025 adj diluted EPS; +12.0% H1 2026) is more constructive than reported operating profit in H1 2026 ((2.6)%), which was depressed by £168m operating-model restructuring costs—a divergence the earnings-quality screen partially captures but that warrants caution on growth-dependent metrics.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Growth shortfall vs ambition.** FY 2025 organic growth of 3.0% and H1 2026 at 2.6% sit below the stated 4–6% medium-term range. Respiratory Health remains exposed to cold/flu incidence ((4.7)% H1 2026 organic; (1.9)% FY 2025). North America was negative in FY 2025 ((0.4)% organic) and only modestly positive in H1 2026. Market reaction to FY 2025 results (“Haleon shares fall 3.4% as revenue growth falls short of the company's own medium-term target,” Proactive Investors, 25 February 2026) underscores sentiment risk.

**Restructuring and operating-model change.** The January 2026 operating-model evolution (six operating units, Chief Growth Officer, Chief Transformation Officer) introduces execution risk. H1 2026 restructuring costs of £168m drove reported operating profit down 2.6% despite strong adjusted performance. Productivity savings of £175–200m over two years are targeted, but delivery at scale is not yet proven.

**Legacy indemnification.** The Q1 2026 trading statement explicitly flags that the Group has **“indemnification obligations in favour of the GSK Group and the Pfizer Group, which could be significant”**—a residual demerger liability not visible in trailing multiples.

**Cyclical and category mix.** Seasonal OTC (Respiratory) and discretionary categories create earnings volatility. Management cites “global geopolitical and macroeconomic uncertainties” (Q1 2026 trading statement). January 2026 media coverage flagged “channel shift concerns” (Proactive Investors, 22 January 2026).

**Governance transition.** Sir Dave Lewis stepped down as Chair on 31 December 2025; Vindi Banga succeeded from 1 January 2026 (RNS, 10 November 2025). Orderly transition, but new chair tenure is untested at Haleon.

**Leverage and refinancing.** Net debt/adjusted EBITDA at 2.5–2.6x is at management’s optimal level—not alarming, but limits M&A flexibility. August 2026 activity (cash tender for 2027 USD notes; $2bn three-tranche bond offering) reflects active balance-sheet management rather than distress. Yahoo fallback shows non-current pension obligations ~£114m (FY 2025)—immaterial vs ~£7.3bn net debt but worth monitoring.

**Accounting / adjusted vs statutory divergence.** H1 2026 adjusted EPS +12.0% vs reported diluted EPS (8.5p, (4.5)%) diverge materially due to restructuring. Value screens reliant on EPS growth should treat adjusted metrics with caution until paths converge.

**Going concern / contingencies.** H1 2026 auditor review: no material going-concern uncertainties identified. FY 2025 forward-looking statements reference “outcome of contingencies such as legal proceedings” without quantified provisions in the results body extract. Full litigation disclosure is not available in the indexed 20-F body extract.
