# 4imprint Group plc (FOUR.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-24T20:58:07.810965+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
4imprint is a high-quality direct marketer of promotional products, overwhelmingly US-focused (~98% of revenue), with a capital-light drop-ship model that has delivered double-digit operating margins and strong cash generation through the post-pandemic cycle. The quantitative screen flags the stock as a buy on cheapness (P/E 13.1, FCF yield 9.4%), quality (ROE 65%, Piotroski pass), dividend (4.6% yield), and financial health, though near-term revenue growth has turned negative (-2% in FY2025; screen growth -6.8%). Valuation appeal rests on sustained ~11% operating margins, net-cash balance sheet, and shareholder returns, set against a cyclical slowdown in new-customer acquisition and emerging US tariff cost pressure. The central debate is whether flat-to-falling orders represent a temporary macro pause or the start of margin compression as supplier tariffs feed through.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, 4imprint offers a rare combination of earnings power, cash conversion, and balance-sheet conservatism in a fragmented promotional-products market where it has consistently gained share. The screen passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with 20/20 data completeness and standout metrics including FCF yield (9.4%), PEGY (0.03), and dividend growth screens, aligning with a business that reported operating margins of 10.8% in both FY2024 and FY2025 (Companies House annual reports).

Business quality is underpinned by: (1) a data-driven direct-marketing engine with revenue per marketing dollar of $7.86–$7.88 in recent years; (2) strong existing-customer retention (1.64m orders flat YoY in 2025 despite macro uncertainty); (3) minimal leverage (Yahoo: total debt $3.4m at FY2025, essentially capital leases); and (4) cash and bank deposits of $132.8m at FY2025 year-end (2025 annual report). The model is intentionally counter-cyclical in investment terms—management continues to fund brand, marketing, and infrastructure ($10m Oshkosh office relocation; prior $20m distribution-centre expansion) through downturns to capture share on recovery.

The screen’s “buy” signal is logically consistent with a cash-rich compounder trading at a mid-teens earnings multiple with a near-5% dividend yield, but conviction is tempered by the new signal status (one week), neutral timing (RSI ~61), and the recent inflection from growth to slight revenue decline.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical demand.** Promotional products track corporate marketing spend; FY2025 new-customer orders fell 12% and revenue declined 2% despite strong retention. Screen-reported growth of -6.8% corroborates the slowdown. Recovery timing is uncertain.

**US tariff exposure.** ~98% of revenue is North America–sourced. FY2024 and FY2025 reports explicitly flag tariff impacts on supplier product costs; FY2025 gross margin held but management warns further cost increases as tariff policy evolves. This is the primary near-term margin risk not fully reflected in historical screens.

**Dividend sustainability.** Regular dividend maintained at 240.0c despite earnings dip; FY2025 cash dividends (Yahoo: $142.8m) exceeded net income ($113.6m). While supported by $132.8m cash deposits, sustained payout above earnings warrants monitoring if profitability softens further.

**Customer acquisition.** Dependence on new-customer growth for long-term share gains; a 12% decline in new-customer orders in 2025 is a structural red flag within an otherwise stable base.

**Valuation/book.** Screen P/B 9.1 and ROE 65% reflect a thin equity base after distributions, not necessarily asset-heavy undervaluation—value case is earnings/FCF-driven, not book-driven.

**Governance/leadership.** Chair transition to Paul Forman (Chair Designate, 2025 annual report); prior board change when Charlie Brady stepped down (Aug 2023, health). No adverse going-concern or covenant language found in available extracts; group carries negligible debt, reducing covenant risk.

**Pension.** Largely de-risked via 2023 buy-in; residual UK scheme obligations remain but balance-sheet volatility has materially reduced per filing language.

**Information gaps.** Principal Risks & Uncertainties section not extracted from filing bodies; no recent RNS trading updates in index. Principal-risk detail (competition, cyber, regulatory) cannot be verified from available sources—treat as unresolved.
