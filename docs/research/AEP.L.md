# AEP Plantations Plc (AEP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-10T20:26:47.243173+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
AEP Plantations Plc is a London-listed sustainable palm oil producer with ~56,000 ha of mature oil palms across Indonesia and Malaysia, operating mills, biogas plants, and third-party FFB processing. The quantitative screen flags a **Strong Buy** (18/22 models, composite 82%) on cheapness (P/E 10.4, yield 5.6%), quality (ROE 15.1%, Piotroski 8/9), cash generation (FCF yield, 2.4× net dividend coverage), and balance-sheet strength (current ratio 8.7, negligible debt). FY2025 was a step-change year: revenue rose 25% to **$465.2m**, PBT **$119.3m** (+35%), and EPS **231.42¢** (+35%), with **$231.8m** net cash and a total dividend of **81¢**. The valuation hook is a net-cash plantation asset trading below book (~$1,497¢ NAV/share pre-split) with accelerating capital returns (dividends, buybacks) and optional upside from the Pinago acquisition and a planned Indonesian subsidiary IPO. The key debate is whether FY2025 peak-cycle earnings and CPO prices are sustainable, or whether replanting drag, commodity mean-reversion, and Indonesian regulatory change will compress multiples again.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy rating is well supported by fundamental quality rather than a single metric. AEP passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—and clears classic value screens (Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Dreman Contrarian) alongside quality filters (Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Piotroski F-Score 8/9). The business earns its place: vertically integrated plantations and mills, long-lived biological assets with replanting discipline, and a fortress balance sheet (net cash, minimal borrowings) that is unusual in cyclical agri-commodities.

The earnings trajectory reinforces the case. After a 2023 trough when CPO prices fell (~$971/mt Rotterdam average vs $1,369/mt in 2022), the group recovered through 2024–2025 as volumes and prices improved. FY2025 FFB production rose 6%, CPO +7%, and the chairman cites resilient ex-mill pricing alongside operational execution. Management is actively unlocking value—rebrand to AEP Plantations, FTSE 250 promotion (September 2025), ten-for-one share split (June 2026), expanded buyback programme, Pinago Group acquisition, and a proposed IPO of PT AEP Nusantara Plantations (~19,000 ha Kalimantan platform). For a value investor, the combination of **asset backing + cash + dividend yield + buyback optionality**, at a sub-11× P/E on peak-but-not-fictitious earnings, fits the screen’s thesis. Failures on deep-value screens (P/B 1.6, no net-net) reflect fair rather than distressed pricing—not a quality veto.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Commodity cyclicality:** Palm oil earnings are highly CPO-price sensitive (2023 vs 2022 demonstrates ~45% EPS swing on price alone). Screen passes do not neutralise cycle risk; PEG failure (1.38) partly reflects this.

**Replanting and yield drag:** Trading update shows own FFB −2.7% YTD from ageing palms, replanting suspensions, and delayed cropping in North Sumatra/Riau—partially offset by Kalimantan and Pinago. Multi-year replanting (~10,000 ha target) may constrain near-term output.

**Indonesia regulatory and policy:** (1) **Danantara/DSI export centralisation** (May 2026)—management expects minimal direct impact as CPO is sold domestically to refineries, but policy uncertainty remains. (2) **EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR)**—2022/2023 annual reports flag compliance costs and market-access risk; group pursuing RSPO membership and maintains NDPE policy. (3) **Subsidiary IPO complexity**—~15% new share issuance in PT AEP Nusantara could dilute group economics if pricing/terms unfavourable.

**Biological asset accounting:** Reported profits vary before/after BA fair-value movements (e.g. 2023: $78.7m PBT before BA vs $77.8m after). Statutory EPS can diverge from cash economics in volatile price periods.

**Governance / technical compliance:** 2023 annual report disclosed a **Companies Act 2006 technical breach**—interim accounts not filed at Companies House before certain 2022–2023 dividends and buybacks (despite sufficient distributable profits). Shareholder ratification proposed. Not a solvency issue, but a process red flag.

**Pension:** Non-current pension/post-retirement obligations **~$8.0m** at FY2025 (Yahoo balance sheet; not prominently discussed in available filing extracts).

**Liquidity and bid-ask:** 2023 chairman noted buyback execution hampered by **low share liquidity**—relevant for a small/mid-cap FTSE 250 name.

**Minority interests:** Non-controlling interests **$4.2m** at FY2025 (down from larger historical balances when more assets were partially owned)—IPO could restructure this.

**Going concern:** No going-concern qualification identified in available filing extracts; net-cash position supports continuity.

**Data gaps:** `macro_context.json` unavailable. Consolidated interim accounts absent from index. Some annual report OCR quality limits access to full risk-committee and notes text.
