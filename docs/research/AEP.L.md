# AEP Plantations Plc (AEP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-12T12:28:19.224522+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
AEP Plantations Plc is a London-listed owner-operator of oil palm (and some rubber) estates in Indonesia and Malaysia, with reporting in US dollars and a capital-allocation story built on replanting, mill expansion, and selective M&A. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy (18/22 models; composite ~77%), anchored on low double-digit earnings yield, ~5% dividend yield, strong liquidity, and filing-aligned free cash flow of roughly £83.6m for FY2025. FY2025 revenue reached $465.2m and profit attributable to shareholders $90.9m (basic EPS 231.42 US cents pre share-split adjustment in later RNS), with net cash of $231.8m at year-end before the Pinago deal. The valuation hook is a cash-rich plantation platform trading below many peers on earnings and yield, with management returning capital via dividends and buybacks while scaling hectarage. The key debate is whether Pinago, CPO price strength, and the Kalimantan IPO unlock per-share value, or whether replanting drag, Indonesian policy noise, FX translation, and one-off acquisition gains mask a softer underlying earnings run-rate (H1 2026 underlying PBT was ~9% lower excluding the $9.1m bargain-purchase gain).

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, AEP fits the “quality compounder at a cyclical discount” bucket rather than net-net or deep book value: the screen passes Graham enterprising/defensive thresholds, earnings and FCF yield models, high dividend and dividend-growth checks, Magic Formula and acquirer’s multiple, Piotroski F-score 8/9, and financial health—while failing net-net, Schloss, deep value (P/B ~1.8), and Buffett-quality ROE gates. That profile matches a plantation business with tangible land and mill assets, minimal UK balance-sheet leverage historically, and shareholder-friendly cash returns (FY2025 total dividend 81.0 US cents per share per Companies House annual extract; ongoing £8m buyback programme from July 2026). Business quality is supported by integrated milling, a large replanting programme (target ~10,000 ha to 2030), and recent step-change growth via Pinago ($158.3m, May 2026), which lifted planted area to 87,392 ha at H1 2026. The screen’s FCF basis overlay flagged TTM vs filing divergence; research treats FY2025 statutory OCF less capex as primary ($113.8m − $30.2m ≈ $83.6m per Yahoo, consistent with the screen’s filing-aligned figure), not blended TTM. Cheapness plus dividend and improving FY2024–FY2025 earnings momentum (EPS up from 170.88c to 231.42c on the annual record) support the strong-buy signal, tempered by commodity cyclicality and policy risk in Indonesia.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Regulatory and country: Indonesian commodity export oversight (DSI platform from September 2026) and past market reactions to export-control headlines (May 2026 share weakness on policy news in the manifest) create headline risk even where the company argues limited direct impact due to domestic CPO sales. Kalimantan subsidiary IPO timing (2027 in H1 report vs Q4 2026 in AGM update) remains uncertain and subject to market and regulatory approval.

Cyclical: Earnings and NAV are levered to CPO and palm-kernel prices (H1 average ex-mill CPO $853/mt, −1.2% YoY). El Niño, biodiesel demand (B50), and inventory cycles can swing margins quickly; gross profit was flat in H1 despite revenue growth.

Operational: Large replanting programme suppresses near-term FFB yields; AGM and interim narratives cite ageing palms, fertiliser suspension on replant blocks, and regional cropping delays. Pinago integration and yield uplift are execution-dependent (Pinago FFB yield cited at 2.5 mt/ha for two post-acquisition months).

Financial structure: Post-Pinago, group bank loans appeared ($13.2m at H1)—modest in context but a change from nil at parent level in prior periods. Cash fell from $231.8m (FY2025) to $109.5m (H1 2026) after acquisition and shareholder returns ($25.9m dividends and buybacks in H1).

Accounting and transactions: $9.1m bargain-purchase gain inflates H1 PBT; purchase price allocation remains provisional under IFRS 3. Biological assets and fair-value movements add volatility. Customer concentration: four Indonesian customers ~36% of H1 2026 revenue ($89.9m of $249.7m)—weekly tendering mitigates but does not eliminate concentration risk.

Governance / pension: Retirement benefit liabilities ~$8.2m non-current at H1 2026; remeasurement gains/losses flow through OCI. No covenant language surfaced in reviewed extracts; leverage screens use consolidated D/E (~2.43 on screen inputs—verify against post-Pinago structure before sizing).

Competitive: Plantation sector is commoditised; edge comes from yields, mill efficiency, and land pipeline—not durable pricing power in the screen’s “moat” sense.
