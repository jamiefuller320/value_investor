# Pollen Street Group Limited (POLN.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-13T07:51:07.208827+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Pollen Street Group Limited (POLN.L) is a FTSE 250 alternative asset manager combining a fee-generating asset manager with an on-balance-sheet investment company focused on European financial and business services private equity and asset-based private credit. The quantitative screen rates the stock a strong buy on cheapness (P/E ~8.9, P/B ~0.8), a ~7% dividend yield, GARP metrics, and risk-family passes, with the share roughly in line with its 200-day moving average and neutral timing. Filings show accelerating fundamentals—total AUM reached £7.1bn at FY2025 and £8.4bn after Private Credit Fund IV’s April 2026 close—while the listed price is described by management as not fully reflecting platform progress. The central debate is whether statutory earnings, progressive dividends, and buybacks compensate for sub-scale ROE, thin liquidity ratios, volatile investment-company cash flows, and sector-wide scepticism toward listed private credit and alt managers.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, POLN offers a hybrid of recurring fund-management fees and a credit-heavy investment book that has delivered high-single to low-double-digit returns on net investment assets, now with the asset manager contributing a rising share of group EBITDA (49% in FY2025 per the Full Year 2025 Trading Update, 26 March 2026). The screen’s strong buy (12/22 models, composite 76%, four of five families: cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk) aligns with filing-backed growth: profit after tax rose 14% to £56.6m in FY2025, EPS 93.7p (+19%), total dividend 58.0p (+8%), and Lynch PEG ~0.63 on ~14% earnings growth. Schloss-style low P/B and high yield fit a business returning £39.4m to shareholders via dividends and buybacks in FY2025 while fee-paying AUM grew 32% to £5.2bn. Quality screens fail (ROE 9.6%, Piotroski 5/9, financial health on weak liquidity), which is consistent with a leveraged investment-company balance sheet rather than a pure asset-light manager—but that is exactly where the valuation discount may sit if AUM and fee income keep compounding toward the stated £10bn medium-term target.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and conduct:** Filings emphasise evolving FCA/listing expectations, market-abuse controls, and conduct risk from fundraising fraud or misrepresentation; no qualified audit opinion on FY2025 accounts (unqualified per trading update), but compliance failure could impair fundraising.

**Private credit and market cyclicality:** Management and risk sections cite investor nervousness on private credit documentation, BDC discounts, and competition for deals—directly relevant to sentiment on POLN’s listed multiple even if portfolio performance remains “robust.”

**Leverage and liquidity:** Investment company debt (~35% of gross investment assets; Pollen Street Limited CH 2024: £188.3m drawn on upsized £240m facility) and group net debt ~£188m against cash ~£12m. Screen flags weak liquidity (current ratio 1.25) and failed financial-health model; viability testing notes potential liquidity pressure in severe combined scenarios, mitigated by long-dated fees and investment cash generation.

**Carried interest and retention:** Board reduced group share of carried interest on Private Credit Fund IV from 25% to 17% to retain key staff—economically rational given fund size but dilutes long-term fee upside versus prior framework (15–25% band for future flagship funds).

**Governance / key people:** Chair Lynn Fordham appointed NED of Czechoslovak Group (RNS, 26 January 2026); Jim Coyle retiring from board after ten years (FY2025 report); CFO transition to Crispin Goldsmith (RNS, 13 January 2025). Succession and time commitments are standard watch items, not red flags by themselves.

**Accounting / earnings quality:** Piotroski 5/9; OCF below net income in FY2025; one-off tax adjustments drove 8.2% effective rate vs guided normalisation higher. PE catch-up fees boosted FY2025 asset-manager margins (39% vs 41% prior year on higher admin costs tied to fundraising).

**Going concern / covenants (filings):** Directors confirm going concern for at least 12 months and three-year viability to 2029 AGM, including stressed scenarios with no new fundraising and material impairments; debt covenants not anticipated to breach even in stress.
