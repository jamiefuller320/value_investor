# Pollen Street Group Limited (POLN.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-12T12:20:55.137493+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Pollen Street Group Limited (POLN.L) is a FTSE 250 alternative asset manager combining a scaling third-party asset management platform (European financial-services private equity and asset-based private credit) with an on-balance-sheet investment company that co-invests and earns recurring portfolio income. The quantitative screen flags a strong buy on cheapness (P/E ~8.9, P/B ~0.8), a ~7% dividend yield with growth, and GARP-style earnings momentum (~14% statutory growth, Lynch PEG ~0.6), while the quality family fails (ROE ~9.6%, Piotroski 5/9, weak liquidity metrics). FY2025 RNS results show AUM up 30% to £7.1 billion, profit after tax £56.6 million (+14%), and a progressive 58.0p dividend (+8%), yet the share price trades below intrinsic narrative per management. The central debate is whether listed-alternatives de-rating and private-credit sentiment overshadow a durable fee stream and 10%+ investment-company returns, or whether the market is correctly penalising balance-sheet leverage, one-off fee uplift, and lower group carried-interest share on Private Credit Fund IV.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, POLN offers a rare combination of sub-9x earnings, sub-1x tangible book (screen inputs), and a cash-return policy (58.0p DPS in FY2025, active buybacks, ~1.4x filing-aligned FCF cover on net dividends per the screen) backed by growing fee-paying AUM (£5.2 billion at YE2025, £5.4 billion at Q1 2026). The screen passes 12 of 22 models across cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk families (composite ~74%, sector-relative ~77%, persistent strong buy ~14 weeks), consistent with a “cheap income plus growth” profile rather than a quality compounder. Business quality is mixed but directionally improving: fund management EBITDA rose 17% to £31.7 million as asset-manager earnings mix reached ~49% of group EBITDA; fundraising outperformance (PE Fund V €1.5 billion final close; Private Credit Fund IV subsequently closed at £2.5 billion in April 2026) supports visibility toward the £10 billion AUM medium-term target. The investment company delivered ~10.6% underlying return on net investment assets, stabilising a dual-engine model where balance-sheet capital aligns with LPs. Quality screens fail for good reason—ROE below typical “moat” thresholds, current ratio ~1.25, and OCF well below the prior year in Yahoo/screen data—so the strong-buy signal is a valuation-and-payout story that still requires acceptance of financial-services cyclicality and listed-alt manager risk.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Regulatory and conduct: RNS principal risks highlight evolving FCA/listing expectations, market-abuse and fundraising fraud risk, with reliance on compliance frameworks—material for an asset manager with continuous capital raising.

Cyclical and market: Filings stress elevated private-credit competition, investor nervousness after defaults/fraud headlines, BDC de-rating, and sensitivity of valuations, exits, and fundraising to rates and geopolitics (including 2026 risk-aversion commentary). Investment underperformance or credit impairment on the £536 million portfolio would hit both NAV and fee-related carry.

Leverage and liquidity: Investment-company gearing (~35% net debt to gross investment assets at group level; subsidiary borrowings ~60% of equity) is within board policy but amplifies credit marks. The screen flags weak liquidity (current ratio ~1.25) and financial-health model failure—consistent with low cash (£11.9 million at YE2025 in RNS) against dividend and GP commitment needs.

Governance and key person: Board transition (Jim Coyle retiring April 2026; Robert Ohrenstein as audit chair from May 2026; Lynn Fordham as chair) and CFO appointment (Crispin Goldsmith, January 2025) are orderly but add transition oversight points. Carried-interest allocation on Private Credit Fund IV cut from 25% to 17% for the group (talent retention trade-off)—economically mitigated by fund size but reduces long-term fee upside.

Earnings quality: ~£8.4 million PE catch-up management fees in FY2025 are non-recurring; management guides FY2026 fund management income as stable with catch-up rolling off. Effective tax rate 8.2% in FY2025 reflects one-off tax adjustments—guided to normalise higher.

Accounting / structural: Combination-related intangibles (~£226 million goodwill plus customer relationships on Yahoo balance sheet) depress tangible P/B and complicate net-net screens (NCAV unavailable per screen). Companies House subsidiary accounts are standalone and unaudited comparatives in places—do not merge with group RNS figures.

Competitive: Fundraising and fee pressure in private credit and PE remain explicit risks; Finastra Universal Banking acquisition (June 2025 news) expands PE footprint but integration and software/AI disruption risks are unquantified in sourced filings.
