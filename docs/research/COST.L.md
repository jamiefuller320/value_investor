# Costain Group PLC (COST.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T11:01:32.708219+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Costain is a UK infrastructure services specialist repositioned around long-term regulated and private-sector programmes in water, transport, energy and defence. The quantitative screen flags a strong buy on a blend of reasonable valuation (P/E ~15.5, P/B ~2.2), improving quality metrics (Piotroski 8/9, ROE ~15%), strong free-cash-flow yield, and low leverage (debt/equity ~13%), with dividend growth re-emerging after pension constraints were lifted.

The investment case rests on a record £7.0bn forward work position—roughly seven times FY2025 revenue—supporting management’s narrative of an FY2027 “step change” as AMP8 water, grid upgrade and transport programmes move from design into construction. FY2025 showed the deliberate trade-off: revenue fell to £1,045.7m as legacy road frameworks completed and HS2 work shifted, yet adjusted operating margin rose to 4.5% and adjusted free cash flow reached £63.1m on a net cash balance of £189.3m.

The central debate is whether margin-led earnings growth and capital returns (doubled interim dividend, £20m buyback) outweigh construction-sector cyclicality, HS2 programme uncertainty, and thin reported margins (~3–4%) that screens fail to classify as a wide moat. H1 2026 confirms the turnaround trajectory—revenue back to +3.4%—but first-half free cash flow remains seasonally negative.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy rating aligns with a business that has moved from turnaround to cash-generative compounder characteristics, albeit in a low-margin industry.

**Quantitative fit.** Costain passes 13 of 22 models across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families, with full data coverage (20/20 metrics). Standout passes include Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Lynch/Neff PEG variants, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Piotroski F-Score (8/9), Financial Health and Earnings Quality. Net debt is effectively nil; FCF dividend coverage (~6.6x on screen inputs) is robust. Failures are instructive rather than disqualifying: Graham Defensive and Deep Value (P/B not below 1, no net-net), Economic Moat (ROE 15.2% vs 18% hurdle), and High Dividend Yield (2.3%— respectable but not top-quartile). For a value investor, the profile is **quality-at-a-fair-price with a capital-return kicker**, not deep value or net-net.

**Business quality.** Primary filings show five consecutive years of adjusted profit growth through FY2025, margin expansion from 3.0% (FY2023) to 4.5% (FY2025), and a forward book that has risen from £5.4bn (FY2024) to £7.0bn (FY2025/H1 2026). Customer mix has diversified: private/regulated customers were 48% of forward work at H1 2026 versus 30% in FY2023, reducing pure central-government dependence. Contract discipline—target-cost, long-duration frameworks, no single-stage lump-sum in the forward book at H1 2026—supports the screen’s quality and risk passes.

**Valuation hook.** At ~15.5x earnings on FY2025 adjusted EPS of 14.5p, the market prices modest growth despite visible backlog and a medium-term margin target above 5%. Pension dividend parity removal (January 2026) and reduced dividend cover target (2.5x adjusted earnings from 3x) unlock higher distributions without stressing the balance sheet.

**Interpretation.** Revenue declined 16% in FY2025 as expected completion of Regional Delivery Partnership road projects and HS2 schedule revisions moved rail work into FY2026+. Profitability improved nonetheless: adjusted operating profit rose 9.3% and margin hit 4.5%, exceeding the 4.5% H2 run-rate target cited in the FY2025 annual report. Adjusted FCF rebounded sharply (£63.1m vs £27.1m), aided by lower capex after FY2024 HR-system investment. Reported EPS rose 23% to 13.9p as adjusting items fell (£2.3m vs £12.0m in FY2024, including restructuring and residual transformation costs).

The FY2025 dividend increased 75% to 4.2p (final 3.2p + interim 1.0p), consistent with restored shareholder-return flexibility after the January 2026 pension agreement removed dividend parity.

### Interim H1 2026 (RNS, 13 August 2026)

- **Revenue:** £543.1m (+3.4% vs H1 2025 £525.4m)—inflection after FY2025 contraction.
- **Adjusted operating profit:** £17.3m (+3.0%); **margin** maintained at 3.2%.
- **Adjusted EPS:** 5.7p (+3.6%).
- **Net cash:** £164.4m (FY2025: £189.3m); average month-end net cash £176.0m.
- **Adjusted free cash flow:** £(1.4)m outflow (H1 2025: £(3.0)m)—seasonally H2-weighted; management guides to higher H2 FCF.
- **Interim dividend:** 2.0p (doubled); target cover reduced to 2.5x adjusted earnings.
- **Forward work:** maintained at record **£7.0bn** (91% of FY2026 and FY2027 consensus revenue visibility per company-compiled consensus).

Divisionally, Natural Resources drove growth (+13.3%, led by Water +15.1% and Energy +25.7%), while Transportation revenue fell 3.2% on road project roll-offs, partially offset by Integrated Transport (+39.5%).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**HS2 and programme risk.** H1 2026 notes HS2 “navigating a change in its programme delivery strategy” with supply-chain discussions ongoing; FY2025 revenue was materially affected by schedule shifts. Costain’s Skanska-Costain-Strabag joint venture exposure adds execution and dispute risk typical of mega-projects.

**Cyclical and contract risk.** Despite improved contract selection, margins remain thin (3–4%) and sensitive to cost inflation, supply-chain performance and contract settlements. The going-concern severe downside explicitly models −30% revenue on work yet to be secured and −300bps margin—acknowledging inherent cyclicality.

**Customer and regulatory concentration.** Forward work remains tied to UK infrastructure spending cycles (AMP8 water ~£100bn industry investment, RIIO-3 grid, RIS3 roads, defence/nuclear). Ofgem/Ofwat determinations and government fiscal choices directly affect customer capex timing—evident in FY2025 water “design phase” revenue plateau before H2 2026 construction ramp.

**Pension.** Surplus accounting (£62.9m) improves the balance sheet, but longevity, discount-rate and Virgin Media-related legislative review risks persist; a 0.25% discount-rate move affects liability by £11.4m per H1 disclosure.

**Governance / capital allocation.** Aggressive dividend doubling and buybacks assume sustained FCF; working-capital unwind could reduce FY2026 net cash toward ~£170–175m guidance from £189.3m peak.

**Competitive.** Sector remains fragmented; win rates have improved but bidding intensity in water and grid frameworks is high.

**Liquidity / leverage.** Currently low risk—net cash, undrawn RCF, bonding utilisation £71.2m of £295m—but performance bonds and working-capital swings are material in contracting.
