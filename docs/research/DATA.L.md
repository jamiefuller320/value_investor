# GlobalData Plc (DATA.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T06:42:03.601494+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
GlobalData is a subscription-led proprietary data and analytics platform serving healthcare, consumer and technology end-markets, with high revenue visibility and durable cash generation. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (P/E 21.4, P/B 2.1, ROE 13.4%, debt/equity 59%), but underlying organic growth has slowed to ~1% while adjusted EBITDA margins have compressed from 41% toward 34%. The investment case rests on whether margin recovery, AI-enabled product monetisation and bolt-on M&A can re-accelerate growth toward management’s £500m revenue ambition without further eroding returns on capital. The central debate is execution: founder-led capital returns and sum-of-the-parts optionality (Healthcare minority stake to Inflexion valued the division at £1.1bn) versus prolonged transformation drag, re-leveraging after aggressive buybacks, and a pending CFO transition.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, GlobalData offers a recurring-revenue asset trading at a modest earnings multiple despite passing ten classic quality-and-value screens (Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Piotroski F-Score, Earnings Quality). The business model — “build once, sell multiple times” subscriptions (~74% of FY25 revenue per the annual report), advance customer payments, low capex intensity (~2.5% of revenue) — supports structurally high cash conversion; FY25 operating cash flow was £83.3m (Companies House annual accounts, year ended 31 December 2025).

The screen’s buy signal is reinforced by balance-sheet optionality post the 2024 Inflexion Healthcare transaction (£434m net proceeds; Healthcare division valued at £1.115bn enterprise value), which transformed net debt of £243.9m (FY23) into net cash of £10.1m (FY24) before FY25 re-levered to net bank debt of £114.2m following M&A, buybacks and transformation spend. Management targets net leverage of 2–3× adjusted EBITDA and has expanded credit facilities (non-Healthcare facility upsized to £245m; Healthcare facility extended to December 2028, per 9 July 2026 trading update), preserving M&A capacity.

The gap between screen and fundamentals is timing: reported revenue grew 13% to £322.1m in FY25, but underlying growth was only 1% (FY24: 4%), and contracted forward revenue underlying growth was 3%. Value renewal rates remain resilient (~89% per H1 2026 trading update; ~88% at December 2025), suggesting customer stickiness even as sales cycles elongate. If the Growth Transformation Plan delivers mid-single-digit organic growth and margin recovery toward 40% adjusted EBITDA (from 34% in FY25), current multiples look conservative; if not, the stock is a capital-return story rather than a compounding growth story.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Growth and cyclicality:** Underlying revenue growth decelerated from 7% (FY23) to 1% (FY25 and H1 2026). Management cites macro-economic uncertainty, elongated sales cycles and transformation disruption. FY25 adjusted EBITDA margin fell 700bps to 34% due to Growth Transformation Plan investment, sales expansion and dilutive acquisition integration — margin recovery toward 40% is promised but unproven. The £500m revenue target by end-2026 (organic plus M&A) looks ambitious against ~£322m FY25 base and ~1% organic run-rate.

**Leverage and capital allocation:** Net bank debt swung from £10.1m net cash (FY24) to £114.2m net debt (FY25) while the group returned >£100m via buybacks and pursued acquisitions. Management targets 2–3× net leverage on adjusted EBITDA; at FY25 adjusted EBITDA £110.2m, current net debt implies ~1.0× — headroom exists, but further buybacks (£45m planned capital returns in 2026) plus M&A could tighten flexibility. Debt facilities upsized to £245m non-Healthcare plus £200m Healthcare (per July 2026 RNS).

**Governance and key person:** Founder-CEO Mike Danson and aligned holders controlled ~61% of voting rights at the July 2026 tender offer (RNS). High insider control reduces minority influence but supports long-term strategy alignment; it also concentrates key-person risk. CFO Graham Lilley steps down 30 September 2026; Robert Kingston (ex-Keywords Studios, Sky) succeeds — transition risk during a pivotal transformation phase.

**Competitive and technology:** GlobalData asserts a moat from proprietary data and AI integration (Al Hub, Ava assistant, Microsoft 365 Copilot integration), but generic AI tools and free/open data sources pose disruption risk to low-end offerings. Competitive intelligence and market-data markets are fragmented but well-funded.

**Accounting complexity:** FY25 statutory PBT and operating profit benefited from a £20.5m non-cash share-based payment credit, distorting year-on-year comparability. Goodwill of £384.6m (Yahoo fallback) on a £689m asset base creates impairment sensitivity if growth assumptions fail — impairment testing language not available in truncated filing extract.

**Regulatory:** Data licensing, privacy and cross-border data flows are inherent risks for a global data vendor; no specific regulatory actions against GlobalData appear in the news manifest. Broader UK market-data regulatory changes (FCA daily trading data publication, July 2026) are industry context, not company-specific.

**Pension / going concern:** Pension and going-concern statements are referenced in the FY25 annual report table of contents (p.55) but the body extract is unavailable — cannot verify auditor going-concern conclusions from primary filings.
