# GlobalData Plc (DATA.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-13T07:56:32.621477+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
GlobalData Plc (DATA.L) is a UK-listed B2B data, analytics and technology business selling subscription intelligence into Healthcare and Non-Healthcare verticals, with a recurring revenue model and heavy investment in AI-enabled products. The quantitative screen rates the stock a buy (11/22 models, composite ~69%), anchored on moderate valuation (P/E ~17.9, P/B ~1.8), FCF yield, dividend growth, Piotroski F-Score 7/9 and GARP-style metrics, with persistent signal over ~15 weeks. The value hook is a cash-generative subscription franchise trading below many data peers, amplified by sizeable capital returns (£45m planned buybacks/tender in 2026, plus prior programmes) while management argues sum-of-the-parts value exceeds market cap. The central debate is whether slowing underlying revenue growth (~1% in FY2025 and H1 2026) and compressed adjusted EBITDA margins (34% in FY2025 vs 41% in FY2024) are a temporary transformation phase, or a structural growth/moat problem in a tougher macro and AI-disrupted market research landscape.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, GlobalData offers a rare combination in UK small/mid-cap industrials: double-digit reported revenue scale (~£322m FY2025), high recurring mix (~80% subscription visibility cited in filings), and demonstrable cash conversion, yet the market prices it like a mature compounder with growth risk (screen passes Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Lynch/Neff PEG, Quality Value, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Composite Value, Earnings Quality, and Dividend Growth). Business quality supports the cheapness case: contracted forward revenue grew to £179.7m at FY2025 (3% underlying), value renewal rates remain high (~89% cited in the July 2026 trading update), and net leverage policy targets 2–3× adjusted EBITDA—consistent with a disciplined acquirer of proprietary content assets. The screen’s risk family still passes despite failing Financial Health (current ratio ~0.90, weak liquidity vs Graham defensive thresholds) and missing “moat” screens (ROE ~13.4%, below Buffett/Economic Moat cut-offs), which fits a “good business at a reasonable price, not a fortress” profile. Capital allocation aligns with value: progressive but rebased dividend, active M&A bolt-ons (e.g. Cambridge Healthcare in H1 2026), and aggressive buybacks/tender offers returning surplus cash. The buy case strengthens if the September 2026 value-creation plan re-accelerates organic growth and restores margins toward the stated ~40% adjusted EBITDA ambition; it weakens if H1’s ~1% underlying growth persists and debt rises to fund returns and integration.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Growth and execution:** Underlying revenue growth has decelerated sharply (1% FY2025; ~1% underlying in H1 2026 per trading update) while the group still targets ambitious scale (£500m revenue ambition referenced historically in filings) and margin recovery toward ~40% adjusted EBITDA. Failure to convert GTP sales investments and new go-to-market structures into consistent organic growth is the primary equity risk.

**Leverage and liquidity:** FY2025 net bank debt of £114.2m reverses the prior year’s net cash position; facilities were expanded in July 2026 (NHC £245m, Healthcare £200m extended). The screen flags weak liquidity (current ratio ~0.90) and fails Financial Health—working capital remains negative on Yahoo FY2025 balance sheet. Leverage policy (2–3× adjusted EBITDA) provides a stated guardrail, but buybacks, tender offers, and M&A concurrently increase refinancing and covenant sensitivity if EBITDA stalls.

**Earnings quality:** FY2025 statutory PBT benefited from a £20.5m non-cash share-based payment **credit** (per FY2025 highlights), contributing to the screen’s warning that statutory vs filing-core EPS growth diverges by >350 bps—GARP metrics may overstate sustainable earnings power.

**Competitive / technology:** Filings emphasise AI augmentation (AVA, AI Hub) as opportunity and defensive use of proprietary data; industry-wide elongation of sales cycles is acknowledged in H1 2026 commentary. Generic AI and large platform competitors threaten pricing and differentiation if proprietary content is perceived as commoditised.

**Governance / key person:** Founder-CEO Mike Danson tendered 20,117,282 shares in the August 2026 tender offer (~4.43% of his holding per RNS)— liquidity event for the largest insider rather than incremental alignment. CFO transition: Graham Lilley steps down 30 September 2026; Robert Kingston joins as CFO (RNS 24 June 2026).

**Regulatory / listing:** FY2025 report references expected Main Market admission (March 2026)—index change and disclosure regime shift can affect liquidity and investor base.

**Going concern:** FY2025 accounts include a Going Concern and Viability section (page reference in index); the available body extract does not include the auditor/director going-concern wording verbatim—confirm in full annual report before relying on it.

**Pension / litigation:** No material pension or litigation quanta surfaced in the extracts reviewed; not screened out as immaterial without full notes review.
