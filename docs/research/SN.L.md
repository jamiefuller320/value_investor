## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Smith & Nephew is a global medtech franchise (Orthopaedics, Sports Medicine & ENT, Advanced Wound Management) trading on a value screen that flags **Strong Buy** (8/22 models; composite score 75%). The investment case rests on a multi-year earnings and cash-flow inflection—FY2025 revenue of $6.2bn (per Q1 2026 RNS) and Yahoo-sourced net income of $625m versus $263m in 2023—combined with disciplined capital return ($500m buyback completed in 2025; a further $500m announced in May 2026) and activist oversight (Cevian ~14%). At roughly 19.8x P/E, 2.4x P/B and 2.7% yield, the stock screens as cheap enough for enterprising value criteria without deep-value net-net status. The central debate is whether innovation-led growth (Sports Medicine now the largest division) and the new RISE strategy can offset persistent US knee weakness (-10.3% underlying in Q1 2026), skin-substitute reimbursement reset, and tariff headwinds (~$60m guided for 2026). Filings coverage in this pack is thin: no annual report or half-year results body is indexed; conviction therefore rests heavily on the Q1 2026 trading update plus Yahoo historicals.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen rates SN.L **Strong Buy** with five factor families passing—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk—on full metric coverage (20/20) and a Piotroski F-Score of 7/9 (positive earnings and OCF, improving ROA and leverage, no dilution). That aligns with observable business improvement: Yahoo data show revenue rising from $5.5bn (2023) to $6.2bn (2025), net income more than doubling to $625m, and free cash flow expanding from $181m to $852m over the same span, with FCF dividend coverage of roughly 2.6x per the screen. Balance-sheet metrics support the risk bucket—current ratio 2.6x, debt/equity 73%, net debt ~$2.6bn against $528.9m equity (Yahoo FY2025)—while management explicitly cites a “strong balance sheet” in the May 2026 RNS. For a value investor, the hook is a FTSE 100 compounder at a sub-20x multiple after a painful orthopaedics cycle, now returning >$835m to shareholders over twelve months (buyback plus ordinary dividend, per filing), with the share roughly 11% below its 200-day moving average and RSI near 40 (neutral timing). Quality screens fail on ROE (11.9%, below 12% thresholds) and moat proxies, so this is an **enterprising** rather than defensive Graham name—cheap enough on FCF yield and earnings quality, but not a deep-value or wide-moat compounder. Cevian’s ~14% stake and completed $250m buyback tranche (August 2026 news) add a governance/catalyst overlay to the quantitative signal.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Operational / cyclical:** US knee implants remain the principal revenue drag (-10.3% Q1 2026 underlying), with deliberate set-placement discipline ahead of the LANDMARK knee launch (H2-weighted recovery assumed). Advanced Wound Bioactives faces a reimbursement reset for skin substitutes ($20–40m guided impact) plus inventory clearing; ENT declined on China VBP-related channel destocking. Q2 2026 news suggests growth undershot internal expectations (1.6% underlying), raising execution risk on the ~6% full-year guide.

**Competitive:** Orthopaedics competes with Stryker, Zimmer Biomet and others in hips, knees and robotics; Sports Medicine strength (REGENETEN, CORI) must be sustained against entrenched rivals. China Volume-Based Procurement timing adds regional uncertainty.

**Regulatory / reimbursement:** Dependence on US payer reimbursement (skin substitutes, hospital budgets) and FDA clearance pathways; forward-looking statements in the Q1 RNS cite regulatory approvals, reimbursement decisions and litigation on patent claims as material uncertainty factors.

**Governance / key person:** CFO **John Rogers** departed for Baxter (August 2026), triggering share weakness despite solid H1 trading per news flow; Orthopaedics president Craig Gaffin also leaving (replaced by Nathan Folkert, announced Q1 RNS). Cevian activism is a positive catalyst but also signals prior dissatisfaction with returns.

**Leverage / liquidity:** Debt/equity 73% and net debt ~$2.6bn are manageable with $852m FCF and investment-grade profile (Fitch **BBB+**, February 2026 news), but the name fails strict low-leverage quality screens. Goodwill/intangibles at 38% of assets limit tangible book ($1.30bn tangible equity per Yahoo).

**Tariffs / macro:** Management guides ~$60m tariff impact in 2026; hospital budget pressure flagged in recent media.

**Pension:** $84m non-current post-retirement liability (Yahoo FY2025)—not a primary concern but worth monitoring.

**Filing gaps:** Absence of indexed annual report and interim results bodies means going-concern language, covenant detail and contingency disclosures cannot be verified in this pack; this is a material research limitation.
