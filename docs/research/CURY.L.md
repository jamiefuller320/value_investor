# Currys plc (CURY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-13T07:54:57.061862+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Currys plc is a UK–Nordics omnichannel electricals retailer whose turnaround is showing up in profits, net cash, and capital returns. The quantitative screen flags a buy on deep value (P/B ~0.6, P/E ~9.8), strong cash generation, dividend growth, and a Piotroski F-Score of 8/9, with five of five factor families passing. The valuation hook is a sub-book equity market cap against ~£191m adjusted PBT and recurring services/iD Mobile growth, partly offset by negative tangible equity, sub-1.0 liquidity, and cyclical big-ticket demand. The central debate is whether margin and cash discipline survive memory-chip cost inflation, online competition, and a CEO transition—not whether the business remains a going concern.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Currys offers a classic “cheap balance sheet + improving earnings” setup rather than a quality compounder. The screen’s buy signal (13/22 models, composite ~71%, conviction ~68%, stable for five weeks) aligns with filing-backed improvement: group revenue rose to £9,254m and adjusted PBT to £191m (+18% YoY) in FY2025/26 (year ended 2 May 2026), with year-end net cash of £176m after £74m shareholder returns and £82m pension contributions (RNS preliminary results, 2 July 2026). Free cash flow of £157m (+5% YoY) on a management basis supports FCF-yield and dividend-growth screens; statutory profit before tax was £153m, so adjusted metrics matter for cross-checking.

Business quality is “good enough” for value, not moat-grade: ROE ~7% fails Buffett/moat screens, gross margins are thin, and the current ratio (~0.89) fails Graham defensive and financial-health models. Offsetting that, leverage is moderate (debt/equity ~40%), the Piotroski profile is strong (leverage down, OCF positive, no dilution), and management is explicitly allocating surplus cash to buybacks (£50m programme launched July 2026, with a prior £50m completed) and a rebased dividend (3.0p full year, per preliminary results). Nordics (~40% of sales) and UK services/B2B/new categories have been profit drivers; early FY2026/27 trading (17 weeks to 29 August 2026) showed group LFL revenue +7%, with consensus adjusted PBT ~£199m cited in the September trading update—supporting GARP/Lynch-PEG passes without requiring heroic growth assumptions.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Competitive and cyclical:** Annual report risk disclosures (July 2026) mark **competition**, **business continuity**, and **supply chain resilience** as increased; agentic AI and disruptive retail are explicit themes. Big-ticket electricals remain consumer-cyclical; management warned of memory-chip shortages pushing cost inflation later in 2026 (Yahoo/Reuters coverage, 2 July 2026). Trading updates note dependence on events (World Cup, heatwaves) for UK market tone.

**Liquidity and leverage:** Principal risk framework lists **liquidity, tax and treasury** as stable but material, citing bank facilities, **covenant cover**, and triennial pension revaluation. Screen fails financial health on weak liquidity (current ratio <1); negative tangible equity limits asset-based downside floors.

**Pension and tax:** FY2025/26 cash absorbed by **£82m pension contributions**; IAS 19 deficit remains a capital-allocation swing factor in strategic reporting. Group remains committed to resolving **open HMRC tax cases** (annual report risk language).

**Governance / key person:** Alex Baldock stepped down as group CEO (RNS, 26 March 2026); Fredrik Tønnesen (ex-Nordics CEO) appointed effective 3 August 2026. Succession reduces execution risk versus an external unknown but leaves strategy continuity as a watch item.

**Regulatory:** Consumer credit and insurance operations carry **financial services regulation** risk (stable rating, high impact if breached).

**Going concern:** Full going-concern wording is referenced in CH accounts (section listed) but OCR in the indexed body is not reliably extractable here; no going-concern qualification is flagged in available RNS summaries. Viability statement is referenced alongside principal risks in the annual report package.
