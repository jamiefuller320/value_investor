# Currys plc (CURY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-12T12:26:49.695154+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Currys plc is a UK–Nordics omnichannel electricals retailer whose turnaround is showing up in profits, cash, and capital returns, while the quantitative screen flags deep value (P/B ~0.6, P/E ~10.6) with strong cash-generation and dividend screens passing. FY 2025/26 (year ended 2 May 2026) delivered adjusted PBT of £191m (+18% YoY) on revenue of £9,254m (+6%), with year-end net cash of £176m after £74m of shareholder returns and heavy pension funding. The investment case rests on recurring, higher-margin Services (credit, iD Mobile, protection) and Nordics operating leverage, against structurally thin retail margins, negative working capital, and intense online competition. The central debate is whether improved earnings and buybacks re-rate the shares or cyclical/consumption and component-cost shocks cap multiple expansion.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen rates CURY.L as **buy** (13/22 models, composite ~69%, all five factor families passed: cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk), with persistent signal since late August 2026, Piotroski F-Score 8/9, and filing-aligned free cash flow (~£346m on statutory OCF less capex per the screen bridge—see Financial Review for management’s narrower FCF definition). That aligns with a business that is no longer in distress: statutory profit after tax rose to £165m in FY 2025/26 from £108m prior year, net cash replaced net debt, and the board targets at least £100m year-end net cash while running successive £50m buybacks and a doubled dividend (3.0p full year). Value investors can underwrite the name on asset backing (goodwill-heavy balance sheet but equity ~£2.4bn vs market implied by sub-1.0 P/B), earnings yield, and capital return discipline, while accepting that “quality” in the Buffett/moat sense fails (ROE ~7%, current ratio ~0.89, thin margins)—consistent with a **good enough** cyclical value rather than a compounder. Operational evidence supports the screen’s cash and earnings-quality passes: LFL momentum (+4% FY, +7% group in the 17 weeks to 29 August 2026 per RNS), Nordics EBIT growth (+26% currency-neutral), and Services/credit scaling (recurring Services revenue £873m, +7%). CEO transition to Fredrik Tønnesen (from 3 August 2026) introduces execution uncertainty but follows a planned handover after eight years under Alex Baldock.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Competitive and cyclical:** Annual report risk appendix (`14270db68af31cc6.txt`) flags **increased** competition, business continuity, and supply-chain resilience risks; management publicly warned of AI-driven **memory chip shortages** pushing up product costs (Yahoo/Reuters, 2 July 2026). Electricals demand remains tied to consumer confidence, big-ticket financing, and promotional intensity versus pure-play online rivals (news debate vs AO World, September 2026).

**Liquidity and working capital:** Filings show **negative working capital** (~**£182m** at 2 May 2026) and inventory up **14%** to **£1,181m** (chip-supply protection cited). Screen **fails Financial Health** on weak liquidity (current ratio **<1**). Principal risk disclosure cites **liquidity, tax and treasury**, including legacy **HMRC** cases and covenant monitoring on **£525m** facilities (going concern note in results, `941f59ac0a40743d.txt`).

**Regulatory:** Consumer credit and insurance activities carry **financial services regulation** risk (stable but material per annual report). Historical regulatory adjusting items appear in adjusted/statutory reconciliations.

**Pension:** Deficit largely closed after **£82m** contribution, but **£13m** annual scheduled contributions to 2030/31 plus potential **shareholder matching** when returns exceed **£80m** (expected **~£5m** matching in guidance) remain cash calls.

**Governance / key person:** **Alex Baldock** stepped down (26 March 2026 RNS); **Fredrik Tønnesen** appointed group CEO from 3 August 2026—strategy continuity likely but leadership transition is a near-term overlay.

**Going concern:** Directors adopt going concern; severe downside (**5%** sales stress over viability period) modelled **without covenant breach**; cyber scenario also modelled (`941f59ac0a40743d.txt`)—supportive but scenario-dependent.

**Accounting:** Material **adjusting items** (£35m cash impact on strategic/regulatory lines in segment tables) separate “adjusted” from statutory performance—investors must track both.
