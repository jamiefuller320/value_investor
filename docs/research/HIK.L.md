## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Hikma Pharmaceuticals PLC is a diversified generic and branded medicines group (Injectables, Hikma Rx, Branded MENA) trading on a modest multiple (screen P/E 12.6, P/B 1.9, yield 3.8%) despite investment-grade balance sheet metrics and a recovering earnings trajectory. FY2025 core operating profit rose 3% to $741m on 6% core revenue growth; H1 2026 accelerated to 9% core profit growth on 4% revenue, with management reiterating full-year core operating profit guidance of $720–770m. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy (10/22 models, 81% composite), but overlays already temper this to Buy on healthcare price-erosion, statutory/adjusted EPS divergence, and a material free-cash-flow basis mismatch (filing-aligned FCF $119m vs screen TTM $14m). The central debate is whether Branded momentum and US manufacturing scale can offset Injectables margin compression, elevated capex, and the withdrawal of medium-term guidance following the February 2026 profit reset.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Hikma offers a rare combination of cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk-screen passes: P/E below 25, P/B below 3, debt/equity 77%, ROE 15.4%, and five value families cleared (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk). Business quality is underwritten by segment diversity—Branded is the #1 pharma company by sales in MENA, Injectables is a top-three US generic injectables supplier by volume, and the group carries BBB/stable ratings from S&P and Fitch with net debt/core EBITDA at 1.6x at year-end 2025 (1.9x at H1 2026).

The screen’s strong-buy signal aligns with a business that is growing core earnings, returning capital (84c total dividend in 2025, +5%; $250m buyback launched February 2026), and trading below sector peers on headline multiples. H1 2026 core operating profit of $405m (+9%) and operating cash flow of $214m (+33%) suggest the February guidance cut may have marked a trough rather than structural deterioration. US domestic manufacturing (Ohio, New Jersey) also positions Hikma relatively well versus import-dependent peers if generic drug tariffs materialise—a point echoed in sell-side coverage.

Where the screen is less convincing: FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Economic Moat, Piotroski F-Score (6/9), and Earnings Quality all fail. Statutory EPS growth is −4.7% while adjusted EPS growth is +1.7%, and FY2025 filing-aligned FCF of $119m covers the dividend only thinly after $317m capex. These are not disqualifiers for a value case, but they explain why deep research should treat the signal as “accumulate on quality at a discount,” not unequivocal strong buy.
