## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Hikma is a diversified generic and branded pharmaceutical group (Injectables, Branded MENA, Hikma Rx) reporting in US dollars, with FY2025 core revenue of $3,349m (+6%) and core operating profit of $741m (+3%) per the 26 February 2026 full-year results release. The quantitative screen flags the stock as a strong buy on cheapness (P/E 12.8, P/B 1.9), quality (ROE 15.4%), dividend (3.8% yield), GARP, and balance-sheet metrics (D/E 77%), though FCF yield and earnings-quality screens fail. The valuation hook is a mid-teens earnings multiple on a globally diversified pharma platform that has compounded core earnings through 2022–2025, now paired with a $250m buyback and 5% dividend increase. The central debate is whether Injectables margin pressure, one-off legal cash outflows, and withdrawn medium-term guidance are temporary reset costs—or evidence that the recovery narrative (reinforced by a strong H1 2026 profit beat in press coverage) still lacks filing-verified runway.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Hikma offers a rare combination of sector-defensive end-markets, geographic diversification (North America, MENA, Europe), and headline valuation that passes multiple classic screens simultaneously: Graham Enterprising, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Financial Health. At P/E 12.8 and P/B 1.9 with a 3.8% yield and ROE of 15.4%, the market prices in sustained Injectables disappointment rather than the Branded segment's double-digit growth (core revenue +10%, core operating profit +19% in FY2025) and Hikma Rx margin improvement.

Business quality is supported by filing evidence: S&P and Fitch upgraded the group to BBB in FY2025; net debt/core EBITDA was 1.6x at year-end ($1,387m net debt on $853m core EBITDA); return on average invested capital was 16.0%; and the board announced a $250m share buyback alongside an 84c total dividend (+5%). The Darwazah family's return to operational leadership (Said Darwazah as CEO from December 2025) and board refresh (Victoria Hull as Chair, Tobias Hestler appointed NED in August 2026 per news) signal a strategy reset focused on execution over medium-term targets.

The screen's strong-buy signal is directionally supported by fundamentals, but conviction is tempered: filing-aligned free cash flow was only $119m in FY2025 (OCF $436m less elevated capex/investment), down from $329m in 2024, and the screen itself notes weak TTM FCF ($14.4m) versus filing-aligned $119m—a data-quality flag rather than a rejection. Failed screens (FCF Yield, Earnings Quality, Economic Moat, Piotroski 6/9) correctly highlight that cheapness here comes with cyclical generic pricing pressure and lumpy cash conversion, not pure balance-sheet deep value.
