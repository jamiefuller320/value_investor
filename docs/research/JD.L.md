# JD Sports Fashion Plc (JD.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-12T12:27:40.141508+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
JD Sports Fashion Plc is a global multi-brand athleisure retailer whose shares screen as a buy on earnings yield (~10%), FCF yield, and composite value metrics (11/22 models; composite 69%), despite failing strict quality, moat, and financial-health screens because of leverage and thin margins. The investment case rests on sustained cash generation, shareholder returns (buybacks and rising dividends), and a valuation that embeds a sharp slowdown after Hibbett/Courir integration and a promotional, low like-for-like environment. The central debate is whether headline cheapness and filing-backed cash flows offset declining adjusted profits, North American weakness, and a capital structure heavy with leases and acquisition-related debt. Recent profit guidance cuts (outside the primary filing pack indexed here) sit awkwardly against a screen that still flags persistent buy conviction.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, JD.L offers a contrarian entry into a scaled FTSE 100 operator with demonstrated ability to outgrow the sports-fashion market organically, now pivoting from acquisition-led expansion toward margin repair and capital discipline. The quantitative screen passes all five factor families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk), with Piotroski F-Score 7/9 and earnings-quality/composite-value passes, while data quality is full (20/20 metrics). That profile fits a “cash-rich retailer at a cyclical trough” template more than a deep-quality compounder: ROE ~13.7% and P/B ~1.3x support return-of-capital stories, but Graham/Buffett/moat models fail on leverage (debt/equity ~107%) and negative recent earnings growth (~−8%). Business quality remains operationally relevant—multi-brand agility, JD-first store rollout, and North America/Europe scale—but the screen’s buy signal is driven chiefly by valuation and FCF, not by improving reported earnings. Aligning the thesis with filings: FY25–FY26 show revenue still growing while adjusted profit and EPS compress, which is consistent with a value opportunity only if management delivers on “profit ahead of revenue” and tighter capex (target 3–3.5% of revenue medium term).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
- **Cyclical demand:** FY26 filing cites promotional global markets, declining store footfall, and negative LFL (**−2.1%**); FY25 already flagged UK/Europe apparel weather and promotional pressure. Screen earnings growth is negative (~−8%).  
- **Leverage and leases:** Large lease liabilities (FY25 interest on lease liabilities **£112m**); interest-bearing borrowings rose to **£679m** post-acquisitions; debt/equity fails conservative screens. Refinancing of **£700m** RCF, **$300m** ABL, and **$700m** Hibbett term loan was in progress per FY25 RNS—status not updated in indexed FY26 extract.  
- **Contingent capital (Genesis NCI):** FY25 RNS defers put/call on **20%** Genesis NCI to FY2029/FY2030 exercise windows, with option pricing tied to EBITDA and a **£1.5bn** cap on total liability—material future cash risk if North America valuations hold up.  
- **Integration and execution:** Hibbett/Courir integration, Heerlen DC delays (FY25 **£76m** impairment), and ongoing US platform integration costs; adjusting items remain sizeable.  
- **Partner/concentration:** Business model depends on major brands (Nike et al.); news flow highlights Nike read-across risk (not quantified in indexed filings).  
- **Governance transition:** FY26 report notes chair stepping down at July 2026 AGM; Peter Agnefjäll appointed chair from September 2026 (news). CEO under pressure in press after guidance cuts (news, not filing).  
- **Going concern / covenants:** Available filing **body extracts do not include** explicit going-concern or covenant test disclosure; full audited notes are not in the indexed text—**gap for verify-before-trade**.
