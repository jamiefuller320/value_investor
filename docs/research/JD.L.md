# JD Sports Fashion Plc (JD.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-13T07:58:00.366794+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
JD Sports Fashion Plc is a global multibrand sports-fashion retailer whose shares screen as a buy on cheapness, cash generation, and contrarian metrics (composite score 70%, 11/22 models, P/E 8.7, filing-aligned FCF yield ~26%), despite failing strict balance-sheet and “quality moat” screens. The investment case rests on a scaled omnichannel platform (4,800+ stores, North America ~40% of FY26 revenue), strong operating cash flow, and capital returns (dividends plus £200m buybacks in FY26, another £200m announced). The central debate is whether depressed multiples already price a cyclical slowdown—negative like-for-like sales, two profit-guidance cuts in 2026, US and footwear weakness—or whether earnings stabilise as infrastructure spend fades and the group harvests Hibbett/Courir synergies. Filings confirm revenue growth with shrinking adjusted and statutory profits; interim RNS/trading-update bodies are absent from the research pack, so near-term trading must be triangulated from news and the FY26 annual report narrative.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, JD.L offers a classic “cash-rich cyclical at a low multiple” profile rather than a Graham-defensive compounder. The screen passes five factor families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk) with high data completeness (20/20), persistent buy signal (15 weeks), and a Piotroski F-Score of 7, supported by positive net income, OCF exceeding earnings, and improving gross margin/asset turnover. Business quality is operational, not balance-sheet pristine: the group outgrew the sports-fashion market organically in FY25 (~5.8% organic sales vs ~3.8% market) and again showed resilient top-line growth in FY26 (+10.5% revenue to £12,662m per the FY26 annual report), while management emphasises full-price discipline and multibrand agility. Cash conversion remains the anchor: FY25 operating cash flow net of lease repayments was £1,245m (FY25 results RNS); FY26 free cash flow was £462m (+36%) with over £1.3bn operating cash flow (FY26 annual report). That underpins progressive dividends (1.20p proposed for FY26, +20%) and buybacks—aligned with the screen’s FCF and earnings-yield passes. What the screen underweights is leverage (debt/equity ~107%, Financial Health fail), negative reported earnings growth (~−8%), and cyclical US/European consumer exposure—acceptable for value only if the market is over-discounting a temporary margin and LFL trough.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Cyclical and regional: FY26 chair commentary cites promotional trading, strained consumer finances, declining footfall, and tariff uncertainty; Q1 FY26 (in FY25 RNS) already showed Group LFL −2.0%. North America is the largest profit pool but also the focus of 2026 guidance cuts in press coverage. Germany/Europe consumer weakness appears in broker notes (news).

Leverage and lease intensity: FY25 RNS shows interest on lease liabilities £112m and rising borrowings ($700m Hibbett term loan outstanding at 1 Feb 2025); FY26 annual report cites net leverage 1.4x and thin net cash excluding leases (~£31m). Screen fails Financial Health, Graham enterprising, and Buffett-quality thresholds on debt and margins. Genesis non-controlling interest put/call: cap unchanged at £1.5bn with exercise deferred to post-FY2029/FY2030 (FY25 RNS post-balance sheet events)—a future cash call not captured in near-term multiples.

Competitive and partner concentration: Heavy reliance on global sportswear partners (Nike, adidas, etc. per annual report narrative); Nike turnaround delays have moved JD shares (news, July 2026). Multibrand model helps but does not eliminate brand-cycle risk.

Execution and governance: Infrastructure programmes (Heerlen DC, omnichannel replatform) absorbed cost and caused impairments (e.g. £76m Derby DC in FY25 adjusting items). Chair transition: FY26 report notes chair not standing for re-election at July 2026 AGM; Peter Agnefjäll appointed chair effective September 2026 (news, August 2026). CEO Regis Schultz remains under market scrutiny after guidance resets (news).

Accounting and adjustments: Elevated adjusting items (£208m FY25; impairments, acquisition amortisation, NCI option revaluation £62m in FY25 RNS)—statutory EPS materially below adjusted. No going-concern language surfaced in extracts reviewed; full “Principal Risks” section in the FY26 annual report PDF was not fully parsed in the body extract—treat detailed risk register as unread in this pass.
