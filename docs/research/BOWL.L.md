# Hollywood Bowl Group plc (BOWL.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:29:03.401469+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Hollywood Bowl is the largest branded ten-pin bowling operator in the UK and Canada, combining estate expansion with a yield-management model that has delivered four consecutive years of record revenue. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (10/22 models; composite ~60%; P/E ~14×, yield ~4.9%, ROE ~22%). Filings show robust cash generation, net cash of £26.0m at H1 FY26, an undrawn £25m RCF, and progressive dividends plus buybacks. The central debate is whether spend-per-game and new-centre growth can offset softening visit volumes and rising labour, rates and competitive-socialising pressure in a cyclical consumer backdrop.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, BOWL offers a cash-generative, asset-light-in-appearance but lease-backed leisure franchise trading below many quality peers on earnings and dividend yield, with management returning capital systematically. The screen passes Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple and Earnings Quality — a rare breadth across cheapness, quality and income families with full data coverage (20/20 metrics).

Business quality supports the signal: FY2025 revenue rose 8.8% to £250.7m with adjusted EBITDA up 4.2% to £91.2m; H1 FY26 revenue grew 9.5% to £141.5m with adjusted EBITDA after rent up 8.9% to £42.2m. Like-for-like revenue has remained positive (FY2025 +0.6%; H1 FY26 +2.3%) despite muted UK consumer confidence, driven by spend-per-game, dynamic pricing and amusement/F&B mix. Canada has scaled from ~9% of group revenue in FY2022 to 16% in H1 FY26, diversifying growth. Dividend policy targets 55% of adjusted PAT (pre-IFRS 16); total FY2025 dividend was 13.28p (+10.1%), with H1 FY26 interim 4.52p (+10.2%) and £15m (FY25) plus £5m (H2 FY26) buybacks announced. The combination of mid-teens P/E, near-5% yield, high ROE and disciplined capex (£25–30m guided for FY26 vs £36.5m in FY25) aligns with the screen's value-and-quality framing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical consumer exposure.** Filings cite economic conditions in the UK and Canada as a principal risk; FY2025 UK LFL game volumes fell 7.5% despite record revenue. Management acknowledges ongoing cost-of-living pressure and increased marketing spend to defend share of wallet. H1 FY26 gross margin was squeezed by NLW and employer NI (~£1m each in centres).

**Competitive and execution risk.** Competitive socialising entrants (adult-focused, city-centre) and non-bowling leisure alternatives are flagged. H1 FY26 impaired £2.8m on a young UK centre facing "intense competition" — a reminder that site selection is not fail-safe. Putt & Play mini-golf saw £2.3m FY2025 impairments; mini-golf is now immaterial to revenue disclosure.

**Lease and leverage.** Although reported net cash is positive, IFRS 16 lease liabilities of ~£236m represent material fixed obligations; property rent pre-IFRS 16 was £22.9m in FY2025 (+11%). RCF covenant: net debt to adjusted EBITDA pre-IFRS 16 ≤1.75× — low risk today but worth monitoring if expansion accelerates and cash returns continue.

**Cost inflation and regulatory.** FY2025 and interim filings warn of April 2026 business-rates revaluation offsetting multiplier relief, living/minimum wage increases from November 2026, and full-year employer NI impact in H1 FY26. Energy is hedged (through FY2027 per FY25 report; 76% electricity to FY20229 per H1 FY26).

**Governance and personnel.** Long-serving CFO Laurence Keen moved to Canada CEO (fixed term to October 2026); Antony Smith (ex-Ten Entertainment, River Island) became Group CFO from February 2026. Auditor change: KPMG resigned; EY appointed for FY2026 after tender (March 2026) — no adverse circumstances disclosed, but transition warrants routine monitoring.

**M&A and contingencies.** Teaquinn/XBEC earn-out capped at CAD 17m, contingent on FY2026 EBITDA; £4.96m current contingent consideration at H1 FY26. No defined-benefit pension liability flagged; FY2025 employer pension costs were £1.3m (defined contribution).

**Accounting.** IFRS 16 creates non-cash profit compression (FY2025 PBT −£3.4m; H1 FY26 −£1.6m vs rent basis). Management emphasises pre-IFRS 16 EBITDA/PAT for covenants and dividends; investors should track both statutory and adjusted metrics.
