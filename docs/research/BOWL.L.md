# Hollywood Bowl Group plc (BOWL.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:05:03.690412+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Hollywood Bowl is the largest branded ten-pin bowling operator in the UK and Canada, combining an expanding estate (92 centres at FY2025 year-end; 77 UK and 16 Canada at H1 FY2026) with a value-led, family-orientated leisure proposition. The quantitative screen rates the name a **buy** (8/22 models; composite 57%, sector-relative 60%), supported by FCF yield (~7.9%), dividend yield (~4.8%), quality metrics (ROE ~21.7%) and earnings-quality passes. Filings show record revenue and improving cash generation, with FY2025 revenue of £250.7m (+8.8%) and H1 FY2026 momentum continuing (+9.5% revenue; adjusted EBITDA after rent +8.9%). The valuation hook is a mid-teens P/E on a cash-generative, returning-capital story (55% adjusted PAT payout plus buybacks). The key debate is whether spend-per-game and new-centre growth can offset cyclical volume weakness, rising UK labour and business-rates costs, and the economic reality of large IFRS 16 lease liabilities beneath a headline net-cash balance sheet.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, BOWL offers a rare combination of **cheap cash yield, dividend discipline and demonstrable business quality** that aligns with the screen’s passed families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk). Revenue has compounded through expansion and yield management: FY2023–FY2025 revenue moved from £215.1m to £230.4m to £250.7m (Yahoo annual series, consistent with FY2025 RNS), while adjusted EBITDA after rent reached £68.4m pre-IFRS 16 in FY2025 and £42.2m in H1 FY2026 alone. Management returns surplus cash systematically—FY2025 total shareholder returns of £37.4m (13.28p dividend; £15m buyback)—and renewed that posture with a 4.52p interim (+10.2%) and a further £5m H2 FY2026 buyback. Operational quality supports the screen’s “Buffett Quality” and “Earnings Quality” passes: high gross margins (~83% on COGS basis in FY2025), ~70% of revenue not exposed to COGS inflation, energy largely hedged, and covenant headroom on an undrawn £25m RCF. The screen’s PEGY (0.03) and FCF yield (7.9%) suggest the market underprices durable cash conversion relative to a franchise still scaling in Canada (16% of Group revenue at H1 FY2026 vs 9% in FY2022). Timing is neutral per the screen (RSI ~44); this is a **fundamental accumulate**, not a momentum trade.

**Trend commentary**

The multi-year pattern is **top-line expansion driven by new centres and Canada**, with LFL volumes fragile but monetisation improving via dynamic pricing, amusements and F&B. Adjusted EBITDA pre-IFRS 16 was essentially flat in FY2025 (+0.9%) after FY2024 UK business-rates rebates and Surrey Quays closure benefits, then re-accelerated in H1 FY2026 (+8.9% adjusted EBITDA after rent). FY2026 capex guidance remains £25–30m (FY2025: £36.5m). Dividend policy anchors to 55% of adjusted PAT pre-IFRS 16.

**Yahoo fallback (valuation cross-check only):** Yahoo `financials_annual.json` reports FY2025 free cash flow of £37.0m and operating cash flow £73.5m—these differ from the company’s adjusted FCF definition (£21.2m) owing to treatment of lease capital payments and expansionary capex; filing figures are preferred for coverage analysis.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and demand:** Indoor leisure remains weather- and confidence-sensitive (FY2025 UK LFL volumes −7.5%; H1 FY2026 modest volume decline offset by SPG). Principal risks explicitly include UK/Canada macro deterioration (interim note 15, mirroring annual report risk register).

**Cost inflation:** FY2025 and H1 FY2026 filings flag National Living Wage, employer National Insurance (full-year H1 FY2026 effect), and April 2026 business-rates revaluation likely erasing multiplier relief—centre labour rose 18.5% in H1 FY2026 (partly new centres; ~£1m each from NI and NLW).

**Competitive:** Competitive socialising entrants in UK catchments; H1 FY2026 impairment at a second UK centre in a contested local market. Putt & Play mini-golf saw £2.3m FY2025 impairments—non-core format underperformance.

**Leverage and covenants:** Headline net cash masks **£235m+ lease liabilities**; net debt per company definition ~£210m. RCF covenant: total net debt to adjusted EBITDA pre-IFRS 16 ≤1.75×—comfortably met today but leverage rises if EBITDA stalls while expansion continues. Going concern and covenant tests pass in stress scenarios, but this is lease-heavy “hidden” gearing.

**Governance and key personnel:** CFO transition—Laurence Keen moves to Canada CEO (fixed term to October 2026); Antony Smith (ex-Ten Entertainment, River Island) joined as Group CFO on 2 February 2026. RNS index records a **Change of Auditor** (16 March 2026) without body extract—details unverified here. Smith’s prior River Island role involved a sanctioned restructuring plan (disclosed in directorate RNS).

**M&A/contingencies:** Teaquinn/XBEC contingent consideration capped at CAD 17m (9.2× EBITDA pre-IFRS 16); ongoing earn-out charges. No defined-benefit pension exposure flagged in available extracts (staff costs include £1.3m pension in FY2025—appears defined-contribution).

**Accounting:** Heavy reliance on adjusted metrics and pre-IFRS 16 EBITDA for dividends, management incentives and bank covenants; IFRS 16 creates ~£1.6m H1 profit compression vs cash rent. Impairment policy reclassified to adjusting items after shareholder feedback.
