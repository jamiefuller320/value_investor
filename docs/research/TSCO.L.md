# Tesco PLC (TSCO.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T07:30:52.469426+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Tesco is the UK's dominant full-line grocer, trading at a modest absolute valuation (P/E 17.4, yield 3.2%) against a business delivering record UK market share, rising adjusted profits, and accelerating free cash flow. The quantitative screen passes all five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—supported by double-digit ROE and positive FCF yield, with timing favouring accumulation near the 200-day moving average.

The investment case rests on a structurally improved UK grocery franchise (Clubcard, Aldi Price Match, online/Whoosh) converting revenue momentum into cash returns via dividends and a £750m buyback programme. FY2025/26 adjusted operating profit reached £3.15bn on group sales of £66.6bn, with retail free cash flow of £2.4bn funding shareholder distributions.

The central debate is whether slowing like-for-like growth (Q1 FY2026/27 group LFL +1.0%) and rising competitive intensity signal peak margins, or whether Tesco's scale and value positioning allow it to sustain £3.0–3.3bn adjusted operating profit guidance through a tougher consumer backdrop. Net debt has edged higher to £10.6bn (retail definition), and equal-pay litigation remains an unquantified tail risk.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
**Why the screen flags a buy:** Tesco passes seven quantitative models spanning Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Piotroski F-Score, and Earnings Quality—unusual breadth for a large-cap consumer name. Key metrics align with value criteria: P/E 17.4 (<25), P/B 2.6 (<3), ROE 15.5% (>8%), and FCF of c.£2.2bn. All five factor families pass, with 20/20 data quality and a persistent buy signal over five weeks (conviction 55%, timing score 0.75).

**Business quality supports the signal:** Filings describe Tesco's strongest UK competitive position in a decade—market share at its highest since 2015, customer Net Promoter Score improving, and adjusted operating profit compounding from £2.83bn (FY2023/24) to £3.13bn (FY2024/25) to £3.15bn (FY2025/26). Management's "save to invest" programme has funded price investment (Aldi Price Match across 2,000+ Express stores) without sacrificing profitability, a hallmark of operational quality the screen's earnings-quality and Piotroski passes likely capture.

**Capital allocation reinforces the case:** Dividend per share has risen from 13.70p (FY2024/25) to 14.5p (FY2025/26), with a £750m share buyback underway (£341m repurchased by mid-June 2026). Insurance & Money Services continues as a higher-margin adjacency (2.7m policies in force, +15% YoY per Q1 trading), while the Tesco Bank banking sale to Barclays (completed November 2024) simplified the group and reduced regulatory capital intensity.

For a value investor, Tesco offers defensive cash generation at a non-premium multiple, with management explicitly guiding FY2026/27 adjusted operating profit of £3.0–3.3bn and free cash flow within the £1.5–2.0bn medium-term range.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Competitive intensity:** The FY2025/26 chair's statement cites "relentless competition" and "even stronger competitive activity in our key UK grocery market." Q1 FY2026/27 LFL of +1.0% (vs tougher comps) triggered a share-price reaction; news coverage flagged UK sales growth slowing amid Middle East uncertainty (June 2026). Discounters (Aldi, Lidl) remain structurally embedded; Tesco's Aldi Price Match is defensive but margin-compressive if price gaps widen.

**Regulatory and fiscal:** The July 2026 debt prospectus warns of increasing compliance costs across consumer law, competition, pension, financial services (PRA/FCA oversight of Insurance & Money Services), and tax rules—costs that "may have a detrimental impact on financial performance if it is not possible to pass on any such additional costs." September 2026 news reported Tesco joining M&S in warning Andy Burnham against further high-street tax rises, signalling ongoing policy risk to store economics.

**Litigation — equal pay:** The prospectus states that if Tesco Stores Limited were unsuccessful in equal-pay claim defences, "the potential quantum of its liability could be material" (referencing note 33, Annual Report 2026). This is a material contingent liability not captured by quantitative screens.

**Leverage and pensions:** Retail net debt rose £1.1bn to £10.6bn in FY2025/26. Total debt per Yahoo is £15.1bn including £7.9bn lease obligations. Pension liabilities appear manageable post the £2.5bn one-off contribution (2021, per older filings), with Yahoo showing non-current pension obligations of £127m (FY2026)—though IAS 19 deficit sensitivities to discount rates remain a background risk.

**Cyclical consumer exposure:** Despite "defensive" classification, Tesco is exposed to UK household spending. Q1 commentary explicitly references Middle East conflict creating customer uncertainty. Booker wholesale LFL −3.2% (contract exit, catering softness) shows B2B cyclicality.

**Governance / going concern:** No going-concern qualification appears in available extracts. Extensive PDMR and buyback transaction RNS flow is routine. Chair Gerry Murphy purchased 10,000 shares at £4.48 in August 2026—a modest positive signal.

**Accounting / earnings quality:** FY2025/26 included £144m unusual items (Yahoo); adjusted metrics exclude adjusting items per company policy. Screen passes Earnings Quality and Piotroski, but adjusted-vs-statutory divergence warrants monitoring.
