# Playtech plc (PTEC.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:06:50.987803+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Playtech has completed a structural pivot from a mixed B2B/B2C group to a predominantly pure-play B2B gambling technology platform, funded by the €2.3 billion Snaitech disposal and a ~€1.8 billion special dividend. FY25 continuing revenue fell 10% to €763.6 million and Adjusted EBITDA 9% to €197.0 million, largely reflecting the Caliente Interactive contract restructure, but underlying regulated B2B growth (ex-Caliente) was +6%, with US revenue nearly doubling. Trading updates through July 2026 guide FY26 Adjusted EBITDA to at least €270 million — well above consensus — driven by Americas momentum, though H2 is expected to soften as Hard Rock Digital revenue normalises and UK gaming duty rises bite. The quantitative screen flags deep value (P/B ~0.9, D/E ~24%) and financial health, but reported ROE is negative and free-cash-flow metrics are distorted by one-offs; the central debate is whether post-transformation earnings power and the investment portfolio justify book-value discount, or whether regulatory friction, customer concentration, and litigation cap re-rating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy signal rests on cheapness (Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value), perceived moat (Economic Moat), and balance-sheet quality (Financial Health) — three of four factor families passed, with composite score 0.58 versus sector-relative 0.54. At P/B 0.9 and D/E ~24%, the market prices Playtech below tangible book (~€1.09 billion per Yahoo balance sheet) despite a net cash position of €28.5 million at year-end (filings) and an undrawn €225 million revolving credit facility.

Business quality is improving on a forward basis rather than in FY25 headline accounts. Management targets €250–300 million Adjusted EBITDA and €70–100 million Free Cash Flow medium-term; H1 2026 trading already guides Adjusted EBITDA above €155 million and full-year at least €270 million (9 July 2026 trading update), implying meaningful progress toward those targets. Strategic assets — 30.8% of Caliente Interactive (€708.7 million carrying value), Hard Rock Digital equity (€178.8 million, fair value), and a scaled US Live/SaaS franchise (+48% SaaS revenue in FY25) — support the screen’s quality/moat pass, even as reported continuing operations posted a €169.5 million loss after tax.

For a value investor, the case is a post-catalyst rerating play: capital returned, debt reduced (2019 bond fully repaid; €300 million 2028 bond outstanding), ongoing buybacks (8.3% of capital repurchased in H2 2025), and earnings momentum in regulated Americas markets. The screen’s FCF basis mismatch (filing Adjusted FCF €29.5 million vs Yahoo TTM −€52.9 million) reflects special dividends, retention bonuses, and definitional differences — a gap that warrants verification before sizing, but does not invalidate the cheapness signal if FY26 guidance materialises.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and tax:** UK Remote Gaming Duty rising to 40% (April 2026) and General Betting Duty to 25% on remote sports (April 2027) weigh on Sun Bingo and UK B2B (−6% revenue FY25). Colombia tax regime remains volatile (19% deposit VAT, constitutional suspension, new 16% GGR consumption tax proposed March 2026). Brazil GGR tax phasing to 15% by 2028 adds margin pressure. These headwinds are acknowledged in filings but partially offset by Americas growth — concentration risk if US/LatAm slows.

**Customer and revenue concentration:** Hard Rock Digital has become one of Playtech’s largest customers following the PMR sports product; management expects H2 2026–2027 revenue at a “lower but more sustainable level.” Caliente Interactive restructuring makes ~31% equity income and dividends material to Adjusted EBITDA (€61.8m investment income segment in FY25). DraftKings, FanDuel, and Caliente collectively drive Americas narrative — loss or repricing of any major partner would disproportionately affect targets.

**Litigation and reputational:** Evolution AB (October 2025) identified Playtech Software Limited as commissioning party behind a 2021 Black Cube report and stated intent to amend its New Jersey complaint. Playtech disputes unlawful conduct; as at approval of FY25 accounts, no claim served on the Group. Outcome uncertain; potential regulatory/reputational overhang in US markets.

**Governance:** May 2026 AGM saw remuneration report pass with only 77.83% support; Samy Reeb re-election 78.26%; resolutions on directors’ allotment powers (44.75%) and pre-emption disapplication (45.09%) failed. Board committed to shareholder consultation — signals dissent on pay and dilution, not operational control, but warrants monitoring.

**Accounting and earnings quality:** Large gap between reported (−€169.5m) and Adjusted (+€44.2m) continuing profit; recurring use of “Adjusted” for investment income, fair-value movements, and incentive accruals. Sun Bingo €52.9m impairment and prior Sports CGU write-offs (FY24 €96.3m) highlight legacy asset risk. Effective tax rate 37.9% on Adjusted profit adds complexity.

**Liquidity and leverage:** Going concern assessment (Final Results) states Directors have “reasonable expectation” of adequate resources for ≥15 months after approval — positive, but reliant on trading performance and undrawn RCF. €300m 2028 bond remains; net cash €28.5m is modest relative to bond stack.

**Competitive:** Live casino and platform markets are intensely contested (Evolution, Light & Wonder, proprietary operator tech). SaaS growth (+48%) is encouraging but from a lower base.
