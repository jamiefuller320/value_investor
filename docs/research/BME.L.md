# B&M European Value Retail plc (BME.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:33:10.921904+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
B&M European Value Retail plc operates UK and French variety discount retail (~799 B&M UK stores, ~147 France, ~342 Heron/B&M Express) and screens as a **buy** on cheapness, cash generation, dividend and quality metrics (6/22 models; composite score 65%; P/E 14.9, yield 3.9%, filing-aligned FCF ~£594m). Filings tell a harder operating story: FY26 revenue rose 3.6% to **£5,775m** but adjusted EBITDA (pre-IFRS 16) fell 25.9% to **£459m**, with statutory profit before tax down 47% to **£227m** as UK execution slipped and costs inflated. The investment case rests on whether “Back to B&M Basics” can restore UK like-for-like growth and margins while France and cash flow support the balance sheet. The central debate is whether depressed earnings are cyclical/self-inflicted and recoverable, or evidence of permanent share loss to sharper discounters (Action, grocery discounters) in a wage- and cost-inflated UK retail environment.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, B&M offers a classic **“cheap on cash, expensive on hope”** setup. The quantitative screen passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk—with standout **FCF yield (~25%)**, **Neff PEGY (0.04)**, **Piotroski F-Score**, **Magic Formula** and **dividend growth** signals, on 20/20 data quality. That aligns with filing evidence of resilient cash conversion: FY26 **post-tax free cash flow of £321m** (flat YoY despite lower profits), **statutory cash from operations £801m**, and **net debt down 16% to £656m** (leverage **1.4x** adjusted EBITDA pre-IFRS 16, within the 1.0–1.5x target).

Business quality is mixed. The model—high-volume, low-price variety retail with strong new-store economics (~12-month payback cited at interim)—remains structurally attractive in a downtrading consumer environment. B&M France grew revenue **13.4%** in FY26 with market-share gains. However, UK quality has deteriorated: flat B&M UK LFL (-0.1% FY26), gross margin pressure, and Heron EBITDA margin collapse to **2.9%**. Management under CEO Tjeerd Jegen (from June 2025) is executing **Back to B&M Basics** (price, promotions, range simplification, availability)—early Q3/Q4 FY26 signs (December UK LFL +3%) and Q1 FY27 Heron recovery (+2.6% LFL) are encouraging but not yet sustained at group level.

The screen’s buy signal is therefore best read as **valuation support from cash and assets**, not endorsement of near-term earnings momentum (screen highlights **-48.5% earnings growth**). Conviction is **new** (1 week at signal; timing neutral; RSI ~65). Filing-aligned FCF **£594m** materially exceeds the screen’s suppressed TTM cashflow figure (£359.5m), strengthening the cheapness case but widening the gap between accounting earnings and cash that requires operational follow-through to close.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Operational / competitive:** Repeated guidance reductions (Oct 2025 interim, Jan 2026 Q3) signal limited visibility. UK execution drift (FMCG price position, SKU bloat, availability at ~86% vs ~98% benchmark cited at interim) exposes B&M to **Action**, Aldi/Lidl and mainstream grocers. Heron remains structurally weak; goodwill **£88m** with reduced impairment headroom (£32m at H1 per interim).

**Accounting / controls:** Oct 2025 disclosure that **freight costs were misrecognised** following a systems integration change did not restate audited history but invalidated prior outlook assumptions; EY review completed Jan 2026 with process remediation—elevates **control risk** until sustained clean reporting.

**Cost / cyclical:** FY26 statutory cost step-up (NMW, employer NI, EPR); FY27 headwinds from **Middle East conflict** on freight, fuel and energy (flagged in FY26 results). Garden/seasonal volatility visible in Q1 FY27 UK LFL.

**Leverage / liquidity:** Pre-IFRS 16 leverage back in range at **1.4x**, but **lease-adjusted net debt/EBITDA 2.9x**; gross debt ~£998m vs cash £342m. Refinancing includes **£250m 6.5% bond (Nov 2024)** and RCF extended to 2030. FY26 preliminary **going concern** affirmed: directors tested **financial covenants** and severe downside scenarios; no covenant breach disclosed.

**Governance / key person:** Luxembourg→**Jersey redomicile** completed Feb/Mar 2026 (enabling future buybacks); adds structural complexity. **CFO turnover** (Mike Schmidt departure Oct 2025; interim Helen Cowing → Peter Waterhouse → Atheeq Akbar from Feb 2027). CEO and multiple directors purchased shares Nov 2025—positive signalling but against a backdrop of profit warnings.

**Pension:** No material pension deficit or scheme risk identified in available filing extracts.

**Impairments:** FY26 **£36m** store lease/PPE impairments; further impairment possible if Heron LFL/margin recovery stalls (interim sensitivity tables).
