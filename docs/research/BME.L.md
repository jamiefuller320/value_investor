# B&M European Value Retail plc (BME.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T06:47:43.907513+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
B&M European Value Retail is a UK-listed variety discount retailer (~797 UK B&M stores, 340 Heron/B&M Express, 151 France) undergoing an operational reset under new CEO Tjeerd Jegen’s “Back to B&M Basics” programme. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cheapness (FCF yield ~26%, P/E 14.4x, dividend yield 4.1%), quality (Piotroski pass, ROE 21%), and risk metrics, but earnings have collapsed: FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance was cut to £440–475m (pre-IFRS 16) from £620m in FY25. The valuation hook is trough-cycle cash generation (£594m FCF per Yahoo, filing-confirmed cash-generative model) against a depressed share price, with redomicile to Jersey completed and buybacks flagged as the preferred capital-return tool. The central debate is whether margin restoration (management targets low double-digit UK EBITDA margins medium-term) materialises within 12–18 months, or whether competitive intensity, UK like-for-like weakness, and Heron underperformance keep earnings depressed despite attractive headline multiples.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, B&M offers a classic “quality at a discount during transition” setup, corroborated by five of four screening families passing (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk). The business retains structural advantages: a large, growing store footprint (long-term UK target ~1,200 stores), France growing strongly (Q1 FY27 revenue +14.6%, LFL +5.3%), and proven cash conversion—the interim filing reports post-tax free cash flow of £51m in H1 FY26 despite margin pressure. Store expansion remains accretive (~12-month payback cited by management). The screen’s FCF yield (25.6%) and Neff PEGY (0.04) suggest the market prices in sustained earnings weakness; if “Back to B&M Basics” restores UK LFL growth, mean reversion in margins could drive substantial upside without requiring heroic revenue growth. Dividend policy (40–50% payout of adjusted earnings) and impending buyback capacity post-Jersey migration add a tangible return-of-capital layer. Offsetting this, screen conviction is only 41% (new signal, one week), timing is neutral with RSI ~66 and price extended above the 200-day MA, and reported earnings growth of −43.4% signals the models may be capturing trough optics rather than sustainable normalised earnings—verify before sizing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Operational / competitive:** UK like-for-like sales remain fragile (H1 FY26 +0.1%; Q1 FY27 −2.3%). Action and hard discounters (Aldi, Lidl, Home Bargains) intensify price competition; interim filing notes increased competition risk in UK and France. “Back to B&M Basics” requires 12–18 months to deliver full benefits—near-term margins sacrificed for price investment, SKU clearance, and availability improvements.

**Accounting / controls:** October 2025 disclosure that overseas freight costs were not correctly recognised in internal assumptions (though not affecting audited historical accounts). EY review completed by Q3 FY26; implementation of IT/finance control recommendations underway. Redomicile delayed pending review completion—now completed (Jersey, March 2026).

**Heron Foods:** £13m H1 FY26 impairment; goodwill headroom fell to £32m (from £99m at FY25 year-end). Sustained underperformance could trigger goodwill write-down (sensitivity: LFL −1.0% in year one triggers impairment per filing).

**Leverage / liquidity:** H1 FY26 leverage 1.6x pre-IFRS 16 exceeded the 1.0–1.5x target. High-yield bonds (£250m at 8.125% due 2030; £250m at 6.5% due 2031) and term facilities maturing March 2030. Going concern affirmed, but covenant headroom should be monitored as EBITDA falls.

**Governance / key person:** CFO Mike Schmidt departed (interim results, November 2025); Helen Cowing appointed interim CFO (from December 2025); Peter Waterhouse interim CFO at AGM notice (June 2026); Atheeq Akbar appointed CFO effective February 2027. CEO Jegen appointed June 2025—leadership transition still in progress. Former Group Trading Director settlement (£11m adjusting item in FY25 per interim notes).

**Regulatory / cost inflation:** Extended Producer Responsibility levy (£14m recognised in H1 FY26 UK), National Minimum Wage +6.7%, employer NI increases—all cited as margin headwinds.

**Structural:** Redomicile from Luxembourg to Jersey completed 2 March 2026 (Luxembourg shares cancelled); ticker continues as BME.L. Luxembourg 15% withholding tax on dividends until migration fully reflected in payment mechanics.
