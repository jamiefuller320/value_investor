# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 3 · Updated 2026-09-12T12:33:28.419677+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc trades on low multiples and a high dividend yield while the quantitative screen flags a persistent strong buy (15/22 models, ~81% composite). The equity story is no longer a simple integrated broadcaster: the agreed sale of Media & Entertainment (M&E) to Sky for up to £1.6bn reframes the listed entity as a global ITV Studios business plus a planned ~£950m capital return, subject to CMA/regulatory clearance expected around H2 2027. FY2025 results were resilient—flat group revenue, group adjusted EBITA down only 1%, and management-reported free cash flow of £187m—but statutory cash conversion weakened (65% profit-to-cash vs 83% in 2024) and linear advertising remains cyclical. The central debate is whether headline cheapness and the cash-return path outweigh execution risk on separation, loss of diversified UK ad earnings, and tight statutory dividend cover from operating cash flow less capex.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, ITV combines classic UK “yield plus rerating optionality” with a corporate catalyst. The screen passes all five metric families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk): P/E ~11.7, P/B ~1.5, dividend yield ~7.1%, debt/equity ~52%, and Lynch PEG ~0.51 on ~23% earnings growth—while timing overlay suggests accumulate (RSI ~43, price below the 200-day moving average). Business quality is mixed but improving strategically: ITV Studios grew external revenue 10% in FY2025 with 28% of studio revenue from streaming platforms; ITVX drove 16% streaming-hour growth and 12% digital ad growth, with digital ~31% of total advertising revenue. Cost discipline (£253m permanent non-content savings since 2019; further £20m guided for 2026) supports margins despite TAR down 5% in FY2025. The screen’s quality/moat models fail (Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Piotroski F-Score 3/9)—consistent with mid-teens ROE, thin broadcast margins, and working-capital volatility—not a deep moat compounder, but a cash-generative restructuring story at a discount. The Sky transaction (RNS, 6 July 2026) crystallises balance-sheet optionality: ~£950m return (~25p per share) after separation costs, a £2.1bn minimum content supply agreement (2028–2032), and Love Productions bolt-on—while leaving a listed studios pure-play targeting 13–15% adjusted EBITA margins and ~1.5x net debt/EBITDA post-completion. FCF basis divergence matters: filing-aligned statutory FCF was £148m (Yahoo/filing cash flow statement) vs company-adjusted £187m cited in results; statutory OCF−capex implies ~1.0× gross dividend cover vs ~1.53× on management’s adjusted measure—yield screens pass, but dividend durability needs monitoring on a statutory basis.
