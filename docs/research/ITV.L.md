# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T12:46:23.078907+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a strong buy on cheapness, dividend yield, earnings/FCF yield and financial health, with the market pricing a cyclical UK broadcaster rather than a transforming content-and-digital group. Filings show resilient adjusted profitability through a soft linear advertising cycle, accelerating ITVX/digital monetisation, and ITV Studios growth ahead of market, while the agreed sale of Media & Entertainment (M&E) to Sky for c.£1.6bn and a c.£950m net cash return reframes the equity as a Studios-led story plus near-term capital return. The central debate is whether headline valuation (screen: P/E 12.4, yield 6.7%, P/B 1.5) adequately compensates for UK advertising cyclicality, regulatory risk on the Sky transaction, and the loss of M&E’s cash-generative broadcast platform post-close. Deep research broadly supports the quantitative signal, but conviction is tempered by earnings volatility, a weak Piotroski trend score (3/9), and event risk around completion timing (potentially H2 2027 if CMA phase two).

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen rates ITV a strong buy (14/22 models passed; composite 80%; sector-relative 83%), spanning cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families, with full data quality (20/20 metrics). That profile fits a value investor looking for a cash-returning UK mid-cap trading below historical earnings power: earnings yield and FCF yield screens pass, dividend yield is 6.7% against a maintained 5.0p ordinary dividend (c.£190m annually in filings), P/E 12.4 and P/B 1.54 sit comfortably inside enterprising-value thresholds, and debt/equity at 52% passes the risk screen.

Business quality is mixed but directionally improving. Primary filings show group adjusted EBITA held broadly flat in FY2025 (£534m vs £542m in FY2024) despite total advertising revenue (TAR) down 5%, as ITV Studios external revenue rose 10% and M&E digital revenue grew 10% to £614m. ITVX has recouped cumulative investment four years early; streaming hours rose 16% in FY2025 and 27% in H1 2026. Cost discipline is real: £253m cumulative permanent non-content savings since 2019, with a further £20m guided for 2026. The screen’s quality/moat failures (ROE 13.5%, thin margins, Piotroski 3/9) reflect structural linear-TV pressure and phasing volatility in Studios, not balance-sheet distress: net debt/EBITDA was 1.0x at December 2025 (FY2025 results), liquidity exceeded £1.3bn across interim updates, and the Board targets investment-grade metrics.

The value hook has sharpened materially. On 6 July 2026 ITV announced the sale of M&E to Sky, unlocking c.£950m net cash return to shareholders (plus a £100m buyback announced at H1 2026), leaving a listed global Studios business with c.60% recurring revenue, 13–15% margin target, and an IP library of 100,000+ hours. For a value investor, the screen captures the pre-deal earnings and dividend yield; the Sky transaction adds a catalyst but also a pro-forma risk that current multiples may not translate cleanly to a smaller, more content-cycle-exposed remnant.

**Annual results**

*FY2024 (year ended 31 December 2024; RNS 6 March 2025)*  
Group total revenue fell 3% to £4,140m; external revenue fell 4% to £3,488m. Adjusted EBITA rose 11% to £542m (margin 16%); adjusted EPS rose 23% to 9.6p. Statutory profit before tax was £521m (prior year £193m), boosted by the BritBox International disposal. TAR grew 2%; digital revenue grew 12% to £556m. ITV Studios adjusted EBITA reached a record £299m (margin 14.7%); M&E adjusted EBITA rose 22% to £250m. Net debt was £431m; leverage 0.7x; profit-to-cash conversion 83%; free cash flow £325m. Ordinary dividend maintained at 5.0p (c.£190m). FY2024 also flagged a proposed Box Clever pension settlement with a c.£25m one-off cash payment into the ITV Pension Scheme in 2025.

*FY2025 (year ended 31 December 2025; RNS 5 March 2026)*  
Group total revenue was flat at £4,121m; external revenue rose 1% to £3,511m. Adjusted EBITA fell 1% to £534m (margin 15.2%); adjusted EPS fell 11% to 8.5p. Statutory profit before tax fell 35% to £338m (prior year included BritBox gain); statutory EPS 5.9p. TAR fell 5% against a strong 2024 Euros comp; digital revenue rose 10% to £614m (digital advertising +12%). ITV Studios total revenue rose 5% with external revenue +10%; adjusted EBITA £297m (margin 13.9%). M&E adjusted EBITA fell 6% to £234m. Net debt rose to £566m; leverage 1.0x; profit-to-cash conversion 65%; free cash flow £187m. Dividend maintained at 5.0p. Sky M&E discussions were ongoing; no certainty of a transaction was stated at year-end.

*Q1 2026 (three months to 31 March 2026; RNS 14 May 2026)*  
External revenue +1% to £766m; Studios external +8%; M&E -2% with digital revenue +12%. TAR -1.5% (better than guidance). Net debt £592m; liquidity £1,368m. Sky discussions continued.

*H1 2026 (six months to 30 June 2026; RNS 31 July 2026)*  
Total revenue +2% to £1,887m; external revenue +1% to £1,603m. Adjusted EBITA flat at £146m; adjusted EPS +22% to 2.2p. TAR +3%; ITVX streaming hours +27%; digital advertising +13%. ITV Studios adjusted EBITA -9% (H2-weighted slate); M&E adjusted EBITA +37%. Estimated H1 LHF (less healthy food) advertising impact c.£20m on TAR. Net debt £652m. ## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and operational**  
UK advertising remains the swing factor. Q3 2025 flagged Budget-related uncertainty and guided Q4 TAR down c.9%; H1 2026 noted macro headwinds with Q3 2026 TAR expected down c.5% despite a strong World Cup July. Linear viewing continues to erode; ITV’s share of commercial viewing is stable but not expanding (32.6% H1 2026). Less healthy food advertising restrictions (effective January 2026; ITV estimated c.£20m H1 2026 TAR impact) are a new structural drag.

**Transformation and M&A**  
The Sky M&E sale is transformative but uncertain. Filings state regulatory approval is required, CMA review has started, a Public Interest Intervention Notice is expected, and phase two could delay completion to H2 2027. Contingent consideration on the c.£950m return is explicitly excluded from that figure. Separation and transaction exceptionals are rising (FY2026 guidance c.£95m, up from £55m). Post-deal, ITV becomes predominantly Studios—a more content-market-cyclical, hit-driven earnings profile.

**Balance sheet, pension and governance**  
Leverage has risen to 1.0–1.1x from 0.7x in 2024; still moderate, but trending up pre-deal proceeds. FY2025 IR presentation cites net pension surplus £207m (31 Dec 2025); Box Clever settlement remains a legacy tail risk. Liberty Global’s secondary placing (October 2025) highlighted long-term shareholder overhang. Screen failures on Buffett Quality, Economic Moat and Piotroski F-Score (3/9: no improvement in ROA, leverage, margins or asset turnover) flag deteriorating or stagnant operational trends despite positive earnings.

**Regulatory and competitive**  
Channel 3 PSB obligations are contractually safeguarded to 2034 under the Sky deal terms in filings, but media merger scrutiny is inherently political. Competition from YouTube, global streamers and consolidated UK pay-TV/broadcaster combinations intensifies post-Sky. FY2024/FY2025 filings reference long-term viability modelling of downside scenarios; no going-concern qualification is stated in available extracts.
