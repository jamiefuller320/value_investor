# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-10T16:37:22.585535+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a **Strong Buy** on classic value metrics (P/E ~12, P/B ~1.6, ~6.8% dividend yield, moderate leverage), but the investment case is increasingly a **corporate-action and cash-return story** rather than a steady broadcast compounder. Management is selling Media & Entertainment (M&E) to Sky for a headline **£1.6bn**, targeting a **~£950m net cash return** to shareholders, leaving a predominantly **ITV Studios** content business. Filings show resilient adjusted earnings through FY 2025 and a solid H1 2026, yet statutory profits are volatile, free cash flow has weakened, and advertising remains cyclical. The central debate is whether the Sky proceeds, Studios growth, and capital returns justify the valuation — or whether regulatory delay, ad-market softness, and post-deal earnings dilution cap upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags ITV across **five families** — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with **15 of 22 models passing** and a **76% composite score** (86% sector-relative). That profile fits a value investor seeking income and rerating optionality at a low-teens multiple, without deep net-net or moat characteristics (Graham Defensive, Net-Net, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, and Piotroski all fail).

Business quality, on a filing basis, is better than the “legacy broadcaster” label implies. **Two-thirds of revenue** now comes from ITV Studios and digital M&E (FY 2025 RNS). ITV Studios grew external revenue **10%** in FY 2025; **ITVX** viewing rose **16%** and digital advertising **12%**. Cost discipline is real: **£253m** of permanent non-content savings since 2019, with a further **£20m** guided for FY 2026. The balance sheet is investment-grade in tone: FY 2025 **net debt £566m**, **1.0x** net debt/adjusted EBITDA, ordinary dividend held at **5.0p** (~**£190m**).

The value hook is therefore **double-barrelled**: (1) cheap cash-generative media asset on screen metrics, and (2) **£950m** prospective cash return plus an ongoing **£100m buyback** (H1 2026 interim) if the Sky deal completes. For a value investor, the screen’s income and multiple support is **directionally confirmed** by filing-backed resilience and explicit capital-return mechanics — but **not** by pristine cash conversion or wide-moat quality scores.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and deal execution.** The **Sky M&E sale** (announced 6 July 2026) is subject to **CMA** review; ITV expects a likely **Public Interest Intervention Notice** and notes completion could slip to **H2 2027** under a Phase 2 review. Until close, the **£950m** cash return and pro forma Studios-only earnings profile remain uncertain. Filings record **£36m** of transaction/separation costs in H1 2026 alone, with FY 2026 exceptional guidance raised to **~£95m**.

**Advertising cyclicality.** TAR fell **5%** in FY 2025 and remains macro-sensitive (Q3 2026 TAR guided **~−5%** despite World Cup benefit in July). **Less healthy food (LHF)** restrictions (from October 2025) created an estimated **£20m H1 2026 TAR headwind**.

**Cash conversion and leverage.** Profit-to-cash conversion fell to **65%** (FY 2025) and **63%** on a 12-month rolling basis at H1 2026. Net debt has risen sequentially (**£431m → £566m → £652m** at H1 2026). Covenant metrics remain within facility terms, but the trend warrants monitoring.

**Pension.** FY 2025 shows a **defined benefit pension surplus of £198m**; FY 2024 flagged a potential **~£25m** one-off Box Clever resolution payment. Remeasurement losses of **£17m** appeared in H1 2026 comprehensive income. Pension remains a long-tail risk despite surplus accounting.

**Quality screen failures.** **Piotroski F-Score 3/9**; ROE **13.5%** fails moat thresholds; current ratio **1.29** fails Graham Defensive. These align with a **value/catalyst** rather than **quality compounder** classification.

**Governance / overhang.** October 2025 **secondary placing** (Liberty Global stake reduction) and ongoing director changes appear in the RNS index without full body text — a potential sentiment overhang not captured by quantitative screens.

**Going concern.** H1 2026 interim review reports **no material going-concern uncertainties** identified by auditors on a going-concern basis; this is comfort on liquidity, not a guarantee on deal timing or ad markets.
