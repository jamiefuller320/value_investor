# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:29:06.370624+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a strong buy on classic value metrics—P/E ~12x, P/B ~1.5x, dividend yield ~6.8%—against a business that has materially pivoted toward ITV Studios and digital (ITVX), with roughly two-thirds of revenue now from non-linear sources per FY2025 results. The valuation hook is a cash-generative, investment-grade balance sheet (net debt/EBITDA ~1.0x at year-end 2025) supporting a maintained 5.0p ordinary dividend (~£190m), while the share trades below its 200-day moving average with RSI ~36. The central debate is whether structural decline in UK linear advertising is adequately offset by Studios growth and ITVX monetisation—or whether the pending sale of the Media & Entertainment (M&E) business to Sky (announced July 2026; filing body not available in source pack) re-rates the remnant Studios entity faster than it removes a high-margin cash engine. Pending regulatory clearance, deal terms, and post-transaction capital allocation leave the earnings path less clean than the screen implies.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags ITV across five model families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with 14 of 22 models passing, composite score 83%, and full data coverage (20/20 metrics). That alignment is grounded in observable fundamentals from primary filings: adjusted ROE ~12% (screen), modest leverage (reported net debt/EBITDA 0.7x at FY2024, 1.0x at FY2025), and a stated commitment to at least 5.0p annual dividend through the transition. FY2024 (6 March 2025 RNS) delivered record Studios profits and 11% group adjusted EBITA growth to £542m; FY2025 (5 March 2026 RNS) showed resilience—external revenue +1% to £3,511m and adjusted EBITA broadly flat at £534m—despite a 5% decline in total advertising revenue (TAR) against a strong 2024 Euros comparator.

For a value investor, the case rests on three pillars visible in filings rather than narrative alone. First, asset-backed cheapness: the screen’s P/B ~1.5x sits against tangible book value that remains thin (£267m at 31 December 2025 per Yahoo balance sheet—used here only because the RNS extracts do not restate this line—but goodwill-heavy intangibles are typical for a content business and partly reflect Studios IP). Second, income: the maintained 5.0p dividend (~6.8% yield on screen inputs) has been funded through strong cash conversion historically (83% in FY2024; 65% in FY2025 as working capital absorbed cash). Third, optionality: management has been in discussions with Sky over an M&E disposal since November 2025, and July 2026 news cites a “transformative” deal with ~£950m planned shareholder returns—potentially crystallising value trapped in a conglomerate discount.

Business quality is better than legacy “declining broadcaster” labels suggest. ITV Studios external revenue grew 10% in FY2025; ITVX recouped cumulative investment four years early; digital advertising rose 12% in FY2025 to £540m. Cost discipline is tangible—£253m permanent savings since 2019, with a further £20m guided for 2026. The screen’s timing overlay (accumulate; RSI ~36; ~8% below 200-day SMA) is consistent with accumulating into a cyclically depressed advertising patch rather than chasing momentum.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Advertising cyclicality and structural shift.** Filings repeatedly cite uncertainty in the UK economic outlook and industry-wide Q4 2025 advertising caution. TAR fell 5% in FY2025; linear viewing declined 7% in H1 2025 (BARB, H1 RNS). The screen’s earnings-quality pass coexists with a failed Piotroski F-Score (3/9)—ROA not improving, OCF below net income, leverage not declining—signalling deteriorating quality trends not captured by headline multiples.

**Pending Sky M&E transaction.** Since November 2025, ITV has flagged discussions with “no certainty” a transaction will complete (FY2025 RNS). Regulatory, political, and pricing risks were widely discussed in press coverage (“Three big problems with ITV’s talks to sell television business to Sky: price, politics and regulation”, The Guardian, 7 November 2025). A blocked or renegotiated deal could remove a catalyst and leave investors with the existing ad-exposed M&E unit.

**Shareholder overhang.** Liberty Global completed a secondary placing of ~193m shares (~5% of issued capital) for ~£135m gross on 22 October 2025, halving its stake to ~5% with a 60-day lock-up—near-term supply pressure despite strategic rationale.

**Regulatory.** Less-healthy food (LHF) advertising restrictions from October 2025 (FY2024 RNS) may constrain M&E revenue. Any Sky combination would face UK competition scrutiny. Media Act 2024 implementation remains ongoing.

**Governance and operational.** Duty of Care is flagged as a principal risk in FY2024 reporting. Exceptional costs are elevated—£107m FY2025 (including £38m corporate transaction-related expenses) with ~£55m guided for 2026—reflecting restructuring and deal activity rather than recurring operations.

**Pension and covenants.** Box Clever resolution may require cash funding. Filings reference downside scenario modelling for longer-term viability (FY2024) but do not surface going-concern qualification—the auditors issued unqualified reports on statutory accounts referenced in RNS extracts. Specific covenant headroom is not disclosed in available bodies.

**Post-deal remnant risk.** If M&E is sold, ITV becomes predominantly Studios—a higher-growth but more competitive, production-cycle-volatile business with margin guidance at the lower end of 13–15% for 2026.
