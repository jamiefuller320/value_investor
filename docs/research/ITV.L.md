# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-10T20:41:00.422113+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a strong buy on cheapness (P/E ~12, P/B ~1.6), a ~6.8% dividend yield, and resilient adjusted profitability through its More Than TV transformation, with two-thirds of revenue now from ITV Studios and digital M&E. The central valuation hook is not the legacy broadcaster multiple but the July 2026 agreement to sell Media & Entertainment to Sky for £1.6bn, targeting ~£950m net cash return to shareholders plus a studios-only remnant with above-market growth ambitions. Filings show FY2025 adjusted EBITA of £534m and a maintained 5.0p ordinary dividend (~£190m), but canonical free cash flow fell to £187m (FY2025 RNS) versus £325m in FY2024, and the screen flags a material FCF basis mismatch that weakens dividend-coverage confidence. The key debate is whether headline yield and low multiples adequately compensate for linear advertising cyclicality, regulatory uncertainty over the Sky deal (CMA review, possible Phase 2 completion in H2 2027), and pro-forma earnings power once broadcast cash flows exit the group.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen passes 14 of 22 models across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families (composite 77%, sector-relative 86%), consistent with a contrarian UK media name trading below book on mid-teens earnings and a high cash yield. Business quality is better than the “declining broadcaster” label implies: FY2024 adjusted EBITA rose 11% to £542m on record Studios profits and ITVX digital revenue growth (+12% to £556m); FY2025 held group adjusted EBITA broadly flat at £534m despite TAR down 5%, with ITV Studios external revenue up 10% and digital revenue up 10% to £614m (all per FY2025 full-year RNS, 5 March 2026).

For a value investor, the asymmetric case is capital return plus simplification. H1 2026 results (31 July 2026) confirm the Sky transaction unlocks ~£950m net cash return (excluding contingent consideration), a £100m buyback commenced ahead of completion, and a standalone ITV Studios positioned for investment-grade metrics. The screen’s Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, Low P/E + High Yield, Magic Formula, and Financial Health passes align with a cash-generative, de-leveraging balance sheet (net debt £566m at FY2025 year-end, 1.0x leverage) and a dividend policy committing to at least 5.0p per share. Timing is neutral (RSI ~37, below 200-day MA), which suits accumulation rather than momentum chasing.

Caveats temper enthusiasm: FCF Yield fails on the screen; Piotroski F-Score is 3/9; Buffett Quality and Economic Moat fail on ROE (~13.5%) and margins. Deep research supports the cheapness/dividend pillar but not uncritical acceptance of yield sustainability on filing-aligned FCF alone.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and transaction**  
The Sky M&E sale (£1.6bn, per market reporting cited in news manifest) is subject to CMA review and likely Public Interest Intervention Notice; H1 2026 RNS states Phase 2 review could delay completion to H2 2027. Until close, value realization and capital return timing remain uncertain. Consent solicitations on 2032 notes and rating-agency substitutions (July–August 2026 RNS headlines) indicate active balance-sheet management around the deal.

**Cyclical advertising**  
Filings repeatedly cite macro uncertainty and TAR sensitivity (FY2025 TAR -5%; H1 2026 guides Q3 2026 TAR ~-5% despite World Cup boost in July). Linear viewing share remains under structural pressure (share of commercial viewing 32.6% H1 2026 vs 33% target).

**FCF and dividend**  
FY2025 filing FCF £187m covers the ~£190m ordinary dividend only narrowly; screen FCF/dividend gross coverage ~36% on canonical basis. Company-adjusted FCF (£325m per screen) is higher but not audited in these extracts—yield-dependent models pass only after FCF basis overlay adjustment.

**Pension and legacy liabilities**  
H1 2026 interim accounts show defined benefit pension surplus £188m but also a £24m defined benefit deficit elsewhere; Box Clever dispute resolution could require material one-off funding (FY2024 RNS: ~£25m if agreed). Remeasurement losses on schemes (£17m) in period.

**Operational restructuring**  
Soap and daytime scheduling changes reduce internal Studios revenue by ~£80m from 2026 (FY2025 CEO statement). Exceptional items rising: ~£95m guided for FY2026 including Sky transaction and separation costs.

**Governance / technical**  
October 2025 secondary placing and Liberty Global stake reduction (news) added supply overhang. Piotroski 3/9 reflects deteriorating working-capital and margin trends on a trailing basis.

**Going concern**  
H1 2026 interim report: directors adopt going concern; PwC review found no material going-concern uncertainties inappropriately undisclosed. Covenant net debt/EBITDA 1.0x with covenant liquidity ~£1.3bn—comfortable but not immaterial at current leverage.
