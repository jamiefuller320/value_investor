# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 2 · Updated 2026-08-16T07:19:32.009843+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV is a UK-listed media group transitioning from a vertically integrated broadcaster-producer into a global content studio, with the proposed sale of Media & Entertainment (M&E) to Sky representing the defining strategic event. The quantitative screen rates the name a **strong buy** (14/22 models; composite 78%), anchored by low multiples (P/E 11.9, P/B 1.6), a ~7% dividend yield, and resilient adjusted earnings through a difficult linear advertising cycle. The valuation hook is twofold: headline cheapness on earnings and yield, plus a potential **~£950 million** net cash return to shareholders on completion of the Sky transaction (announced July 2026), leaving a listed ITV Studios business. The central debate is whether the market is correctly pricing (a) post-deal residual earnings power and capital returns, (b) UK advertising cyclicality and regulatory clearance risk on the Sky merger, and (c) whether reported free cash flow truly covers the dividend once working-capital swings and deal costs are included.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, ITV offers a recognisable Graham/Lynch profile: double-digit earnings yield, sub-3× P/B, a maintained **5.0p** ordinary dividend (~7% yield on screening inputs), and debt/equity of ~52%. The screen passes five metric families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with models including Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, Low P/E + High Yield, Lynch PEG, Magic Formula, and Acquirer's Multiple.

Business quality is better than the share-price narrative suggests. Filings show successful execution of the “More Than TV” strategy: **two-thirds of revenue** now from ITV Studios and digital M&E (FY 2025 full-year results, March 2026); ITVX digital advertising up **12%** in FY 2025; ITV Studios external revenue up **10%**. Cost discipline has delivered **£253m** of permanent savings since 2019. Management has returned **>£1.6bn** to shareholders since 2018 via dividends and buybacks.

The screen signal is not unqualified. **FCF Yield** fails because filing-aligned FCF (**£148m** / $148m on the screen) diverges from company-adjusted FCF (**£325m** per screen overlay). Statutory FY 2025 free cash flow in the annual RNS was **£187m**—enough to notionally cover the ~**£190m** dividend but with little headroom. Piotroski F-Score is **3/9** (weak trend quality: leverage not declining, margins not improving). Buffett Quality and Economic Moat fail on ROE (~13.5%) and margin thresholds. The investment case therefore rests on **adjusted earnings resilience + yield + optionality from the Sky deal**, not on pristine cash-conversion trends.
