# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 3 · Updated 2026-09-02T11:12:53.695908+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a **strong buy** on quantitative value metrics (15/22 models passing; composite score 82%; P/E 11.6, P/B 1.5, dividend yield 7.1%), underpinned by resilient cash generation, a fixed 5.0p ordinary dividend (~£190m annually), and a pending **£950m** shareholder return from the agreed sale of Media & Entertainment (M&E) to Sky for up to **£1.6bn**. The investment case rests on whether today’s valuation adequately prices a business transitioning from integrated broadcaster-producer to a **Studios-centric pure play**, with ITVX and linear broadcast economics largely exiting at completion (expected H2 2027, subject to CMA/regulatory clearance). The central debate is valuation methodology: screens reflect consolidated pre-deal earnings and yield, while post-deal ITV will be smaller, less diversified, and more exposed to global content cycles—offset by capital return, a **£2.1bn** minimum content-supply commitment from Sky/M&E (2028–2032), and management’s target of investment-grade leverage at ~1.5x net debt/EBITDA.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen aligns with a credible value setup. ITV passes five model families—**cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk**—with a sector-relative composite of 86% and full data quality (20/20 metrics). Headline multiples are attractive for a business generating adjusted EBITA margins above 15% at group level historically, with debt/equity of 52% and net leverage of 1.0x at FY 2025 (filing). Dividend-oriented screens (High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Low P/E + High Yield) and cash-yield models (FCF Yield, Earnings Yield, Acquirer’s Multiple) all pass, consistent with a contrarian UK media name trading below long-run earnings power.

Business quality supports the screen, with caveats. **ITV Studios** is a scaled global producer (10% external revenue growth in FY 2025; adjusted EBITA £297m at 13.9% margin) benefiting from streaming-platform demand and high-margin catalogue/digital distribution (Zoo 55). **M&E** remains the UK’s largest commercial broadcaster and ad-funded streamer: digital revenue reached **£614m** (+10%) in FY 2025, ITVX streaming hours +16%, and cumulative ITVX investment was recouped four years ahead of plan. Management has delivered **£253m** of permanent non-content savings since 2019. Two-thirds of revenue now comes from Studios and digital M&E—the strategic pivot the screen partially captures via earnings resilience and dividend continuity.

What the screen underweights is **event risk and pro-forma economics**. The Sky transaction unlocks ~25p/share of cash return and simplifies the equity story, but completion is 12–18 months away, may require Phase 2 CMA review, and leaves a less diversified earnings base. Failed screens (Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Piotroski F-Score 3/9) flag middling ROE (13.5%), thin broadcast margins, and deteriorating year-on-year financial momentum indicators—consistent with a cyclical advertising business, not a wide-moat compounder. For a value investor, the strong-buy signal is best read as **“cheap consolidated cash flow plus catalyst,”** not “quality compounder at any price.”
