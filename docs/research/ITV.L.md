# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-09T05:58:24.413347+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a strong buy on composite value metrics (14/22 models, 75% composite, 6.8% dividend yield, P/E 12.2), underpinned by a resilient balance sheet, sustained ordinary dividends of 5.0p, and a credible “More than TV” pivot toward ITV Studios and ITVX digital revenues. Primary filings show adjusted EBITA holding broadly flat in FY2025 (£534m vs £542m in FY2024) despite a 5% decline in total advertising revenue, with digital revenue reaching £614m (+10%) and ITV Studios external revenue growing 10%. The central debate is no longer linear-TV decline alone but the proposed £1.6bn sale of Media & Entertainment (M&E) to Sky — a transformative deal promising ~£950m net cash return to shareholders but subject to CMA/public-interest scrutiny and analyst scepticism on post-deal earnings power. At ~£0.70–0.75, the stock embeds a cyclical UK ad recovery and deal completion; deep research broadly supports the screen’s value case but flags rising net debt, weakening profit-to-cash conversion, and structural uncertainty that cap conviction.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen identifies ITV as cheap on multiple classic value dimensions: earnings yield, FCF yield, high dividend yield (6.8%), Lynch/Neff PEG-style growth-at-a-reasonable-price signals, and acquirer’s-multiple-style EV/EBIT metrics, with debt/equity at 52% and full data quality (20/20 metrics). Five of five factor families pass — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — giving sector-relative composite of 86%. For a value investor, the hook is a cash-generative UK media incumbent trading below book (P/B 1.6) with a visible capital-return framework (ordinary dividend maintained at 5.0p since 2023; £235m buyback completed April 2025; £100m buyback announced July 2026 ahead of the Sky proceeds).

Business quality, while not meeting Buffett/moat screens (ROE 13.5% vs 18% hurdle; Piotroski F-Score 3/9), is improving strategically. FY2024 RNS (6 March 2025) showed group adjusted EBITA up 11% to £542m and adjusted EPS up 23% to 9.6p, with ITVX recouping cumulative investment four years early. FY2025 RNS (5 March 2026) confirmed two-thirds of revenue now from Studios and digital M&E, with ITVX viewing +16% and digital ad revenue +12%. ITV Studios’ adjusted EBITA margin of 13.9% sits within the 13–15% target range despite mix headwinds. The screen’s filing-aligned FCF of $148m (FY2025) is lower than the TTM screen figure ($212m), reflecting working-capital pressure — a nuance the action note already flags.

The strong-buy case rests on: (1) sum-of-the-parts optionality if Sky closes (~£950m net cash return plus a pure-play global Studios asset); (2) sustained income (5.0p ordinary dividend, ~£190m/year); (3) digital offset to linear ad decline (digital revenue £614m, +10% FY2025); and (4) management cost discipline (£45m permanent non-content savings guided for 2025, expanded further in Q3 trading update). Timing is neutral (RSI 41, below 200-day MA), which suits a patient accumulator rather than a momentum buyer.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and deal execution:** The Sky M&E sale (~£1.6bn enterprise value, ~£950m net cash return) is the dominant risk. H1 2026 RNS states CMA review is underway, a Public Interest Intervention Notice is expected, and Phase 2 could delay completion into 2027. The Guardian (7 November 2025) flagged price, politics, and regulation as obstacles. Post-close, ITV becomes predominantly ITV Studios — a different risk profile (content commissioning cycles, streaming platform concentration at 28–29% of Studios revenue) without M&E’s UK ad cash engine. Contingent consideration on the Sky deal is explicitly excluded from the £950m figure.

**Cyclical advertising:** Q3 2025 trading update cited UK macro softness ahead of the Autumn Budget, guiding Q4 TAR −9% and full-year TAR −6%. Linear viewing share continues to erode (commercial viewing 31.8% Q1 2026 vs 34.0% Q1 2025). Less-healthy-food advertising restrictions from October 2025 (flagged in FY2024 RNS) add regulatory headwind.

**Balance sheet and cash:** Net debt rose £221m from FY2024 to H1 2026 despite buybacks and dividends. FY2025 FCF fell to £187m (−42% vs £325m). RCF covenants (leverage <3.5x) provide headroom at 1.0x but would tighten if ad downturn coincided with deal delay. Piotroski F-Score 3/9 (screen) reflects deteriorating ROA, leverage, margins, and asset turnover trends.

**Pension and governance:** Defined benefit surplus (£198m FY2025) is positive, and Box Clever transfer (October 2025) removed a long-running dispute; a £6m PPF payment and provision release are noted in FY2025 accounts. Liberty Global halved its stake via secondary placing (22 October 2025, Financial Times), pressuring the share price and signalling reduced strategic support from a historically influential holder.

**Screen failures:** Graham Defensive (current ratio 1.29 vs 2.0 hurdle), deep value/net-net, Buffett quality, and economic moat screens all fail — confirming this is a relative-value/catalyst name, not a fortress compounder.

**Going concern:** FY2025 accounts confirm going concern with liquidity under severe-but-plausible downside scenarios; no auditor emphasis of matter cited in the RNS extract.
