# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-19T10:51:25.469341+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a strong buy on cheapness, dividend yield, and resilient adjusted earnings, with the share price below its 200-day moving average and timing flagged as accumulate. The investment case rests on a transformed group—two-thirds of revenue from ITV Studios and digital M&E—plus a proposed sale of the broadcast/streaming arm to Sky that management says could unlock roughly £950 million of net cash to shareholders, leaving a global content studio as the remaining listed entity. Valuation looks optically attractive (P/E ~11.9, yield ~7.0%, P/B ~1.6), but the central debate is whether headline metrics understate structural risk: cyclical UK advertising, regulatory uncertainty on the Sky transaction, and material divergence between statutory earnings, adjusted free cash flow definitions, and the screen’s FCF basis overlay.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen passes 14 of 22 models across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families (composite 78%, sector-relative 88%), anchored by low P/E, high dividend yield, moderate leverage (debt/equity ~52%), and double-digit adjusted EPS growth in the screen inputs. That aligns with a business that has genuinely shifted mix: FY 2025 group external revenue rose 1% despite total advertising revenue (TAR) down 5%, with ITV Studios external revenue up 10% and digital revenues up 10% to £614 million, while group adjusted EBITA fell only 1% to £534 million on tight cost control (£253 million cumulative permanent savings since 2019). ITVX metrics are improving (streaming hours +16%, digital advertising +12% in FY 2025; H1 2026 streaming hours +27%, digital advertising +13%), supporting the screen’s quality and dividend passes even as moat-style models fail (Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Piotroski F-Score 3/9). For a value investor, the hook is a cash-generative, dividend-paying media name trading below historical earnings power, with a potential catalyst in the Sky M&E disposal and early £100 million buyback—but the screen’s FCF Yield failure and flagged FCF basis mismatch (filing-aligned ~£148m vs company-adjusted ~£325m in FY 2024 and ~£187m company-reported FCF in FY 2025) mean the yield case needs filing-level reconciliation, not screen metrics alone.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory / transaction:** The Sky M&E sale (~£1.6 billion headline coverage in news; filing cites ~£950 million net cash return) is subject to CMA review, expected Public Interest Intervention Notice, and possible Phase 2—management indicates completion could slip to **H2 2027**. Failure, delay, or unfavourable remedies would remove the primary re-rating catalyst and leave a cyclical ad-exposed broadcaster.

**Cyclical advertising:** TAR remains the earnings swing factor (down 5% FY 2025; H1 2026 +3% but Q3 2026 guided down ~5% on macro headwinds). Q3 2025 flagged widespread advertiser caution ahead of the Budget; less-healthy-food (LHF) regulation estimated at **~£20 million H1 2026 TAR impact**.

**Competitive / structural:** Linear viewing share under pressure (share of commercial viewing 31.7% FY 2025 vs 32.2% prior year); intense competition from global streamers and YouTube. Post-Sky, the listed stub is almost entirely ITV Studios—exposed to streaming commissioning cycles and US strike-type disruptions (2024 demonstrated ~£80 million revenue phasing impact).

**Leverage / liquidity:** Net debt rose to £652 million at H1 2026 (from £431 million at FY 2024); covenant net debt/adjusted EBITDA 1.0x per interim filing. Going-concern language is clean—PwC interim review (31 July 2026) reported **no material going-concern uncertainties**—but working-capital outflows drove weaker cash conversion (65% FY 2025; 63% rolling at H1 2026).

**Pension:** Defined benefit **surplus** £188–198 million (H1/FY 2025 filings)—a positive, though FY 2024 flagged a potential **~£25 million** Box Clever scheme payment on proposed settlement; ongoing remeasurement volatility (H1 2026 remeasurement loss £17 million).

**Governance / overhang:** Liberty Global secondary placing (October 2025) added supply; multiple directorate changes in the period. Adjusted vs statutory earnings gap widened (exceptionals £107 million FY 2025; £95 million guided FY 2026 including Sky separation costs).

**Accounting / screen quality:** Piotroski F-Score 3/9; screen fails FCF Yield, Graham Defensive (current ratio <2), and moat models—consistent with thin margins and inconsistent cash conversion. Do not treat 7% dividend yield as fully covered without reconciling company-adjusted FCF (~£187m FY 2025 filing) against dividend ~£190m and buyback commitments.
