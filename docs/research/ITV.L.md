# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-03T20:32:08.115886+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a strong buy on cheapness, dividend yield (~6.7%), earnings/FCF yield, and moderate leverage, with neutral technical timing (RSI ~39, below the 200-day moving average). The investment case rests on a cash-generative UK media group that has pivoted toward ITV Studios and digital M&E, now amplified by the announced sale of the broadcast/streaming arm to Sky for ~£1.6bn and a planned ~£950m net cash return to shareholders. Valuation looks compelling on mid-teens P/E and a maintained 5.0p ordinary dividend (~£190m annually), but the central debate is whether post-transaction ITV Studios alone justifies the current price, and whether CMA/public-interest scrutiny delays or reshapes the Sky deal. Deep research broadly supports the screen on valuation and capital-return optionality, while flagging structural linear-ad decline, weak quality momentum (Piotroski 3/9), and execution/regulatory risk around the break-up.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with 20/20 data completeness. Key metrics include P/E 12.2, P/B 1.5, dividend yield 6.7%, ROE 13.5%, and debt/equity 52%. Models such as Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Magic Formula, and High Dividend Yield pass; Graham Defensive, Economic Moat, Buffett Quality, and Piotroski F-Score fail, reflecting sub-moat returns (ROE below 18%), thin margins versus screen thresholds, and deteriorating balance-sheet momentum (F-Score 3/9).

For a value investor, the business quality case is stronger than the pure quality screens suggest. Filings describe a vertically integrated producer-broadcaster executing the “More Than TV” strategy: ITV Studios is a scaled global content business (59% international revenue, 28% from streaming platforms in FY2025), while ITVX has driven double-digit digital viewing and advertising growth and recouped cumulative investment ahead of plan. Cost discipline is material—£253m permanent savings since 2019—with two-thirds of group revenue now from Studios and digital M&E. The Sky transaction crystallises option value in the legacy broadcast asset, leaving a focused Studios franchise with stated ambitions for above-market organic growth at 13–15% EBITA margins, investment-grade balance sheet, and continued shareholder returns (5.0p dividend, £100m buyback announced at H1 2026). The screen’s strong-buy signal aligns with a de-rated UK media name offering yield plus a hard catalyst, albeit not a deep net-net or moat compounder.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory / transaction:** The Sky M&E sale (announced 6 Jul 2026) is subject to CMA review and expected Public Interest Intervention Notice. Filings warn a Phase 2 review could push completion to H2 2027. Until close, ITV remains a combined entity with rising transaction/separation exceptional costs (FY2026 guidance ~£95m vs £55m previously, including ~£36m Sky-related costs in H1 2026).

**Cyclical advertising:** TAR fell 5% in FY2025 and remains macro-sensitive; Q3 2025 flagged Budget-related Q4 caution. H1 2026 benefited from the Men’s Football World Cup; Q3 2026 TAR is guided down ~5% despite strong July.

**Structural / regulatory revenue headwinds:** Less healthy food (LHF) advertising restrictions (from Oct 2025) estimated at ~£20m TAR impact in H1 2026. Linear viewing decline persists; SDN multiplex revenues falling on contract renewals (disclosed in H1 2025 filing).

**Quality / screen failures:** Piotroski F-Score 3/9 (no improvement in ROA, leverage, margins, or asset turnover). Current ratio 1.29 fails Graham Defensive. ROE 13.5% and margins sit below moat/quality screen hurdles—consistent with a capital-intensive, cyclical media model rather than a wide-moat compounder.

**Pension / contingencies:** H1 2026 shows a defined benefit pension surplus of £188m (31 Dec 2025: £198m) but remeasurement losses of £17m in the period. FY2024 flagged a potential ~£25m Box Clever pension settlement payment. Covenant calculations include an “other pension asset” of £33m—pension remains a balance-sheet sensitivity if discount rates or longevity assumptions move.

**Liquidity / covenants:** Going concern: PwC’s H1 2026 review report states no material going-concern uncertainties identified. Net debt £652m at 30 Jun 2026 with £1.1bn+ committed undrawn facilities; covenant net debt/adj. EBITDA 1.0x. Not a distress case, but leverage has risen from 0.7x at end-2024.

**Governance / overhang:** Liberty Global secondary placing (~£135m, Oct 2025) pressured the share price; large shareholder selling may recur.

**Post-deal risk:** Pro-forma ITV Studios-only earnings, corporate cost allocation, and reinvestment of ~£950m proceeds are not fully modelled in available filings; standalone value depends on deal terms, timing, and Studios growth delivery.
