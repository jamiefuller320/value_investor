# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 3 · Updated 2026-09-13T08:06:10.213068+00:00 · Mode: gap_fill_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a strong buy on cheapness, dividend yield, and broad value-model pass rates, against a business in the middle of a defining strategic shift: the agreed sale of Media & Entertainment (M&E) to Sky for up to £1.6bn, with a planned ~£950m cash return to shareholders and a post-completion pure-play ITV Studios listing. The valuation hook is a low headline multiple and a ~7% dividend yield on a name that has rebuilt digital momentum (ITVX) while growing Studios exposure. The central debate is whether today’s screen metrics—built largely on the integrated group—still describe the residual equity story after regulatory clearance, separation costs, pension mitigation, and cyclical UK advertising. Filing-backed cash generation in FY2025 was materially below the prior year, and statutory free cash flow sits closer to one times dividend cover than management-adjusted measures imply.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen (15/22 models passing, composite 79%, all five families: cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk) flags ITV as unusually cheap for a FTSE communications name: P/E ~11.7, P/B ~1.5, dividend yield ~7.2%, debt/equity ~52%, with Lynch PEG ~0.51 on ~23% earnings growth in the screen inputs. That aligns with a classic value setup: market scepticism toward linear TV, plus event risk around the Sky transaction, has compressed the rating despite resilient adjusted profits and an explicit capital-return pathway.

Business quality is mixed relative to the screen. Filings show strategic execution under “More than TV”: FY2024 group adjusted EBITA rose 11% to £542m; FY2025 adjusted EBITA was resilient at £534m (down 1%) despite a 5% decline in total advertising revenue (TAR), with Studios external revenue +10% and digital revenue +10%. ITVX viewing and digital ad growth remain positive operational signals. However, the screen fails high-bar quality checks (Piotroski F-Score 3/9; Buffett/moat models on ROE and margins), and overlays already flag dividend sustainability and free-cash-flow definition divergence (statutory OCF−CapEx cover ~1.0× vs management “cash generated” ~1.53×). For a value investor, the strong-buy signal is best read as “mispriced integrated cash flows plus optional upside from monetisation,” not as proof of durable moat quality—especially as the listed entity is poised to become Studios-centric with regulatory and execution risk attached.
