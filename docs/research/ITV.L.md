# ITV plc (ITV.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-23T06:53:28.775533+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a strong buy on cheapness, dividend yield, and balance-sheet metrics (P/E ~12, yield ~7%, debt/equity ~52%), but the investment case is now dominated by the proposed £1.6bn sale of Media & Entertainment (M&E) to Sky and a planned ~£950m net cash return to shareholders. Filings show a business in transition: FY2025 adjusted EBITA was resilient at £534m (-1% y/y) despite a 5% decline in total advertising revenue (TAR), with ITV Studios and digital offsetting linear weakness; statutory earnings fell sharply as the prior year included the BritBox International disposal gain. The central debate is whether headline valuation and the Sky catalyst outweigh cyclical UK advertising exposure, FCF definition uncertainty (filing FCF £187m vs company-adjusted £325m), and a protracted CMA/regulatory path that may delay completion until H2 2027.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags ITV across five factor families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with 14 of 22 models passing and a composite score of 78% (88% sector-relative). That aligns with a classic value setup: low double-digit P/E, sub-2x P/B, and a ~7% dividend yield supported by a decade-long 5p ordinary dividend policy (~£190m p.a.). Business quality is better than the “legacy broadcaster” label implies: two-thirds of revenue now comes from ITV Studios and digital M&E; ITVX viewing rose 16% in FY2025 with digital ad revenue up 12%; Studios external revenue grew 10%. Management has delivered £253m cumulative permanent cost savings since 2019, net debt/EBITDA leverage remains ~1.0x, and liquidity exceeded £1.3bn at year-end 2025 — consistent with the screen’s risk-family pass. For a value investor, the hook is a cash-rich catalyst (Sky sale + buybacks/dividends) on a stock trading below book and with an earnings yield above 8%, while the underlying Studios franchise offers global IP, streaming-platform diversification (28% of Studios revenue), and industry-leading margins (13–15% target range). The screen’s FCF-basis overlay is material: filing-aligned FCF of £148m (Yahoo) understates management’s adjusted £325m, which partly explains why FCF Yield failed despite dividend models passing — verify-before-trade on post-deal, post-M&E cash generation.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory / M&A.** The Sky M&E sale (~£1.6bn headline; ~£950m net cash return excl. contingent consideration) requires CMA approval, with a likely Public Interest Intervention Notice and possible Phase 2 review pushing completion to H2 2027 (H1 2026 interim). Until close, value realisation and capital return timing remain uncertain; separation costs are rising (exceptionals guided to ~£95m in 2026 vs £55m previously).

**Cyclical advertising.** TAR remains the swing factor for the pre-deal group: -5% in FY2025, Q4 2025 down ~6% full-year on soft UK demand, and H1 2026 estimated £20m TAR hit from less-healthy-food (LHF) advertising restrictions (October 2025). Q3 2026 TAR guided down ~5% despite strong July World Cup performance.

**FCF and dividend sustainability.** FY2025 FCF of £187m covers the ~£190m ordinary dividend only narrowly; screen net FCF/dividend coverage is ~0.79x on filing basis. Profit-to-cash conversion fell to 65% (FY2025) and 63% on a rolling 12-month basis (H1 2026), driven by working capital. The screen explicitly flags FCF basis mismatch — yield-dependent models pass on adjusted definitions, not filing OCF less capex.

**Earnings quality.** Piotroski F-Score 3/9 (screen): no improvement in ROA, leverage, current ratio, or margins. Statutory EPS (5.9p) materially below adjusted EPS (8.5p) due to £107m operating exceptionals in FY2025 (restructuring, transaction fees). Studios H1 2026 margin compressed to 10.6% on revenue phasing.

**Competitive / structural.** Linear viewing share continues to erode (commercial viewing 31.7–32.6%); ITVX growth is real but partly offsets rather than fully replaces broadcast ad declines. Post-Sky, the listed entity becomes predominantly ITV Studios — a different cyclicality (content commissioning, strike risk, streaming budget cycles) that screens may not fully capture.

**Governance / overhang.** John Malone’s Liberty Global halved its stake (October 2025, per news); secondary placing followed. Multiple directorate changes in 2025–2026.

**Filing language.** Going concern is affirmed under severe-but-plausible downside scenarios; no auditor emphasis of matter cited in preliminary extracts. Forward-looking statements explicitly flag liquidity events, advertising market changes, and regulatory shifts as principal uncertainties.
