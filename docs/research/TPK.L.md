# Travis Perkins plc (TPK.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T06:37:52.761182+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Travis Perkins plc is the UK's largest building materials distributor, trading at a material discount to book (P/B 0.8x on the quantitative screen) after a multi-year earnings reset driven by weak UK construction volumes, Merchanting share loss, and heavy non-cash impairments. Filings show a stabilising revenue base (FY2025 like-for-like +0.3%) and sharply improved balance-sheet quality — net cash before leases of £55m at H1 2026 and leverage back within the 1.5–2.0x target at 1.9x — while adjusted operating profit remains well below the 2022–23 peak. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy on cheapness, dividend continuity, and financial health (FCF yield ~21%, five models passed), but flags timing to wait given RSI ~71. The central debate is whether operational turnaround under CEO Gavin Slark and Toolstation UK momentum can restore earnings power before cyclical recovery, or whether competitive Merchanting margins and Benelux drag cap the rerating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, TPK offers a classic distressed-quality setup: dominant UK footprint, substantial freehold property backing, and cash generation that has decoupled from reported losses. The screen passes FCF Yield, Composite Value, Financial Health, Earnings Quality, and Dividend Growth — three of four factor families (cheapness, dividend, risk) — with composite score 72% versus sector-relative 55%. Primary filings support the cash and balance-sheet pillars: FY2025 free cash flow of £205m (RNS, 17 March 2026) and net debt/ adjusted EBITDA reduced from 2.5x (FY2024) to 2.1x (FY2025) and 1.9x (H1 2026), with £861m liquidity headroom at June 2026. Statutory ROE is negative (-9.1% on the screen) and adjusted EPS has fallen from 36.6p (FY2024) to 30.8p (FY2025), so the value case rests on asset backing and cash conversion rather than near-term earnings momentum. Toolstation UK is the quality anchor (FY2025 adjusted operating profit +29% to £44m; H1 2026 UK margin expansion), while Merchanting turnaround is early but directionally positive (General Merchant gross-margin rebuild in H1 2026). The screen's "buy — wait for pullback" timing note is consistent with recent share strength after H1 2026 results and overbought technicals; accumulation on weakness aligns signal with risk/reward.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and competitive:** Filings repeatedly cite subdued UK construction, price/margin pressure from over-capacity, and delayed recovery despite government housing ambitions. H1 2026 notes geopolitical-driven building-materials inflation as hard to forecast. Merchanting adjusted margin fell from 3.9% (FY2024) to 3.3% (FY2025).

**Accounting / impairments:** £222m adjusting items in FY2025 and £139m in FY2024 — branch, CCF goodwill, and Toolstation Benelux write-downs. Repeated non-cash charges raise questions on prior capital allocation and asset carrying values; branches are stated to remain cash-positive under cautious assumptions.

**International / strategic:** Toolstation Benelux remains loss-making (£11m FY2025; £7m H1 2026); H1 2026 RNS states divestment discussions are underway. Toolstation France closure completed in 2024 at material cost.

**Operational / IT:** Oracle ERP implementation (July 2024) caused customer-facing disruption and ~£50m working-capital outflow; largely resolved by FY2025 but remains a execution-risk reference point.

**Leverage and covenants:** IFRS 16 net debt £621m at FY2025 and £544m at H1 2026; covenant net debt/adjusted EBITDA limit <4.0x with headroom under stress scenarios per H1 2026 going-concern disclosure. Target leverage not yet achieved on a sustained basis until H1 2026.

**Pension:** Defined-benefit surplus £118m (FY2025); Virgin Media pension case may affect TP and BSS schemes — directors state no adjustment made yet (H1 2026 note 8).

**Governance / key person:** CEO turnover — Pete Redfern departed March 2025 (ill health); Gavin Slark appointed from 1 January 2026. Interim chair-led period and board restructuring add transition risk, partially mitigated by Slark's sector track record (Grafton, BSS, SIG).

**Dividend:** Progressive cut from 18.0p (FY2023) to 14.5p to 12.0p; H1 2026 interim 4.0p vs 4.5p. Coverage depends on adjusted earnings recovery.

**Liquidity:** Strong near-term (£861m headroom at H1 2026), but FY2026 guidance flags £6m higher interest expense post-bond refinancing, partially offset by cash interest income.
