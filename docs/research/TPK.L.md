# Travis Perkins plc (TPK.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T17:36:15.930524+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Travis Perkins is the UK’s largest building-materials distributor, trading at a deep discount on book value (P/B 0.7x) with a screen-highlighted free cash flow yield of roughly 17% on ~£226m FCF. The investment case rests on a cyclical trough in UK construction meeting a materially strengthened balance sheet: net cash before leases of £55m at H1 2026 (versus £103m net debt a year earlier), leverage back within the 1.5–2.0x target at 1.9x, and over £860m liquidity headroom. Adjusted operating profit has stabilised—£133m in FY2025 and flat H1 2026 ex-property—while Toolstation UK grows and new CEO Gavin Slark pursues a General Merchant turnaround. The central debate is whether cheapness and cash generation compensate for repeated impairments (£222m in FY2025), negative statutory earnings (FY2025 loss £176m), subdued Merchanting margins (3.3% adjusted), and persistent cyclical headwinds that management expects to continue through H2 2026.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags TPK on **cheapness** (P/B at the 100th percentile of peers; composite value pass) and **risk/financial health** (FCF Yield, Financial Health, Earnings Quality passes). That profile fits a value investor seeking asset-backed cash generators at a cyclical low.

**Business quality (mixed but improving on balance sheet):** The group owns a nationwide branch network (719 Merchanting, 590 Toolstation UK stores), long supplier relationships, and a freehold property portfolio that continues to monetise (£51m property cash proceeds in FY2025). Toolstation UK is the quality anchor: FY2025 adjusted operating profit rose 29% to £44m on 2.7% revenue growth, with H1 2026 UK profit up 4.8% to £22m and ROCE improving. Merchanting remains the drag—FY2025 adjusted operating profit fell 18% to £122m on flat like-for-like sales—but H1 2026 shows early gross-margin recovery (£11m expansion) under new leadership.

**Why the screen’s “buy” makes sense:** At ~0.7x tangible book (~£990m per FY2025 balance sheet) and strong operating cash flow (£375m FY2025; £162m H1 2026), the market prices in prolonged earnings weakness rather than insolvency risk. Deleveraging from 2.5x net debt/adjusted EBITDA (FY2024) to 1.9x (H1 2026) and net-cash-before-leases status support the screen’s financial-health pillar. The stock has carried a stable buy signal for eight weeks with high data quality (19/20 metrics).

**Caveat:** ROE is negative (-9.1% per screen) and adjusted EPS fell from 36.6p (FY2024) to 30.8p (FY2025). Value here is a **balance-sheet and cash-flow** story pending operational recovery, not a clean earnings compounder.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / demand:** UK construction remains “subdued” and “depressed” per H1 2026 and Q1 2026 updates. FY2024–FY2025 revenue fell from £4,837m to £4,565m; volume sensitivity is high given Merchanting operational gearing. A prolonged housing/RMI downturn would pressure margins despite cost actions.

**Competitive intensity:** FY2024–FY2025 filings cite over-capacity, promotional activity, and inability to pass through manufacturer price increases in Merchanting. CCF and new-build exposure remain weak.

**Impairments / accounting:** FY2025 £222m adjusting items (£111m Merchanting, £99m Benelux) drove statutory loss £176m. Branch-level impairments hit 196 sites; goodwill written down at CCF and Toolstation Benelux (£105.5m). Pattern of recurring non-cash charges weakens reported earnings quality and could recur if markets stay soft.

**Toolstation Benelux:** H1 2026 operating loss £7m; strategic review initiated with **possible divestment**. FY2025 impairment £105.5m signals prior capital misallocation.

**Execution / IT:** FY2024 Oracle ERP implementation caused customer-facing disruption and ~£50m working-capital outflow; FY2025 normalisation aided cash but operational turnaround under third CEO in two years (Redfern → Slark from Jan 2026) adds transition risk.

**Leverage / covenants:** Headroom is comfortable (1.9x vs <4.0x covenant; £861m liquidity at Jun 2026). IFRS 16 leases (£599m) mean reported net debt £544m despite net cash before leases. Refinancing cost ~£6m higher interest in 2026 per FY2025 guidance.

**Pension:** Net pension asset is favourable, but Virgin Media litigation uncertainty over historical benefit changes could require future adjustment.

**Governance:** Leadership churn (Pete Redfern resigned Mar 2025; Gavin Slark appointed effective Jan 2026). Dividend cut (12.0p from 18.0p in 2023) signals capital preservation priority.

**Going concern:** H1 2026 interim states going concern basis; downside recession scenarios show sufficient liquidity and covenant headroom for 12+ months. No material going-concern qualification from Deloitte’s interim review.
