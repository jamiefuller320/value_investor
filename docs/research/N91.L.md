# Ninety One Group (N91.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T07:32:10.137044+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Ninety One is a dual-listed (LSE/JSE) active asset manager with emerging-markets heritage, now showing a sharp recovery in scale: group AUM reached **£184.0bn** at 30 June 2026, up from **£171.8bn** at 31 March 2026 and **£139.7bn** a year earlier (Q1 2027 update RNS). The quantitative screen rates the name a **buy** (13/22 models; composite 54%; all five families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk) on **P/E 12.2×, P/B 2.9×, yield 6.4%, ROE 28.5%**, with low leverage (D/E ~15%). The valuation hook is a high-return, capital-light franchise trading below typical asset-manager multiples whilst returning cash via dividends and a **£55m** buyback. The central debate is whether AUM momentum and partnerships (State Street, Sanlam) can offset structural **fee compression**, **regional outflows**, and market-beta sensitivity — issues flagged at the FY2026 results but not fully resolved in available filings.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Ninety One presents a rare combination in UK financials: absolute cheapness, demonstrated profitability, and a shareholder-return programme that the screen captures across multiple model families (Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Buffett Quality, High Dividend Yield, Magic Formula, among others). The business is genuinely capital-light at the equity level: consolidated total debt is modest (**£104.3m**, largely lease obligations, per Yahoo fallback for FY2026), and the screen’s risk family passes on balance-sheet metrics.

Business quality is anchored in **active EM and global equity/debt franchises**, a dual-listing structure that preserves South African client and talent links, and recent operational momentum. Filing-sourced AUM data show **+7%** growth in the first quarter of FY2027 and **+32%** year-on-year — a scale recovery that supports fee revenue if margins hold. Management is allocating capital to shareholders: an **£55m** repurchase programme launched on **3 June 2026** (RNS buyback notices), extended to the JSE in July, with **~7.1m shares** repurchased for **~£15.2m** by late August; the AGM (**22 July 2026**) approved a **final dividend** for the year ended 31 March 2026 and renewed buyback authority for up to **10%** of issued capital.

The screen’s buy signal aligns with fundamentals on headline multiples and returns, but **conviction is low (35%)** and the signal is **new (one week)**, with **neutral timing** (RSI 37; price below 200-day moving average). That profile suits a **patient accumulation** thesis rather than aggressive sizing: the market is pricing cyclical and structural headwinds into an otherwise cheap, cash-generative manager with insider buying via the Marathon Trust (directors’ associate) through summer 2026.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical AUM and market beta.** AUM growth to **£184bn** is positive, but a material portion likely reflects market appreciation; a risk-off episode would compress fees and potentially reverse the scale narrative. The stock screens cheap partly because the market discounts this beta.

**Fee compression and competitive pressure.** FY2026 earnings-call summaries in the news manifest cite **fee pressure** alongside AUM growth. Active managers face structural passive competition; margin defence is the key unverified variable absent from filing extracts.

**Regional outflows.** News summaries (Yahoo Finance, **6 June 2026**) reference **regional outflows** despite aggregate AUM growth — suggesting geographic mix risk, potentially including South Africa and developed-market institutional channels.

**Dual-listing and regulatory complexity.** Operations span UK and South Africa (B-BBEE annual compliance report RNS, **8 July 2026**; JSE/LSE parallel listings). FX (GBP/ZAR), withholding tax, and BEE compliance add governance and operational overhead not captured by valuation screens.

**Governance signals.** AGM results (**22 July 2026**): **21.64%** voted against **disapplication of pre-emption rights** (Special Resolution 17); **4.46%** against the **directors’ remuneration policy**. The board committed to shareholder engagement. Climate strategy resolution saw **3.15%** against but **11.95%** votes withheld — elevated abstention on ESG.

**Key-person and insider concentration.** CEO **Hendrik du Toit** and CFO **Kim McFarland** remain central; substantial share vestings occurred in **June 2026** (PDMR RNS). **Forty Two Point Two / Marathon Trust** (directors’ associate) made **large open-market purchases** in **July–September 2026** (news and RNS) — supportive, but reinforces key-person linkage.

**Accounting and cash-flow quality.** Yahoo shows large non-cash **investment securities gains** flowing through operating cash reconciliation. Without the Integrated Annual Report body, **earnings quality, seed-capital marks, and variable compensation accruals** cannot be fully assessed. Goodwill/intangibles step-up in FY2026 warrants explanation.

**Going concern / covenants.** No going-concern qualification appears in available extracts. The dormant warranty subsidiary affirms going concern; **group-level contingency, litigation, and covenant language is not available** in indexed bodies. Lease-related debt (**£104.3m**) is modest relative to equity.

**Pension.** Defined benefit pension balance **£0.6m** (FY2026 Yahoo) — immaterial.
