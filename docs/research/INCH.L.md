# Inchcape plc (INCH.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:01:16.924717+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Inchcape plc is a globally diversified automotive distributor trading on roughly 13x earnings with a 4% dividend yield and double-digit free cash flow yield, which aligns with the quantitative screen’s buy rating across cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk families. The investment case rests on a capital-light distribution model, disciplined capital return (dividends at 40% of adjusted EPS plus ongoing buybacks), and contract-led growth in the Americas and Europe & Africa, partially offset by APAC restructuring and a weaker Australia. Valuation looks attractive on cash generation and shareholder returns, but the central debate is whether FY2026 guidance for >10% adjusted EPS growth is achievable against rising finance costs, contract exits, and cyclical auto exposure. The screen’s earnings-basis overlay flags statutory versus adjusted EPS divergence, warranting caution despite headline cheapness.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen rates INCH.L as **buy** (6 of 22 models passed; composite score 63%; data quality 20/20), with passes in FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple and Earnings Quality. Headline metrics — P/E 13.2, P/B 2.7, dividend yield 4.0%, ROE 19.4%, FCF yield 11.6% — point to a cash-generative distributor trading below intrinsic quality implied by 29% ROCE (FY2025, Companies House annual accounts extract) and 31% ROCE in H1 2026 (half-year results RNS).

Business quality supports a value case. Inchcape is the leading independent global automotive distributor across ~40 markets, operating an asset-light model connecting OEM partners to customers via distribution, logistics, retail channels and aftersales (31% of H1 2026 gross profit). Management’s Accelerate+ strategy — scaling via new distribution contracts (ten wins in FY2025; five in H1 2026) and bolt-on M&A (Askja in Iceland; Silver Star in Bulgaria) while exiting dilutive contracts — targets organic volume growth of 3–5% and adjusted EPS growth of >10% medium term. Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly: leverage of 0.4x at FY2025 year-end rising to 0.5x at H1 2026, a £250m buyback programme (increased from £175m in July 2026), and interim DPS of 10.8p (+14%).

For a value investor, the hook is buying a high-ROCE, FCF-rich franchise at a mid-teens multiple while management actively shrinks the share count and returns surplus cash. The screen’s adjusted signal of **hold** (statutory and filing-adjusted EPS growth diverge in sign while growth-dependent screens pass) is the main counterweight — deep research partially validates cheapness on cash and returns, but does not fully resolve the earnings-quality question.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and regional.** Automotive distribution is inherently cyclical. Australia drove a 320bps APAC margin contraction in H1 2026 (fuel disruption, OEM supply constraints, consumer shift to lower-priced/NEV vehicles). Americas and Europe benefited from supportive TIV, but management flagged short-term supply disruption linked to the Middle East situation.

**Restructuring and adjusting items.** H1 2026 adjusting charges of £64m (vs £14m prior year) include contract exits across 15 immaterial agreements (~1.5% of FY2025 new-vehicle volumes). Goodwill/intangible headroom is sensitive in several CGUs (Americas Hino, APAC Indonesia/Philippines per interim notes). Yahoo shows tangible book value of only £66m against £1.19bn goodwill/intangibles — acquisition and contract-intangible risk is material.

**Leverage and liquidity.** Reported net debt £672m at H1 2026 (including leases); adjusted net debt £329m. Committed facilities £1,040m (£320m drawn). Vehicle inventory funding of £1,840m sits in trade payables — classification judgement noted in interim accounts. £16m cash in Ethiopia requires prior approval for repatriation.

**Finance costs.** Adjusted net finance costs rose 29% YoY in H1; inventory financing interest £28m. Rising rates and inventory levels could pressure adjusted PBT even if volumes grow.

**OEM and competitive.** Distribution contracts are OEM-dependent; Inchcape is exiting multiple Stellantis and other brands in APAC. EV transition, emerging OEM competition and margin pressure are flagged as principal risks unchanged from the annual report framework (interim RNS risk section).

**Governance.** Board change: Juan Pablo Del Río stepped down May 2026; Felipe Del Río appointed (continuing Del Río family representation post-Derco acquisition).

**Pensions.** IAS 19 net post-retirement surplus £19m at H1 2026 (modest).

**Going concern / covenants.** Interim accounts adopt going concern; reverse stress testing concludes covenant breach scenarios are remote under modelled sensitivities (demand downturn, sterling strength, working-capital adverse movement combined).

**Earnings quality.** Screen overlay notes statutory versus adjusted EPS growth divergence; H1 statutory EPS 23.3p vs adjusted 35.5p illustrates the gap. FY2025 statutory net profit £273m vs continuing operations £273m with significant adjusting/restructuring noise in FY2026.
