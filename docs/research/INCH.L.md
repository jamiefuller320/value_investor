# Inchcape plc (INCH.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:23:16.081106+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Inchcape is a global automotive distributor operating across ~40 markets under its Accelerate+ strategy, combining contract wins, bolt-on M&A, and capital returns. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness (P/E 13.5, P/B 2.7), dividend yield (~4%), GARP, and risk metrics, but conviction is low (26%) and the screen’s earnings-basis overlay adjusts the signal to Hold owing to statutory versus adjusted EPS divergence and FCF measurement gaps. The valuation hook is a capital-light distributor trading below historical earnings power, returning cash via dividends (40% of adjusted EPS policy) and ongoing buybacks (£250m programme), with management guiding >10% adjusted EPS growth in FY2026. The key debate is whether APAC restructuring (Australia weakness, £62m H1 adjusting charges) is transient noise against Americas/Europe momentum, or a structural margin headwind in a cyclical, OEM-dependent model.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Inchcape offers a differentiated distribution platform—long-dated OEM contracts, high inventory turns, and aftersales (~31% of H1 gross profit)—rather than a pure auto retailer. The screen passes seven models including Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Magic Formula, and Acquirer’s Multiple, consistent with a business generating ROCE of 31% (H1 2026 filing) and leverage of 0.4–0.5x net debt/EBITDA. Business quality is supported by geographic diversification (Americas 37% of H1 revenue, Europe & Africa 39%, APAC 24%), ten new distribution contracts in FY2025, and disciplined capital allocation: ~£1.3bn returned to shareholders since 2019, FY2025 leverage 0.4x (Companies House annual accounts), and the buyback increased to £250m in July 2026. The buy case ties screen cheapness to a franchise that converts earnings to cash (FY2025 operating cash flow £385m per filing; FCF £337m per Yahoo fallback where filing body lacks a clean FCF line) and commits to medium-term adjusted EPS CAGR >10%. Offsetting this, adjusted H1 2026 EPS was flat at 35.5p despite 9% revenue growth, and statutory PBT fell 33% to £124m—so the screen’s quality signal rests more on balance-sheet strength and capital returns than on near-term earnings momentum.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and regional:** Automotive distribution is volume- and macro-sensitive. H1 2026 APAC adjusted operating profit halved (−51%) on Australia fuel disruption, supply constraints, and mix shift to lower-priced/NEV vehicles. Middle East-related supply disruption affected Africa aftersales; Americas saw short-term supply disruption.

**OEM/customer concentration:** Revenue depends on distribution contracts with global OEMs; the group exited 15 immaterial/dilutive contracts in H1 2026 (~1.5% of FY2025 new-vehicle volumes). Contract wins and losses can move margins materially—five new wins in H1 2026 but ongoing portfolio optimisation.

**Leverage and liquidity:** Reported leverage is low (0.4–0.5x), but inventory financing of £1,840m (H1 2026) and rising adjusted net finance costs (£62m vs £48m H1 prior) increase sensitivity to rates. Private Placement notes require interest cover ≥3:1 on adjusted EBITA (trailing 12 months)—compliant at H1 2026; going concern affirmed with covenant stress-testing through 2027. £16m cash in Ethiopia subject to transfer restrictions.

**Accounting/adjusting items:** H1 2026 statutory PBT £124m vs adjusted £188m—a £64m gap from restructuring and contract exits. FY2025 statutory versus adjusted divergence also visible. Goodwill and indefinite-life intangibles £1,154m at H1 2026; impairment headroom sensitive in Americas (Hino, Ditec) and APAC (Indonesia, Philippines) CGUs per interim note.

**EV transition:** Filing language flags margin pressure, supply chain disruption, and NEV transition as principal risks; group remains powertrain-agnostic but must manage OEM mix shifts.

**Pension:** IAS 19 net post-retirement surplus £19m at 30 June 2026 (FY2025: £21m)—not a material deficit risk.

**Governance:** Board changes in 2025–26 (Sarah Kuljaars stepped down; Tracy Clarke appointed); insider buying noted in press (~£747k, February 2026).
