# Vesuvius plc (VSVS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:00:12.238791+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Vesuvius is a global molten-metal flow engineering business (Steel and Foundry consumables and services) trading at a depressed multiple after a cyclical earnings trough: FY2025 trading profit fell to £151.1m (return on sales 8.4%) from £188.0m in FY2024, yet revenue was broadly flat at £1,809.5m. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness (P/B ~0.9, EV/EBITDA ~7.9), dividend yield (~5.8%), and financial health, with timing overlay at “accumulate”. The valuation hook is asset-backed cyclical recovery — management targets 12.5% RoS and deleveraging as steel markets recover and a £55m cost programme matures — but H1 2026 operational failures in North American Steel (~£6m lost profit) and delayed India ramp-up (~£2m) have pushed full-year profit guidance to only “slightly ahead” of FY2025. The central debate is whether sub-book equity and a maintained progressive dividend compensate for weak ROE (4.2%), an FCF/dividend coverage gap (FY2025 FCF £36.0m vs dividends ~£58m), and execution risk during a leveraged turnaround (net debt/EBITDA 1.9–2.0x).

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Vesuvius offers a classic cyclical deep-value setup: a technology-led niche franchise (Flow Control leadership, new-product sales ratio 20.5–21.0%) priced below tangible book, with self-help already delivering £30.8m cumulative cost savings against a £55m 2028 target and bolt-on M&A (PiroMET, Molten Metal Systems) expanding exposure to faster-growing non-ferrous foundry and EEMEA steel markets. The screen passes four factor families — cheapness, dividend, GARP (PEGY ~0.05), and risk (Financial Health model) — with composite score 57% (61% sector-relative), and timing metrics (RSI ~46, price ~5% below 200-day MA) favour staged entry. Business quality is mixed: pricing turned net positive in H2 2025, Foundry H1 2026 trading profit rose 32.7% constant currency on MMS integration, and H1 2026 free cash flow swung to +£27.5m with 81% cash conversion — but Steel RoS has compressed from 11.4% (FY2024) to 8.9% (FY2025) and statutory ROE is low. The screen’s dividend-yield overlay downgrades the headline signal to “hold” because FCF yield and earnings quality fail — consistent with filing FCF of £26.2m (Yahoo/cash-flow basis) versus the screen’s higher TTM figure. Net: cheap enough to merit accumulation for patient cyclical value, but not a clean quality compounder until RoS reverts toward the 12.5% medium-term target.

## FINANCIAL REVIEW

**Annual trend (FY2022–FY2025)**

Revenue has drifted lower through the downcycle: Yahoo shows £2,047m (2022) → £1,930m (2023) → £1,820m (2024) → **£1,809.5m (2025, filing)**. Trading profit peaked well above current levels and has compressed sharply: **£188.0m (FY2024, filing)** → **£151.1m (FY2025, Final Results, 12 Mar 2026)**, with RoS falling from 10.3% to **8.4%**. Adjusted EPS declined from 43.3p to **34.2p**; statutory EPS from 33.5p to **21.1p**. EMEA accounted for ~80% of the FY2025 profit decline. Dividend per share was maintained and nudged higher: **23.6p** total (interim 7.1p + final 16.5p proposed), cover ~1.5x on adjusted EPS — progressive policy intact but thin on FCF.

- Net debt/EBITDA: **2.0x** pro-forma (Dec 2024: 1.3x), at the top of management’s 1.0–2.0x target range; covenant headroom remains comfortable (covenant max 3.25x; severe downside scenario peaks ~1.9x per going-concern disclosure).

- FCF: **£36.0m** (FY2024: £57.8m); cash conversion **75%**. Capex normalising: **£81.0m** net (guidance **£70–75m** for 2026).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical/end-market:** Steel and foundry volumes remain sensitive to global industrial production; FY2025 weakness concentrated in EMEA (EU auto/foundry decline, Chinese steel export pressure). Recovery thesis depends on trade protection, declining Chinese exports, and H2 2026 European improvement — not yet fully visible in H1 profit.

**Operational execution:** H1 2026 North American raw-material quality failure, maintenance gaps, and India Vizag ramp delays caused market share losses and ~£8m estimated profit impact; product quality was not compromised but capacity was. Management expects resolution by year-end — a key verify-before-trade item.

**Leverage and capital allocation:** Net debt/EBITDA at range ceiling despite buybacks (£100m programme completed by Apr 2025) and two acquisitions (~£97m combined). FCF in FY2025 did not fully cover dividends and buybacks; H1 2026 cash improvement helps but sustainability depends on earnings recovery and capex normalisation.

**Competitive/pricing:** European Advanced Refractories faces import competition from China; customers switching to lower-margin products in downturn (mix headwind £30.4m volume/mix in FY2025).

**Pension:** Residual **£31.3–31.6m** net liability, mainly US/Germany; UK de-risked but ROW plans remain.

**Regulatory/litigation:** Legacy US asbestos product claims (pre-acquisition subsidiaries) — filings state no material adverse effect to date but ongoing. Environmental/closure provisions **£65.6m** total (Dec 2025).

**Going concern/covenants:** Directors affirm going concern to Jun 2027; severe downside (~22% trading profit decline) still leaves covenant headroom. PwC interim review (Jul 2026) noted no material going-concern uncertainty.

**Governance/management:** No major board upheaval; directorate changes in Mar/May 2026 appear routine. Operational failures raise questions on internal controls in North America — flagged but not fully resolved in filings.
