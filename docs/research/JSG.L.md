# Johnson Service Group PLC (JSG.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-30T07:11:33.768592+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Johnson Service Group PLC (JSG.L) is a UK and Republic of Ireland textile-services operator focused on HORECA linen rental (~73% of FY2025 revenue) and industrial workwear (~27%). The quantitative screen rates the name a **strong buy** (9/22 models; composite 85%), supported by reasonable valuation (P/E ~16.5, P/B ~2.0, yield ~3.2%), solid quality metrics (ROE ~12.7%, Piotroski 7/9), and filing-aligned free cash flow of ~£100.6m in FY2025. Primary filings show a consistent earnings trajectory: adjusted profit before tax rose from £44.5m (2023) to £54.8m (2024) and £64.5m (2025) on revenue growth to £535.4m. The investment case rests on a cash-generative rental model, margin progression toward a ≥14% adjusted operating margin target, and disciplined capital return (progressive dividend, active buybacks). The key debate is whether hospitality softness (HORECA organic revenue −2.0% in H1 2026) and elevated leverage from buybacks offset valuation appeal—and whether reported FCF (~£100.6m filing basis vs ~£27.4m screen TTM) is truly representative.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, JSG offers **quality at a modest discount**, not deep net-net exposure. The screen passes five families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—reflecting a business that combines mid-teens earnings growth with shareholder-friendly capital allocation rather than distressed pricing.

**Business quality.** JSG operates route-based textile rental with high customer retention (workwear retention cited at 94% in the Jan 2026 trading update) and recurring revenue. Management has repositioned the group as a dedicated textile-services provider, exited non-core activities, and expanded in Ireland and luxury HORECA via bolt-on M&A (e.g. Empire Linen £20.6m in 2024). Energy costs are largely hedged (85% electricity, 90% gas fixed for 2026 per the Jul 2026 H1 update), supporting margin visibility.

**Quantitative alignment.** At P/E 16.5 and P/B 2.0, the stock fails classic deep-value screens (Graham Defensive, net-net, Schloss) but passes enterprising Graham, FCF yield, Neff PEGY, quality value, dividend growth, Magic Formula, Piotroski, composite value, and earnings quality. Debt/equity ~62% and dividend yield ~3.2% sit within conservative thresholds. FY2025 filing-aligned FCF of £100.6m implies strong cash conversion and ~5.8× net dividend coverage (Yahoo fallback for cash-flow detail where filing bodies lack full statements).

**Capital allocation as a value catalyst.** The board targets leverage of 1.0–1.5× adjusted EBITDA, maintains a progressive dividend (4.8p total for 2025; 2.5× cover), and has returned £90.3m via buybacks since Sep 2022, with a £55m programme launched May 2026. At ~£146, the stock trades near recent buyback prices (~147p), suggesting management views current levels as attractive.

**Why strong buy, not merely hold.** Organic growth has slowed (FY2025 organic revenue +1.4%; H1 2026 group organic −0.7%), but adjusted PBT growth remains robust and margin improvement is on track. For a value investor seeking **compounding cash returns at a reasonable multiple** rather than asset plays, the screen signal is well supported by filing evidence on earnings and balance-sheet capacity.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / end-market exposure.** HORECA (~73% of revenue) is directly tied to UK/ROI hospitality activity. H1 2026 organic HORECA revenue fell ~2.0%; management cites challenging hospitality trading but expects seasonal recovery. The 2025 annual report warns of economic uncertainty and increased labour and premises costs affecting end customers.

**Leverage and capital allocation.** Net debt rose materially in FY2025 as buybacks (£54.7m) outpaced organic deleveraging. H1 2026 net debt (~£190m incl. leases) increased further with the £55m buyback programme. Leverage remains within the 1.0–1.5× target, but continued repurchases at elevated prices reduce balance-sheet flexibility if hospitality weakens.

**Liquidity.** Current ratio ~0.93 (screen/Yahoo) and negative working capital are structural for rental businesses but failed the Financial Health screen (weak liquidity). This warrants monitoring against covenant headroom (filings reference significant headroom under committed facilities; full covenant text not extracted).

**Competitive / pricing.** Management acknowledges pricing discipline has caused market churn in some instances, though customers return for service quality. Textile rental is competitive and scale-dependent; JSG’s acquisition roll-up strategy carries integration risk.

**Governance / key person.** Orderly CFO succession (Ryan Govender replacing Yvonne Monaghan, retired Feb 2026) and board refresh (Lysanne Gray NED/Audit Chair from Jun 2026) are positive. AIM-to-Main Market transition (2025) may have shifted the shareholder register.

**Pension.** Defined benefit pension obligation ~£4.9m (Yahoo FY2025); not a primary risk but present on balance sheet.

**Accounting / FCF.** The filing vs TTM FCF divergence is a red flag for automated screens. Restructuring/M&A charges (£3.9m in 2025 per Yahoo) and high depreciation inflate the gap between statutory (£37.1m net income) and cash metrics.

**Principal risks section.** The 2025 annual report references Principal Risks and Uncertainties (p.50), but the body extract does not include the full risk register or going-concern language; no going-concern qualification is evident in available extracts.
