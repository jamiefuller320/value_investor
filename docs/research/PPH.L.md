# PPHE Hotel Group Limited (PPH.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T07:32:54.527299+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
PPHE Hotel Group is a Guernsey-listed owner-operator of upper-upscale hotels and leisure assets across the UK, Continental Europe and Croatia, with a portfolio valued at roughly £2.4bn (December 2025 external valuations). The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (8/22 models; composite 54%, sector-relative 69%), anchored on a headline P/E of 4.1x, FCF yield of 6.9% and 2.6% dividend yield. The valuation hook is the gap between EPRA net asset value of £26.97 per share (30 June 2026) and the market price implied by screening metrics, plus ongoing balance-sheet simplification (Waterloo freehold acquisition, New York site exit, Rome refinancing). The central debate is whether operational momentum and asset backing justify accumulation after the failed £930m Fattal takeover — blocked by ~33% shareholder Euro Plaza Holdings — or whether governance friction, rising leverage and normalised earnings that remain thin in H1 offset the screen signal.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, PPHE offers asset-heavy European hospitality exposure at multiples that screen as deep value: earnings yield 24.5%, FCF yield 6.9%, P/B 2.0x on IFRS book, and ROE 25.4%, with all five metric families passing (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk) and full data quality (20/20). Business quality is supported by a predominantly freehold/long-leasehold portfolio under Park Plaza (Radisson licence), art'otel and Arena brands; H1 2026 trading showed RevPAR up 3.9% to £113.5 and like-for-like EBITDA up 8.0%, with the UK — the largest profit pool — delivering 5.2% RevPAR growth and 8.5% EBITDA growth. Management is pivoting from a concluded sale process to “operational delivery plus balance sheet simplification,” crystallising value via the Park Plaza London Waterloo freehold buy (£147.9m) and Manhattan development site sale ($33.5m). Adjusted EPRA earnings per share has held at 125p on an LTM basis, the interim dividend is maintained at 17p, and FY26 consensus (company-compiled, 26 August 2026) points to revenue of £475–483m and EBITDA of £140–147m — consistent with the progressive dividend policy and GARP characteristics the screen rewards. Timing is neutral (RSI 39, price below 200-day MA), favouring staged accumulation rather than momentum chasing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Governance and control.** The strategic review (launched November 2025) culminated in a failed £930m cash approach from Fattal Hotel Group (May–June 2026). Euro Plaza Holdings (~33% of issued capital) opposed the bid; Fattal withdrew on 19 June 2026, and the formal sale process closed on 2 July 2026 with no deliverable proposals. This removes a near-term takeover catalyst and highlights a persistent blockholder veto over change-of-control outcomes.

**Leverage and interest burden.** Combined EPRA LTV rose to 40.6% post-Waterloo. H1 2026 financial expenses were £26.5m (six months); Yahoo FY2025 net interest was £57.4m. Refinancing risk exists on floating-rate tranches and 2028 viability horizon referenced in going-concern disclosures.

**Cyclical and regulatory headwinds.** Management cites Middle East conflict impacts on booking windows, UK business-rate inflation, Dutch VAT increase (January 2026), and broader fiscal/geopolitical volatility. UK development pipeline projects are under review/paused.

**Accounting complexity.** IFRS 16 lease structures, income units at Westminster Bridge, fair-value property accounting, and the Waterloo lease-termination treatment (£145.6m gain) make reported earnings volatile. H1 2025 comparatives included property impairment references (£23.7m in normalisation table). Companies House Headco accounts (FY2025) record £11.6m impairment on intercompany loans and a prior-period restatement flagged by auditors — subsidiary-level, but indicative of group financing complexity.

**Structural/minority interests.** Non-controlling interests (£182.3m at June 2026) include Arena Hospitality Group (Zagreb-listed) and third-party income-unit investors, complicating equity attribution.

**Liquidity.** H1 2026 investing cash outflows of £165.4m (chiefly Waterloo acquisition) reduced cash from £123.5m to £91.6m; operations remained cash generative (£29.5m net operating cash after interest/tax).

**Competitive.** Upscale city-hotel markets in London, Amsterdam and Berlin remain contested; PPHE outperformed UK market RevPAR in H1 but faces OTAs, alternative accommodation and international brand competition.
