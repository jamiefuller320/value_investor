# Jupiter Fund Management Plc (JUP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:26:10.522523+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Jupiter Fund Management is a UK-listed active asset manager undergoing a credible operational turnaround: calendar 2025 delivered the first year of positive net flows since 2017 (£1.3bn), AUM reached £54.0bn, and H1 2026 accelerated to £73.7bn following the £100m all-cash acquisition of CCLA. The quantitative screen flags the stock as cheap (P/E ~9x, P/B ~1.0x, yield ~3.5%, low leverage) with broad model support across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families. The valuation hook is a cyclically depressed earnings base normalising as flows, scale and cost synergies (target cost:income 70%) feed through, plus active capital return via dividends and buybacks. The key debate is whether 2025’s £120.3m performance-fee windfall and integration-driven AUM mask structurally lower fee margins (60bps group average post-CCLA) and whether client flows survive the next market or geopolitical shock.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Jupiter offers a classic “quality at a reasonable price” setup in a sector the market often penalises cyclically. The screen passes 17 of 22 models—including Graham Enterprising, Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, FCF Yield, Magic Formula and Piotroski F-Score—with debt/equity around 5% and ROE ~12%. Business quality is improving on leading indicators filings emphasise: investment performance (77% of pre-existing mutual fund AUM above peer median over three years at H1 2026), gross inflows up 46% year-on-year in H1 2026, and positive net flows in five consecutive quarters through Q1 2026 per the April trading update.

The CCLA deal (completed 2 February 2026 for £100.3m cash) adds scale, a new non-profit client channel and targeted synergies now raised to at least £17m (from £16m), with ~£8m expected in 2026. Management is returning surplus capital: FY2025 ordinary dividend of 10.1p (per H1 filing comparatives) plus special distribution tied to performance fees, and a 2026 buyback of up to £30m or 3% of issued capital. Net management fees fell to £310.7m in 2025 (from £332.9m in 2024) as 2024’s AUM losses annualised through revenue, but underlying profit before tax rose to £138.3m on performance fees; H1 2026 underlying PBT of £50.7m (+67%) and cost:income of 77% (from 82%) suggest the earnings trajectory is inflecting. The screen’s buy signal aligns with a business that is cheaper than its improving fundamentals, though the screen itself flags an FCF basis mismatch (filing £56.6m vs TTM £150.4m) that warrants verification before sizing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and flow risk.** Asset-management earnings remain highly correlated with markets and client sentiment. FY2024’s £10.3bn net outflow and the lagged FY2025 revenue hit demonstrate how quickly the P&L can deteriorate. H1 2026 saw two segregated mandate redemptions (not performance-related) and CCLA outflows of £0.7bn; further consolidation in local-government pensions or charity mandates could recur.

**Fee margin and mix.** Blended average fee margin fell to 60bps in H1 2026; CCLA’s 42bps structurally dilutes margins even as scale rises. Performance fees were £120.3m in FY2025 but only £8.9m in H1 2026—underlying earnings quality depends on recurring management fees, not fee crystallisations.

**Integration and goodwill.** CCLA added £29.2m goodwill plus £51.9m of acquired intangibles; total goodwill £523.6m represents a substantial share of equity. Impairment headroom exists today but is sensitive (+1% discount rate = £66m headroom reduction per H1 notes). Integration costs, lease impairments (£3.5m in H1) and synergy delivery remain execution risks.

**Investment performance.** While 77% of pre-existing Jupiter mutual fund AUM beats peers over three years, only 44% of CCLA mutual fund AUM does—underperformance here drove expected post-deal outflows.

**Regulatory and operational.** Principal risks unchanged from FY2025 AR: market disruption, investment performance, regulatory, technology/cyber, financial, outsourcing and people risks. Provisions increased to £3.2m at H1 2026 (from £0.6m) for claims/regulatory matters. Going concern: directors and EY interim review report no material uncertainties (H1 2026).

**Capital allocation.** Aggressive buybacks (2026 programme up to £30m; 23.7m shares cancelled in H1 2026) and the CCLA deal consume cash that could buffer a downturn; this is mitigated by undrawn RCF and £345m cash, but seed investments and working-capital swings (FY2025 receivables drag) make reported FCF volatile.

**Screen-specific flag.** FCF basis mismatch (filing £56.6m vs screen TTM £150.4m) and suppressed Yahoo quarterly cashflow mean yield/FCF-dependent models may overstate sustainable cash generation.
