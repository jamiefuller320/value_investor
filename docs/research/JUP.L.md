# Jupiter Fund Management Plc (JUP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-03T20:40:43.366181+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Jupiter Fund Management is a UK-listed active asset manager undergoing a credible operational turnaround after years of net outflows and Merian-related disruption. FY2025 filings show the first calendar year of positive net flows since 2017 (£1.3bn), AUM up 19% to £54bn, and a sharp recovery in investment performance, amplified by £120.3m of performance fees. The quantitative screen flags a **Strong Buy** on cheapness (P/E 8.4, P/B 0.9), dividend/GARP metrics, low leverage, and a Piotroski score of 7/9. The central debate is whether structurally lower management-fee earnings (underlying PBT ex performance fees fell to £62.2m in FY2025) and an elevated 82% cost:income ratio will normalise as CCLA integration and the 70% cost target bite—or whether earnings remain lumpily tied to performance fees and flow momentum in a cyclical sector.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Jupiter offers a **quality-cyclical compounder at a distressed multiple**: the stock trades below book (P/B 0.9) despite improving franchise indicators, while debt/equity (~5%) and a strong regulatory capital surplus (£146m post-CCLA, per FY2025 annual report) limit balance-sheet risk.

The screen’s **17/22 model pass rate** (composite 77%) aligns with the business inflection visible in filings:

| Screen family | Relevance to Jupiter |
|---|---|
| **Cheapness** | Deep Value, Schloss Low P/B, Acquirer's Multiple — multiple compression persists despite earnings recovery |
| **Quality / risk** | Economic Moat, Financial Health, Piotroski 7 — active UK franchise, CCLA non-profit channel, capital returns |
| **Dividend / GARP** | Dividend Growth, Lynch/Neff PEG — ordinary policy at 50% of pre-performance-fee EPS; FY2025 total ordinary dividend 25p plus 11.4p performance-fee return |
| **Failed screens** | Buffett Quality (ROE 12%), Earnings Quality (OCF below earnings), High Dividend Yield — flag that reported profits and cash conversion are not yet cleanly aligned |

Management under CEO Matthew Beesley is executing a coherent four-pillar plan (scale, simplify, broaden appeal, deepen relationships). The CCLA acquisition (£100m cash, completed February 2026) adds ~£15bn AUM and a sticky non-profit client base with 15 consecutive years of long-term inflows. Combined with systematic/global equity flow strength and ongoing share buybacks, the screen’s Strong Buy signal is **fundamentally supported**, though conviction is tempered by earnings-quality gaps and integration execution.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / revenue quality**  
Asset-management revenues remain highly sensitive to AUM levels, market returns, and flow momentum. FY2025 headline profit growth was **performance-fee dominated** (£120.3m); recurring management-fee economics weakened (net management fees down, ex-fee underlying PBT down 21%). Cost:income at **82%** remains far from the **70%** medium-term target despite headcount cuts.

**Integration and execution**  
CCLA integration carries regulatory, cultural, and synergy-realisation risk (£16m target; ~£17m one-off integration costs per RNS). FY2024 annual report explicitly cites **declining industry revenue margins and rising regulatory costs**. BNY middle/back-office outsourcing increases **outsourcing and supplier dependency**.

**Investment performance / flows**  
Principal risks listed in FY2025 annual report include **investment performance risk**, **regulatory risk**, **technology/cybersecurity risk**, **financial risk**, **people risk**, and **outsourcing risk**. A reversal in systematic/global equity performance—or renewed UK retail risk-aversion—could quickly restore outflows.

**Balance sheet / intangibles**  
Goodwill and intangibles remain large (Yahoo fallback: **£506m** vs tangible book **£400m** at FY2025), reflecting Merian and other acquisitions. Historical **Merian/Knightsbridge goodwill impairment** (FY2023) demonstrates write-down risk if franchises underperform.

**Governance / pension**  
No acute governance flags in available extracts. Yahoo balance sheet (fallback) shows **pension obligations** in prior years (£15.8m non-current plus £21.3m current at FY2023); FY2025 filing bodies do not detail pension funding — **verify in full annual notes before sizing risk**.

**Screen-identified weaknesses**  
Earnings Quality failure (operating cash below earnings) and sub-Buffett ROE suggest reported profits may overstate through-cycle cash generation, particularly when performance fees and receivables move.

**Going concern:** Affirmative in H1 2024 interim accounts; **not located in H1 2025 extract** — not necessarily absent from full filing, but unverified here.
