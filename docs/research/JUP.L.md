# Jupiter Fund Management Plc (JUP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-10T16:28:28.734824+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Jupiter Fund Management is a UK-listed active asset manager undergoing a credible operational turnaround: AUM has risen from £45.3bn at end-2024 to £73.7bn at H1 2026, net flows turned positive for the first calendar year since 2017, and the cost:income ratio is improving toward a 70% medium-term target. The quantitative screen flags the stock as a strong buy on cheapness (P/E 8.5, P/B 0.9), quality, dividend, GARP, and risk metrics, with low leverage (D/E ~5%) and a Piotroski F-Score of 7/9. The valuation hook is a sub-book multiple on a business now generating scale through the £100m CCLA acquisition and sustained inflows, with management returning capital via dividends and an active buyback. The central debate is whether FY2025’s £120.3m performance-fee windfall masked underlying margin pressure (underlying PBT ex performance fees fell to £62.2m), and whether CCLA integration, fee-margin dilution, and asset-management cyclicality will allow earnings to catch up to the re-rated narrative. Macro context data was not available in the source pack.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Jupiter offers a classic “quality at a reasonable price” setup within financials: the screen passes Graham Enterprising, Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, and Piotroski criteria, with dividend support (3.7% yield) and minimal balance-sheet risk. Business quality is improving on filing evidence, not just on multiples. Net flows of £1.3bn in FY2025 reversed a £10.3bn outflow in FY2024; H1 2026 added a further £0.7bn despite two segregated mandate redemptions. Investment performance is strong—77% of pre-existing Jupiter mutual fund AUM beat peer medians over three years at June 2026—supporting flow sustainability. The CCLA deal (completed February 2026 for £100.3m cash) adds ~£15bn AUM, a new non-profit client channel, and raised synergy targets to at least £17m annualised. Cost discipline is tangible: cost:income improved from 82% (FY2025) to 77% (H1 2026). Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly—50% payout of underlying pre-performance-fee EPS, special distributions linked to performance fees, and a 3% buyback programme (8.8m shares cancelled by 17 July 2026). The screen’s earnings-quality failure (operating cash below earnings) and Buffett Quality fail (ROE 12%) warrant monitoring, but the operational inflection and sub-book valuation align with the strong-buy signal.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Performance-fee dependency.** FY2025 performance fees of £120.3m (£86.3m net of associated compensation) dominated reported earnings. Underlying profitability excluding performance fees declined year on year. H1 2026 performance fees were modest (£8.9m), so forward earnings depend heavily on management-fee growth and cost savings rather than fee crystallisations.

**Fee-margin compression.** Group average fee margin fell to 60bps in H1 2026 from 65bps at FY2025, partly from CCLA's 42bps margin. Revenue growth from AUM scale must outpace margin erosion.

**CCLA integration and outflows.** CCLA saw £0.7bn net outflows in H1 2026 (expected post-acquisition); only 44% of CCLA mutual fund AUM outperformed over three years at June 2026. Integration synergy delivery (£17m target, £2.5m achieved in H1) and client retention remain execution risks. Goodwill of £29.2m and intangibles of £51.9m were recognised on acquisition.

**Flow fragility.** Two segregated mandate redemptions in Q2 2026 (not performance-related) reduced net flows. Institutional channel saw small H1 outflows. Industry-wide passive competition and fee pressure persist; the annual report cites regulatory risk, market risk, and operational risk as principal risks (unchanged at H1 2026).

**Governance.** Chair David Cruickshank intends to retire; succession search underway (interim report, May 2025). No material governance red flags in filings, but leadership transition adds uncertainty.

**Earnings quality (screen flag).** The quantitative screen failed Earnings Quality (operating cash below earnings). FY2025 Yahoo operating cash flow was £59.3m vs net income £100.4m, though H1 2026 cash generation was strong (£142.6m from operations)—suggesting timing/working-capital distortion rather than structural cash weakness, but this warrants verification.

**Pension.** Yahoo balance sheet shows no current pension liabilities at end-2025 (present in 2022–2023); no pension red flags in current filing bodies.

**Contingencies.** Provisions increased to £3.2m at June 2026 from £0.6m at yearend; filing notes claims and regulatory actions may be recognised or disclosed when obligations are probable. No covenant concerns—the RCF is undrawn.

**Going concern.** Both H1 2025 and H1 2026 interim filings confirm going-concern basis with no material uncertainties after stressed scenario review.
