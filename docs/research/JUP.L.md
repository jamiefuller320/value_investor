# Jupiter Fund Management Plc (JUP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-09T06:09:03.939501+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Jupiter Fund Management is a UK-listed active asset manager undergoing a credible operational turnaround under CEO Matthew Beesley: net flows turned positive in calendar 2025 for the first time since 2017, investment performance improved materially, and the £100m acquisition of CCLA (completed February 2026) adds scale and a new non-profit client channel. The quantitative screen flags the stock as a strong buy on cheapness (P/E 8.5, P/B 0.9), dividend yield (3.7%), low leverage (D/E ~5%), and a Piotroski F-Score of 7/9. The central debate is whether headline earnings recovery—heavily boosted by £120.3m of performance fees in FY2025—is sustainable, and whether management can drive the cost:income ratio from 82% toward its 70% medium-term target while integrating CCLA. Valuation appears attractive relative to improving leading indicators, but earnings quality and integration execution warrant a measured overlay rather than unqualified conviction.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Jupiter offers a classic “improving business at a depressed multiple” setup. The screen passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with 20/20 data completeness. At sub-1.0x book and single-digit earnings yield, the market prices in persistent margin compression and flow fragility; filing evidence suggests those fears are easing. FY2025 AUM reached £54.0bn (+19% YoY), net flows were +£1.3bn (versus −£10.3bn in FY2024), and three-year investment performance rose to 68% of mutual fund AUM above peer median (from 61%). Underlying profit before tax grew 42% to £138.3m, and the group returned capital aggressively via ordinary dividends (4.4p total for 2025 plus an 11.4p performance-fee-linked distribution), buybacks (~1.6m shares repurchased in 2025), and ongoing 2026 repurchases (share count cited at 518.1m in August 2026 news).

Business quality is adequate rather than exceptional: ROE of 12.0% fails the Buffett Quality screen, and operating cash conversion lags reported earnings (screen Earnings Quality fail). Nevertheless, the balance sheet is robust—regulatory capital surplus of £146m post-CCLA per the FY2025 annual report, and 4.8x coverage at H1 2025 interim—and goodwill impairment risk has not recurred since the £76.2m charge in FY2023. Cost discipline (non-compensation costs down to £89m; headcount 442 FTE) and a scalable platform (BNY middle-office outsourcing) support operating leverage if flows hold. The CCLA deal is strategically coherent: £15bn+ AUM, 15 consecutive years of net inflows, £16m synergy target by 2027, and day-one accretion to management-fee EPS per the July 2025 RNS. For a value screen, Jupiter scores as cheap enough to absorb integration noise while offering asymmetric upside if the flow turnaround persists.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Flow and revenue cyclicality.** FY2024’s −£10.3bn outflow demonstrates sensitivity to key-person departures (Value team) and retail risk-off sentiment. Management-fee revenue lagged the AUM recovery in FY2025; ex-performance-fee underlying PBT declined. Future flow reversals remain the primary earnings risk.

**Performance-fee dependence.** FY2025 underlying PBT was substantially flattered by £120.3m of performance fees (versus £31.2m in FY2024). This revenue is inherently lumpy and may not repeat, masking weaker core profitability.

**Cost:income trajectory.** Despite headcount and non-compensation cost reductions, the ratio deteriorated to 82% (from 73% in FY2023). The 70% target requires both further cost savings (£15m run-rate by end-2026, plus £16m CCLA synergies by end-2027) and sustained revenue growth—neither is assured.

**Goodwill and intangible asset risk.** Goodwill of £494.4m (H1 2025 interim balance sheet) exceeds tangible book value (£400m per Yahoo). A £76.2m impairment was taken in FY2023; further underperformance could trigger additional charges.

**CCLA integration.** £100m cash consideration, ~£17m one-off integration costs (RNS), cultural preservation commitments, and platform migration create execution risk. Regulatory capital surplus expected to compress post-deal (target >2.5x coverage).

**Operational and regulatory.** Filings cite principal risks including market disruption, investment performance, regulatory change, technology/cyber security, outsourcing/supplier (BNY middle office), people, and financial risk. Chair succession (David Cruickshank retiring April 2026; Nathan Bostock appointed successor per FY2025 report) adds governance transition risk.

**Earnings quality.** Screen flags operating cash below earnings; Yahoo shows FY2025 operating CF £59.3m vs net income £100.4m. Working-capital movements and seed-capital flows distort asset-manager cash metrics, but the gap warrants monitoring.

**Pension.** Yahoo balance sheet shows non-current pension obligations of £15.8m (2023); not prominently discussed in available filing extracts—legacy exposure may remain but is not quantified in indexed bodies.

**Going concern.** H1 2024 interim accounts state directors have no material uncertainties and adopt going-concern basis for at least 12 months. No contrary language found in indexed bodies.
