# Jupiter Fund Management Plc (JUP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-10T20:32:35.287264+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Jupiter Fund Management is a UK-listed active asset manager undergoing a credible operational turnaround under CEO Matthew Beesley, with the first calendar year of positive net flows since 2017 (£1.3bn in FY2025) accelerating into H1 2026 (£0.7bn) and bolstered by the February 2026 acquisition of CCLA (£100.3m cash). The quantitative screen flags the stock as a strong buy on cheapness (P/E 8.5, P/B 0.9), dividend yield (3.7%), and improving quality metrics (Piotroski 7/9), yet the screen also fails Earnings Quality (operating cash below earnings) and Buffett Quality (ROE 12%). The central debate is whether sustained flow momentum, CCLA synergies (target ≥£17m run-rate), and progress toward a 70% cost:income ratio can re-rate a franchise still trading below tangible book and heavily goodwill-weighted, or whether performance-fee volatility, CCLA integration outflows, and fee-margin dilution cap the recovery.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Jupiter offers a classic “trough-to-turn” setup: the shares screen cheap on absolute and sector-relative bases (composite 74%, sector-relative 70%), pass five factor families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk), and sit near the 200-day moving average with neutral timing (RSI ~45). Business quality is improving in ways the screen partially captures but cannot fully model.

**Scale and flows.** AUM rose from £45.3bn (FY2024) to £54.0bn (FY2025) and £73.7bn at H1 2026, with net inflows of £1.3bn in FY2025 reversing £10.3bn of outflows in FY2024—the first positive flow year since 2017. H1 2026 added £0.7bn of net new business; gross inflows were £10.8bn (+46% YoY). Investment performance supports the flow story: 77% of pre-existing Jupiter mutual fund AUM beat peer medians over three years at June 2026 (up from 68% at year-end 2025).

**Strategic repositioning.** The £100m all-cash CCLA acquisition (completed 2 February 2026) adds ~£15bn AUM and opens the UK non-profit client channel while preserving the CCLA brand. Management has raised synergy targets to at least £17m (from £16m), with ~£8m expected in 2026. Combined with prior cost programmes (non-comp compensation costs down to £99m at FY2025 year-end vs £126m in 2021; headcount 442 FTE vs 585 in 2021), this supports the medium-term 70% cost:income target (77% in H1 2026, down five points YoY).

**Capital returns.** Ordinary dividend policy targets 50% of underlying pre-performance-fee EPS; FY2025 total dividends were 10.1p (including a 5.7p special linked to performance fees). An active buyback/cancellation programme is running (3% mandate; 23.7m shares cancelled in H1 2026). Regulatory capital of £244m at June 2026 exceeded requirements by more than 3×.

**Valuation hook.** At ~8.5× earnings and ~0.9× book, the market prices Jupiter as a structurally challenged mid-tier AM rather than a recovering franchise with positive flow inflection and accretive M&A. Re-rating requires proof that underlying (ex-performance-fee) earnings can grow sustainably—not just one-off fee crystallisations.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and earnings quality.** Asset management revenues remain tied to AUM levels and market returns. FY2025 profitability was heavily performance-fee dependent (£120.3m); excluding these, underlying PBT fell. The screen flags operating cash flow (£59.3m per Yahoo/screen) below reported earnings—H1 2026 cash generation improved (£142.6m from operations) but remains working-capital sensitive. Performance fees are inherently lumpy and may not repeat.

**CCLA integration.** H1 2026 CCLA outflows of £0.7bn and weaker investment performance (44% of CCLA mutual fund AUM beating peers over three years) pose integration and reputational risk. Exceptional integration costs were £15.3m in H1 2026; property impairments of £3.5m were recognised on CCLA premises. Synergy delivery (£17m target) is not guaranteed.

**Fee margin compression.** Group average fee margin fell to 60bps (from 66bps pre-CCLA), diluting the revenue benefit of higher AUM. Continued shift toward lower-margin products or institutional mandates could pressure margins further.

**Goodwill and intangibles.** Goodwill of £523.6m at June 2026 exceeds tangible equity; the stock screens on P/B 0.9 using accounting book value that is substantially non-tangible. Impairment headroom exists but is not large relative to equity.

**Flow fragility.** H1 2026 net flows were impacted by two segregated mandate redemptions (non-performance-related) and CCLA outflows. Geopolitical volatility in Q2 2026 affected client sentiment; management acknowledges uncertainty on whether risk-appetite shifts are sustained.

**Regulatory and legal.** The interim report notes provisions for claims/regulatory action (£3.2m at June 2026, up from £0.6m). Principal risks (unchanged from FY2025 annual report per H1 2026) include regulatory change, conduct risk, and operational resilience. FCA oversight and potential client/compensation claims are ongoing industry hazards not fully captured by value screens.

**Capital structure.** While net debt is low (D/E ~5% per screen; subordinated debt redeemed; leases ~£42m), the £100m RCF provides liquidity but covenants and renewal risk (expires 2027) warrant monitoring. Going concern: directors and auditors (EY review) confirm no material uncertainties over at least 12 months (H1 2026 interim).

**Governance.** No material governance red flags in the filing pack; ongoing share buybacks and cancellations (23.7m shares in H1 2026) align management with per-share value, though compensation ratio remains elevated at 48% before performance fees.
