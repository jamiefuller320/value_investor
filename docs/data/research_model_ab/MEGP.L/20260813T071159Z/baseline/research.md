# ME Group International plc (MEGP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:12:35.278626+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ME Group International is a UK-listed operator of ~49,000 self-service vending machines across 16 countries, spanning photobooths (Photo.ME), unattended laundry (Wash.ME), and ancillary kiosks. The quantitative screen rates the name a **strong buy** (15/22 models passing; composite score 84%; P/E 7.8×, yield 6.9%, ROE 28.2%, debt/equity 28%). The investment case rests on a cash-generative, net-cash balance sheet funding a structural pivot from mature photobooths towards faster-growing laundry, at a valuation that embeds little credit for the mix shift. The central debate is whether laundry expansion and capital returns (8.64p FY25 dividend, £18m buyback) offset cyclical and regulatory pressure on photobooths—evidenced by German ID-photo rule changes, an April 2026 France wobble, and FY26 profit guidance cut to £69–74m PBT—and whether the 6.5% interim dividend reduction signals a durable trim rather than a timing adjustment.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal aligns with a business that scores well on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families simultaneously—a relatively rare combination for a mid-cap UK industrial. ME Group reported record FY25 profitability (PBT £78.2m; EBITDA margin 38.2%) while redeploying capital into Wash.ME, which added a net 1,145 laundry units and now contributes 46% of group EBITDA versus photobooths at 49%. Return on equity (~28% per screen) is supported by asset-light site partnerships, high machine-level margins (Wash.ME EBITDA margin 49–51%), and a net-cash position of £26.5m at 31 October 2025 (gross cash £56.5m; total borrowings ~£30m). Filings confirm no customer exceeds 2% of turnover, covenants were “comfortably” met in FY24–25, and the dividend policy targets >55% of PAT—FY25 payout was 58% at 8.64p total. For a value investor, the hook is a high-yield, low-multiple compounder trading below book on some metrics (P/B 2.2×) despite double-digit laundry growth and active capital return (buyback launched March 2026; £4.5m repurchased by July 2026). The screen’s adjusted strong buy reflects interim-quality concerns (H1 EPS down, net cash down to £7.5m) rather than a fundamental balance-sheet break.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory.** German passport-photo rules (May 2025) require certified capture, costing ~£3m H2 FY25 revenue; management expects continued FY26 impact before recovery as next-generation biometric booths deploy H2 2026. Broader risk of government centralisation of ID-photo capture is explicitly flagged in principal-risk tables (annual and interim).

**Cyclical / macro.** Continental Europe is 68% of revenue; France is the largest market. April 2026 trading update and H1 filings cite consumer-confidence weakness linked to Middle East conflict; warm-weather laundry demand also cited in FY25. Asia Pacific H1 revenue −13.2% (yen −9.1%, Japanese passport rule changes).

**Governance / control.** A Concert Party holds 30–50% of voting rights, constraining buybacks until a Rule 9 waiver (approved February 2026 GM: 83.5% of independent votes). June 2025–December 2025 strategic review ended with no acceptable offer (5 December 2025 RNS). **Audit delay:** FY25 audited accounts delayed to March 2026 (Forvis Mazars needed extended time; no material issues flagged); shares temporarily suspended 2–23 March 2026.

**Accounting.** Cash-in-transit restatement (FY24–25) and H1 FY25 dividend recognition timing restatement are disclosed; no equity impact but worth monitoring controls around machine cash.

**Dividend / liquidity.** Interim dividend cut to 3.60p (−6.5%) despite stated >55%-of-PAT policy commitment. Net cash fell from £26.5m to £7.5m in six months; gross cash £52.8m remains, and annual report notes covenant compliance (EBITDA/debt, debt/equity, interest cover, FCF and profit tests). Pension obligations modest at £4.6m.

**Competitive / operational.** Photobooth estate broadly flat (~30,500 units); next-gen rollout behind plan (3,079 of 8,000 target by FY27). Supplier concentration (foreign manufacturers) and cyber risk on biometric data feeds are disclosed principal risks.
