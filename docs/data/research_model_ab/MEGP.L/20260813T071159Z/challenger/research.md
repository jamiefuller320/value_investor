# ME Group International plc (MEGP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:14:58.518391+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ME Group is a cash-generative operator of more than 49,000 self-service machines across 16 countries, pivoting from mature photobooths (Photo.ME) towards higher-margin unattended laundry (Wash.ME). The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy (15 of 22 models; composite 84%; P/E 7.8×, yield 6.9%, ROE 28.2%, debt/equity 28%), reflecting cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families together. Filings confirm record FY25 profit before tax of £78.2m, a net-cash balance sheet and a dividend policy targeting more than 55% of profit after tax. The debate is whether laundry expansion and capital returns (FY25 total dividend 8.64p; £18m buyback) offset photobooth regulation, a France-led April 2026 demand wobble, FY26 profit-before-tax guidance of £69–74m, and a 6.5% cut in the interim dividend.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal fits a business that is cheap on earnings and cash flow yet still scores on quality and financial health: Graham Enterprising, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Magic Formula and Financial Health all pass, with data quality 20/20. FY25 filings show EBITDA margin of 38.2% and Wash.ME total laundry EBITDA of £55.5m (margin 49.4%), 46.1% of group EBITDA, after a net 1,145 laundry units were added. By H1 FY26 laundry had overtaken photobooths, contributing 53.9% of group EBITDA at a 51.2% margin. Site partnerships are diversified: the FY25 results PDF states there were no major customers (a single customer contributing at least 10% of revenue) in FY25 or FY24. Covenants (EBITDA/debt, debt/equity, interest cover, free cash flow and profit tests) were “comfortably” met in FY24 and FY25. Net cash was £26.5m at 31 October 2025 (gross cash £56.5m; borrowings £12.4m non-current and £17.6m current, leases excluded from net cash). For a value investor the hook is a high-yield, low-multiple compounder funding a mix shift and an £18m buyback while still net cash. The screen’s one-week “new” signal and neutral timing (RSI 54; price 0.1% below the 200-day average) argue for accumulation rather than urgency; H1 EPS decline and implied free-cash-flow coverage of the dividend below 1× are the quality caveats, not a balance-sheet break.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Regulatory. Principal-risk tables in the annual and interim filings flag “centralisation of the production of ID photos” and wider acceptance of home-made photographs. German rules from May 2025 require capture in citizens’ offices or by certified photographers; management expects the FY25 £3.0m H2 revenue hit to continue in FY26, with recovery thereafter as biometric booths deploy. Japan has also changed passport-application rules.

Cyclical. Consumer spending and travel ID demand drive photobooth volumes; laundry is needs-based but still felt April softness. Continental Europe is the profit engine; France is the largest market. The 1 June trading update attributes April weakness to “lower consumer confidence due to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East”. FY25 laundry demand was also dented by a warm European summer. Sterling translation is a disclosed risk (majority of profit earned outside the UK).

Governance and key person. A Concert Party holds more than 30% but less than 50% of voting rights, which blocked buybacks until a Rule 9 waiver (independent votes 83.54% in favour, 26 February 2026 GM). Serge Crasnianski remains CEO and Deputy Chairman; Vladimir Crasneanscki was appointed Deputy CEO (2 February 2026) and Tania Crasnianski stepped back to a non-executive role (announced 28 April 2026). A strategic review ended with no acceptable offer (5 December 2025). FY25 audited accounts were delayed because Forvis Mazars needed more time; the company said it had not been told of material audit issues; shares were suspended from 2 March until publication on 23 March 2026.

Accounting. Cash-in-transit restatement and H1 dividend-recognition restatement are disclosed; neither altered EPS, but they highlight controls around machine cash. French tax-reassessment provisions lifted the FY25 tax rate.

Dividend and liquidity. The interim dividend was cut 6.5% even as the board restated a policy of more than 55% of annual profit after tax. Net cash fell from £26.5m to £7.5m in six months; gross cash £52.8m and going-concern language remain supportive, but implied free-cash-flow cover of the ordinary dividend is below 1× on FY25 figures. Pension: a closed defined-benefit scheme; post-employment benefit obligations £4.6m at 31 October 2025 — modest versus £213.0m equity.

Competitive and operational. Photobooth estate is broadly flat; next-generation rollout is behind the FY27 8,000-unit target. Principal risks include reliance on foreign manufacturers and cyber attack on secure ID data feeds. The ASDA laundry partnership (up to 700 UK machines) is a growth contract, not a current 10% customer, but will raise site-partner concentration as it is built out.
