Disclaimer* Harbour Energy PLC 05 November 2026 Harbour Energy plc (“Harbour” or “the Company”) Trading and Operations Update 5 November 2026 PRODUCTION GUIDANCE AND FREE CASH FLOW OUTLOOK UPGRADED; FURTHER $150 MILLION SHARE BUYBACK ANNOUNCED Harbour Energy today provides the following unaudited Trading and Operations Update for the nine months to 30 September 2026 (“the period”). Highlights Record nine months’ production of 501 kboepd; Q3 production up 9% vs Q3 2025 Full year production guidance upgraded to 500-505 kboepd (490-500 kboepd previously) 2026 free cash flow outlook increased by $800m to c.$2.6bn Net debt reduced to $5.1bn with c.$1.0bn of debt repaid since completion of the LLOG acquisition At least $1.2bn of shareholder returns for 2026, including a further $150m buyback BASF shareholding reduced from c.47% at the beginning of the year to c.16% at period-end Linda Z Cook, Chief Executive Officer, commented: “We delivered another excellent quarter with production up 9% year-on-year driven by continued strong operational performance and the successful integration of LLOG and Waldorf. This enabled us to further upgrade our 2026 production guidance and, given the portfolio’s material exposure to both higher oil and European gas prices, our free cash flow outlook. As a result, we are accelerating debt reduction and delivering material shareholder returns, with at least $1.2 billion now expected to be returned to shareholders for 2026, including a further $150 million share buyback announced today. We also advanced our growth projects including through the addition of a second drilling rig in the US, progressing the San Roque Vaca Muerta oil licence in Argentina and reaching key milestones in Mexico, supporting future reserves and production.” Harbour made material progress against its strategic priorities during the period [1] : Sustaining production at scale Production for the period averaged 501 kboepd (2025: 473 kboepd), up 6% and split broadly 40% liquids, 40% European gas and 20% other gas; Q3 production was 484 kboepd, up 9% year-on-year – Addition of LLOG in the US (from February) and Waldorf in the UK (from July) more than offset Indonesia and Vietnam divestments and UK natural decline – Increased Norway production driven by strong reliability across key hubs, infill drilling at Njord and accelerated project delivery including start-up of the Skarv satellites in August a year ahead of schedule – New wells online in Q3 in Argentina and in the US where initial rates from the latest Who Dat and Buckskin wells have been better than expected Unit operating costs of $13.9/boe (2025: $12.8/boe) Total recordable injury rate of 1.5 per million hours worked (2025: 1.0) Net equity greenhouse gas emissions intensity of 13 kgCO 2 e/boe (2025: 13 kgCO 2 e/boe) [2] Building a competitive portfolio Strategic transactions completed, materially strengthening our portfolio and free cash flow outlook – US: LLOG portfolio successfully integrated following acquisition completion in February, adding a new core country with an operated, oil-weighted portfolio and significant growth potential – UK: Waldorf acquisition completed in July and integrated by period end, supporting financial and operational synergies and enhancing the resilience of the UK business – Indonesia: Divestment of non-core producing assets completed in May, improving overall portfolio quality High-return, short-cycle infrastructure-led investments progressed – Norway: Gjøa North and Ofelia sanctioned in Q2; five further projects targeted to reach final investment decision (FID) by year-end with the potential to deliver 100% Norway reserves replacement – GoA: Who Dat East approved in August while the Salamanca FPS subsea pump project is on track for FID by year-end supporting reserves and production; second drilling rig arrived in September enabling the acceleration of high return investment opportunities – Argentina: Three gas wells drilled on our Aguada Pichana Este gas licence in the Vaca Muerta during Q3 with nine wells connected during the period Growth projects advanced, supporting future reserves replacement and cash flow – Argentina: Applications for the San Roque conventional licence extension and the 250 km 2 unconventional licence (Harbour 24.7%) progressed, with the potential to unlock more than 200 new drilling locations in the Vaca Muerta black oil window supporting oil reserves and production growth over time – Argentina: Southern Energy, a 6 mtpa LNG export project (Harbour 15%), remains on track for around year-end 2027 start-up; project financing secured for the dedicated San Matias pipeline – Mexico: At Zama, MOU signed for the Lakam Ha FPSO and invitations to tender for major FEED packages issued; continue to target FID-readiness for Zama and Kan (both Harbour operated) around year-end 2027 Replenishing the portfolio through successful exploration and new licence awards – Exploration success during the period included Omega Sør (Norway), which is being fast-tracked towards a potential FID by year-end, and EZZ-2 (Egypt), which was tied into existing infrastructure and began production in Q3 – Awarded 12 leases (eight as operator) in the US Big Beautiful Gulf 3 lease sale, building on success in earlier rounds and adding exploration prospects near key hubs; spud of Kingsroad exploration well now expected around year-end – Awarded nine exploration licences (four as operator) in the 2025 Norway APA licensing round; participated in recent 2026 APA licensing round with results expected in Q1 2027 Greensand CCS (Harbour 40%) [3] in Denmark achieved first CO₂ injection in September, becoming the first full-scale CO 2 storage site in operation in the EU with capacity to store up to 400,000 tonnes of CO₂ per year in its initial phase Ensuring financial resilience Realised, post-hedge oil and European gas prices of $85/bbl and $16.1/mscf (2025: $71/bbl, $13.4/mscf);
