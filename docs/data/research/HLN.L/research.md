# Haleon plc (HLN.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T00:59:08.337477+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Haleon is a pure-play global consumer healthcare business (Sensodyne, Panadol, Advil, Centrum, Voltaren) that screens attractively on valuation and cash-return metrics: P/E ~20x, P/B ~2.0x, ~2% yield, debt/equity ~50%, and strong free cash flow. Filings show a credible margin-expansion and deleveraging story—adjusted operating margin reached 22.9% in FY 2025 and 24.3% in H1 2026, with net debt/adjusted EBITDA at 2.6x (FY 2025) and 2.5x (H1 2026)—supported by a £500m buyback and rising dividends. The central debate is growth quality: organic revenue growth of 3.0% in FY 2025 and 2.6% in H1 2026 sits below management’s 4–6% medium-term ambition, held back by North America and a weak cold/flu season in Respiratory Health. The quantitative screen’s earnings-basis overlay adjusts the raw signal to Hold, reflecting divergence between statutory and adjusted EPS trends. For a value investor, the case rests on cash compounding and capital returns rather than top-line re-rating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The buy rating from the quantitative screen is driven by five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with six passing models (Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Piotroski F-Score, Earnings Quality). Key metrics align with a defensive consumer franchise trading at a modest premium to deep value but below typical healthcare growth multiples: P/E 20.3, P/B 1.98, ROE 10.1%, FCF ~£2.2bn (Yahoo; filing-adjusted FCF FY 2025 £1,913m).

Business quality supports the screen’s quality and risk passes. Haleon owns category-leading oral health brands (32–33% of revenue; organic growth +7.9% FY 2025, +7.3% H1 2026), generates high gross margins (adjusted 65.2% FY 2025; 66.5% H1 2026), and converts earnings to cash reliably (FY 2025 FCF £1,913m; H1 2026 FCF £769m). Productivity initiatives—£800m gross supply-chain savings targeted by 2030, plus operating-model simplification—are delivering measurable margin expansion even as organic sales grow only mid-single digits.

Capital allocation reinforces the dividend and GARP screens: FY 2025 total dividend rose 7.6% (final proposed 4.9p); H1 2026 interim dividend 2.4p (+9%); £457m of a £500m 2026 buyback completed by end-H1; and an August 2026 cash tender for $2.0bn of 2027 notes signals active balance-sheet management. Leverage is at management’s ~2.5x target, down from 2.8x at end-2024.

The value hook is therefore “quality compounder at a reasonable price”: investors are paid via FCF yield, buybacks, and dividends while awaiting organic growth to re-accelerate toward guidance. The screen’s adjusted Hold overlay correctly flags that statutory reported operating profit fell 2.6% in H1 2026 due to £168m of operating-model restructuring costs—growth-dependent metrics should be treated cautiously until adjusted and reported paths converge.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Growth shortfall vs ambition.** FY 2025 organic growth of 3.0% and H1 2026 at 2.6% sit below the stated 4–6% medium-term range. Respiratory Health remains structurally exposed to cold/flu incidence ((4.7)% H1 2026 organic; (1.9)% FY 2025). North America was negative in FY 2025 ((0.4)% organic) and only modestly positive in H1 2026 (+2.0%). The market reaction to FY 2025 results (“Haleon shares fall 3.4% as revenue growth falls short of the company's own medium-term target,” Proactive Investors, 25 February 2026) underscores this as the primary sentiment risk.

**Restructuring and operating-model change.** The January 2026 operating-model evolution (six operating units, Chief Growth Officer, Chief Transformation Officer) introduces execution risk. H1 2026 restructuring costs of £168m drove reported operating profit down 2.6% despite strong adjusted performance. Productivity savings of £175–200m over two years are targeted, but delivery is not yet proven at scale.

**Cyclical and category mix.** Seasonal OTC (Respiratory) and discretionary categories (e.g. Smokers’ Health in North America) create earnings volatility not fully captured by trailing multiples. Management explicitly cites “global geopolitical and macroeconomic uncertainties” (Q1 2026 trading statement).

**Channel and competitive dynamics.** January 2026 media coverage flagged “channel shift concerns” (Proactive Investors, 22 January 2026). Private-label and retailer consolidation in US drug channel remain background risks, though filings note consumption outperforming market in several periods.

**Governance transition.** Sir Dave Lewis stepped down as Chair on 31 December 2025; Vindi Banga succeeded from 1 January 2026 (RNS, 10 November 2025). Orderly transition, but new chair tenure is untested at Haleon.

**Leverage and pension.** Net debt/adjusted EBITDA at 2.5–2.6x is at management’s optimal level—not alarming, but limits M&A flexibility. Yahoo balance sheet shows non-current pension obligations of ~£114m (FY 2025)— immaterial relative to £7.2bn net debt but worth monitoring. August 2026 tender offer for 2027 USD notes reflects proactive refinancing rather than distress.

**Accounting / adjusted vs statutory divergence.** H1 2026 adjusted EPS +12.0% vs reported diluted EPS (8.5p, (4.5)%) diverge materially due to restructuring. The quantitative screen’s earnings-basis overlay adjusts the signal from Buy to Hold on this basis—appropriate caution for value screens reliant on EPS growth.

**Going concern / contingencies.** H1 2026 auditor review: no material going-concern uncertainties identified; standard caveat that future events may change this. FY 2025 forward-looking statements reference “outcome of contingencies such as legal proceedings” without quantified provisions in the results body extract. Full litigation disclosure (legacy GSK separation liabilities, product liability) is not available in the indexed 20-F body extract—**verify before trade**.
