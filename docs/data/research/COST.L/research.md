# Costain Group PLC (COST.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:42:27.446385+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Costain is a UK infrastructure solutions specialist — transport, water, energy and defence — that has rebuilt earnings quality after a painful contracting reset, and now trades on modest multiples (P/E ~15.5×, P/B ~2.2×, yield ~1.9%) despite a net-cash balance sheet and a record £7.0bn forward work position. FY2025 revenue fell to £1,045.7m as legacy road frameworks rolled off and HS2 was rescheduled, yet adjusted operating margin expanded to 4.5% and adjusted free cash flow reached £63.1m. H1 2026 interim results (13 August 2026) confirm profit growth, net cash of £164.4m, and unchanged full-year guidance, with management targeting a revenue and margin step-change from FY2027 as AMP8 water and grid investment ramp. The central debate is whether margin expansion and capital returns (doubled interim dividend, £20m buyback) justify the screen’s buy signal while reported revenue is still declining and Transportation margins remain thin — or whether the £7bn order book and customer diversification into regulated utilities and aviation de-risk the cyclical construction narrative.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags Costain as a buy across five factor families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk — with 20/20 data completeness and passes on Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Lynch/Neff PEG variants, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Piotroski F-Score and Financial Health. That profile fits a post-turnaround contractor: low leverage (debt/equity ~10% on screen inputs), ROE ~15.1%, and FCF of £48.5m (Yahoo, consistent with FY2025 filing adjusted FCF of £63.1m).

Business quality has improved materially. Management’s stated KPI — adjusted operating margin — rose from 3.0% (FY2023) to 3.4% (FY2024) to 4.5% (FY2025), exceeding their 4.5% run-rate target per the FY2025 annual report extract. The contract portfolio is now predominantly long-term target-cost frameworks with no single-stage lump-sum contracts at year-end FY2025. Forward work grew from £5.4bn (FY2024) to a record £7.0bn (FY2025), maintained through H1 2026, with customer mix shifting toward private/regulated clients (48% of forward work at H1 2026 vs 30% in FY2023 per interim RNS).

Balance-sheet strength supports the value case: net cash £189.3m at FY2025 year-end (FY2024: £158.5m), undrawn £100m RCF extended to September 2030, and bonding facilities of £295m with utilisation of £71.2m at H1 2026. The January 2026 pension agreement removed dividend-parity constraints and eliminated scheduled deficit contributions through January 2031, with the scheme in a £62.9m IAS 19 surplus at 30 June 2026 — a material unlock for shareholder returns.

For a value investor, the hook is a cash-rich, improving-margin business on mid-teens earnings multiples, with visible multi-year revenue from regulated infrastructure cycles (AMP8 water ~£100bn industry capex; RIIO-3 grid upgrade) and a credible path to >5% adjusted operating margins — the medium-term ambition repeated in both the FY2025 annual report and H1 2026 interim. The screen’s neutral timing signal (RSI ~59, price slightly above 200-day MA) suggests accumulation rather than urgency.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Contract and programme execution.** HS2 remains a material exposure. FY2025 revenue was reduced by a revised HS2 schedule; H1 2026 notes the programme is “navigating a change in its programme delivery strategy” with Skanska-Costain-Strabag JV discussions underway. Sector news (“UK Construction Stocks To Watch After HS2 Old Oak Common Cost Overruns”, 6 August 2026) highlights industry-wide cost pressure, even if Costain’s portfolio is now target-cost weighted.

**Revenue vs margin tension.** Four years of declining revenue may concern cyclical screens despite margin gains. Transportation division margins (2.5% H1 2026) lag Natural Resources (6.9%) and remain vulnerable to road project mix and HS2 pacing.

**Customer and regulatory concentration.** Forward work still includes 29% central government (H1 2026), with HS2, National Highways and defence nuclear programmes prominent. Water and energy exposure introduces Ofwat/Ofgem regulatory risk on allowed returns and cost recovery — mitigated by long frameworks but not eliminated.

**Working capital and cash timing.** FY2026 net cash guidance (~£170–175m) assumes partial unwind of historic working capital benefits and higher shareholder returns. H1 adjusted FCF was negative; reliance on H2 weighting introduces execution risk.

**Pension.** Technical provisions funding >101% triggered contribution holidays, and the January 2026 agreement removed dividend parity and future cash contributions through January 2031. However, the scheme retains longevity, inflation and investment risks; interim notes flag potential Virgin Media / NTL judgement implications under the Pension Schemes Bill. IAS 19 surplus (£62.9m) could reverse with discount-rate or mortality changes.

**Governance and going concern.** Interim going-concern assessment (directors’ report, H1 2026) adopts a 12+ month horizon with covenant headroom under base case and “severe but plausible” downside (−30% revenue on work to be secured, −300bps margin). No going-concern qualification. Principal risks unchanged from FY2025 annual report pages 36–39 (not fully extracted in annual body files): (1) safety/environment, (2) securing work/customer spending, (3) contract/economic management, (4) project mobilisation/delivery, (5) supply chain, (6) talent, (7) financial resilience, (8) information security, (9) climate/sustainability.

**Competitive and talent.** Framework bidding remains competitive (TfL, National Grid, water AMP8). Skills shortages in engineering/construction are a stated principal risk.

**Accounting.** Long-term contract revenue recognition and HS2 programme estimates are flagged as significant judgements (unchanged from FY2025 annual report page 120 per interim note 2).
