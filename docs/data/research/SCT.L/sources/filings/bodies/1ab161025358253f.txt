The Group delivered strong growth in gross invoiced income, gross profit and underlying operating profit, along with excellent cash generation. Chief Executive Officer’s Review Performance and market conditions Our outstanding performance in FY2026 significantly exceeded our expectations at the beginning of the year. Softcat’s continued success reflects the strength of our special culture, the depth of our customer relationships and the benefits of sustained investment in our people, capabilities, systems, data and office network. Our proposition spans the full stack of modern infrastructure and is increasingly aligned to the technologies customers need most as they prepare for AI adoption at scale. We remain focused on enhancing our offering within a disciplined strategic framework, while continuing to invest in the capabilities, tools and operating model required to capture the significant long-term opportunity ahead. During the year, customer numbers grew by 3.4%, with growth across all segments, while gross profit per customer increased by 20.6%, reflecting further progress in selling more to existing customers across our technology portfolio. AI is manifesting in very positive ways across our business. As it becomes more deeply embedded in the applications used by organisations every day, it is placing greater demands on the infrastructure layer where our offering is focused. Customers need more powerful and versatile compute, faster and lower-latency storage, more resilient networking and connectivity, stronger cyber security, and data architecture and governance capable of supporting AI safely and at scale. The market remains in the early stages of this adoption cycle, and we are very well positioned to benefit given the breadth and depth of our offering, the scale and diversity of our customer base, and our trusted adviser status with both customers and vendors. Our market continues to be influenced by geopolitical uncertainty and industry-wide supply challenges for memory and storage products, driven by unprecedented AI infrastructure investment. Against this backdrop, customers remain disciplined and selective, with greater scrutiny of discretionary projects and a continued focus on demonstrable business outcomes and return on investment. However, IT infrastructure is becoming ever more foundational to organisational resilience and growth, and demand remains strong in areas that are difficult to defer, including security, hybrid infrastructure, networking, cloud optimisation, data platforms, AI enablement and managed services. While uncertainty around memory pricing persists, our close relationships with a range of key vendors help us advise customers, identify alternatives and provide flexible solutions. We have also been modernising our own systems, data and digital platforms for several years with investments in data architecture, automation and AI-enabled tools beginning to enhance the way our people work. Alongside this, we are developing in-house agents across key processes and adopting AI functionality within our enterprise systems. These investments are improving customer and employee experience, increasing the effectiveness of our go-to-market activity, and strengthening the scalability of our business as we continue to grow. Customer technology priorities and industry trends AI and other factors continue to stimulate demand across our entire technology proposition, and there is broad recognition from customers that their infrastructure estates must evolve at pace. These environments are often highly complex, built over many years across a large number of different vendor’s technologies, which makes trusted, independent advice and support increasingly important. Softcat is very well placed in this environment, combining access to leading vendor innovation with a deep understanding of customer environments to help organisations plan, implement and integrate technology fit for the age of AI. Our latest customer experience survey reinforces this direction of travel. Customers continue to prioritise maximising the impact of their IT investments, using technology to drive innovation, while strengthening governance and compliance. Cost control, business continuity, sourcing and procurement also remain prominent priorities. Together, these findings show that customers are looking for partners who can help them simplify increasingly complex estates, improve resilience and control, and translate technology investment into measurable outcomes. We deliver this support through a combination of product procurement, technical advice, solution design, integration, multi-vendor logistics, financing and ongoing support services, aided by the commercial benefits of our vendor accreditations. As vendors build the support and service structures required for a new generation of technology, they are increasingly relying on partners with the reach, skills and customer intimacy to deliver successfully. Softcat brings together decades of know-how and its proprietary data to augment the capabilities of both customers and vendors, reinforcing our position as one of the very best partners for each. Gross profit, revenue and gross invoiced income Our outstanding FY2026 performance demonstrates the strength of our business model and the benefits of consistent strategic execution. We continued to support a diverse range of new and existing customers with their IT infrastructure requirements, drawing on our deep expertise, comprehensive product and services offering, and the exceptional service delivered by our highly engaged employees. We are also benefitting from the recent investments we’ve made in systems and data, people and our office network. These investments have strengthened our capabilities, improved our internal operations and supported further market share gains and long-term sustainable growth. They also position us well to capture the benefits of AI, both within our own business and for customers, as more organisations move from experimentation to broader deployment. Gross profit (GP), our primary measure of income, grew by 24.7% to £616.3m reflecting the strength of our underlying business performance. Growth was supported by the contribution from previously disclosed larger solutions projects, the pull-forward of some customer orders due to memory shortages, and a positive vendor rebate contribution. GP growth was broad-based across customer segments and the technology portfolio. Growth in corporate was particularly strong, led by small and medium together with enterprise, both of which delivered strong double-digit growth, supported by high single-digit growth in public sector. By technology area, GP growth was also broad-based, with strong double-digit growth across data centre infrastructure, networking and security, and workplace, the latter supported by a clear improvement in demand for client devices. By product type, software, hardware and services GP all grew double-digit. Hardware growth was primarily driven by datacentre and networking infrastructure, including the contribution from larger solutions projects, together with client devices. Software GP growth was broad-based with strength in security solutions and Microsoft CSP, while services growth was primarily driven by the contribution from externally provided partner services. Revenue is reported in accordance with IFRS 15 with some transactions (generally hardware, professional services and internally delivered support and managed services) reported gross (principal) and others (generally software and externally provided support and managed services) reported net (agent) which can make revenue trends hard to understand. We therefore continue to report GII to help provide a clearer view of underlying growth and to support understanding of key balance sheet movements. FY2026 revenue grew overall by 35.4% driven by: (1) hardware revenue growth of 42.0% reflecting strong datacentre, networking, server and compute sales, supported by larger solutions projects. Hardware accounts for a higher mix of revenue than GII and is the main reason total revenue growth exceeds GII growth during the year; (2) software revenue growth of 22.6% which was slightly behind GII growth of 25.3%, primarily reflecting a mix shift into lower margin products including Microsoft CSP agreements; and (3) services revenue growth of 21.1% which was also behind GII growth, reflecting a higher share of externally provided services at lower margin (when reported on a net basis). GII increased 30.5% to £4,719.7m, mainly driven by strong growth in hardware, as discussed above. Software GII grew by 25.3% with strength in cybersecurity licencing software together with Microsoft CSP deals, while services growth of 30.1% was driven by a growing share of partner provided business. As shown in the table below, GII growth decelerated slightly to 28.5% in H2 compared with 33.3% in H1, primarily reflecting the greater contribution in H1 from larger solutions projects and some customer orders pulled forward due to memory shortages. During FY26, GII grew ahead of GP with gross margin declining year-on-year to 13.1% (FY2025: 13.7%). The decline was driven by strong performance in lower margin products including client devices and more public sector work going via framework arrangements. In addition, Microsoft grew strongly with margin impacted by the fee structure changes which caused a mix from EAs (Enterprise Agreements) into CSPs (Cloud Solution Providers) and larger solution deals at low margins had a dilutive impact, with these two factors mainly impacting H1. Gross profit growth accelerated in H2, despite the stronger base comparative and lower contribution from the large solution projects. This acceleration was driven by broad based performance across all technologies and customer segments but with particular strength in small and medium customers and a higher number of deals over £0.5m. Operating profitability and investment in future growth Underlying operating profit of £215.1m (FY2025: £180.1m) increased by 19.4% year-on-year. This reflects GP growth of 24.7%, partially offset by a rise of 27.7% in underlying operating costs, which includes both the ongoing and one-off cost investments we have made in the year to drive future growth. Underlying operating cost growth was driven by increased commissions in line with commissionable GP growth and a 16.0% increase in wages and salaries. During the year, average headcount grew by 10.8% (7.7% year-on-year excluding Oakland), while closing headcount grew by 9.7% year-on-year (8.0% growth excluding Oakland). In addition to the annualised impact of the step up in Employers’ National Insurance Contributions, underlying operating costs also include significant investment in expanding our internal IT team and corporate development capability, investment in our data and digital strategy, and office refurbishment costs. In addition, several one-time items were also recognised in underlying operating costs, including the one-off free share award to employees, an overperformance element of variable pay and employee incentives, and advisory costs relating to the acquisition of GDT. As a result of the above, the ratio of underlying operating profit to gross profit has decreased to 34.9% (FY2025: 36.4%). Statutory operating profit of £196.4m (FY2025: £172.9m) increased by 13.6% year-on-year, reflecting the impact of non-underlying costs of £18.7m (FY2025: £7.2m). Non-underlying costs Non-underlying costs recognised during the year include system development costs of £15.6m relating to the implementation of the new cloud-based sales system and HR system. In addition, there is a £3.1m charge relating to the acquisition of Oakland. Corporation tax charge The effective tax rate (ETR) for FY2026 was 25.8% (FY2025: 25.4%). The difference between the ETR and the UK statutory rate of 25.0% primarily reflects the disallowable deferred consideration relating to the acquisition of Oakland and a small impact from non-deductible expenses. Our tax strategy continues to be focused on paying the right amount of tax in the right jurisdiction, at the right time. Cash flow and cash conversion Cash and cash equivalents at the FY2026 balance sheet date increased by £17.6m to £199.9m (FY2025: £182.3m). Our capital allocation policy continues to prioritise investment in long-term organic growth to facilitate further share gains in our expanding addressable market; secondly to maintain a progressive ordinary dividend. Remaining excess capital is then either allocated to compelling strategic investments or is returned to shareholders, subject to net debt leverage being within our target range. Alternative Performance Measures The Group uses several non-Generally Accepted Accounting Practice (‘non-GAAP’) financial measures in addition to those reported in accordance with IFRS. The Directors believe that these non-GAAP measures which are set out below, assist in providing additional useful information on the underlying trends, sales performance and position of the Group. Consequently, non-GAAP measures are used by the Directors and management for performance analysis, planning and reporting. These non-GAAP measures comprise gross invoiced income (or ‘GII’), underlying operating profit, underlying cash conversion and underlying basic EPS. Gross invoiced income is a measure which correlates closely to the cash received by the business and therefore aids the user’s understanding of working capital movements in the statement of financial position and the relationship to sales performance and the mix of products sold. Gross invoiced income reflects gross income billed to customers adjusted for deferred and accrued revenue as reported in the IFRS measure. Going Concern Overview These financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis covering at least the twelve month period from the date of signing the financial statements. In considering the going concern basis for preparing the financial statements, the Directors consider the Group and Company’s objectives and strategy, its principal risks and uncertainties in achieving its objectives and its review of business performance and financial position, which are all set out in the Strategic Report (see pages 14 to 17) and Chief Financial Officer’s review sections (see pages 20 to 23 of the Annual Report). The Directors have undertaken a fully comprehensive going concern review. The Group has modelled four scenarios in its assessment of going concern. These are: • The base case; • A severe but plausible downside scenario; • Reverse stress testing; and • A scenario incorporating the proposed acquisition of GDT. Further details, including the analysis performed and conclusion reached, are set out below. The Directors have reviewed detailed financial forecasts for a twelve-month period from the date of this report to October 2027. The modelling has been extended beyond this period to provide visibility over the 31 January 2028 £50m Revolving Credit Facility (‘RCF’) covenant reporting date and to provide a robust basis for the downside analysis. Liquidity and financing position At 31 July 2026, the Group held instantly accessible cash and cash equivalents of £199.9m, with net current assets of £339.2m. Note 1 to the financial statements in the Annual Report includes the Group’s objectives, policies and processes for managing its capital, its financial risk management and its exposures to credit risk and liquidity risk. Operational cash flow forecasts for the going concern period are sufficient to support the Group in maintaining its target liquidity headroom throughout the going concern assessment period with the existing RCF remaining undrawn. Challenging economic environment The Directors have, in all four scenarios, considered the principal challenges to short term business performance which are expected to be; • a deterioration in economic conditions across the markets in which the Group and its customers operate, including the effects of inflation and interest rates; and • higher risk of credit losses. Despite the challenging economic environment, the Group and Company have traded well, delivering double-digit year-on-year growth in gross profit and operating profit growth ahead of expectations. The Board continue to monitor the economic environment and organise operations accordingly. Base case The base case takes into account the FY2027 Budget and three-year plan, including estimated growth and increased costs across the going concern period. The key inputs and assumptions in the base case include: • continued GII growth; • working capital assumptions reflecting recent historical experience and trends; • employee commissions incurred in line with gross margin; • increased levels of cost reflecting continued investment in our people and the business; and • ordinary dividends paid in accordance with the Group’s dividend policy with additional distribution of excess cash, either through special dividends or buybacks, based on historical trends. • The base case also reflects the net proceeds from the equity issue completed in September 2026, which are not contingent on completion of the proposed acquisition. The Group has taken a measured approach to the base case and has balanced expected trading conditions with the opportunities available to the business. The Group remains highly cash generative and maintains significant liquidity throughout the going concern period, with the existing RCF remaining undrawn. Year to date trading to the end of September 2026 is consistent with the base case forecast. Severe but plausible case Given the potential economic challenges facing the Group’s customer base and supply chain, the Directors have also modelled a severe but plausible scenario. This scenario considers the combined impact of lower sales and margins, slower customer payments and increased credit losses. The key inputs and assumptions, compared to the base case, include: • a 5% reduction in GII/revenue; • a 0.5% reduction in gross margin; • extending debtor days by an additional three days; • additional bad debt or other material profit and loss write-offs of £5m per annum; • a reduction in commission costs in line with gross margin; and • ordinary dividends continuing to be paid in accordance with the Group's dividend policy. The purpose of this scenario was to consider if there was a significant risk that the Group and Company would not meet its target liquidity headroom or breach the Group’s debt covenants in any of the months in the going concern period. Even at these lower levels of activity, which the Directors believe is a highly unlikely outcome, the Group continues to be profitable and maintains a positive cash balance at all times, with no breach of the Group’s debt covenants. The Directors therefore did not consider it necessary to assume further mitigating actions in assessing this scenario. Reverse stress test The Directors have performed reverse stress testing to understand the level of deterioration that would be required to threaten the going concern status of the Group. Given the Group’s significant cash resources and liquidity under the base case, the reverse stress testing considered the level of deterioration that would be required to result in a breach of the Group’s financing covenants. The analysis indicates that an exceptionally significant reduction in profitability, together with a substantial deterioration in the Group’s forecast liquidity position, would be required before a covenant breach occurs. The Board considers the assumptions required to result in such an outcome, and the events that could lead to them, to be remote. Proposed GDT acquisition As part of the going concern assessment, the Directors have also considered the potential impact of the proposed acquisition of GDT, announced on 17 September 2026. A separate scenario was prepared incorporating GDT’s forecast financial performance, the anticipated acquisition consideration and financing structure including covenants, associated financing costs and the expected post-acquisition liquidity requirements and the desired group leverage. Under this scenario, the combined Group retains sufficient liquidity and remains compliant with the expected financing covenants throughout the assessment period. The Directors also performed additional stress testing of the expected financing covenants, including a scenario assuming materially all available external debt facilities were drawn, which is materially worse than the base GDT acquisition scenario. The testing demonstrated significant headroom against the covenants. The Directors consider the circumstances necessary to result in a covenant breach under these scenarios to be remote. Accordingly, the analysis performed in respect of the proposed acquisition does not change the Directors’ conclusion regarding the appropriateness of the going concern basis of preparation. Going concern conclusion Based on the forecasts and scenarios modelled, together with the performance and financial position of the Group and Company to date, the Directors consider that the Group and Company have sufficient liquidity headroom to continue in operational existence throughout the going concern assessment period to October 2027. Accordingly, at the October 2026 Board meeting, the Directors concluded from this analysis it was appropriate to continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the consolidated financial statements. Should the impact of these conditions be even more prolonged or severe than currently forecast by the Directors under the severe but plausible case scenario, the Group and Company would need to implement additional operational or financial measures. In relation to the identified potential climate change-related risks and opportunities, the Directors do not believe there would be a material impact on cash flows in the going concern period. Business combinations Acquisitions in the prior period On 4 April 2025, the Group acquired 100% of the share capital of Oakland Group Services Limited, a non-listed company based in the United Kingdom and specialising in the provision of data platform, data strategy, data governance, data analytics and artificial intelligence consultancy. The Group acquired Oakland Group Services Limited because it significantly enhances the Group’s capability in providing these services to existing and new customers. The business was acquired for a total cash consideration of £8.0m with total identifiable net assets of £2.7m, generating goodwill of £6.7m. The purchase agreement included additional contingent consideration with service conditions and contingent consideration without service conditions. The additional contingent consideration without service conditions of up to £1.6m is payable only if the average profit performance for Oakland’s 2026, 2027 and 2028 financial years exceeds targeted levels agreed by both parties. The additional contingent consideration will be payable on 31 March 2027 and 31 March 2028. The additional contingent consideration liability recognised, represents the present value of the Group’s probability-weighted estimate of the cash outflow. It reflects management’s probability-weighted estimate of achieving the base case, downside case and upside case targets. The liability for additional contingent consideration with service conditions is not classified as consideration transferred under IFRS 3, is disclosed as other employment costs. As part of the acquisition, the Group obtained a minority equity interest in a private limited company. The investment does not provide the Group with control or significant influence over the investee and has therefore been recognised as a financial asset.
