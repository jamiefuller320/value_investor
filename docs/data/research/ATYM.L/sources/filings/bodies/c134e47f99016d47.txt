(“Atalaya” or the “Company”) Atalaya Acquires Tharsis to Expand Position in the Iberian Pyrite Belt Acquisition to further enhance growth options within core region Atalaya Mining (LSE: ATYM) is pleased to announce that it has completed the acquisition of 100% of the shares of Tharsis Mining, S.L. (“Tharsis Mining”), a private Spanish company that owns the past-producing Tharsis and La Zarza projects (“Tharsis Project” and “La Zarza Project”, respectively), which are located west of Atalaya’s Riotinto complex in southwest Spain (the "Acquisition"). The transaction consolidates Atalaya’s footprint in the Iberian Pyrite Belt through the acquisition of two large-scale polymetallic deposits which are expected to become important growth options for the Company and be sequenced beyond the development of Proyecto Touro. Total consideration for the Acquisition is €125 million, consisting of €75 million in upfront cash consideration paid on completion and deferred cash consideration of €50 million payable within 12 months of the closing date. The upfront consideration was satisfied from Atalaya’s existing cash resources. Tharsis Mining is a private Spanish company that owns 100% interests in the Tharsis Project and the La Zarza Project, two past-producing assets that are located in Andalucía in southern Spain and within the Iberian Pyrite Belt. Tharsis Mining was founded in 2018 and has since focused on the objective of re-starting mining operations at both projects. In addition, Tharsis Mining’s licence package extends beyond the currently defined deposits and includes numerous prospective targets that remain only partially explored. Prior to the Acquisition, Tharsis Mining was wholly owned by members of the López Magdaleno family (the “Sellers”), who control various business interests including the Magtel Group, a Spanish technology-based company with over 35 years of experience in the design, construction, and maintenance of projects in the energy, engineering, telecommunications, digital transformation and infrastructure sectors. Within the Riotinto District, Atalaya is advancing several projects including San Dionisio and Proyecto Masa Valverde, and in Galicia, Atalaya expects to soon begin development of Proyecto Touro. The acquisition of Tharsis Mining immediately expands Atalaya’s presence in the Iberian Pyrite Belt by providing 100% ownership of two substantial past-producing mines (which mainly produced pyrite) that are located west of Proyecto Riotinto, the Company’s flagship asset. Similar to Riotinto, the Tharsis Project and the La Zarza Project have long operating histories, significant remaining resources and benefit from their location in a world-class mining district and stable geography. The combined Mineral Resources at the Tharsis Project and the La Zarza Project are approximately 227.7 Mt containing approximately 1.5 Mt of copper, along with approximately 3.5 Mt of zinc, 1.4 Mt of lead, 7.5 Moz of gold and 157.4 Moz of silver. Within this Mineral Resource, the open pit components total approximately 199.2 Mt, making the projects the only large-scale deposits in the Iberian Pyrite Belt potentially mineable by open pit, alongside Riotinto. The deposits have been drilled extensively by Tharsis Mining and other past operators, and the polymetallic nature of their mineralisation provides significant optionality and the opportunity to benefit from enhancements to recoveries and improving payment terms for complex concentrates. In the Riotinto District, Atalaya has its own polymetallic resources at deposits including San Dionisio, San Antonio and Proyecto Masa Valverde, and the Company is currently advancing engineering works associated with the addition of a polymetallic circuit at Riotinto, which could provide learnings that may be applied at Tharsis Mining. The transaction also provides exposure to a package of licenses with meaningful exploration potential, reinforcing Atalaya’s position within one of the world’s premier volcanogenic massive sulphide (“VMS”) provinces. Atalaya believes it is the ideal operator for Tharsis Mining, given Atalaya’s technical, operational and exploration capabilities, financial strength and longstanding stakeholder relationships in the Riotinto District and in Andalucía. Atalaya plans to leverage its regional platform to advance technical studies, key permitting steps and exploration activities across Tharsis Mining’s portfolio. In addition, it is expected that various operational and administrative synergies could be realised between Riotinto, Masa Valverde and any future mining operations at the Tharsis Project and the La Zarza Project. Transaction Terms Atalaya has completed the acquisition of 100% of the shares of Tharsis Mining, which, through its subsidiaries, owns the Tharsis Project, the La Zarza Project and associated exploration interests in the province of Huelva, Spain. The share purchase agreement was executed and the Acquisition completed simultaneously, providing Atalaya with full ownership and control of Tharsis Mining. Total consideration for the Acquisition is €125 million, consisting of €75 million in upfront cash consideration paid on completion and deferred cash consideration of €50 million payable within 12 months of the closing date, which is secured by a bank guarantee and will accrue interest at 12-month EURIBOR plus 1.5%. Existing Atalaya shareholders will experience no equity dilution as a result of the Acquisition. Payment of the upfront consideration was satisfied from Atalaya’s existing cash resources, which remain robust with over €260 million in pro-forma net cash following the initial payment. It is expected that ongoing cash generation will further support Atalaya’s balance sheet strength. At closing of the Acquisition, all outstanding balances and loans between Tharsis Mining and the Sellers (or their affiliates) were settled, extinguished or acquired by Atalaya as part of the total consideration. Certain identified legacy and contingent liabilities, currently estimated at approximately €7 million, will remain obligations of the acquired Tharsis Mining group following completion. Tharsis Project Overview The Tharsis Project is a past-producing project located in Andalucía, southern Spain, and approximately 100 km west of Seville, 50 km north of Huelva and 50 km west of Atalaya’s Proyecto Riotinto complex. The Tharsis Project is located next to the village of Tharsis and benefits from good road connections and the same established regional infrastructure as Proyecto Riotinto, including access to power networks, a skilled mining workforce and the deep-water port at Huelva. The Tharsis Project consists of several VMS deposits that contain copper-zinc-lead-silver-gold mineralisation. The deposits were first mined in the 1850s and were exploited principally for their pyrite in order to produce sulphuric acid, with 34 Mt of raw pyrite mined by various operators via open pit and underground methods. In 2001, mining activities ceased. Figure 2: Tharsis Project Aerial View Tharsis Mining acquired the Tharsis Project in 2018, after which it completed a 77,536 metre drilling campaign from 2020-2022. This campaign formed the basis for a Mineral Resource Estimate which was completed by CSA Global in 2022. La Zarza Project Overview The La Zarza Project is a past-producing project located in Andalucía which sits approximately 85 km northwest of Seville and 25 km west of Atalaya’s Proyecto Riotinto complex. The La Zarza Project is located next to the village of La Zarza and benefits from the same modern infrastructure as Proyecto Riotinto, including access to modern roads, skilled labour and the port at Huelva. Mineralisation at La Zarza consists of typical VMS deposits, as well as associated “Silicatado” deposits, both containing copper, zinc-lead and precious metals. La Zarza was first mined by the Romans and Phoenicians, who focused on copper and gold-silver gossans, and then on a small scale in the 1850s. In the 1860s, mining was conducted on a larger scale via open pit and underground methods, with a focus on extracting pyrite for the production of sulphuric acid. Mining activities stopped in 1991, after various operators had extracted approximately 40 Mt of pyrite over the decades. Glossary of Terms Ag Silver Au Gold CIM Canadian Institute of Mining, Metallurgy and Petroleum Cu Copper g/t Grams per tonne Indicated Mineral Resources An Indicated Mineral Resource is that part of a Mineral Resource for which quantity, grade or quality, densities, shape and physical characteristics are estimated with sufficient confidence to allow the application of Modifying Factors in sufficient detail to support mine planning and evaluation of the economic viability of the deposit. Geological evidence is derived from adequately detailed and reliable exploration, sampling and testing and is sufficient to assume geological and grade or quality continuity between points of observation. An Indicated Mineral Resource has a lower level of confidence than that applying to a Measured Mineral Resource and may only be converted to a Probable Mineral Reserve. Inferred Mineral Resource An Inferred Mineral Resource is that part of a Mineral Resource for which quantity and grade or quality are estimated on the basis of limited geological evidence and sampling. Geological evidence is sufficient to imply but not verify geological and grade or quality continuity. An Inferred Mineral Resource has a lower level of confidence than that applying to an Indicated Mineral Resource and must not be converted to a Mineral Reserve. It is reasonably expected that the majority of Inferred Mineral Resources could be upgraded to Indicated Mineral Resources with continued exploration. kt Thousand tonnes Measured Mineral Resources A Measured Mineral Resource is that part of a Mineral Resource for which quantity, grade or quality, densities, shape, and physical characteristics are estimated with confidence sufficient to allow the application of Modifying Factors to support detailed mine planning and final evaluation of the economic viability of the deposit. Geological evidence is derived from detailed and reliable exploration, sampling and testing and is sufficient to confirm geological and grade or quality continuity between points of observation. A Measured Mineral Resource has a higher level of confidence than that applying to either an Indicated Mineral Resource or an Inferred Mineral Resource. It may be converted to a Proven Mineral Reserve or to a Probable Mineral Reserve. Mineral Resources A concentration or occurrence of material of intrinsic economic interest in or on the Earth's crust in such a form and quantity that there are reasonable prospects for eventual economic extraction. The location, quantity, grade, geological characteristics and continuity of a Mineral Resource are known, estimated or interpreted from specific geological evidence and knowledge. Mineral Resources are sub-divided, in order of increasing geological confidence, into Inferred, Indicated and Measured categories.
