We saw continued momentum in Sports Medicine & ENT, including double-digit growth in Joint Repair, and improved performance in Advanced Wound Management, led by acceleration in Advanced Wound Devices. Orthopaedics returned to growth, although US reconstruction remained under pressure in a softer market. “We continue to see the benefits of our investment in innovation, with more than 50% of Q3 growth coming from products launched in the last five years and many key products growing at double-digit rates. US regulatory approvals for LANDMARK and TESSA during the quarter represent important milestones, and early customer feedback following first cases has been extremely positive. “We have revised our full-year guidance to reflect the slower growth in US Orthopaedics, which we expect to continue through the fourth quarter. Despite this, we remain confident in delivering profit growth ahead of revenue growth, together with strong free cash flow and cash conversion. “We remain focused on improving performance, enhancing operational efficiency and allocating capital to the highest-value opportunities across our business, in line with the RISE strategy, while continuing to consider all opportunities that could accelerate value creation in the best interests of shareholders.” Delivering our RISE strategy We continued to make progress delivering against our RISE strategy, with important product milestones across the portfolio supporting our pillars to REACH more patients and to INNOVATE and enhance the standard of care. In September we received 510(k) clearance from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and completed the first surgical case with the cementless version of our new LANDMARK Total Knee System. LANDMARK is a next-generation knee replacement solution designed to replicate healthy knee anatomy and the knee’s natural motion and support a more personalised approach to total knee arthroplasty. LANDMARK was designed to leverage the full capabilities of our robotics-assisted CORI Surgical System. We also achieved an important clinical milestone for our new TESSA Spatial Surgery System, completing the first femoral cases, and submitted an application to the FDA for regulatory clearance for its use in tibial tunnel placement. Other advances included the launches of the new EVOS™ Pelvic Plating System, expanding the EVOS Plating Platform, the F LOW FLEXTEND ™ COBLATION ™ Wand, which brings new functionality for hard-to-reach hip, knee and shoulder, and the LYNX ™ COBLATION Laryngeal Wand in ENT . We also progressed the international launches of the CARTIHEAL AGILI-C™ Cartilage Repair Implant and ALLEVYN™ COMPLETE CARE Dressing. We continued to invest in next generation technology, with all R&D programmes on track and an exciting pipeline for 2027. These include CORI Hip, the first solution that provides robotic execution on both sides of the hip, including handheld acetabular reaming and a robotic femoral neck cut, particularly relevant to the fast-growing Direct Anterior Approach, and the CORI Hip Impactor which we expect to be the only tracked automated impactor on the market. During the quarter we announced a partnership with Imperial College London to accelerate innovation in surgical robotics. This five-year partnership is designed to deliver breakthrough research and innovation for robotic surgery for musculoskeletal conditions. Update on our Orthopaedics business The Board of Smith+Nephew is committed to delivering long-term shareholder value and we are actively focused on improving performance, driving sharper execution, enhancing operational efficiency and optimising our portfolio composition. We continue to prioritise investment behind our higher growth and margin Sports Medicine and Advanced Wound Management businesses where we have, through our RISE strategy, identified a clear path to market leadership which will deliver significant value. Under the 12-Point Plan we have made considerable progress strengthening our Orthopaedics business unit, including improving product supply and portfolio simplification, which has resulted in significant improvements in margins and returns on capital in the business. We continue to focus on improving commercial performance as recent product launches scale and additional products come to market. Given this strengthened position, the Board has for some months been considering strategic options for our Orthopaedics business. We note press comment as regards a possible combination between Johnson & Johnson’s Depuy Synthes business and Smith+Nephew. We confirm that, as part of that review of options, we approached Johnson & Johnson earlier in the year, and subsequently discussions did take place, but those conversations have now ended. We will continue to actively explore the full range of strategic options for the business as we develop the best outcome for our shareholders, patients, customers and colleagues. Share buyback on track On 6 May 2026, Smith+Nephew announced a share buyback of $500 million to be completed within twelve months. The buyback demonstrates our commitment to disciplined capital allocation, balancing investment in strategic growth with the return of surplus capital to shareholders, in line with our framework. The buyback is on track with $250 million completed by the end of the third quarter. Full year guidance updated The Group is updating its full year guidance to reflect our outlook for US Hips and Knees in the fourth quarter and market conditions. As a result, we now expect to deliver: Full year underlying revenue growth of around 3% (previously “around 4%”). On a reported basis, the guidance equates to around 4.0% based on exchange rates prevailing on 30 October 2026. As a result of the lower revenue outlook, trading profit growth is now expected to be around 4% (previously “around 8%”), with around $1.25 billion of trading profit (previously “around $1.3 billion”) including marginal dilution from the Integrity acquisition Free cash flow is now expected to be around $750 million (previously “around $800 million”), with strong trading cash conversion of around 95%, in line with original guidance Adjusted Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) is expected to be around 9% (previously “more than 10%”). Adjusted ROIC excludes the impact of Integrity Orthopaedics. As previously disclosed, we expect the net year-on-year impact of tariffs to be broadly neutral to trading profit, and the headwind from skin substitutes to be towards the upper end of the previously guided $20 to $40 million range. The acquisition of Integrity Orthopaedics is expected to be marginally dilutive to trading profit in 2026, broadly neutral in 2027 and accretive from 2028, as previously guided. All percentages compare to the equivalent 2025 period. ‘Underlying revenue growth’ reconciles to reported revenue growth, the most directly comparable financial measure calculated in accordance with IFRS, by making two adjustments, the ‘constant currency exchange effect’ and the ‘acquisitions and disposals effect’, described below. The ‘constant currency exchange effect’ is a measure of the increase/decrease in revenue resulting from currency movements on non-US Dollar sales and is measured as the difference between: 1) the increase/decrease in the current year revenue translated into US Dollars at the current year average exchange rate and the prior revenue translated at the prior year rate; and 2) the increase/decrease being measured by translating current and prior year revenues into US Dollars using the same exchange rates. The ‘acquisitions and disposals effect’ is the measure of the impact on revenue from newly acquired material business combinations and recent material business disposals. This is calculated by comparing the current year, constant currency actual revenue (which includes acquisitions and excludes disposals from the relevant date of completion) with prior year, constant currency actual revenue, adjusted to include the results of acquisitions and exclude disposals for the commensurate period in the prior year. These sales are separately tracked in the Group’s internal reporting systems and are readily identifiable. About Smith+Nephew Smith+Nephew is a portfolio medical technology business focused on the repair, regeneration and replacement of soft and hard tissue. We exist to restore people’s bodies and their self-belief by using technology to take the limits off living. We call this purpose ‘Life Unlimited’. Our 17,000 employees deliver this mission every day, making a difference to patients’ lives through the excellence of our product portfolio, and the invention and application of new technologies across our three global business units of Orthopaedics, Sports Medicine & ENT and Advanced Wound Management. Founded in Hull, UK, in 1856, we now operate in around 100 countries, and generated annual sales of $6.2 billion in 2025. Smith+Nephew is a constituent of the FTSE100 (LSE:SN, NYSE:SNN).
