About B&M European Value Retail plc B&M European Value Retail plc is a value retailer with 797 stores in the UK operating under the "B&M" brand, 339 stores under the "Heron Foods" and "B&M Express" brands, and 156 stores in France also operating under the "B&M" brand as at 26 September 2026. It is a constituent of the FTSE 250 index. The B&M Group was founded in 1978 and listed on the London Stock Exchange in June 2014. For more information, please visit www.bandmretail.com Chief Executive’s Review Back to B&M Basics fully implemented In my first presentation of Interim Results a year ago, I outlined a three-phase strategic growth plan for B&M UK, starting with the immediate priority of restoring sustainable LFL sales growth to our UK business through a set of actions we called Back to B&M Basics . Twelve months on, we have implemented all four Back to B&M Basics workstreams in full, sharpening price, improving on-shelf availability in key brands, boosting the effectiveness of our promotions and reducing SKU count across our FMCG ranges. We gave ourselves between 12 to 18 months to see the financial impact of our actions. Today, I am pleased to report these actions are delivering. The return of UK LFL sales growth of +3.2% in Q2 is an encouraging milestone. This was led by General Merchandise, which benefitted from an extended garden season following a slow weather-related start in Q1. It also included non-seasonal categories, including our Home ranges, where we are making everyday life more affordable for customers facing mounting cost-of-living pressures. Encouragingly, our UK FMCG LFL saw improvement across Q2, ending the quarter flat. This improvement has continued in early Q3 trading, with positive FMCG LFL, giving us confidence that the actions we have taken under Back to B&M Basics are working. Our General Merchandise LFL has also remained positive, which is an important contributor to our Golden Quarter sales. We have made further progress in restoring our UK General Merchandise category margin during the first half. We anticipate continued year-on-year improvements in the second half, which together with the restoration of UK LFL sales is a key driver of returning our UK adjusted EBITDA margin (pre-IFRS 16) to low double-digit levels over time, which we continue to see as a medium-term outcome for B&M UK. Phase 2: Boosting the productivity of our UK estate With Phase 1 now starting to deliver, we are making progress with our Phase 2 plans, where we see an opportunity to transform the customer experience of B&M and boost the productivity of our UK estate through investing in new store formats. This is a departure from our previous approach, which adopted a single retail format across different sized stores in a variety of locations. Now, we have identified six store clusters with common customer profiles and shopping missions, which we are re-laying stores to match. Alongside re-lays, we are also refitting some stores where we identify an opportunity to boost sales by enhancing the customer store experience. In August 2026, we launched our new B&M 2.0 concept store in Wigan, which embodies the design and layout elements of the B&M store of the future. Wigan 2.0 materially enhances the customer journey by introducing a landing zone, a more intuitive flow that plays to B&M's strength in General Merchandise and seasonal categories, wider aisles and a more efficient checkout experience enabled by the first installation of self-checkouts in B&M. As with Back to B&M Basics , our approach to B&M 2.0 is one of test-and-learn to determine which design elements drive the best sales outcomes. Our Wigan 2.0 store has been recognised by The Institute of Grocery Distribution (IGD), which selected it as IGD's Store of the Month for November 2026, commending it as an excellent example of global retail execution. Early trading at the store has been encouraging, with strong LFLs alongside positive category mix benefits. We plan to adopt the most successful format elements from Wigan 2.0 in all our new UK stores as they roll out, as well as in each store we refit, in order to progressively leverage these benefits to our wider UK estate. All re-lays and refits will be funded through internally generated cash, with the investment requirements in FY27 balanced by a slower pace of UK store rollouts than in recent years. We now expect 15-20 gross new UK stores in FY27, versus the 25-35 gross we previously guided to, reverting to 25-35 gross in FY28. The majority of this year’s new stores will be opened in the second half. This is reflected in the phasing of our capital investment in FY27, which will be weighted more towards H2. France: achieving critical scale B&M France continues to perform strongly, delivering mid-single-digit LFL sales growth across H1 (+5.9%) through higher footfall and market share gains. With 156 stores, we see B&M France achieving breakthrough scale and deeper awareness with customers, who are attracted by our focus on compelling value, newness and depth of choice across our Home categories. France’s Q2 LFL performance of +6.5% was delivered off a high base in Q2 FY26 (+9.4%), underscoring the strength and depth of the performance. B&M is taking share in France, growing transaction volumes alongside competitors that in many instances face operational and financial challenges. B&M France ended H1 with a 9.4% share of the discount market 1 , up +100bps since the end of FY26 and +140bps year-on-year. We still see a strong growth runway for B&M France and now expect to open 15-20 new stores in FY27, versus the 15 we guided to with FY26 Results. France remains several years behind the UK in market consolidation, which may yet provide us with opportunities to scale the pace of our store expansion there. To date, we have not seen a material impact of the social unrest in France on our business. Heron Foods: delivering growth as cost-of-living pressures intensify Heron delivered a second consecutive quarter of LFL growth, demonstrating the relevance of our everyday low price (EDLP) convenience offer in some of the UK’s most economically challenged urban areas at a time of rising cost-of-living pressures. We continue to review Heron’s customer proposition and plan to evolve its value offer through a wider range of front-of-store convenience categories. Leadership talent is a vital aspect of Heron’s future success and I am delighted to announce the appointment of Michael Fletcher as Managing Director of Heron Foods, effective 2 November 2026. Michael has extensive UK and International experience of a broad range of retail and wholesale operations, including senior roles at Tesco, Co-op and SPAR. His recent career has been particularly focused on developing and delivering award-winning propositions within the UK convenience sector, which will be of tremendous value to Heron as we position the business for future success. Michael takes on this role from Tony Dobbs, who has decided to step down after 35 years with Heron. I would like to thank Tony personally for everything he has contributed to Heron and the wider B&M Group. He leaves with my sincere thanks and very best wishes for the future. Culture and values Strategy alone cannot deliver organisational change. Culture plays a vital role in ensuring the transition we are bringing about is rooted in strong organisational foundations. That is why we embarked upon an extensive internal consultation in late 2025 to understand from our colleagues who we at B&M are today, and who we want to be in the future. The result is our new B&M DNA: a set of five core values which reflect the qualities and behaviours that colleagues told us matter most. Delighting our Customers, working as One Team, operating a business that is Simple and Agile, providing Opportunity for All and always Behaving Like an Owner: these values capture what makes B&M unique and provide a shared foundation for how we work together and serve our customers every day. Restoring Group profitability on solid financial foundations The operational performance we are now delivering for B&M is built on solid financial foundations. Our balance sheet leverage stood at 1.3x at the end of H1 FY27, well within our target range of 1.0 – 1.5x, reflecting prudent inventory management and the phasing of our capital investment for the year. We expect leverage to remain within this range during H2 FY27 now that our normal seasonal inventory build ahead of our Golden Quarter is behind us. We are restoring Group profitability through re-establishing UK LFL sales growth and improving our UK category margin while managing continued cost headwinds and investing in our business. This will support our ambition of achieving low double-digit adjusted EBITDA margins for B&M UK in the medium term as an outcome. Current trading and outlook Early Golden Quarter trading has been positive in each of our operating businesses, with continued LFL growth at B&M UK in both FMCG and General Merchandise. Taking into account year-to-date performance and current trading, we expect to deliver FY27 Group adjusted EBITDA (pre-IFRS 16) in a range of £450m - £500m (FY26: £459m) and Group adjusted PBT of £280m - £330m (FY26: £284m). The elements of B&M’s long-term growth algorithm remain in place. We have a clear growth story anchored in boosting productivity of our existing estate while deploying new stores into a substantial white space opportunity, both in the UK and France, generating considerable job creation in the communities we serve. We are restoring margins through improving category profitability as sustainable UK LFL growth returns. Our business enjoys strong cash conversion, which provides us with the ability to both invest in the organic growth of our business and support attractive shareholder returns. The past twelve months have challenged us. Yet they have also provided us opportunities to test and learn new execution, format and design elements with the potential to improve sales outcomes beyond the restoration of LFL growth we are bringing about. With Phase 1 delivering, this is now our focus as we continue to transform B&M and further our ambition to be the nation's favourite place to shop. Basis of consolidation This Group financial information consolidates the financial information of the Company and its subsidiary undertakings, together with the Group's share of the net assets and results of associated undertakings, for the period from 29 March 2026 to 26 September 2026. Acquisitions of subsidiaries are dealt with by the acquisition method of accounting, acquisitions of associates are dealt with by the equity method of accounting. The results of companies acquired are included in the consolidated statement of comprehensive income from the acquisition date. Control is achieved when the Group is exposed, or has rights, to variable returns from its involvement with the investee and has the ability to affect those returns through its power over the investee. Specifically, the Group controls an investee if and only if the Group has: Power over the investee (i.e. existing rights that give it the current ability to direct the relevant activities of the investee); Exposure, or rights, to variable returns from its involvement with the investee, and The ability to use its power over the investee to affect its returns. When the Group has less than a majority of the voting or similar rights of an investee, the Group considers all relevant facts and circumstances in assessing whether it has power over an investee, including: The contractual arrangement with the other vote holders of the investee; Rights arising from other contractual arrangements; and The Group’s voting rights and potential voting rights. The Group re-assesses whether it controls an investee if facts and circumstances indicate that there are changes to one or more of the three elements of control. Consolidation of a subsidiary begins when the Group obtains control over the subsidiary and ceases when the Group loses control of the subsidiary. Assets, liabilities, income and expenses of a subsidiary acquired or disposed of during the year are included in the statement of comprehensive income from the date the Group gains control until the date the Group ceases to control the subsidiary, excluding the situations as outlined in the basis of preparation. Restatement When preparing the 28 March 2026 accounts, the Group voluntarily restated the prior year reserves balances to reflect alignment between the share premium balance between the parent company and the Group. This difference arose as previously in the Group financial statements the credit previously recognised in retained earnings for share options accounting was reclassified to share premium when employee share options were exercised. This was not reflected in the parent company. The reclassification aligns the presentation in the Group accounts with that of the parent company. The comparative period ended 27 September 2025 has also been restated. The overall impact of the restatement was to increase retained earnings and decrease share premium by £10 million at 29 March 2025, as previously reported, and by a further £3 million at 27 September 2025. There was no impact on the profit or net assets in either period. No restatement was required for the period ended 28 March 2026 as the error had already been identified and reflected when this set of accounts was prepared. Reallocation of other reserve Our other reserve balance of £10m represented the statutory reserve brought forward from the apportionment of profit within each Luxembourg company. This was previously represented as the ‘legal reserve’ until the redomicile to Jersey completed early in 2026. Following completion of the redomicile in March 2026, this period we have recycled this reserve balance to the retained earnings reserve. Going concern As a value retailer, the Group is well placed to withstand volatility within the economic environment. The Group’s forecasts and projections, taking into account reasonably plausible changes in trading performance, show that the Group will trade within its current banking facilities. In assessing the Group’s going concern at the half year, the Directors have considered the business activities including the Group’s principal risks and uncertainties. The Directors have also considered the Group’s current cash position, the repayment profile of its obligations, its financial covenants and the resilience of its 12-month cash flow forecast to a series of severe but plausible downside scenarios. Having considered these factors, the Board is satisfied the Group has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. Consequently, the Directors are confident that the Group and Company will have sufficient funds to continue to meet its liabilities as they fall due for at least 12 months from the date of approval of the condensed consolidated interim financial statements and therefore have prepared the financial statements on a going concern basis. Critical judgments and key sources of estimation uncertainty Impairment The Group assesses at each reporting date whether there is an indication that an asset may be impaired. If any indication exists, or when annual impairment testing for an asset is required (for goodwill or indefinite life assets), the Group estimates the asset’s recoverable amount. The Group bases its impairment calculation on detailed budgets and forecasts which are prepared separately for each of the Group’s cash-generating units (CGUs) to which the individual assets are allocated. Indications of impairment might include (for goodwill and the brand assets, for instance) a significant decrease in the like-for-like sales of established stores as a consequence of sustained negative publicity. The B&M UK and Heron Foods impairment calculations reflect assumptions that are based upon management’s judgment. The key assumptions include the anticipated like-for-like sales performance and gross margin rates, which are based upon the historical performance of the entity and strategic decisions in relation to this entity. At the half year an analysis is carried out to assess if an additional review is required on an interim basis. This is the case if we identify a significant indicator of impairment having arisen in the period since the last full impairment review. Full details of the last full annual impairment review are disclosed in our Annual Report and Accounts for the year ended 28 March 2026. Assets with indefinite life Our B&M UK and B&M France segments each had significant headroom in respect of their indefinite asset base as at March 2026 (£3,500m and €938m respectively). Neither have exhibited performance which would constitute a significant indicator of impairment, and as such management consider that an interim test is not required. Heron’s headroom was much lower at £16m, however Heron’s performance has exceeded that projected in their impairment review, and there is no significant indicator that it will not do so for the remainder of the year. As such, any interim test carried out would not be expected to have a worse result than that reported at the year end date. Management therefore consider that an interim test is not required. The next full impairment review of assets with indefinite lives will be reported in the Group’s Annual Report and Accounts for the year ended 27 March 2027. Assets supported by the trade of our stores Management consider that each store within the Group represents a CGU, and as such a review of each store’s performance was carried out to identify significant indicators of impairment that would require an additional interim test for impairment. No stores were identified in the review of B&M France.
