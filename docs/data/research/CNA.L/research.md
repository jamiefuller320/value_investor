# Centrica plc (CNA.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T06:53:44.225922+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Centrica is a UK-integrated energy group pivoting from cyclical trading and upstream exposure towards regulated and contracted infrastructure (Sizewell C, Grain LNG, meter assets, nuclear life extensions), while retaining a large British Gas retail franchise. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cheapness (P/E ~10.2, yield ~3.7%), dividend quality, and balance-sheet strength (debt/equity ~74%, adjusted net cash per filings), though an FCF basis mismatch warrants caution on cash-flow-dependent models. FY2025 adjusted EBITDA fell to £1,417m from £2,305m as commodity-normalised earnings cooled, but the balance sheet remains robust (£1.5bn adjusted net cash at year-end; £709m at H1 2026 after Severn CCGT and heavy capex). The central debate is whether infrastructure investment and transformation can restore earnings towards the £1.7bn–£2.0bn EBITDA targets by 2028–2030, or whether regulatory intervention, retail bad debt, and trading volatility cap the re-rating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Centrica offers a rare combination in UK utilities: net cash, a growing dividend (5.5p FY2025, +22%; 2.0p interim H1 2026, +9%), active capital return (£2bn buyback completed in 2025), and screen-level cheapness. Nine of 22 quantitative models pass, spanning Graham Enterprising, earnings yield, low P/E plus high yield, dividend growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, composite value, and financial health — with full data quality (20/20 metrics).

Business quality is improving structurally, if not yet in near-term earnings momentum. Management is recycling capital from Spirit Energy disposals and non-core European energy-solutions exits (>£80m proceeds, February 2026 RNS) into rateable assets: Sizewell C (£1.3bn capped stake), Grain LNG, an fast-growing meter asset provider (>2.3m meters at H1 2026), Severn CCGT (£367m, May 2026), and nuclear life extensions including Sizewell B to 2055 under a £70.50/MWh CfD. Retail operational metrics — record NPS, 4.4-star Trustpilot, customer growth across divisions in 2025 — suggest franchise durability despite margin pressure.

The screen's buy signal aligns with statutory-adjusted valuation disconnect: statutory FY2025 operating profit was just £106m (derivative re-measurements and £508m impairments), while adjusted operating profit was £814m and ROE ~19.9% on the screen. Normalised earnings power, not peak-2023 commodity windfalls, supports the multiple. Timing is neutral (RSI ~35, below 200-day MA), favouring accumulation rather than momentum chasing. The screen's adjusted overlay to Hold on FCF divergence is the main qualifier: company-adjusted FY2024 FCF was £989m inflow, but FY2025 reported a £167m outflow after £1.2bn capex — a deliberate investment phase, not distress.

FY2025 marks a step-down from the 2023–2024 peak but remains profitable on an adjusted basis. The 39% adjusted EBITDA decline reflects lower Infrastructure earnings (£728m vs £1,357m segment EBITDA under revised segmentation), challenging Gas and Power Trading, pausing of Rough storage, and nuclear outages, partly offset by resilient Retail (£574m EBITDA) and Home Services improvement. Statutory results were heavily distorted by £303m net derivative remeasurement losses and £508m impairments (late-life gas fields and nuclear, excluding Sizewell C).

Cash generation turned negative on a free-cash-flow basis as capex more than doubled, driven by Sizewell C, Grain LNG, and MAP. Adjusted net cash fell £1.4bn, reflecting £827m buybacks, £237m dividends, £609m JVs/associates investment, and pension deficit payments (£150m). The balance sheet remains a strength: directors stress-tested going concern through December 2028 with unqualified audit opinion (preliminary results, note 1).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and policy.** Centrica faces persistent Ofgem scrutiny. In May 2026, British Gas agreed an Alternative Action Agreement over legacy prepayment meter installations: £20m to Ofgem's Voluntary Redress Fund, customer redress for 2018–21, and up to £70m energy debt write-off for vulnerable customers (interim results, Regulatory Update). Sector consumer debt is projected at ~£7bn (Energy UK, cited in filing). April 2026 news flagged Chancellor Reeves's proposals to break the gas–electricity price link, hitting UK power stocks including Centrica. Ofgem price-cap mechanics and regulatory reconciliation timing remain critical accounting judgements (preliminary results, note on regulatory scheme accounting). VAT removal on domestic electricity from October 2026 is welcomed but policy uncertainty persists.

**Cyclical and commodity exposure.** Optimisation (trading/LNG) remains volatile: FY2025 Centrica Energy AOP £150m vs £307m prior year; 2026 guidance ~£250m, below the £300m–£400m medium-term range. Nuclear valuation is highly sensitive to baseload power prices (±10% price move ≈ £118m write-back / £116m impairment at H1 2026). Electricity Generator Levy (55% on revenues above £82.61/MWh to March 2028) adds fiscal drag on generation revenues.

**Pension.** IAS 19 deficit widened sharply to £295m (from £21m), following triennial review assumptions agreed February 2025. Deficit contributions: £150m in 2025, £140m committed for 2026–2027 plus £44m balancing payment in 2028. Technical provisions deficit improved to ~£300m on a roll-forward basis, but cash calls are real and recurring.

**Execution and transformation.** ~1,300 role reductions (~14% of customer operations) announced alongside a £0.6bn transformation programme cost. Delivery risk on £1.1bn 2026 capex, Sizewell C construction, and MAP scale-up. Customer counts fell 1% at H1 2026; bad debt rising in Home Retail.

**Impairments and accounting complexity.** FY2025 £508m exceptional impairments; statutory vs adjusted divergence is large and persistent (derivative remeasurements). FCF definitions differ materially across filing, screen TTM, and Yahoo — dividend sustainability screens may overstate comfort if based on TTM FCF.

**Going concern.** Filing language is supportive: directors have "reasonable expectation" of adequate resources for 12 months, with modelling to December 2028; auditor unqualified (preliminary results). Largest stress-test risk identified: increased margin outflows in trading businesses.
