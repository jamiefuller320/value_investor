## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Imperial Brands PLC (IMB.L) screens as a strong buy on cash yield, dividend quality, and contrarian valuation (composite score 73%; 11/22 models passed across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk). The investment case rests on a highly cash-generative combustibles franchise in five priority markets (~70% of adjusted tobacco operating profit), a progressive dividend (~6% yield) and an active £1.45bn buyback, with NGP scaling as a secondary growth leg under Strategy 2030. Valuation looks inexpensive on earnings and free cash flow (P/E ~12.5x; FCF yield ~15.6%), though reported EPS fell in FY25 and the share trades below its 200-day moving average (RSI ~38). The central debate is whether pricing power and capital returns can offset structural volume decline, regulatory tightening, and NGP investment needs — with no interim filings in the source pack to bridge FY25 year-end to the current period.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, IMB offers a rare combination of high current cash return and improving operational discipline. The quantitative screen passes FCF Yield, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality — a breadth that suggests cheapness is supported by business quality rather than a single distressed metric. Management's challenger strategy (launched 2021, evolved to Strategy 2030 in March 2025) has delivered the strongest constant-currency tobacco and NGP net revenue growth in over a decade (+4.6% in FY24 per the annual report), pricing-led combustibles growth (+7.8% tobacco pricing in FY24), and double-digit NGP net revenue growth (+26% CC in FY24). Capital allocation is explicit and shareholder-friendly: progressive dividends (153.42p in FY24, +4.5%), leverage maintained at the lower end of 2.0–2.5x adjusted net debt/EBITDA, and surplus returns via buybacks (£1.25bn committed FY25; £1.45bn programme cited in recent news). FY25 free cash flow of £3.17bn (Yahoo fallback — see Financial Review) comfortably covers dividends and buybacks, which aligns with the screen's 15.6% FCF yield and Neff PEGY of 0.02. The stock sits in a neutral timing band (below 200-day MA, RSI sub-40), offering a patient entry for income-oriented value accounts willing to underwrite tobacco-sector ESG and regulatory risk.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and structural.** Annual report language flags evolving tobacco and nicotine regulation (EU Tobacco Products Directive review, product approval pathways in the US/Greece, prohibition-leaning policies in markets such as Australia where ~30% of product is illicit). Combustible volumes structurally decline; pricing must continuously offset volume — a model that works until tax/regulatory caps bind.

**NGP transition.** FY24 narrative notes NGP losses being reduced while scaling (+26% CC revenue); Strategy 2030 commits double-digit NGP growth and acquisitions (e.g. Black Buffalo, May 2025 news). Execution and regulatory approval risk in new categories remain material.

**Leverage and pensions.** Net debt ~£8.2bn with a stated 2.0–2.5x adjusted net debt/EBITDA covenant framework; buybacks continue while debt ticked higher in FY25. Pension/post-retirement liabilities ~£800m require monitoring in a higher-rate environment.

**Governance and ownership.** RNS (30 Jun 2026): Spring Mountain Investments (Kenneth Bryan Dart) reduced stake from **3.99% to 2.91%**. News (Jun 2025): insider holding reportedly down **59%** over the prior year — not corroborated in filing bodies but worth flagging.

**Earnings quality.** Reported EPS decline FY24→FY25 (Yahoo) contrasts with adjusted profit growth narrative; screen shows **-38.2% growth** on one metric — likely trailing earnings distortion, but unresolved without full adjusted reconciliation from filing tables.

**Operational restructuring.** August 2025 reports of thousands of job cuts targeting **£320m annual savings by 2030** introduce execution and morale risk alongside efficiency upside.

**Filing gaps.** Absence of interim accounts limits visibility on current-year trajectory; OCR extracts lack full going-concern, contingency, and covenant disclosure text — standard UK annual report sections are referenced but not reliably extracted.
