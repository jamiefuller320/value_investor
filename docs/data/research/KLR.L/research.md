# Keller Group plc (KLR.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:36:26.333646+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Keller Group is the world’s largest geotechnical specialist contractor, generating roughly £3bn of revenue across North America, Europe/Middle East and Asia-Pacific, with North America accounting for around 60% of group sales. The quantitative screen flags a strong buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (P/E 13.8x, FCF yield 8.1%, PEG 0.58, ROE 25.3%), supported by a multi-year earnings recovery that has accelerated into H1 2026. Filings show covenant-basis net cash at FY 2025, record order book (£1.9bn at June 2026), and upgraded FY 2026 guidance (consensus revenue £3,337m, underlying operating profit £242m). The central debate is whether megatrend exposure (US infrastructure, data centres) and capital returns (enhanced dividend, £100m buyback) justify current multiples, or whether cyclical construction risk, H1 working-capital drag and geopolitical/supply-chain shocks will cap further re-rating. Deep research broadly confirms the screen’s quality and cash-flow case but warrants accumulation rather than maximum conviction given cyclicality and limited deep-value screen passes.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, Keller offers a rare combination: a **global #1 franchise** in a fragmented niche, **structural margin improvement** (underlying operating margin from 3.7% in 2022 to 7.1% in FY 2025 and 7.3% in H1 2026), and a **balance sheet that has swung from net debt to net cash** on a covenant basis while returning capital aggressively.

Business quality rests on geographic and end-market diversification (largest customer ~3–4% of revenue; top 10 ~19%), pricing pass-through on inflation, and pivoting capacity toward high-growth subsectors—notably US data centres and infrastructure (I-40 highway remediation). Management has paired operational delivery with a capital-allocation framework: target leverage 0.5x–1.5x (currently well below), dividend cover 2.5x–3.5x, and a multi-year buyback programme (£50m completed in 2025; £100m tranche launched March 2026).

The screen fails on pure deep-value criteria (not listed among passed models), which is appropriate: at 3.2x P/B the stock is not a distressed asset play—it is a **quality compounder trading at a modest earnings multiple** after a sustained turnaround under James Wroath (CEO from August 2025).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / market:** Keller’s own principal risk register (Annual Report 2025) flags that construction demand is highly cyclical—revenue falls harder in downturns than many industries. H1 2026 benefited from US infrastructure and data-centre megatrends, but South Florida residential remains soft, UK trading is challenging, and western European infrastructure spend has yet to materialise. The interim update warns that renewed inflation (Iran conflict, US tariffs) could pressure customer project timing.

**Working capital / liquidity:** H1 2026 saw a **£87.5m working-capital outflow** (inventory +£36.7m, receivables +£76.8m), driving cash conversion down to 36%. This is seasonally normal (management cites H2 bias) but is a recurring risk in a project-based contractor and is not fully captured by static quality screens.

**Geopolitical / supply chain:** Filings cite Middle East conflict impacts (productivity dips, deferred remediation spend at Moretrench Industrial), renewed supply-chain pressure from the Iran conflict, and US steel tariffs. These are actively monitored but remain exogenous.

**Operational / project:** Principal risks include ineffective project management (contract disputes, ground conditions, weather), health & safety (AFR 0.05, TRIR 0.60 in H1 2026, both slightly worse YoY despite higher activity), and skills retention in tight labour markets.

**Governance / key person:** Former CEO **Michael Speakman passed away on 3 April 2026** (RNS, 9 April 2026). Current CEO James Wroath (from August 2025) leads; transition risk appears manageable but is recent. Key management remuneration £9.0m (FY 2025, annual report extract).

**Pension:** Net DB liabilities £15.2m— modest relative to group scale; UK scheme de-risked via buy-in.

**Accounting / non-underlying items:** Ongoing ERP implementation costs (£4.7m in H1 2026, classified non-underlying) and finance transformation restructuring (£0.4m) create adjusted/statutory divergence. Goodwill not impaired at H1 review, but remains sensitive to a construction downturn.

**Leverage / covenants:** Covenant headroom is ample today, but drawn guarantees (£212.4m) and lease-adjusted statutory net debt (£102.6m) remind that IFRS and covenant measures differ; screens using simplified net-debt metrics may overstate net-cash comfort.

**Competitive:** Risk of losing market share in tight markets; mitigated by scale and product breadth, but margins in APAC (6.1% vs 7.7% prior H1) show pricing pressure.
