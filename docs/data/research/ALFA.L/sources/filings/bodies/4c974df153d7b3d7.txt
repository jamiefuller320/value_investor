accordance with AIC guidance issued in October 2020. ​ ​ ​ Chair’s Statement Dear Shareholders Proposed Combination with JPMorgan UK Small Cap Growth & Income plc Following the end of our financial year, on 18 September 2026, the Board announced that it had agreed heads of terms with the board of JPMorgan UK Small Cap Growth & Income plc (“JUGI”) for a combination of the Company and JUGI. The Board regularly evaluates both the Company's performance and the attractiveness and appeal of the Company to investors and this combination is the culmination of a competitive private review process undertaken by the Board. The Board is conscious that over the past five years the Company has bought back more than half of its issued share capital, which has been essential to limit the discount and discount volatility. Whilst this has been in the best interests of shareholders, it has significantly reduced liquidity in the Company’s shares and the Board is concerned about the impact that ongoing share buybacks are likely to continue to have on the size and scale of the Company, the liquidity in the Company's shares and the Company's ability to appeal to investors and grow over time. The Board has negotiated the investment management fee down on two occasions in the past few years, resulting in a reduction in the ongoing charges ratio (“OCR”). However, as a result of the continuing buyback, this improvement in the OCR will be difficult to sustain with the OCR expected to rise in the current financial year, further reducing the Company’s attractiveness when compared to its peers. Investment performance has also been challenging for a significant period during which time the Board, working with the Investment Manager, has robustly assessed and challenged the Company's portfolio managers and their investment process which has resulted in some changes in approach. Despite this, recent performance remains below both the reference index and a number of similar peers, limiting the appeal of the Company to potential investors. The combination of the Company and JUGI will be implemented through a proposed members' voluntary winding up of the Company by way of a scheme of reconstruction of the Company under Section 110 of the Insolvency Act 1986 (the "Scheme"). Pursuant to the Scheme, the Company’s shareholders will be entitled to receive new shares in JUGI or to elect some or all of their shares for cash (at a 2% discount to NAV less the costs of the transaction), subject to an aggregate limit of 35% of the Company’s share capital. Following implementation of the proposals, the enlarged JUGI will continue to be managed by JPMorgan Funds Limited in accordance with its existing investment objective and policy. The Board considers that the proposals are in the best interests of shareholders, offering shareholders the opportunity to rollover their holdings into an investment trust with exceptional long-term performance within the UK Smaller Companies sector. Those who elect to do this will also benefit from holding shares in a larger, and so more cost efficient, company which should offer better trading liquidity. The Company will publish a circular setting out full details of the proposals and convening the necessary general meetings to implement the Scheme. At the same time, JUGI will publish a circular to convene a general meeting of JUGI shareholders to approve the issue of new JUGI shares pursuant to the proposals. It is anticipated that such shareholder documentation will be published in late October/early November 2026. Subject to the relevant conditions being satisfied, it is expected that the Scheme will be completed before the end of 2026, although the expected timetable in respect of the Scheme remains subject to change. Performance For the year ended 30 June 2026, the Company’s net asset value (“NAV”) total return, calculated on the basis that all dividends received are reinvested in additional shares, fell by 4.1%. The share price total return, calculated on the same basis, fell by 3.6%. These returns compare to a total return of 6.1% generated by the Deutsche Numis Smaller Companies plus AIM (ex investment companies) Index (the “reference index”). The Board is acutely aware that these are a disappointing set of results, particularly in terms of the performance relative to the reference index, as it extends the NAV underperformance record to cover one, three and five years. It notes that most of the other investment trusts with similar mandates also underperformed the index during the year, where a significant contribution came from the performance of the Precious Metals and Mining sector, which returned more than 60% over the year, driven in part by the performance of gold. However, the analysis shows that stock-specific issues were the major component to the Company’s underperformance rather than asset allocation. As stated above, the Board has worked with the Investment Manager to understand the causes of underperformance. A detailed review of performance and investment activity for the year is included in the Investment Manager’s Review. Earnings and Dividends The revenue return per share (“EPS”) for the year ended 30 June 2026 was 13.90p (2025: 13.52p), an increase of 2.8% compared to the previous year. The Company has continued to benefit from strong earnings resilience from the underlying portfolio holdings. Recurring income is increasingly the predominant source of dividends, with only 6.4% of investment income for the year coming from special dividends. This can be compared to the position 10 years ago when the proportion of revenue generated from special dividends was higher, and illustrates a trend by companies to return capital to shareholders through share buy backs. Due to the timing of the AGM this year (see Annual General Meeting below), the Board will be unable to seek shareholder approval for the payment of a final dividend in the usual manner. The Board is therefore declaring a second interim dividend for the financial year of 9.5p per share, which will take the full year dividend to 14.0p per share, an increase of 6.1% on the full year dividend paid in 2025. The second interim dividend will be paid on 27 November 2026 to shareholders on the register on 6 November 2026, with an associated ex-dividend date of 5 November 2026. Gearing The Board has given the Investment Manager discretion to vary the level of gearing between 5% net cash and 25% net gearing (at the time of drawdown). During the year, the Company repaid the £40 million revolving credit facility from The Royal Bank of Scotland International and negotiated a £40 million evergreen secured revolving credit facility with Bank of America. At the year end, the Company had drawn £25 million of this facility and, at that time, the gross level of borrowings was offset by cash and cash equivalents of £12.1 million resulting in net gearing of 4.9% (2025: 6.6%). The cost of borrowing at the year end was 4.73%. Ongoing Charges The ongoing charges ratio (“OCR”) for the year ended 30 June 2026 was 0.77% (2025: 0.85%). The reduction is attributable to a lower management fee cost following the reduction that took place in April 2025 and lower administrative expenses compared to 2025. Discount Control and Share Buy Backs At the year end, the discount of the share price to the NAV was 8.7% (2025: 9.0%). Over the year, the Company bought back 16.3 million shares, equating to 26.5% of the issued share capital, at a total cost of £83.7 million and a weighted average price of 509.5p per share. The weighted average discount at which the shares were repurchased was 9.2%. The Board calculates that this added 14.8p per share to the NAV per share for remaining shareholders. Since the year end, a further 2.7 million shares have been repurchased. All shares bought back are held in treasury. The Board remains committed to its discount management policy and will continue to be active in the market if the discount exceeds 8% and it believes it to be in the best interests of shareholders to do so. The Board is pleased to note that the discount has narrowed over the last 12 months, although it remains frustrated that achieving this has required a significant reduction in the size of the Company. The pace of buy backs meant that the Board convened a General Meeting on 31 March 2026 at which shareholders granted authority to the Board to renew the buy back authority for a further 14.99% of the issued share capital. The renewed authority will lapse at the AGM, at which time the Board will seek a renewed authority. Full details of the Board’s discount control policy can be found in the Overview of Strategy. Outlook Sentiment towards the UK economy has been negative for some time given the challenges it continues to face in terms of low GDP growth and high debt levels exacerbated by high interest rates. This shapes the outlook particularly for smaller UK companies which are more exposed to the domestic economy, including its high energy costs, than larger more globally oriented companies. Given the tight fiscal constraints, there is great uncertainty over how the government will tackle the policies needed to stimulate growth in the imminent Budget. What is clear is that the valuations of these companies are at historic lows, and in many cases with yields as high as large companies. The attractions of the asset class are further evident from the string of bids we have seen for such companies over the past year. The Board is confident that the asset class contains significant opportunities for investors and that its recent recommendation to combine your Company into a larger, more cost effective, investment trust managed by JP Morgan Funds Limited offers shareholders an improved way to benefit from exposure to smaller UK companies over the period ahead. Liz Airey Chair 21 October 2026 Overview of Strategy (extract) Business The Company is an investment trust and its Ordinary shares are listed on the Main Market of the London Stock Exchange. Investment Objective The Company’s objective is to achieve long-term capital growth by investment in UK-quoted smaller companies. Investment Policy The Company intends to achieve its investment objective by investing in a diversified portfolio consisting mainly of UK-quoted smaller companies. The portfolio will normally comprise approximately 50 individual holdings representing the Investment Manager’s highest conviction investment ideas. In order to reduce risk in the Company without compromising flexibility, no holding within the portfolio should exceed 5% of total assets at the time of acquisition. The Company may use derivatives for portfolio hedging purposes (i.e. only for the purpose of reducing, transferring or eliminating the investment risks in its investments in order to protect the Company’s portfolio). Within the Company’s Articles of Association, the maximum level of gearing is 100% of net assets. The Directors have set parameters of between 5% net cash and 25% net gearing (at the time of drawdown) for the level of gearing that can be employed in normal market conditions. The Directors have delegated responsibility to the Investment Manager for the operation of the gearing level within the above parameters. Board Investment Limits The Directors have set additional guidelines in order to reduce the risk borne by the portfolio: - Companies with a market capitalisation of below £50 million should not represent more than 5% of total assets. - Companies involved in “Blue Sky” products should not represent more than 5% of total assets. - No more than 40% of the portfolio should be invested in companies that are constituents of the FTSE AIM All-Share Index. Investment Process The Investment Manager’s investment process combines asset allocation, stock selection, portfolio construction, risk management, and dealing. The investment process is led by Quality, Growth and Momentum; with the focus on earnings momentum providing flexibility through economic cycles. The Investment Manager’s stock selection led process involves using a proprietary screening tool, “the Matrix”. The Matrix reflects Quality, Growth, Momentum and Valuation based factor analysis of the universe. That analysis is combined with extensive research and meetings with senior management of these potential investments. Final portfolios are compiled and managed with continued use of these insights, along with risk analysis and portfolio construction inputs. Reference Index The Company’s reference index is the Deutsche Numis Smaller Companies plus AIM (ex investment companies) Index. Delivering the Investment Objective The Directors are responsible for determining the Company’s investment objective and investment policy. Day-to-day management of the Company’s assets has been delegated, via the Alternative Investment Fund Manager (the “AIFM”), to the Investment Manager. Key Performance Indicators (“KPIs”) The Board assesses the performance of the Company against the range of KPIs shown below over a variety of timeframes, but has particular focus on the long-term, which the Board considers to be at least five years. KPI Description Net asset value (“NAV”) total return performance The Board measures the Company’s NAV total return performance against the total return of the reference index (the Deutsche Numis Smaller Companies plus AIM (ex investment companies) Index) and the Close Peer Group. Share price total return performance The Board measures the Company’s share price total return performance against the total return of the reference index and the Close Peer Group. Discount/premium to NAV The Board compares the discount or premium of the Ordinary share price to the NAV per share with the discount/premium of the peer group and also to the Company’s committed discount target on a rolling 12 month basis. Ongoing charges The Board monitors the Company’s ongoing charges ratio (“OCR”) against prior years and other similar sized companies in the Close Peer Group. Sustainability Disclosure Requirements (“SDR”) In November 2023, the Financial Conduct Authority (“FCA”) published its sustainability disclosure requirements and investment labels regime (“SDR”) to address concerns about misleading sustainability claims. SDR includes an opt-in labelling regime for sustainable investment products, additional disclosure requirements and restrictions on the use of sustainability terms. It also established anti-greenwashing (“AGW”) rules. Investment trusts and their managers are in scope of the SDR. Although investment trusts are not directly in scope of the AGW requirements, the rules apply indirectly to them, mostly via obligations imposed on their managers. Although Environmental, Social and Governance (“ESG”) factors are taken into consideration by the Investment Manager as part of its investment analysis, the Company itself does not have an explicit sustainability objective and so under SDR is categorised as “Non-labelled” rather than “Labelled” or “Other”. Discount Control Policy The Board operates a discount control mechanism which targets a maximum discount of the share price to the cum-income net asset value of 8% under normal market conditions. In pursuit of this objective, the Board closely monitors the level of the discount and buys back shares in the market when it believes it is in the best interests of shareholders as a whole to do so. At each Annual General Meeting, the Board seeks shareholder approval to buy back up to 14.99% of the Company’s share capital. Share buy-backs will only be made where the Board believes it to be in the best interests of shareholders as a whole and the making and timing of share buy-backs will be at the discretion of the Board. The Company has a tender offer mechanism in place and the Board intends to continue to seek shareholder approval at each Annual General Meeting to enable it to carry out tender offers on a discretionary basis in circumstances where the Board believes that share buy-backs are not sufficient to maintain the discount at an appropriate level, although it expects that buy-backs should be the primary mechanism for managing the discount. Viability Statement As set out in more detail in the Chair’s Statement, it is proposed that the Company combines with JPMorgan UK Small Cap Growth & Income plc (“JUGI”). The combination, if approved by each company's shareholders, will be effected by way of a scheme of reconstruction and winding up of the Company under section 110 of the Insolvency Act 1986 and the associated transfer of part of the assets and undertaking of the Company to JUGI in exchange for the issue of new ordinary shares in JUGI (the “Scheme”). The outcome of the general meetings to make the Scheme effective represents a material uncertainty in the context of the preparation of these financial statements on a going concern basis. Notwithstanding this material uncertainty, for the purposes of this viability statement, the Board has decided that five years is an appropriate period over which to report. This takes into account that the Company, which does not have a fixed life, is a long-term investment vehicle and the Board considers that this period reflects a balance between looking out over a long-term horizon and the inherent uncertainties of looking out further than five years. In assessing the viability of the Company over the review period, the Directors have focused upon the following factors: - The principal risks and uncertainties detailed above and the steps taken to mitigate these risks, together with the emerging risks identified by the Board. - The Company is invested in listed securities that are readily-realisable in normal market conditions and there is a spread of investments held across a diversified range of sectors. This should enable the Company to meet any short-term liabilities if required. - The Company is closed ended in nature and therefore it is not required to sell investments when shareholders wish to sell their shares. - The level of share buy backs undertaken by the Company and the impact these have had on the Company’s size. - The ability of the Company to continue to buy back shares under the authorities provided by shareholders. - The Company’s long-term performance record. - The Company’s level of gearing. The Company had net gearing of 4.9% as at 30 June 2026. The Board has set overall limits for borrowing and reviews regularly the Company’s level of gearing, cash flow projections and compliance with banking covenants. The Company has a £40 million secured facility with Bank of America which is typically drawn down for short term periods and can be repaid without any significant financial penalties. The facility is an ‘evergreen’ facility which will continue until such time as the Company or the lender provide notice of termination in accordance with the agreement. - The Company has cash and money market funds which at 30 June 2026 amounted to £12.1 million. These balances allow the Company to meet liabilities as they fall due. - The level of ongoing charges. - There are no capital commitments currently foreseen that would alter the Board’s view. - The robustness of the operations of the Company’s third party service suppliers. The Directors have also reviewed the revenue and ongoing expenses forecasts for the coming year and considered the Company’s Statement of Financial Position as at 30 June 2026 which shows net current liabilities of £13.3 million at that date, and do not consider this to be a concern due to the liquidity of the portfolio which should enable the Company to meet any short-term liabilities if required. In assessing the Company’s future viability, the Board has assumed that shareholders will wish to continue to have exposure to the Company’s activities in the form of a closed ended entity and the Company will continue to have access to sufficient capital. Over the year to 30 June 2026 the net asset value (“NAV”) fell by 4.1% in total return terms compared to a positive total return of 6.1% from the Deutsche Numis Smaller Companies plus AIM (ex Investment Companies) Index (the “reference index”). We are obviously disappointed to underperform the reference index and to see a falling NAV at a time when the market has generated a positive return. The majority of the underperformance was concentrated in the first half of the period, when a Value-led market weighed heavily on our Quality Growth & Momentum (“QMG”) style; Quality was the worst-performing factor in the UK, and Growth remained out of favour, while the rally in lower quality, more cyclical companies ran directly counter to our positioning. Precious metal mining companies, which were not held in the portfolio during the first half of the year, also performed well during that period, increasing in significance in the reference index. The second half of the year, whilst still challenging, was more encouraging, and we believe process enhancements (as described in greater detail below) have created a better balance to the portfolio. Reducing the portfolio's underweight exposure to Value has been positive as Value has continued to perform well, and the tilt towards Quality is now contributing more positively. Disappointingly, there were a number of stock specific issues during the year that had a negative impact on performance. These are detailed below. However, with the portfolio's valuation relative to the reference index at a multi-year low, we believe this provides a supportive foundation for future returns. We are encouraged that markets now appear to be refocussing on company fundamentals. What were the key drivers of performance? The style tilts that come with our process had a negative impact on performance over the period, with these style headwinds delivering around half of the underperformance. The other half came from stock specifics. Key positive drivers of performance were a cluster of high-conviction holdings that demonstrate the characteristics we look for. Applied Nutrition (+150%) was the standout holding as the market rewarded successive upgrades in revenue growth guidance and earnings expectations, execution of growth strategy across geographies globally, and the acquisition of US manufacturer Nutrablend. Balfour Beatty (+64%) was the next largest contributor, with strong execution across the business, supportive growth in infrastructure end markets, and strong order books providing good forward revenue visibility. Hunting (+51%) also performed well, delivering some attractive business wins and the quality of its business continues to improve with market leading propositions. Volex (+48%) also contributed strongly, supported by momentum in data centre related demand, as well as margin expansion. Alpha Group (+31%) also performed well following the announcement of its acquisition by Corpay. On the negative side, ME Group (-54%) was the largest detractor, with a combination of a period undergoing strategic options of potential business sale, a delayed audit and end market headwinds all detrimental to what is typically a resilient business model. While frustrated about the self- inflicted share price weakness from the strategic review, we believe the outlook for ME Group is one of strong growth, particularly driven by the UK laundry rollout, and that trading in France will improve as already evidenced in more recent months. Pan African Resources (-32%) was exceptionally strong through the first half of the year, before we invested, and a large benchmark name given rising gold prices. We invested later in the period given our increasing comfort about the quality improvements of the business. However, with the price of gold falling more recently, the company’s share price has also fallen. Meanwhile, start-up issues in the Australian side of the business have been frustrating. We continue to hold Pan African Resources, where the gold price has stabilised, South African operations have been reporting well, and we believe the company can resolve the Australian teething issues. Mortgage Advice Bureau (-39%) is a company well versed in tough end markets, namely the UK housing sector, but, added to that, the market has concerns over how a business such as this might be disintermediated by AI technology. We continue to invest in Mortgage Advice Bureau which, despite negative sentiment affecting its housing end markets, is expected to deliver strong growth, aided by the refinancing market. We believe its valuation is underpricing that growth. Trustpilot (-45%) was also a detractor, as the market was concerned in its ability both to remain relevant in an AI world and to create a brand in the US. We exited the holding mid-way through the period. Jet2 (-29%) has suffered from negative sentiment surrounding UK consumer spending, with a late booking cycle for holidays making it more challenging to dynamically price to maximise profitability this summer. We continue to hold Jet2, where recent data has shown that, despite the late booking cycle, consumers are travelling. Jet2 continues to be a leader in its market, with high customer retention rates, and the likely acquisition of EasyJet by Apollo could create further opportunities for key air traffic slots or market share. How challenging is lacklustre UK economic growth to the portfolio? The UK stock market is not the UK economy. While UK GDP is forecast to grow by slightly more than 1% in 2026, the companies we hold are forecast to deliver much stronger growth and investors are being asked to pay very little for that growth. One reason for this more attractive growth rate is the diversification of revenue: approximately 50% of the portfolio's revenues are generated overseas, so many holdings are UK-listed but globally exposed businesses. Many UK smaller companies depend far more on their own end markets, structural growth themes and management execution, than on the domestic macro backdrop. Our process deliberately targets exactly these characteristics - quality companies with pricing power, structural growth and strong balance sheets that can compound value and thrive even in a low-growth world. That said, subdued domestic GDP is not entirely without impact: sentiment towards the UK market has been negative, and particularly towards smaller companies given their more domestic tilt than their larger counterparts. This has been a headwind for demand for the asset class, and it has left it trading at historically depressed valuations as shown in the charts later in this review. We view that as an opportunity rather than a threat. This valuation depression is also a key driver of the degree of bid activity we have seen, and continue to see, across the asset class. Approximately half of the portfolio’s revenue is UK-generated and domestically exposed company valuations are already at low levels, meaning that even modest improvements in end markets can drive strong share-price reactions. Our focus is on businesses where we feel confident they can meet or beat earnings expectations, and we feel valuations are not reflecting the outlook we expect. In short, weak GDP shapes sentiment more than it constrains the underlying earnings power of the portfolio holdings, and disciplined stock selection remains the most reliable driver of returns in this environment. The portfolio has delivered 16.3% earnings per share (“EPS”) growth over the last year, compared to just 5.6% for the reference index. How has the Iran conflict impacted markets and companies? The conflict in the Middle East was the defining macro event of the period, impacting markets primarily through the change in the oil price. A major escalation in March, centred on disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, sent Brent crude above $100 a barrel and reignited inflation fears just as price pressures had begun to stabilise. In the initial days of the conflict, the shock hit smaller companies share prices hardest, however, encouragingly, despite the macro economic volatility, smaller companies have outperformed larger companies since the conflict started, not just in the UK but in many other regions. As the conflict de-escalated and progress was made towards a US–Iran deal to reopen the Strait, the oil price fell back from an April peak of nearly $120 to around $80, allowing risk appetite to return. Supply risks have not come to pass on oil and related commodities. The episode has left a lasting mark on monetary policy: with inflation of 3.1% at 31 August 2026, the Bank of England has held interest rates at 3.75% and has raised the prospect that future rate increases may be needed if inflation pressures persist. Our response has been consistent with our process; we have remained anchored in well financed businesses with pricing power, structural growth and strong management teams, which we believe are best placed to compound value through this kind of uncertain, headline driven backdrop. What process enhancements have you made? Our investment approach remains firmly anchored on the Quality, Growth & Momentum (“QGM”) style. We have, however, refined aspects of the process to ensure stock selection, rather than factor exposure, is the primary driver of returns, particularly through the use of our extensive risk tools. Specifically, we have made the following changes. Firstly, we have taken a broader approach to what defines Quality; both quantitative and qualitative characteristics. This has been seen in portfolio actions, for example providing a better understanding of how sectors such as UK contractors and mining businesses have improved their Quality characteristics over recent periods and are now suitable candidates for our QGM process. Secondly, we are controlling the extent of our factor exposures so that outcomes are increasingly determined by individual company fundamentals. That shift is already visible in the portfolio's risk profile, with stock-specific risk now more prominent than factor risk. Thirdly, we have strengthened the integration of macro awareness through risk tools, reflecting the shifting interest rate environment and broader backdrop, including scenario analysis to understand how holdings behave under different conditions, such as stagflation. This aligns with the control of factor exposures, and increased awareness about how different macro environments may impact the portfolio. Fourthly, while we retain our QGM tilts, we have enhanced the valuation discipline to mitigate the risk of de-rating even when earnings are delivered. In practice, this means being more sensitive to highly valued stocks where continued earnings upgrades become critical, and acting earlier and being more decisive in selling holdings that continue to underperform, or where the investment case has played out. The Matrix remains central as a tool for identifying where the best QGM opportunities are evolving from, with earnings momentum a large influence. Importantly, these are enhancements rather than a change of philosophy: the aim is for greater consistency in returns and better protection in tougher more macro driven market environments, giving our stock selection a fairer chance to shine. We believe the process enhancements created better balance in the portfolio during the second half of the year, which should benefit during periods where macro and style volatility are high and Value as a style is in favour. Investment decisions are made on a longer term view and we hope to see improved outcomes in the year ahead. How is the portfolio positioned to navigate more volatile market conditions? Building greater balance and resilience into the portfolio was a deliberate focus over the year and was a direct outcome of our process enhancements. We remain in volatile and ever changing stock markets, with factors such as inflation, interest rates, and global conflicts making the direction of markets less clear at both a style and sector level. The risk profile of the portfolio at the end of the year was more prominent towards stock-specific than factor risk. We believe this means that performance should be driven more by individual company fundamentals rather than broad style, sector or market movements. Our risk tools have helped us to better understand the risk exposures at each stock level, helping portfolio construction. That balance is reinforced by genuine diversification of revenue: approximately 50% of the portfolio's revenues are generated overseas, so many holdings are UK-listed but globally exposed. We retain a healthy spread across structural themes including UK infrastructure, quality industrials, hard assets (those linked to assets such as commodities and infrastructure) and defensive, cash-generative compounders. Given the combination of attractive valuations and more resilient outlooks in many of the UK domestic holdings, we feel confident they deserve a place in a balanced portfolio. This includes companies which are resilient and have end market strength (for example, UK contractors and food producers), as well as businesses going through a more challenging environment but where we believe that is reflected in outlooks and valuations (for example, travel companies such as Jet2 and On the Beach). Importantly, this is a balance achieved without diluting our style: we retain our clear QGM tilts, but with tighter control of factor exposures and a greater emphasis on stock selection. We believe this positions the portfolio to be more resilient through volatile, macro-driven markets while still being able to deliver returns from businesses with strong balance sheets, pricing power and structural growth. Portfolio turnover has been higher; is that a sign of enthusiasm for opportunities? Turnover in the portfolio has averaged 25% over the last 5 years, reflecting our long-term buy-and-hold philosophy. However, turnover for the year ended 30 June 2026 was higher than usual, at 37%. Markets are currently in a period of shorter cycles, both economically and in terms of style performance. The decade of 2010-2020 of one macro environment is long behind us. Last year's higher activity was driven by two related factors. Firstly, genuine enthusiasm for new ideas: our research continues to generate a strong pipeline and there is healthy competition for capital within the portfolio, meaning new opportunities have to earn their place by displacing existing holdings. Given factor rotation in the market in recent years, we believe we are able to capture good QGM opportunities at very attractive valuations. Secondly, and just as important, it reflects the deliberate portfolio actions that sit behind our process enhancements - controlling factor exposures, being more decisive in selling holdings that have continued to underperform or where valuations have become stretched, and taking earlier entry positions in companies where we see earnings momentum potential. The higher turnover is not a change in philosophy; it is a combination of a rich opportunity set and a more decisive, disciplined approach to shaping the portfolio's risk profile, with the aim of letting stock selection drive returns. As referred to above, it is also impacted by the shorter cycles we are seeing in markets which could structurally increase turnover levels.
