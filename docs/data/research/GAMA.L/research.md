# Gamma Communications plc (GAMA.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:17:48.723696+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Gamma Communications is a European B2B cloud-communications platform with ~89% recurring revenue, strong adjusted cash conversion, and a channel-led SME franchise supplemented by enterprise and continental expansion. FY2025 revenue rose 11% to £645.8m and adjusted EBITDA 13% to £141.7m (Companies House FY2025 annual report), but statutory PBT fell 8% to £87.7m after £10.6m of exceptional items, reflecting restructuring and acquisition-related costs. The quantitative screen rates the stock a **hold**: it passes cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families (P/E 16.5, ROE 17.2%, FCF yield supportive) yet fails financial-health and Piotroski thresholds, with filing-aligned FCF of £64.1m versus a higher screen TTM figure. The central debate is no longer purely operational — a prolonged PE takeover process (Providence, Epiris, Waterland) has dominated the narrative since April 2026, while the Starface acquisition swung net cash of £153.7m to net debt of £9.3m and left UK SME gross profit under pressure amid copper-to-fibre migration. For a value investor, quality is evident but upside is capped near-term by overbought technicals (RSI 83) and event risk.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
A **hold** is appropriate for a value-oriented process that weights business quality against balance-sheet and timing gaps.

**What the screen likes:** Nine of 22 models pass, including Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth and Acquirer's Multiple. Headline metrics are respectable rather than deep-value: P/E 16.5, P/B 2.7, dividend yield 1.9%, ROE 17.2%, debt/equity ~13% on a screen basis. Composite score (50%) sits slightly below the Communication Services sector average (55%), consistent with a neutral stance rather than a conviction buy.

**Business quality supports the pass set:** The FY2025 annual report highlights 89% recurring revenue, gross margin rising to 54%, adjusted cash generated by operations of £131.8m (93% conversion), and ROCE of 27.8%. Gamma owns carrier network capability and a 1,500+ channel-partner ecosystem — structural assets that screens capture imperfectly. German scale post-Starface (£152.2m, February 2025) provides a credible growth leg offsetting UK macro weakness.

**Why not accumulate:** (1) **Post-acquisition leverage** — first use of a £130m revolving credit facility; net debt £9.3m at year-end after £216.2m of acquisition, buyback and dividend outflows; Yahoo shows goodwill/intangibles of ~£397m and **negative tangible book** (~£12m), a screen "high leverage" flag. (2) **UK profit mix** — chair's statement confirms UK SME gross profit declined despite cloud volume growth, with fibre transition compressing margins. (3) **Timing** — price 27% above 200-day MA, RSI 83; screen timing is neutral but action note flags overextension. (4) **Event overhang** — ongoing takeover discussions since April 2026 limit visibility on standalone capital allocation (buybacks of up to ~£125m signalled, dividend frozen at 2025 levels for two years). (5) **Governance transition** — CFO Bill Castell departs March 2026; successor appointment is a watchpoint.

The hold signal therefore aligns with a **quality compounder trading at fair-ish multiples**, not a mispriced deep-value entry — especially with the stock bid up on M&A speculation.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**UK operational headwind:** FY2025 chair's statement cites difficult UK macro, PSTN/copper switch-off (January 2027), migration to lower-margin full-fibre connectivity, and **declining UK SME gross profit** despite volume growth — a structural margin risk screens underweight.

**Integration and leverage:** Starface integration cited as progressing well, but goodwill/intangible accumulation and first-time debt leave limited balance-sheet slack if German growth slows or integration slips. RCF covenants are not extractable from the indexed filing bodies (detailed notes not parsed); covenant compliance is an **unresolved verify-before-trade item**.

**Competitive intensity:** Enterprise segment faces "ongoing competitive pricing pressure" (FY2025 chair); connectivity pricing called out explicitly. Large vendors (Cisco/Webex partnership) both partner and compete.

**Regulatory:** Operates as a regulated telecoms carrier (Ofcom references in market section); PSTN switch-off is regulatory-driven migration risk as well as opportunity.

**M&A / takeover process:** Since 7 April 2026 (Bloomberg sale exploration), the company has confirmed discussions with **Providence Equity**, **Epiris** (Rule 2.4 possible offer, 24 June 2026), and subsequently **Waterland** (Reuters, 21 Aug 2026). PUSU deadlines repeatedly extended — latest to **2 September 2026** (RNS, 5 Aug 2026). No certainty any firm offer will be made. This creates binary outcome risk and may constrain ordinary-course buybacks.

**Governance / key person:** CFO Bill Castell leaving 31 March 2026; board changes flagged for early 2026. Main Market migration adds reporting obligations.

**Going concern:** FY2025 report table of contents includes a viability statement and going concern assessment (page 43), but the indexed body extract does **not** contain the detailed going-concern wording — cannot confirm specific assumptions from primary text.

**Pension:** No pension deficit or scheme risk identified in available filing extracts.
