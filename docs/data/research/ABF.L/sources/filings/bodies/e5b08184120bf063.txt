George Weston, Chief Executive of Associated British Foods, said: "Our results this year reflect a challenging operating environment and macroeconomic headwinds. We have focused on what we can control, taking targeted actions to drive performance while making good operational and strategic progress and continuing to invest in long-term growth. "In Primark, we took significant steps to re-energise our customer proposition this year, both enhancing our product offer and strengthening our value credentials, underpinned by increased investment in marketing and digital engagement. Our key priority areas, the UK and womenswear, delivered growth and outperformed our other markets and categories. We are now building on what has worked well and applying the same focus and investment in our European markets and other product categories. We also progressed new avenues for growth, including expansion into new markets through an asset-light, margin-accretive franchise model, and our decision to introduce home delivery in Great Britain in the future. "In AB Foods, profit was impacted by US consumer weakness in certain categories and lower European sugar prices. However, this masked encouraging growth across a number of brands and businesses, supported by continued investment in product innovation, marketing and targeted acquisitions. Multi-year capital projects completed in Grocery and Ingredients this year provide new capacity and capabilities to drive growth. The integration of Hovis is well underway and we expect to drive significant cost synergies to invest in marketing and innovation and create a sustainably profitable UK bakeries business. While the outlook for Sugar remains challenging in 2027, future years should benefit from the recent uplift in world sugar prices and our restructuring actions to meet demand more efficiently. "Looking ahead, while we remain cautious about consumer sentiment and commodity costs, we will continue our sharp focus on driving financial performance and delivering the growth opportunities from capital investments across the Group. We are on track to complete the demerger of Primark from AB Foods in December 2027, positioning both businesses to deliver on their medium and long-term growth prospects." Primark In Primark, we are focused on like-for-like sales growth driven by our strengthened customer value proposition and supported by increased investment in our marketing and digital capabilities. We expect space growth in Europe, the US and our franchise markets to contribute around 4% to sales growth in 2027. For the foreseeable future, we continue to target white space growth to contribute around 4% to 5% per annum to our growth in total sales. Primark's adjusted operating margin is expected to be approximately 10% in 2027, reflecting continued investment in like-for-like sales growth. This does not include the impact of the expected dis-synergy costs as Primark moves towards demerger readiness. In 2027, the guidance reflects that there will be 53 weeks in the financial year. AB Foods In Grocery, adjusted operating profit is expected to be broadly in line with 2026, with sales growth in most brands and businesses offset by higher input costs. In 2027, our outlook includes a one-off impact from consolidating Hovis losses in the first year of ownership, ahead of delivering significant synergies to invest in marketing and innovation and to drive profit accretion. In Ingredients, adjusted operating profit is expected to be broadly in line with 2026, with good growth in specialty ingredients offset by start-up costs in our new yeast facility in India. In Sugar, we expect an adjusted operating loss in the range of £70m to £170m. There are a number of factors that could materially influence the outcome, either positively or negatively, in particular: significantly higher gas costs; production levels in Africa resulting from El Niño weather impacts; and currency movements, especially the Malawian kwacha. The higher end of the guidance range assumes all of these variables have a negative impact on performance. Despite an expected significant reduction in European sugar production in 2027, it is likely that the European sugar market will remain in surplus in the short term due to the high inventory levels carried over from 2025. However, the recent positive turn in world sugar pricing should benefit future years. In Agriculture, we expect adjusted operating profit to be ahead of 2026. Strategic and operational summary Our strategic priority is to re-energise Primark's core customer proposition to drive like-for-like sales growth and we made good progress in 2026. We are reasserting Primark's position as the leader in disruptive value, combining style and quality with the lowest prices in the market. In July, we invested in new, lower prices across hundreds of key lines while also improving quality, style, fit and the overall shopping experience. We launched these changes across all markets and categories through our 'Iconic Value' campaign to coincide with the arrival of our autumn/winter ranges. The initial customer response has been encouraging everywhere, with positive volume growth in key lines including nightwear, leisure and performance wear. At the same time, we are developing more defined category strategies and tailored growth plans. We started with womenswear as our largest and most strategic category. Our strengthened product offer reflects investment in key ranges such as performance wear, the introduction of new collections such as 'By Coleen' and 'The Primark Scene', and an improved, more co-ordinated fashion offer. Womenswear delivered strong like-for-like sales growth in 2026 and outperformed other categories in all markets. We are now applying the same focus and investment to other categories, including a clear strategic plan and overhaul of our kidswear category aimed at creating a simpler, stronger offer. To drive customer engagement, we increased investment in brand and marketing. In the UK, our first fully integrated marketing campaigns across channels delivered encouraging results, strengthened brand metrics and increased customer engagement. We also introduced initiatives such as our 'Spotlight' partnerships with local influencers in both the UK and selected European markets, and our 'Major Finds' promotions in all markets, both of which drove footfall in stores and attachment sales. Growth in these initiatives has been supported by continued investment in digital engagement. Our investments in paid media, social media and CRM increased customer engagement, with website traffic growth of 22% in 2026, and contributed to like-for-like sales. The focus is now to extend and scale these initiatives in our European markets as part of our overall plan to drive greater localisation. We have used learnings from the UK, adapted for local market relevance and customer needs, supported by ongoing investment in digital capabilities and assets. Primark’s digital strategy is to deliver incremental growth and support our strong, store-led model. Click & Collect in Great Britain was a key contributor to like-for-like sales growth, while also driving footfall and attachment sales and increasing the effectiveness of our marketing activations. The next evolution is to offer home delivery in Great Britain in the future. This will enable us to reach new customers and drive incremental growth, in the same way Click & Collect has done. The economics of home delivery have evolved over recent years, including the introduction of return policies, minimum basket sizes and delivery fees as standard, creating an opportunity for us to drive profitable growth through this channel. At the same time, our digital advancements will enable us to maximise the potential of home delivery to accelerate our digital flywheel and drive growth in our store model. In September, we acquired a highly automated depot in Sheffield in the UK, which provides the fulfilment capability and capacity to both offer home delivery and continue growing Click & Collect. We have significant white space to increase Primark's share of consumer spend and extend our presence in existing and new markets. There is still a lot of runway to grow in our European markets. Last year saw our highest number of store openings in the US to date. We are clear on our commercial strategy in the US and we have sharpened our understanding and focus on our key target customer. We will now focus on consolidating our position and driving growth across our enlarged footprint through our new commercial plan for the near to medium term before we expand again. Alongside expansion through our own stores, our franchise partnerships are an asset-light and margin-accretive route into new markets. Since year end, we have opened stores in Bahrain and Qatar and we have a strong pipeline for the Middle East. In particular, we have expanded our partnership with Alshaya Group to include the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia and expect to open the first store in spring 2027. We also signed an agreement with El Puerto de Liverpool to open franchise stores in Mexico and we are actively exploring the first locations. Looking ahead, our vision is to 'Build a Better Primark': a stronger, simpler and more scalable business that creates long-term value for customers and shareholders. Growth will come from our strengthened customer proposition, deeper category strategies, increased investment in brand, marketing and digital, improved availability and more locally relevant market propositions. We will continue to expand our store estate and grow through franchise partnerships. We also see a significant opportunity to develop an omnichannel operating model, starting in the UK. We are actively assessing the omnichannel opportunity in Europe. Underpinning this will be a focus on simplifying our operating model, strengthening our supply chain and using technology, automation and data to improve productivity and effectiveness at scale. ESG summary During 2026, Primark focused on further integrating sustainability into its operating and sourcing model. As of July 2026, 81% of its clothing units sold contained recycled or more sustainably sourced materials 4 , compared to 74% in 2025. In 2026, 64% of its cotton clothing units sold contained cotton from Primark preferred cotton sources 5 . Primark’s approach includes scaling a more circular approach to design and sourcing, from keeping materials and clothes in use for longer, through to reducing textile waste in its supply chain. As of July 2026, 6% of all of Primark’s clothing unit sales are circular by design, increasing to 21% of all jersey and 15% of all denim clothing unit sales. Primark also delivered progress in carbon reduction. Primark's Scope 3 GHG emissions reduced by 21% compared to 2025 and by 24% against the 2019 baseline. These reductions were achieved through a decrease in supplier greenhouse gas emissions, improved availability of primary data from supplier factories and greater use of recycled or Primark preferred sources. Underpinning the Primark Cares strategy is Primark's long-standing commitment to respecting human rights in its supply chains. In the 2025 calendar year, Primark conducted over 2,300 social audits in its tier one supplier factories. During the year, Primark undertook a review of its sustainability activities to focus on Primark's strengths as a business. The evolved strategy will concentrate on three priorities: circularity 6 , supply chain resilience, and supporting women in the Primark value chain. Primark will publish its updated strategy next year. Primark Group includes franchise revenue, which in 2026 comprised three stores in Dubai and one in Kuwait. For this reason, they continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the consolidated financial statements. The forecast for the going concern assessment period to 4 March 2028 has been reviewed in light of the latest trading performance and is the best estimate of cash flow in the period. The demerger of Primark is expected to complete during this assessment period. However, the structure, terms and timing of the proposed demerger remain within the Group’s control. The demerger would not proceed on terms that the Board considered would leave either the Food business or Primark without adequate resources to continue in operational existence following completion. As part of the going concern assessment, the directors have therefore considered two scenarios: firstly the Group post a demerger of Primark and secondly, the Group being maintained in its current composition if the demerger does not complete as planned during the assessment period. In the demerger scenario, assumptions have been made around how cash and debt would be split between the Food and Primark businesses. It has also been assumed that the Group would no longer be impacted by Primark cash flows from December 2027. The Board’s treasury policies are in place to maintain a strong capital base and manage the Group’s balance sheet and liquidity to ensure long-term financial stability. These policies are the basis for investor, creditor and market confidence and enable the successful development of the business. The financial leverage policy requires that, in the ordinary course of business, the Board prefers to see the Group’s ratio of total net debt including lease liabilities to adjusted EBITDA to be well under 1.5x. At the end of this financial year, the financial leverage ratio was 1.1x. At the end of the financial year, the Group had total cash and cash equivalents of £1.0bn and undrawn committed facilities of £1.8bn, which includes an undrawn £1.5bn RCF that matures in June 2029 and an additional RCF taken out in February 2026 that matures in February 2029, both of which are free from performance covenants and expected to be extended as required. In the demerger scenario, the going concern assessment reflects the anticipated post-demerger liquidity and financing arrangements for the Food business based on the information available at the date of the assessment. In reviewing the cash flow forecast for the period, the directors reviewed the trading for both Primark and the Food business in light of the experience gained from events of the last three years of trading and emerging trading patterns as well as considering potential significant acquisitions. The directors have a thorough understanding of the risks, sensitivities and judgements included in these elements of the cash flow forecast. Separate base case forecasts were considered for the Food business following completion of the demerger, and for the Group in its current composition. The post-demerger forecast reflects the Food business's expected trading performance, cash flows, liquidity and financing arrangements, together with the effects of separation and its standalone operating arrangements. As a downside scenario, the directors considered a situation in which inflationary costs are not fully recovered through pricing, there are high levels of volatility in the sugar market without price adjustments, there is an adverse movement to the cash conversion cycle within the Group and severe IT outages occur leading to a period of non-operation. This downside scenario was modelled without taking any mitigating actions within the Group's control. The downside scenario was applied both to the Food business following completion of the demerger, and to the Group in its current composition. Under this downside scenario, both the Food business post-demerger and the Group in its current composition had sufficient forecast liquidity throughout the period. In addition, the directors also considered the circumstances which would be needed to exhaust the Group’s total liquidity over the assessment period – a reverse stress test. As the demerger is anticipated to take place towards the end of the assessment period, we have prepared the reverse test calculation based on the current Group structure. This indicates that, on top of the downside scenario outlined above, annual profit before tax would need to decline by a further 33% without any price increases or other mitigating actions being taken before total liquidity is exhausted. The likelihood of these circumstances is considered remote for two reasons. Firstly, over such a period, management could take substantial mitigating actions, such as reviewing pricing, taking cost-cutting measures and reducing capital investment. Secondly, the Group has significant business and asset diversification and would be able to, if it were necessary, dispose of assets and/or businesses to raise considerable levels of funds. Operating segments The Group has five operating segments, as described below. These are the Group’s operating divisions, based on the management and internal reporting structure, which combine businesses with common characteristics, primarily in respect of the type of products offered by each business, but also the production processes involved and the manner of the distribution and sale of goods. The Board is the chief operating decision-maker. Inter-segment pricing is determined on an arm’s length basis. The segment measure of profit or loss is adjusted operating profit, as shown on the face of the consolidated income statement. Segment assets comprise all non-current assets except employee benefits assets, income tax assets and deferred tax assets and all current assets except cash and cash equivalents, current asset investments and income tax assets. Segment liabilities comprise trade and other payables, derivative liabilities, provisions and lease liabilities. Segment profit or loss, assets and liabilities include items directly attributable to a segment as well as those that can be allocated on a reasonable basis. Unallocated items comprise mainly corporate assets and expenses, cash, borrowings, employee benefits balances and current and deferred tax balances. Segment non-current asset additions are the total cost incurred during the period to acquire segment assets that are expected to be used for more than one year, comprising property, plant and equipment, investment properties, right-of-use assets, operating intangibles and biological assets. Businesses that are closed or disposed in the year are shown separately and comparatives are re-presented. The Group comprises the following operating segments: Retail Buying and merchandising value clothing and accessories through the Primark and Penneys retail chains. Grocery The manufacture of grocery products, including hot beverages, sugar, vegetable oils, balsamic vinegars, bread and baked goods, cereals, international foods and meat products, which are sold to retail, wholesale and foodservice businesses. Ingredients The manufacture of yeast and bakery ingredients as well as specialty ingredients focused on enzymes, precision extraction, health and nutrition and pharmaceutical delivery systems. Sugar The growing and processing of sugar beet and sugar cane for production of a range of sugar and other products in Africa, the UK and Spain. Agriculture The manufacture of specialty feed ingredients, premix and compound animal feed, as well as the provision of other products and services for the agriculture sector. The Food subtotal presented comprises Grocery, Ingredients, Sugar and Agriculture. This subtotal does not represent a separate operating or reportable segment and is provided to illustrate the composition of the Group following the proposed demerger. Geographical information In addition to the required disclosure for operating segments, disclosure is also given of certain geographical information about the Group’s operations, based on the geographical groupings: United Kingdom; Europe and Africa; The Americas; and Asia Pacific. Revenues are shown by reference to the geographical location of customers. Profits are shown by reference to the geographical location of the businesses. Segment assets are based on the geographical location of the assets.
