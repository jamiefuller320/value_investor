# Associated British Foods plc (ABF.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:03:13.570349+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Associated British Foods is a FTSE 100 conglomerate spanning Primark retail and a global food portfolio (Grocery, Ingredients, Sugar, Agriculture), controlled by Wittington Investments. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness (P/E 15.7, P/B 1.3), modest leverage (D/E ~8%), a 3.0% yield, and FCF yield, with nine of 22 models passing across all four factor families. The central investment hook is a planned demerger of Primark from FoodCo (target: effective before end-2027), which could clarify a sum-of-the-parts discount, alongside ongoing share buybacks and a balance sheet the group describes as strong (leverage 1.2x at H1 FY2026). The key debate is whether valuation already reflects a cyclical earnings trough — group adjusted operating profit and EPS are guided below FY2025, Sugar has moved from breakeven to a guided £25–60m loss, and Primark’s European recovery remains uneven — or whether demerger optionality and Primark’s UK turnaround justify accumulation at current multiples.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, ABF offers a rare combination of defensive diversification, balance-sheet capacity, and a identifiable corporate catalyst at a screen-attractive price. The screen passes Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Acquirer’s Multiple, Magic Formula, Dreman Contrarian, Dividend Growth, Neff PEGY, Earnings Quality, and Financial Health — covering cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk in a single name with full data quality (20/20 metrics). That profile is unusual for a FTSE 100 consumer name and aligns with a patient, catalyst-aware value approach rather than pure deep-value distress.

Business quality is mixed but not weak. Primark (~£9.5bn revenue) retains a differentiated value-fashion model, UK like-for-like recovery (+1.3% H1 FY2026, market share to 7.0%), and white-space store growth (~4–5% p.a.). FoodCo (~£9.8bn revenue) combines branded grocery (Twinings, Ovaltine), specialty ingredients, and global supply-chain assets. The board’s demerger decision (21 Apr 2026), supported by Wittington, aims to create two FTSE 100-eligible pure plays with clearer capital allocation — a structural unlock the market may not fully price while earnings are depressed.

Capital returns reinforce the case: FY2025 total dividend 63.0p, interim maintained at 20.7p, plus £250m buyback programme (partially completed). Net debt of £3,027m at 28 Feb 2026 (1.2x leverage) and £2.2bn liquidity (RCFs £1.8bn undrawn) support resilience. At ~15x earnings and ~1.3x book, the stock screens cheap relative to a portfolio generating ~£1.7bn adjusted operating profit (FY2025) even before any demerger re-rating — provided the investor accepts near-term earnings pressure and execution risk on separation.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / commodity:** Sugar is the dominant swing factor. European surplus, lower selling prices, higher gas (Middle East conflict), potential onerous beet contracts for 2026/27, and African production/currency risks (Malawi, Tanzania) drove H1 loss of **£27m** and a full-year guided loss of **£25–60m**, with management warning of further deterioration in FY2027 at the upper end of the range. Agriculture compound-feed volumes are weak.

**Competitive / operational:** Primark European LFL **−5.6%** (H1) and **−3.6%** (Q3) in a difficult clothing market; US trading volatile despite space growth. Allied Bakeries/Hovis integration addresses structural UK bread decline but is **marginally dilutive in FY2027** before accretion, with restructuring costs ahead.

**Regulatory:** Hovis deal cleared by CMA (Jun 2026); demerger requires Listing Rules Significant Transaction disclosure, tax clearances, and shareholder approval — estimated **~£75m** separation costs and **<£45m** dis-synergies.

**Governance / structure:** Wittington Investments (Weston family) remains controlling shareholder, committed to majority stakes in both post-demerger entities — limits minority influence and creates related-party complexity. Demerger triggered **£30m** deferred tax derecognition in Azucarera (Primark Spain leaving Spanish tax group).

**Pension:** UK DB scheme shows a large **£1.68bn accounting surplus** — favourable for covenant but exposed to discount-rate and inflation moves; not a funding crisis but an accounting volatility item.

**Geopolitical / macro:** Filings cite Middle East conflict impacts on energy, freight, fabric costs, and consumer sentiment; US tariff and oils-demand weakness in Grocery; cyber-risk elevated.

**Going concern / liquidity:** Directors affirm going concern to FY2027; leverage 1.2x and **£2.2bn** liquidity provide headroom. No covenant stress flagged. Net debt rose **£398m** in H1 FY2026 on lower cash.

**January 2026 profit warning** (Primark weakness) preceded partial UK recovery — execution risk on turnaround remains live.
