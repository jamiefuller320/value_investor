Outlook The Group has strong plans in place for the balance of the year, and its proven Branded Growth Model will support delivery of further growth through its upcoming key trading period. We continue to monitor the current macroeconomic and geopolitical environment and will take action as required in the second half of the year. This year the Group now expects net regular interest to be approximately £18m, Trading profit to grow around 3-5% ahead of FY25/26, with full year adjusted Profit before taxation and adjusted earnings per share now projected to be at the top end of current market expectations. With an increasingly resilient business, and a strong balance sheet from which to acquire more higher growth brands, the Group remains confident in delivering further strategic progress over the medium term. Total headline revenue was £372.0m, ahead 0.8% and Non-branded revenue was £28.0m, a 12.2% decrease on the prior period. In the second quarter, Grocery branded headline revenue increased by 1.1% to £180.8m. Non-branded revenue trends improved in the second quarter; over the first half revenue was impacted by contract exits on stuffing custard and homebaking. Divisional contribution increased by 3.2% to £95.6m and margins advanced by 60 basis points to 25.7%, reflecting the Group’s strong focus on efficiencies, cost control and positive branded mix benefits of the trading performance. During the first half of this financial year, the underlying benefits of this model were partially offset by impacts of hotter weather in the UK, reducing demand for categories such as gravy, stock and soup and in turn, subduing branded revenue growth. However, over recent years, the Group has built a significantly more resilient business; the brand portfolio now benefits from the strength of the Branded Growth Model strategy, possessing less seasonal bias, and is now more diverse with revenues generated from expansion into new categories, overseas growth and execution of the M&A strategy. The Group’s well-established model of generating value is through leveraging the strength of its market leading brands, launching insightful new products, investing in its brands using a variety of different marketing techniques and building strategic retail partnerships with customers. The Grocery business continued to launch new products in the period, including Loyd Grossman premium Italian cooking kits, Ambrosia custard pouches, in addition to benefitting from other recent launches such as Angel Delight bubble jelly, OXO bone broth and Batchelors microwaveable pasta ‘n’ sauce. Marketing support for the Group’s category leading brands is a vital element of the Branded Growth Model, to ensure effective communication to a wide range of consumer groups. The Group utilises a blend of different channels to communicate its brands, including; TV and radio advertising, out of home media and digital and social media, the latter to drive greater connection with younger demographic audiences in particular. Additionally, the Group continues to deliver effective and impactful instore activity across many of its brands and categories. For example, Batchelors teamed up with the release of the new Spiderman movie as part of a major promotional campaign, which involved creating instore theatre, often sited at the end of aisle in retailers, to deliver maximum impact and returns. Together, both the innovation programme and retailer partnerships have resulted in a further increase in share of Grocery’s distribution points in major retailers, increasing by 19 basis points compared to the same period a year ago. Another of the Group’s main strategies is to leverage the equity of its leading brands to expand into adjacent categories. Revenue growth from new categories increased by 15%, as the Group’s brands continue to demonstrate their brand stretch capabilities. The main current initiatives are Ambrosia porridge pots, Cape Herb & Spice and FUEL10K yogurt & granola pots; these have all delivered both year-on-year revenue growth and increased market share. Ambrosia porridge pots are now well established in their category, are listed in all major food retailers with a range of five variants and supported by the wider Ambrosia brand media strategy. Cape Herb & Spice strong growth reflected additional new retailer listings compared to the prior period and benefitted from consumer usage in BBQ and salad occasions during the summer months. FUEL10K also grew strongly and is well positioned for younger consumers seeking an on-the-go protein boost for breakfast. The Group’s acquired brands of recent years; The Spice Tailor , FUEL10K and Merchant Gourmet all delivered double-digit revenue growth in the period. Benefitting from the Group’s Branded Growth Model, The Spice Tailor launched products including Yakisoba Noodle kits, reflecting our expansion into pan Asian cuisine, FUEL10K ’s growth was delivered by continued strong momentum from the core Granola range, while Merchant Gourmet introduced new Gourmet Baked Beans and Meals in Minutes ranges. Operating profit Operating profit was £63.7m in the period, £9.5m lower than the prior period. Trading profit increased by £1.7m to £72.2m, as described above and net interest on pensions and administrative expenses was a credit of £10.2m, £3.6m lower than the prior period, reflecting a lower opening pensions surplus compared to the prior year. Non-trading items 10 were £7.7m, £6.0m higher than the prior period and are primarily due to SG&A organisational restructuring costs and the disposal of a non-trading entity. Finance costs In May 2026, the Group increased available facilities under the RCF to £367.5m. The RCF margin is 1.5% above SONIA and matures in May 2031. In September 2026, the Group arranged a new term loan facility of £175.0m with its bank group which matures in September 2028, including an option to extend a further 12 months with a margin of 1.55% above SONIA. Following the signing of the term loan, the Group’s £275.0m bridge facility was cancelled. In October 2026, the term loan was drawn down in full, and together with existing cash reserves, the Group repaid its £330.0m Notes on 15 October 2026. The refinancing reduces expected financing costs and provides additional flexibility to support the Group’s growth strategy. As a result, FY26/27 net regular interest is now expected to be approximately £18m. Net finance cost was £8.6m in the first half of the year, a decrease of £1.2m compared to the prior period. Net regular interest 6 was £0.6m lower at £7.5m, due to higher interest receivable and lower SONIA rates on facilities compared to the prior period. Interest on the Group’s £330m Senior secured notes (“Notes”) of £5.8m was in line with the prior period. Other interest of £1.1m reflected accelerated write off of the cancelled bridging facility and lower discount unwind of provisions compared to the prior period. Taxation The taxation charge for the period was £14.1m which is £2.5m lower than the prior period. This was substantially due to a charge on operating activities of £13.8m (2025/26 H1: £15.9m). Tax on operating activities reflects the rate of UK corporation tax (25%) owing to the Group’s large UK presence. The Group continues to be able to offset a proportion of cash tax payable through available brought forward losses. Cash tax payable in FY26/27 is now expected to be c.£10m. Principal risks and uncertainties The Group’s Annual Report for the 52 weeks ended 28 March 2026 reported our enterprise risk management process on pages 62 to 64, with the principal risks disclosed on pages 64 to 69. The Group operates its enterprise risk management programme for which the Board maintains responsibility as defined by the Code. The Executive Leadership Team formally reviews the enterprise risk management programme each month to ensure the completeness and accuracy of the identified material risks and emerging risks, and the effectiveness of associated material controls that manage residual risk against defined risk appetite thresholds. The material risks identified both top-down from the Board and bottom-up from management teams underpin the identification of principal risks. The Group believes that there has been no significant change to our principal risks. However, it continues to closely monitor the macro-economic environment, geopolitical situation, and cyber activity risks, as it reported an increase in both these related principal risks in the last Annual Report. The complete list of principal risks for the Group is as follows (in alphabetical order): Climate change, Food safety, Impact of government legislation on our products, Legal compliance, Macroeconomic and geographical instability, Market impacts on our business, People, Product portfolio, Supply chain interruption, and Technology and cyber.
