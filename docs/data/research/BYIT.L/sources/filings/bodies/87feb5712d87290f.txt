Outlook The demand drivers in our markets are strengthening, with software spend forecast to grow ahead of IT budgets as a whole and AI, cyber security and infrastructure the areas attracting most incremental investment. We are continuing to invest in our services capability to take a larger share of that spend. We upgraded our full year guidance on 17 September 2026, following a first half that was ahead of our expectations. The Board continues to expect GP growth for the full year in the low to mid-teens in percentage terms, with low to mid single-digit percentage growth in operating profit. This excludes any contribution from CyberOne, with the acquisition expected to complete by the end of October 2026, as we expect its post-acquisition operating profit in FY27 to be offset by amortisation costs of the intangible assets acquired, but with a positive contribution to earnings per share forecast for FY28. Our second half will be measured against a stronger comparative, and our costs are slightly weighted towards the second half, when commission accelerators apply and the sales intakes joining in November begin to build capacity for FY28. Microsoft’s next set of partner incentive changes takes effect in October. These are separate from the changes made in January 2025, which have now annualised. The revised incentives increase the rewards on growth products and enhance the incentives available on CSP, while reducing the rebates on certain legacy products, and are expected to be positive for the Group. They have been appropriately planned for and are included in our upgraded guidance. The c.£4.5m of cost normalisation we set out at our FY26 results falls across the full year. It reflects previously flagged costs returning to normal levels, including bonuses and share-based payments, and the sales and technical capacity added against clear customer demand. Following normalisation, and as that capacity becomes fully productive, the Board expects operating profit to grow more broadly in line with GP from FY28, with operating leverage building thereafter. Over the last two years we have deliberately evolved BTG from a licensing-led reseller into a customer-centric, solutions and services-led business, aligning our teams around the sectors and verticals our customers operate in, investing in technical expertise, expanding our managed services and acquiring specialist capability where it accelerates us. The strategy is straightforward: win more customers, grow our share of wallet with existing customers, and build higher-value recurring services that can be sold through both Bytes and Phoenix. The first half shows that strategy working. BTG delivered a strong first half. GII grew 19.6% and GP grew 17.8%, with double-digit GP growth in both our public and private sector businesses, while our Microsoft-related GII and GP also grew broadly in line with the Group’s GII and GP as a whole. The strategic changes we made in FY26 are delivering, with trading ahead of the expectations we set at the start of the year, and as a result we upgraded our guidance for FY27 in September. The market backdrop has remained supportive. Customers are increasing their technology budgets across the areas in which we operate, with software spend continuing to grow ahead of wider IT budgets. Cyber security remains a clear priority, reflecting the continued threat environment and increasing regulatory requirements. AI is creating new areas of investment in data, cloud and infrastructure, while demand for infrastructure more generally remains strong in parts of the market. Vendor pricing has also remained firm, with suppliers continuing to invest heavily in AI. The acquisition of CyberOne strengthens our capabilities across the cyber security lifecycle, which is strongly underpinned by AI related demand. Customers are increasingly focused on extracting greater value from their technology investments while managing growing complexity, and this plays directly to our strengths. Through Bytes and Phoenix we have built long-standing positions as trusted technology advisers, supported by our own IP in Quantum and License Dashboard, which allows us to support customers across software, AI, data, cloud and cyber security. The changes we have made in recent years have been deliberate, giving us the specialist expertise, solutions and managed services to support customers throughout the technology lifecycle, deepen those relationships and increase our participation in their technology spend. Operating profit increased 6.6%, with operating profit as a percentage of GP of 36.4% (HY26: 40.2%). The first half included around half of the c.£4.5m of cost normalisation for FY27 as described at our FY26 results, while we have also continued to invest in technical delivery. As these costs annualise and the additional capacity is utilised, we expect operating profit to grow more closely in line with GP from FY28. Cash generation remained strong, with cash conversion of 45.7%, reflecting the normal weighting towards H2. We returned £41.3m to shareholders in the half and closed the period with £68.4m of cash and no debt. Customers As a value-added reseller, our growth comes from selling more software and services to existing customers, as well as winning new customers. Our focus is on helping customers achieve a clear outcome, whether that is reducing cost, managing risk or delivering transformation. In the first half, 98.2% of our GP came from customers we traded with during the same period last year, with a further £1.8m of GP generated from new customers. Among those is Samsonite, the global travel luggage brand, where over five years we are consolidating around 300 of its sites onto a single managed network platform, with the potential to extend to around 1,000 sites globally. The changes we have made to our sales organisation, as described further below, with greater alignment of Bytes and Phoenix are intended to make us more effective in how we serve those customers. Customer-centricity: sector alignment and common services In July 2026 we completed the second realignment, with Bytes focused almost entirely on the private sector and Phoenix on the public sector. Bytes already generated 87% of its GP from the private sector and Phoenix 97% from the public sector, so the change affected a minority of sales relationships. It was carefully sequenced, with a small number of colleagues moving within the Group and account managers retaining their existing customer relationships, and no customer accounts changed hands. The change gives each business a clearer sector focus and makes it easier for customers and vendors to work with us. It also removes internal competition for the same customers and allows the two businesses to collaborate more closely. In particular, it gives us the opportunity to build common services capabilities across the Group and deploy them through both businesses. This is paramount of our services strategy; building a capability once and selling it to both private and public sector customers, increasing the addressable market for each investment we make and reducing duplication across the Group. Customer-centricity: private sector sales segmentation At the start of FY26 we moved our private sector sales organisation from a generalist model to a structure aligned to customer size: enterprise, corporate, mid-market and channel. Our technology and vendor specialist teams were moved into the same segments. Customers of different sizes have different requirements. Larger organisations typically have established internal teams and need additional specialist expertise, while smaller organisations are more likely to require design, implementation and support. The new structure allows us to reflect those differences in our sales approach. Private sector GP grew 14.6% in the first half, with growth in corporate, mid-market and channel. The new structure is now established and is improving the quality of customer engagement. Account managers are working with customers facing similar issues, while technical and vendor specialists are involved earlier in the sales process. The approach also aligns more closely with how our major vendors organise their own teams, which has supported commercial discussions and lead generation. We have also appointed a new Chief Marketing Officer at Bytes, with a focus on making our marketing activity more targeted by segment. Our enterprise segment is progressing more gradually. These are our largest customers and relationships are still largely centred on Microsoft enterprise agreements. Our objective is to broaden those relationships into managed services, but these opportunities take longer to develop and close. This remains an important area of focus, as managed services provide a deeper and more recurring relationship with customers. Howdens is an example of what that looks like when it works. We have worked with them for more than ten years and are now their strategic cyber security partner, advising on security transformation, governance, AI security and risk management across the group. Private sector GP/GII margin was 12.2% (HY26: 12.5%), reflecting the continued migration of customers from enterprise agreements to CSP. This changes the reported margin profile, which is why we continue to manage sales performance primarily on GP. We will continue to refine the structure where this improves customer engagement, supported by our investment in change management and sales leadership. Public sector Public sector GP grew 23.1% in the first half, with our GP/GII margin stable at 3.5%. Public sector customers buy primarily through frameworks, tenders and enterprise agreements. Our approach remains to establish a position and then build profitability over the life of the contract by adding higher-margin products and services as requirements develop. We have retained the Microsoft licensing contract for the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs for a further three years, covering around £167m of the department’s technology spend over the term. Phoenix continues to build expertise by vertical, which is important to public sector customers and reflected in framework evaluations. Our work with the University of Oxford, where we support more than 40 technology vendor relationships alongside commercial and procurement advice, and our wider credentials in the academic sector are examples of this approach. In healthcare, we continued to support the NHS in deploying and adopting Microsoft Copilot for around 500,000 employees, following the award of one of the largest Microsoft End Customer Investment Fund projects globally. The work includes establishing use cases, setting guard-rails, training users and supporting change, alongside the underlying licence. With Phoenix focused on the public sector, we expect to develop more opportunities of this type. Services Services is an increasingly strategic part of our offering. It covers the advice, design, implementation and ongoing management that customers need to get value from the technology they buy. The recurring element of this work, particularly managed services, also provides deeper and longer-term customer relationships. Vendors are increasingly directing investment towards partners that can help customers deploy and adopt their technology. Services GII grew 41.0% in the first half and we continued to invest in the technical capacity required to support this growth, with technical delivery headcount increasing by 13.3% since February. As a result, our services GP grew by 12.0% to £9.3 million against the comparative period across both internally and externally delivered work. With the additional investment the services margin fell slightly from 24% to 19% but we expect the utilisation of the capacity we have added to generate higher returns, with security currently a strong area of customer demand. Our objective is to provide customers with a broader managed services offering covering the cloud platform, user access, endpoints and security. These capabilities can be developed across the Group and sold through both Bytes and Phoenix, improving the economics of our investment in services. Acquisition of CyberOne The acquisition of CyberOne after the period end, (exchange of contracts on 9 October 2026 with completion expected before the end of October), accelerates our Services strategy. CyberOne provides AI-enabled managed cyber security services built on Microsoft technology, including managed detection and response from its own accredited security operations centre. Around 90% of its income is recurring under multi-year contracts. The acquisition gives Bytes its own 24x7 security operations capability and reduces our reliance on third parties for an important part of customers' security requirements. It also provides a capability that can help customers make greater use of Microsoft security products they already own. The initial consideration of £19.4m will be funded entirely from existing cash resources, with further performance-related payments over the following 16 months to a maximum of £18.0m. The acquisition is expected to be earnings-enhancing in the first full financial year of ownership. Our full year guidance excludes any contribution from CyberOne. Phoenix will continue to operate its own security capability for public sector customers, where accreditation and tenancy requirements differ. Artificial intelligence AI is the largest change on our customers’ technology agendas, and it is now a meaningful driver of growth across our core business rather than a separate activity alongside it. Adopting AI is not a single purchase and before an organisation can deploy at scale it needs its identity and access controls in order, its data governed, its security tested, and its people trained and supported through the change, each of which we sell and deliver. With Microsoft’s incumbent position in workspace technology, it means many of our customers already hold much of that governance framework under their existing M365 licensing. Our role is to get customers to the point at which AI can be adopted safely, drive integration, and create real value. Our work with the NHS is the clearest example, and we are recognised as a Microsoft Frontier Partner in this agentic era. We have continued to build our AI capability across both businesses, with dedicated practice leadership and development programmes to grow the skills our customers now expect of us, and our teams are increasingly asked to advise on governance and guard-rails as much as on the technology itself. A second opportunity is emerging behind the first which we are well positioned to benefit from. In the past, managing software spend has meant managing licences, which are countable and broadly predictable. AI is charged by consumption, which makes cost far harder to forecast and control, and customers are already asking us how to manage it. The discipline is shifting from counting licences to managing the performance of what an organisation actually uses, and our licensing and software asset management skills and experience give us the right foundation to manage this. We expect helping customers manage the economics of AI to become an increasingly important part of the advice we give. We also apply AI inside our own business, to improve efficiency and to make it easier for customers to deal with us. Proprietary agents built by our own teams are now fully embedded within customer projects, driving efficiencies and releasing significant time for increased focus on customer delivery without adding headcount. These sit alongside our existing technologies, and everything our agents produce is reviewed in detail by our highly skilled teams before it reaches a customer. Vendors Our value to vendors comes from finding opportunities for their technology to deliver business goals for customers, and then enabling implementation and driving adoption so that an outcome is achieved. Microsoft is our largest vendor relationship, accounting for around half of our GP, growing broadly in line with the first half. It remains a key gateway to growth, engagement and trust with our customers, accounting for a significant share of their software budgets and incremental investment, particularly into cloud, cyber security, and data and AI, and it increasingly directs its own investment towards partners who can drive adoption and consumption. Microsoft partner incentives evolve over time, just as technology and the way organisations use it evolve, and we have a long-established track record of absorbing these changes while maintaining our GP levels. The changes made on 1 January 2025, which reduced the incentives available on enterprise agreements, have now annualised and are behind us. A further set of changes take effect from October 2026. These reduce the rebates available on certain legacy products, increase the rewards on growth products including those carrying security and AI capability, and enhance the incentives available on CSP. The CSP element is the most significant for us, because it rewards moving customers onto longer, multi-year commitments, which deepens the relationship and improves the visibility of the gross profit that comes with it. That work takes time and will not all land in a single year, but it is a positive for the Group and the direction we have been building towards. We have prepared for this appropriately, by aligning our sales coverage and services around the products Microsoft is now rewarding, and by launching Seller Insights, which gives every account manager a prioritised view of the funding available to each customer, the evidence from that customer’s own usage that they should be using it, and what makes the conversation timely. These changes are planned for and included in our upgraded FY27 guidance. Our customers value breadth, but not at the expense of depth, and we continue to broaden our share of non-Microsoft work by deepening a focused set of relationships in the areas where customer budgets are moving fastest. In security we work with vendors including Check Point, CrowdStrike, Darktrace, Google Wiz, Mimecast, Netskope, Rubrik and Zscaler. In hybrid platforms, where customers are refreshing infrastructure alongside their cloud investment, we work with Dell, Evepure (formerly Pure Storage), HPE, Lenovo, Nutanix, and VMware by Broadcom, and we continue to grow our cloud business with AWS. Vendor awards won during this period include: AvePoint partner of the year & Technical Innovation award, Checkpoint Exposure Management Partner of the Year UK & Infinity Partner of the Year UK, Mimecast EMEA Partner of the Year – June 2026, Palo Alto Technical Partner of the Year 2026, Rubrik 2026 EMEA Partner Award & Pipeline Contributor. People Our hiring in the first half was measured, with headcount only increasing 2% since February to 1,353, as we concentrated on utilising the sales capacity added over the previous two years rather than adding to the cost base. That discipline is visible in our productivity, with GP per employee increasing by 13% as front-line sales headcount increased by just 4.4% year on year and sales support headcount reducing as we completed the reorganisation of our sales teams. Technical delivery headcount increased 13.3%, to support the growth in our solutions business. Having made efficient use of existing capacity, we will add more in the second half, with sales intakes joining both businesses in November, which we expect will carry full quota by the year end. New sales intakes take time to scale and reach full productivity, so this is an investment in FY28 and beyond, and a clear signal of our confidence in the growth aspirations of the business ahead. Our people are the lifeblood of our organisation, and the way our teams have supported customers through a period of considerable internal change has been exceptional. Phoenix was ranked 51st in the Best Small to Medium Workplaces in Europe list. We continue to invest in developing our people, including the technical and AI skills our customers increasingly expect of us. Gross invoiced income GII reflects gross income billed to our customers and has a direct influence on our movements in working capital. However, it does not capture all the IT spend we help our customers with because, in some cases, our vendor partners invoice the customer directly and pay us a fee that is a percentage of their sales value, and which we recognise within our GII, revenue and GP. GII has increased by 19.6% year on year, to £1,605.2m (HY26: £1,342.0m), driven by strong growth in software, hardware and services. Growth came through in both the public sector (+ 20.6%) and the private sector (+ 17.3%), with our mix remaining weighted to the public sector, which contributed 70% of total GII (HY26: 70%). Private sector GII benefited from the transition of more customers to Microsoft’s CSP programme (where BTG invoices the customers) from Microsoft’s EA programme (where Microsoft invoices the customers and pays BTG a fee). Revenue Revenue is reported in accordance with IFRS 15, with hardware and internal services reported gross (principal) and software and external services reported net (agent), which means revenue reflects changes in the mix of business but is often not a good indicator of underlying growth. This reporting of revenue as a mix of GP and GII across the four income streams has given rise to an 18.7% increase, with growth in internal services and hardware (both reported gross) and growth in software and external services (both reported net). Given revenue is a mix of metrics, we focus on GP to provide a consistent measure of our sales and profit performance. Gross profit GP, our primary measure of sales performance, has grown by £14.7m, up 17.8% year on year to £97.1m (HY26: £82.4m). Breaking this down by income stream, starting with the Group’s two most strategic focus areas, software GP grew strongly by 15.8% to £83.5m, with GP/GII% largely maintained at 5.4%, while services GP grew by 12.0% to £9.3m, following continuing investment in delivery heads. We continue to be supported by Microsoft providing services funding, for both internal investments and customer engagements. Hardware GP more than doubled off a small base to reach £4.3m. Looking across our two main customer sectors, public sector GP has grown by 23.1% and private sector GP increased by 14.6%, both returning to double-digit growth following the reductions in the Microsoft enterprise agreement incentives which affected the prior year. The GP growth again demonstrates the Group’s strategy of winning new customers and then expanding share of wallet. Our objective is to ensure we build our profitability within each contract by adding additional higher-margin products into the original agreement as the customers’ requirements grow and become more advanced. This process is further enhanced by focusing on selling our wide range of solutions offerings and higher-margin security products, while maximising our vendor incentives through achievement of technical certifications. We track these customers individually to ensure that the strategy delivers value for the business, and our other stakeholders, over the duration of the contracts. As in previous years, with higher margins available in the private sector it contributed 60% of total GP (HY26: 61%) despite our GII being weighted to the public sector. Our GP/GII margin was broadly flat at 6.0% (HY26: 6.1%). In the public sector, our margin (GP/GII) increased slightly to 3.5% (HY26: 3.4%) and in the private sector it dropped slightly to 12.2% (HY26: 12.5%), the latter in part due to switching customers from direct fee EA contracts to indirect billing CSP agreements which in absolute terms are more profitable as demonstrated by the strong GP growth.
