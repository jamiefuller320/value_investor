# Clarkson PLC (CKN.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:05:57.356121+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Clarkson PLC is the leading global integrated shipping services group — shipbroking, capital markets, port support and maritime data — with a long record of cash generation, net cash, and 24 consecutive years of dividend growth. The quantitative screen rates the stock a buy (8/22 models; P/E 19.6, P/B 2.8, yield 2.4%, ROE 15.3%, debt/equity 11%), consistent with a quality compounder trading at a modest multiple. Filings show FY2024 record underlying profit of £115.3m, a softer FY2025 (guided ≥£90m underlying PBT), then a record H1 2026 (£61.5m underlying PBT) driven by Middle East trade disruption. The central debate is whether current earnings reflect durable franchise strength or cyclical/geopolitical volatility that will mean-revert — and whether reported versus underlying EPS growth (flagged by the screen’s earnings-basis overlay) warrants treating headline growth metrics with caution.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Clarkson offers a rare combination: market leadership in a niche, asset-light economics, conservative leverage, and a progressive dividend policy backed by substantial free cash resources. The screen’s passes across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk align with filing evidence: underlying ROE-equivalent profitability has exceeded £100m in three straight years to 2024; pension schemes show a net surplus; going-concern stress tests model profitability down to £30m without liquidity stress; and litigation contingencies are described as immaterial.

The business model earns largely USD-denominated broking commissions while investing in people, data (Clarksons Research, Sea platform) and bolt-on M&A — Link Group, Zuma Labs and Serpac in H1 2026 — to widen product breadth. Recurring research revenue (~91% of Research sales) and a forward order book (US$231m for 2025 invoicing at end-2024) provide some earnings visibility, though spot broking remains cyclical. At ~20x earnings with 15%+ ROE and net cash, the screen’s buy signal is plausible if normalised earnings sit well above trough levels. The adjusted screen signal of hold — on statutory versus filing-adjusted EPS divergence — is the main reason to size positions conservatively rather than treat this as a deep-value re-rating story.

**Trend summary**  
Revenue and underlying profit peaked in FY2024, softened in FY2025 (weak H1, tariff/FX pressure), then re-accelerated sharply in H1 2026 on elevated freight rates, derivatives activity and Financial division capital-markets work — heavily linked to Strait of Hormuz disruption. Balance sheet remains strong throughout; acquisition-related costs and Pillar Two top-up tax (£1.3m in H1 2026) are incremental drags. Impairment losses on financial assets (£7.7m H1 2026 vs £6.0m H1 2025) reflect ongoing client credit provisioning in volatile markets.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and geopolitical earnings.** Principal risks unchanged from annual reports: macro-economic and geo-political factors rank first. H1 2026 record profit explicitly cites Hormuz turbulence; prior guidance cuts (May 2025) show how quickly tariff policy and freight-rate normalisation can compress profits. Goodwill impairment is flagged if any market sees a sustained downturn.

**Foreign exchange.** Majority of broking revenue is USD; reporting is GBP. May 2025 trading statement quantified a potential £9.5m profit hit from weaker USD versus prior expectations at prevailing rates. H1 2025 head-office costs rose on USD weakness.

**Key personnel and governance.** CEO Andi Case has led since 2008; long tenure is a strength and concentration risk. CFO/COO Jeff Woyda retiring after 20 years; Niamh Staunton (ex-BP) appointed CFO from November 2026 — orderly handover stated, but transition risk remains. Company secretary change (October 2025).

**Pension.** Three defined-benefit schemes with combined surplus (~£12–14m at recent interims); actuarial volatility and asset-ceiling constraints noted on Plowrights scheme. Not a balance-sheet threat at current levels but requires monitoring if discount rates or asset returns shift.

**Regulatory and compliance.** Sanctions complexity, cyber risk, and breaches in rules/regulations are standing principal risks. Pillar Two global minimum tax applies from 2026 (Greece, UAE, China, Switzerland operations). Professional indemnity insurance carried; no material litigation expected per interim contingency note.

**Accounting and APM reliance.** Material gap between underlying and reported EPS (H1 2026: 147.6p vs 131.2p basic) due to acquisition-related costs; screen’s earnings-basis overlay adjusted signal to hold. Forward order book and “underlying” metrics require investor judgement on normalisation.

**Customer credit.** Rising impairment on trade receivables in volatile markets; allowance £26.4m at June 2025.
