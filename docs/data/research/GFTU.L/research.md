# Grafton Group plc (GFTU.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:28:15.634191+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Grafton Group is a European construction-products distributor and merchant operator trading at a modest absolute valuation (screen P/E 12.4×, P/B 1.2×, yield 3.6%) despite a balance sheet that is net-cash before IFRS 16 leases (£274.0m at FY2025) and a long record of free cash flow generation. FY2025 results showed revenue up 10.4% to £2.52bn and adjusted operating profit up 7.1% to £190.2m, with adjusted EPS of 75.4p and free cash flow of £168.3m — a recovery from the 2024 earnings trough but still well below the 2022 cyclical peak. The quantitative screen rates the name a **strong buy** (12/22 models, composite 83%), yet timing is flagged **wait** (RSI ~74, ~12% above 200-day SMA). The central debate is whether geographic diversification (Ireland, Iberia) and acquisition-led growth can offset a structurally weak Great Britain construction market (~5% LFL decline in H1 2026), and whether goodwill on UK/GB distribution assets — where auditors flagged limited impairment headroom — is adequately priced in.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Grafton offers a rare combination: **cheapness, quality cash conversion, dividend discipline, and capital return**, all visible in both the screen and primary filings.

**Quantitative screen alignment.** The name passes five factor families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with 12 of 22 models clearing (Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Schloss Low P/B, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Piotroski F-Score, Composite Value, Earnings Quality, Financial Health, among others). Key metrics: P/E 12.4, P/B 1.20, current ratio 2.11, FCF yield supported by filing-defined FCF of £168.3m on adjusted operating profit of £190.2m. Data quality is full (20/20 metrics present).

**Business quality.** Grafton operates a federated model across ~470 branches and ~10,000 colleagues in four geographies (Island of Ireland, Great Britain, Northern Europe, Iberia). FY2025 adjusted ROCE was 10.9% (+60bps YoY); adjusted operating margin 7.3% (–30bps, before property profit). Management has returned £428.3m via buybacks since May 2022 (share count down ~22%) while maintaining dividend cover at 2.0× on a 37.75p full-year payout (+2.0%). Net debt inclusive of leases was £123.4m (0.39× EBITDA), with EBITDA interest cover of 41.5× — consistent with investment-grade framing in filings.

**Valuation hook.** Statutory P/E on 70.3p basic EPS implies a lower multiple than adjusted 75.4p; either way, the market prices Grafton as a cyclical distributor near trough-region earnings, not as a cash-generative platform targeting >10% adjusted EPS CAGR to 2030 (Capital Markets Event, June 2026). H1 2026 trading maintains FY2026 adjusted operating profit guidance of £190m–£200m (consensus ~£193.7m), suggesting the recovery narrative is intact despite GB weakness.

**Why strong buy, not immediate chase.** The screen’s timing overlay (RSI overbought, wait-for-pullback) is appropriate: share price has run ahead of a still-subdued UK RMI cycle, and goodwill impairment sensitivity on GB distribution assets adds a latent accounting risk not captured by ratio screens alone.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / geographic concentration shift.** Construction markets are inherently cyclical (Annual Report). Great Britain — ~30% of revenue, <25% of adjusted operating profit per May 2026 update — showed LFL declines of 5.0% (4M 2026) and 5.1% (H1 2026) with “no meaningful improvement anticipated.” London/Southeast exposure (~60% of GB revenue) faces weakest housing starts in 40+ years (6,000 units in 2025). Recovery optionality exists but timing uncertain.

**Goodwill / impairment.** Goodwill of £659.1m; auditors flagged **limited headroom** in former UK Distribution CGUs at 30 September 2025, with impairment testing sensitive to 2026–2030 revenue growth and margin assumptions. Segment reorganization to Island of Ireland / GB / Northern Europe / Iberia reallocated goodwill — residual GB risk remains.

**M&A and integration.** Active acquirer (Salvador Escoda 2024, HSS Hire Ireland, Cygnum March 2026, Mercaluz April 2026). Integration execution and price paid are not fully dissected in available extracts; deferred/contingent consideration items appear in FY2025 cash flow notes.

**Inflation, supply chain, geopolitical.** 2026 trading updates cite Middle East conflict-related inflation, fuel costs, and supply chain risk (actively managed, no material disruption to date). Sustained cost inflation could compress volumes.

**Leverage structure.** Headline net cash is flattering before £397m+ IFRS 16 lease liabilities (Yahoo balance sheet). Filing-defined net debt/EBITDA 0.39× is comfortable; lease-adjusted targets of 1.0–2.0× for 2030 allow room but reduce flexibility in a downturn.

**Pension.** Defined benefit obligations present but modest (non-current pension liabilities ~£4m per Yahoo; £2.4m cash contributions above IAS 19 charge in FY2025). Remeasurement gains in equity, not a primary concern.

**Governance / disclosure gaps.** Principal risks and viability statement sections are indexed in the Annual Report table of contents but the body extract is truncated — full principal-risk register not available in the corpus. No Brexit-specific risk language located in indexed bodies (“Brexit silence” per prior research overlay).

**Market timing.** Quantitative screen flags overbought conditions; near-term entry risk even if fundamentals support accumulation on weakness.
