Definitions The Board of Volex considers that the current consensus market expectation for revenue is $1,411.1 million (with a range of $1,377.0 million to $1,437.2 million) and for underlying operating profit of $153.9 million (with a range of $151.5 million to $156.3 million). The Group presents some significant items separately to provide clarity on the underlying performance of the business. This includes significant one-off costs, such as acquisition-related costs, the non-cash amortisation of intangible assets acquired as part of business combinations and share-based payments. Further detail on adjusting items is provided in note 3. Underlying operating profit is operating profit before adjusting items and share-based payment expense. Underlying free cash flow is the net cash before financing activities and excluding costs of acquisitions, the interest element of lease payments, adjusting items and share-based payments. Cash conversion is defined as cash generated from operations before adjusting operating items, less net capital expenditure, as a percentage of underlying operating profit. Net debt (before operating lease liabilities) represents cash and cash equivalents, less bank loans net of debt issue costs and finance leases but excluding operating lease liabilities. Total lease liabilities include $37.6 million of operating lease liabilities (H1 FY2026: $32.9 million). Covenant leverage is net debt (before operating lease liabilities) divided by underlying EBITDA adjusted for depreciation of right-of-use assets and pro-rated for acquisitions and disposals. Organic revenue growth is calculated using constant exchange rates by taking the total reported revenue (excluding the impact of acquisitions and divestments) divided by the comparative period’s revenue at the current year’s exchange rates. Return on capital employed is calculated as the last twelve months’ underlying operating profit as a percentage of average net assets excluding net cash / debt. RESULTS FOR THE HALF YEAR TO 30 SEPTEMBER 2026 Overview Volex delivered a record first half, with revenue and underlying operating profit both at their highest ever levels. These results validate the strategy of developing embedded relationships with category leaders in structurally growing markets, for whom we provide mission-critical components for increasingly complex systems. Organic revenue grew by 23.6% at constant exchange rates, and total revenue grew by 23.9% to $723.6 million. This included an $11.3 million contribution from KST since we took full ownership in July 2026, partly offset by a $7.4 million foreign exchange headwind. First-half revenue also included approximately $10 million brought forward from the second half. This reflected a planned build of buffer stock, agreed with customers in Complex Industrial Technology, to support continuity of supply as we consolidate our US manufacturing sites. Excluding this pull-forward, organic growth was 21.8%. Sequentially, first-half revenue was 9.2% higher than in the second half of FY2026 on an organic constant currency basis. Strong positioning in five attractive end-markets gives Volex a broad base of structural growth opportunities and provides resilience by limiting dependence on any individual category. In H1 FY2027, all five end-markets grew, including Medical and Consumer Electricals, both of which had experienced headwinds in FY2026. EV & Electrification saw organic growth of 46.8% as our track record on existing programmes led to us being awarded a greater share of our largest EV customer’s requirements, making it the largest end-market percentage growth in the Group. Complex Industrial Technology contributed organic growth of 41.2%, led by Data Centre and aerospace and defence customers and benefited from c.$10 million of revenue brought forward from H2. Excluding the pull-forward, organic growth in Complex Industrial Technology was 34.7%. Data Centre revenue grew 47.0% organically year-on-year. As previously outlined, revenue was lower than in H2 FY2026, when Volex fulfilled additional short-term customer volumes that did not repeat as supply conditions normalised. Excluding Data Centre, Group organic growth was 19.5% demonstrating broad-based growth across the rest of the portfolio. Off-Highway grew 12.6% organically, despite a prior-year comparative that included a one-off defence vehicle programme. Consumer Electricals and Medical both returned to organic growth of 9.5% and 1.8% respectively. In addition, KST has strengthened our position in the Medical end-market with a range of advanced solutions providing connectivity between the patient and diagnostic and treatment equipment. Our operating model combines the responsiveness of a local manufacturing partner with the scale, capability and resilience of a global platform. Consolidating our manufacturing capabilities into larger Centres of Excellence brings together technical expertise, equipment and automation under one roof, lowering costs, driving operational scale and enhancing responsiveness, while our multi-location footprint gives customers flexibility as global trade conditions change. Inflation remains well managed across the Group. Contractual pass-through mechanisms with customers largely protect our profit from movements in copper prices, and we recover wider cost inflation through regular price negotiations. Alongside this, continued productivity gains, including automation and operational efficiencies, help to offset labour and overhead cost increases. The underlying operating margin for the first half of the year was 11.1%, up 130 basis points on the prior period (H1 FY2026: 9.8%) and ahead of the 10.2% achieved in FY2026, driven by our optimisation and efficiency programmes and operating leverage on strong organic growth, with a further contribution from a more favourable mix including KST. This represents further progress towards our medium-term targets, set out at the April 2026 Capital Markets Day, of $2 billion revenue, a 12% underlying operating margin and a return on capital employed of around 20%. Realising our strategy The Group’s strategy is clear and consistent: to scale with category-leading customers in structurally growing markets, where increasing product complexity drives higher-value solutions, margin expansion and long-term growth. This strategy is enabled by Volex’s scalable global platform and disciplined approach to capital allocation. The strategy is built around four priorities. 1. Target and scale with category leaders We are selective about the customers we partner with. We focus on businesses that lead their markets, invest at the frontier of their industries and generate long-term structural demand. We engage early in their design cycles and embed ourselves into how their products are built. The resulting relationships are durable and hard for competitors to displace, and often last at least for the life of a technical platform. They give us strong visibility over multi-year programmes, with our role becoming more critical as our customers’ products evolve and become more complex. The first half shows this clearly: a larger share of our largest EV customer’s requirements drove organic growth of 46.8% in EV & Electrification. 2. Move up the complexity curve in every market In every end-market, we aim to take on more demanding applications. This requires deep engineering expertise, rigorous quality standards and close collaboration with customers’ development teams. Greater complexity makes us harder to substitute, supports pricing and drives margin progression. In Complex Industrial Technology, we have partnered with our customers through successive generations of high-speed data interconnects. The next generation of higher-speed products is expected to ramp up during FY2028. Each generation is a more technically demanding and higher-value product. In Consumer Electricals, power cord relationships are developing into internal appliance harnesses worth several times more per unit. Continued investment in vertical integration and research and development supports this progression. 3. Scale capability across new markets and geographies We grow organically by taking proven capabilities into adjacent markets and regions through measured, well-evidenced steps rather than speculative expansion. Each step builds on the last, which reduces time to revenue and lowers execution risk. Off-Highway is a good example. The capabilities we built with European construction and agricultural equipment manufacturers are now being replicated in North America and Asia. In the first half, we further expanded our existing operations in China and Indonesia. Our medium-term plan anticipates around $500 million of incremental revenue from organic growth, delivered through a series of connected initiatives rather than a single transformational investment. 4. Acquire capability that accelerates organic growth Acquisitions play a specific role: to add capabilities or market positions that strengthen organic growth, rather than simply adding revenue. In July 2026, we acquired the remaining 64.3% of KST, taking full ownership following the initial investment in 2017. KST extends our Medical business into patient-to-device cables and adds competitive manufacturing capacity in Asia. A scalable operating model Our operating model is what allows us to execute these priorities consistently as the Group grows. Our sites operate autonomously within a group-wide management framework. Accountability for customer service and operating performance sits with site general managers. This keeps decisions close to the customer and lets us respond quickly to changing requirements. Regional leadership teams and a common platform of standards in safety, quality and continuous improvement support them. Consolidating manufacturing into larger Centres of Excellence brings people, equipment and process expertise together, improving efficiency and scalability. Our multi-location footprint facilitates the transfer of complex production between sites without disrupting customers. Revenue by reportable segment Volex operates as a globally integrated business, built around the needs of its customers. Our international manufacturing footprint allows us to serve customers efficiently and responsively wherever they operate. As customers continue to diversify their supply chains to reduce single-country risk, bring production closer to end-markets and respond to a changing political landscape, our ability to manufacture in multiple strategic locations, and to transfer complex products between facilities without disruption, has become a key differentiator for our category-leading customer base. Our regional structure, with reporting lines through Regional Chief Operating Officers, is designed to meet these needs directly. We therefore present segmental information by region, with customer revenue attributed to the geography in which each customer relationship is managed. This reflects our customer-centric approach and the way we run the business. Revenue in North America grew by 35.8% to $390.4 million (H1 FY2026: $287.5 million). It is our largest customer region at 54.0% of Group revenue (H1 FY2026: 49.2%). In this region, we primarily serve category leaders in technology, defence and electrification. The region includes products that we manufacture within the US, shipments to the region and exports worldwide, where the customer relationship is managed in North America. Growth was led by EV & Electrification, continued strong Data Centre demand and aerospace and defence customers, including c.$10 million brought forward ahead of the transfer of production from Spokane to California. KST, whose customer relationships are managed in North America, also contributed. Revenue in Europe grew by 4.9% to $229.3 million (H1 FY2026: $218.6 million) and accounts for 31.7% of Group revenue (H1 FY2026: 37.4%). Growth came from Complex Industrial Technology customers across the region and a modest return to growth for Medical customers. Revenue in Asia grew by 33.5% to $103.9 million (H1 FY2026: $77.8 million) and accounts for 14.3% of Group revenue (H1 FY2026: 13.3%). Growth came from expansion in the Indian domestic market, including Off-Highway sales, and a recovery in Consumer Electricals as new domestic appliance projects ramped up. Revenue by customer sector EV & Electrification Revenue from EV & Electrification increased to $133.6 million (H1 FY2026: $89.9 million), representing organic growth of 46.8%. This was the fastest growing end-market during the half, adding $42.6 million of the $135.8 million organic increase. The main driver was a greater share of our largest EV customer’s requirements. This is the return of several years of investment in vertical integration. We extrude our own cable, mould our own components and make our own pins, which gives us a cost position and a speed of response that competitors buying in these parts find hard to match. Most of the growth with this customer came from charging products, so it is not tied directly to the customer’s vehicle production. However, growth was not confined to one customer. Demand from charging infrastructure customers increased, and we secured new programmes in energy storage, where a fast-growing US customer is supplied from our Mexican operations. We are completing the vertical integration of our North American operations, with cable extrusion and moulding capacity in Mexico expected to be fully operational during the next financial year. This will extend our cost advantage to products made for the US market and will support new in-vehicle programmes with our largest EV customer from FY2028. We expect second-half EV & Electrification revenue to be broadly in line with the first half. Off-Highway Revenue in the Off-Highway end-market increased to $144.3 million (H1 FY2026: $136.8 million), representing organic growth of 12.6%. Reported growth was 5.5% after an adverse currency impact of $8.6 million, mainly from the translation of Turkish lira sales. This was achieved against a strong prior-year comparative that included a one-off defence vehicle programme. Performance was supported by increased demand in material handling and further growth in India. As agricultural, construction and defence vehicles adopt more advanced electronic systems, demand for higher-level assemblies should increase. In North America, we are taking the capabilities built with European construction and agricultural equipment manufacturers to new customers, supported by added local capacity. Consumer Electricals Consumer Electricals revenues increased to $138.3 million (H1 FY2026: $125.6 million), representing organic growth of 9.5%. This is an expected return to growth following the decline in FY2026, when the business faced increased competition in Europe, flagged in the results announcement for FY2026. Long-standing partnerships with major global OEMs continue to support growth in harness products, programme wins secured during the prior year began to contribute, along with the benefit from passing through higher copper costs to customers. Appliances are becoming more complex. They are being connected into home energy-management systems and must meet stricter energy-efficiency standards. This greater complexity creates more opportunity for Volex. Our manufacturing base, combining geographic diversification, automation and vertical integration, allows us to compete on price while offering the quality, safety and global fulfilment customers value. Relationships that started with power cords are developing into internal appliance harnesses worth several times more per unit. Medical Revenues in the Medical end-market increased to $89.1 million (H1 FY2026: $76.1 million), representing organic growth of 1.8%. This marks a return to growth following the customer destocking in FY2026. As anticipated, demand has stabilised as customers have brought inventory back into line with end-user demand, and we expect structural growth drivers to support further recovery. In July, we completed the acquisition of the remaining 64.3% of KST. The acquisition extends our Medical offering into patient-to-device cables and, because KST has limited customer overlap with the wider Volex Group, it creates cross-selling opportunities with our global healthcare technology customers. KST contributed $11.3 million of revenue since completion. Complex Industrial Technology Sales to Complex Industrial Technology customers grew significantly, with organic revenues up 41.2% to $218.3 million (H1 FY2026: $155.5 million). Data Centre revenue, which now represents 57.6% of revenue in this sector, was well ahead of the prior-year period, with 47.0% organic growth, but below the second half of FY2026, primarily because of additional volumes in that period that did not repeat. In that period, having secured key components early, we met customers’ near-term capacity needs when other suppliers could not. Supply conditions have since normalised, as we guided to at the full year results. Underlying demand remains strong, the next generation of higher-speed products is expected to ramp up during FY2028, and the Board’s view of the medium and long-term opportunity is unchanged. Our Data Centre products are high-speed copper cables that connect equipment within and between racks over short distances. Over these distances copper uses less power and generates less heat than optical alternatives. With power availability an increasingly important constraint in data centre build-outs, that efficiency gives copper an advantage, and we continue to develop each new generation of higher-speed products with our customers. Revenue from the sector’s other customer segments grew by 34.0% organically, or around 20% excluding revenue brought forward from the second half. US defence customers continued to increase orders in line with their programme cycles, against a backdrop of elevated defence spending in the US and Europe. Customers also built buffer stock, bringing forward c.$10 million of revenue, to support continuity of supply through the transfer of production from our Spokane, Washington facility to California. Underlying gross margin The underlying gross margin for the first half of the year remained broadly consistent with the comparative period at 23.0% (H1 FY2026: 23.3%). Continued focus on operational efficiencies and a beneficial revenue mix had a positive impact in the first half of FY2027 compared to the same period a year ago. The acquisition of KST also provides an enhancement to gross margin period-on-period. These gains largely offset an adverse movement in foreign exchange rates in addition to the impact of higher copper costs and labour cost inflation in Türkiye, which has not yet been fully mitigated by a corresponding devaluation of the local currency. For our domestic Türkiye-based customers, we retain contractual rights to pass through inflationary cost increases; however, price increases for export customers are subject to negotiations. In parallel, we continue to focus on productivity and efficiency initiatives and foreign exchange hedging in Türkiye. Underlying operating profit Underlying operating costs increased by $7.2 million to $86.1 million (H1 FY2026: $78.9 million), $1.3 million of which are attributed to the KST acquisition. Inflationary pressures contributed to increased labour costs year-on-year. The remaining increase reflects business growth and investments in expanding capabilities and capacity. Foreign exchange rate changes had a positive impact on underlying operating costs in the period, arising from retranslation of balance sheet items. Underlying operating profit rose by 40.9% to $80.6 million (H1 FY2026: $57.2 million), benefiting from the operational leverage created by the strong organic growth. Our optimisation and efficiency programmes were an important element: consolidating into larger Centres of Excellence, automating production lines and growing central costs much more slowly than revenue. The underlying operating margin for the first half was 11.1%, 130 basis points higher than the 9.8% reported in H1 FY2026 and 90 basis points above the FY2026 result. Operating leverage, as well as productivity and cost optimisation improved operating margins by 150 basis points, while improvements in product mix contributed a further 100 basis points and the acquisition of KST contributed 20 basis points in improved margins. Offsetting this were adverse impacts from inflation of 20 basis points, foreign exchange rates of 60 basis points and the impact of investment in capacity and growth of 60 basis points which arose from additional facility and people costs required to deliver sustainable growth. Adjusting items and share-based payments The Group presents some significant items separately to provide clarity on the underlying performance of the business. This includes significant one-off costs such as restructuring and acquisition-related costs, the non-cash amortisation of intangible assets acquired as part of business combinations, and share-based payments, as well as associated tax. Adjusting items and share-based payments were a net credit of $6.0 million in the period (H1 FY2026: charge of $10.6 million), comprising the non-cash gain of $26.8 million on remeasuring our previously held 35.7% interest in KST at the acquisition date, less costs of $20.8 million. The gain on KST arises because, under IFRS 3, our existing 35.7% stake had to be revalued to fair value when we took full ownership in July. The stake was carried at $3.2 million and has been revalued to $30.0 million, based on the price paid for the remaining 64.3% and adjusted for a discount for lack of control. The gain is non-cash, will not recur, and is excluded from underlying profit. The costs are made up of $6.4 million (H1 FY2026: $4.8 million) of amortisation of acquired intangible assets, $6.9 million (H1 FY2026: $2.6 million) of share-based payments expense, $4.6 million (H1 FY2026: $1.2 million) of acquisition-related costs and $nil (H1 FY2026: $2.0 million) related to site closure costs. They also include listing migration costs of $2.9 million (H1 FY2026: $nil) relating to the move to the Main Market. Net finance costs Net finance costs were consistent at $10.4 million (H1 FY2026: $10.4 million). The financing element for leases for the period was $1.5 million (H1 FY2026: $1.4 million). Finance costs also included $0.2 million for the unwinding of the discount on deferred consideration for the Murat Ticaret acquisition (H1 FY2026: $0.8 million). The net interest expense on defined benefit obligations increased to $0.9 million (H1 FY2026: $0.5 million). In September 2022, the Group entered into a four-year interest rate swap in respect of $50 million of drawn debt, fixing the interest rate on that portion of the debt to reduce its exposure to changes in interest rates. The swap matured in September 2026. Taxation The Group’s income tax expense for the period was $2.5 million (H1 FY2026: $8.5 million), representing an effective tax rate (“ETR”) of 3.3% (H1 FY2026: 22.4%). The main driver was an $11.7 million one-off deferred tax credit from remeasuring deferred tax liabilities on acquired intangible assets in Türkiye, which is reported as an adjusting tax item. This is the result of the corporate income tax rate reduction for production activities to 12.5% for tax years beginning in 2027, which was enacted during the period. The underlying ETR was 24.2% (H1 FY2026: 22.7%), the increase mainly driven by the cancellation of inflation accounting in Türkiye enacted in H2 FY2026. This was partly offset by increased benefits from tax incentives and a lower adverse impact from currency volatility in Türkiye. The main factors affecting the underlying ETR are the rate of currency devaluation in Türkiye (though this will be partly mitigated by the reduced tax rate going forwards), developments surrounding the Group’s uncertain tax positions, the continuing availability of certain tax incentives and reduced rate regimes and the jurisdictional profit mix. Cash tax paid during the period was $34.3 million (H1 FY2026: $7.1 million). This includes $15.8 million of tax payments in respect of KST’s pre-acquisition profits reported within cash used by adjusting items. The underlying cash tax paid was $18.5 million, driven by the increase in the Group’s profits, timing of tax payments and the exhaustion of tax losses in a major jurisdiction during the prior year. Net debt and cash flows Underlying EBITDA increased by 35.1% to $99.4 million (H1 FY2026: $73.6 million). Underlying free cash flow was an inflow of $22.3 million, compared to $4.8 million in H1 FY2026. This included an underlying working capital outflow of $29.1 million (H1 FY2026: $32.4 million), together with net capital expenditure of $23.7 million (H1 FY2026: $21.3 million) and underlying tax and net interest payments of $23.2 million (H1 FY2026: $14.3 million). Historically, the Group has been more cash generative in the second half of the financial year. The statutory working capital outflow reflected several factors: Continued investment in inventory to support customer demand, including in the Data Centre market, which has a longer cash cycle due to typically operating through a hub model. This resulted in a cash outflow of $50.8 million (H1 FY2026: $30.4 million cash outflow) An increase in receivables leading to a cash outflow of $19.2 million (H1 FY2026: $3.8 million) reflecting the growth in revenue achieved in the half An inflow relating to payables of $31.5 million (H1 FY2026: $2.2 million inflow) as a result of the increased inventory required to support our customers Our cash conversion reflects the pace at which we are growing. Revenue increased by $139.7 million year-on-year and supporting that growth requires more inventory and receivables. We also hold more stock than a pure contract manufacturer so that we can respond quickly to customers, which is a large part of why they choose Volex and why the work carries better margins. This approach is fundamental to our strategy and contributes towards our competitive advantage. Net interest decreased to $7.0 million (H1 FY2026: $7.2 million) and included $2.3 million related to KST tax payments. The increase in cash tax payments was mainly due to a payment relating to KST. Net debt (before operating lease liabilities) rose to $203.7 million (FY2026: $121.5 million), primarily due to the movement in working capital, acquisition of KST (initial net cash consideration of $35.8 million, completion bonuses of $9.0 million and tax and interest payments of $18.1 million) and the share buyback. Operating lease liabilities increased to $37.6 million (FY2026: $30.8 million) following the extension of a lease in Tijuana. Our major manufacturing sites hold customer qualifications and certifications that are costly and time-consuming to replicate, particularly in regulated markets such as Medical. Long-term leases at these locations provide security of tenure and cost certainty, supporting customer confidence in our supply continuity. As a result, the Group’s statutory net debt position increased to $241.3 million (FY2026: $152.3 million). On 1 April 2026, we exercised $100 million of the uncommitted accordion, increasing the committed revolving credit facility to $500 million. On 30 September 2026, $273.9 million of the facility remained undrawn. The facility matures in June 2029, with a further $100 million uncommitted accordion available. Investing in our business Organic investment is central to our strategy. We deploy capital where it strengthens our capabilities, sets us apart from competitors and delivers attractive returns. Every project is assessed with rigour: we focus on customer-backed opportunities rather than speculative capacity, prioritise investments that fit our strategic priorities and typically target a cash payback within two years. In the first half, we continued to expand our global manufacturing footprint. New facilities in Suzhou, China and Batam, Indonesia were secured towards the end of the period, with hiring underway ahead of revenue. Further expansion in Pune, India will continue into the second half as well as a second Indian site in Bangalore. We plan capacity in step with customer programmes, informed by close dialogue with our customers and a deep understanding of our end-markets. We also continued to develop our Centres of Excellence and progressed the previously announced consolidation of our US manufacturing sites into a single specialist site in California. Investment in capability is as important as investment in capacity. Many of our new programmes are highly automated, and we continue to retrofit automation to existing lines to improve productivity, drive efficiencies and produce cost savings. Further investment in vertical integration strengthens control over our supply chain and supports our margins. In the first half, we continued to invest in cable extrusion and moulding capacity in Mexico, extending vertical integration to products for the US market, with these projects expected to complete in FY2028. As we are making significant investments in the long-term fundamentals of the business, margin progression is unlikely to be linear. The second half will include additional dual running and ramp-up costs as we complete the consolidation of our US manufacturing into a single specialist site in California and bring new capacity in China, Indonesia and India on stream ahead of revenue. Net capital expenditure for the first half was $23.7 million (H1 FY2026: $21.3 million), approximately 3.3% of revenue (H1 FY2026: 3.6%), 80% of which was within our Centres of Excellence, which now deliver approximately 49% of Group revenue. We also made $4 million of incremental operational investment to support future growth. This covered additional facility costs, higher depreciation from recent capital investment and the recruitment of additional sales and engineering staff. Increased research and development activity is enabling us to bring new products to market, particularly for EV & Electrification and Data Centre customers. The rate of investment will increase in the second half of the year, with expected capital expenditure of approximately 5% of revenue for the full year. These investments continue to generate strong returns, with return on capital employed of 23.3% for the twelve months to 30 September 2026 (FY2026: 21.0%; H1 FY2026: 20.2%), ahead of our medium-term target of around 20%. Acquisition strategy Acquisitions remain a core part of Volex’s growth model. Our approach is disciplined and repeatable and is focused on acquiring capability rather than simply adding revenue. We target businesses whose capabilities, customer relationships or geographic reach strengthen the Group’s organic offering and can be scaled across the Volex platform. Our pipeline is actively managed and prioritises businesses in complex, high-specification markets that we understand well, with embedded customer relationships and high-mix product portfolios. As global supply chains continue to shift, we also consider the location of each target, favouring cost-competitive sites with favourable trade dynamics. Valuation discipline is central: we target a return on capital employed of at least 15% within two years, with operating margins of around 15%, or a clear path to achieve them in the short term. We only take on businesses that need significant integration or restructuring where we have the management capacity and expertise to deliver. Since restarting our acquisition programme in FY2019, we have completed 13 transactions, representing in aggregate, investment of approximately $500 million. Each has been chosen for its own strategic merit, adding capability, geographic reach or customer access, and creating value for both Volex and the acquired business. In July 2026, we acquired the remaining 64.3% of KST for total commercial consideration of up to $89.4 million, including deferred payments, accounted for as remuneration, of up to $14.7 million, equivalent to an enterprise value of 10x KST’s FY2025 EBITDA. KST operates at margins above the Group’s and meets our acquisition criteria. Integration and trading are progressing in line with the acquisition plan. With a strong balance sheet, a $500 million committed facility and an active pipeline, we are well placed to pursue further acquisitions that meet our criteria. These will support the c.$300 million of acquired revenue target, outlined in our medium-term plan. Customers will draw down the buffer stock built in the first half ahead of the consolidation of our US manufacturing, and we expect this to be offset by a full six months’ contribution from KST. The Group’s balance sheet remains strong following the acquisition of KST and the completion of the share buyback programme. With substantial headroom under our $500 million committed facility, we have the capacity to keep investing in customer-backed growth and to pursue acquisitions that meet our return criteria, alongside additional shareholder returns where appropriate. Our partnerships with category leaders in structurally growing markets, our progress up the complexity curve and the scalability of our global platform underpin the Board’s confidence in the Group’s prospects and in its ability to deliver its medium-term targets of $2 billion revenue, a 12% underlying operating margin and a return on capital employed of around 20%. Going Concern The Group’s financial statements have been prepared on the going concern basis, which contemplates the continuity of normal business activity with the realisation of assets and the settlement of liabilities in the ordinary course of business. When assessing the Group’s going concern status, the Directors have specifically considered whether there are any material uncertainties that may cast significant doubt on the Group’s ability to continue as a going concern. In making this assessment, the Directors have taken into account the Group’s financial position, including its cash and cash equivalents and access to a committed borrowing facility of $500 million, which matures in June 2029. The facility also includes an additional $100 million uncommitted accordion. Under the terms of the facility, covenant leverage must remain below 3.0x and interest cover must be in excess of 3.0x. The Directors have reviewed the facility's terms, including covenant requirements and remaining duration, and are satisfied with the Group’s continued compliance and significant headroom. The Directors have prepared a cash flow forecast for the period to the end of March 2028, which is based on the latest FY2027 forecast. The Directors have performed sensitivity analysis on the cash flow forecast using a base case and severe but plausible downside scenario, which take into account the principal risks and uncertainties set out in the Annual Report, including potential tariff impact. This downside scenario models a 15% reduction in year-on-year revenue, equivalent to the worst result in the past 20 years and demonstrates that the Group would still maintain substantial covenant and liquidity headroom throughout the going concern assessment period. The Directors have also specifically considered the potential impact of climate-related physical and transition risks as part of their assessment and do not believe these risks will have a material impact within the going concern period. The Directors have also conducted a reverse stress test to assess the extent of deterioration in trading conditions that would be required to breach the Group’s financial covenants or result in insufficient liquidity headroom within the going concern assessment period. This reverse stress test assumed the simultaneous occurrence of further material adverse factors, including a revenue decline materially beyond historical experience. The analysis indicates that a revenue reduction of 56.6% in the remainder of the year against the comparative period would be required to trigger leverage covenant non-compliance. Significant liquidity and interest cover covenant headroom remained even under the reverse stress test. The Directors consider such a scenario to be severe and remote, given the Group’s historical trading resilience, broad customer base and the ability to take mitigating actions. 1. Tax charge Interim period income tax expense is recognised at an amount determined by multiplying the jurisdictional profit before tax for the period by management’s best estimate of the annual income tax rate expected for the full year in each jurisdiction, adjusted for the tax effect of discrete items recognised in full during the interim period. As such, the effective tax rate in the interim financial statements may differ from management’s estimate of the effective tax rate for the annual financial statements. The Group’s income tax expense for the period was $2.5m (H1 FY2026: $8.5m), representing an effective tax rate (‘ETR’) of 3.3% (H1 FY2026: 22.4%). The main driver was an $11.7m one-off deferred tax credit from remeasuring deferred tax liabilities on acquired intangible assets in Türkiye, which is reported as an adjusting tax item. This is the result of the corporate income tax rate reduction for production activities to 12.5% for tax years beginning in 2027, which was enacted during the period. The underlying ETR was 24.2% (H1 FY2026: 22.7%), the increase mainly driven by the cancellation of inflation accounting in Türkiye enacted in H2 FY2026. This was partly offset by increased benefits from tax incentives and a lower adverse impact from currency volatility in Türkiye. The main factors affecting the underlying ETR are the rate of currency devaluation in Türkiye (though this will be partly mitigated by the reduced tax rate going forwards), developments surrounding the Group’s uncertain tax positions, the continuing availability of certain tax incentives and reduced rate regimes and the jurisdictional profit mix. Cash tax paid during the period was $34.3m (H1 FY2026: $7.1m). This includes $15.8m of tax payments in respect of KST’s pre-acquisition profits reported within cash used by adjusting items. The underlying cash tax paid was $18.5m, driven by the increase in the Group’s profits, timing of tax payments and the exhaustion of tax losses in a major jurisdiction during the prior year. The Group operates in a number of different tax jurisdictions and is subject to periodic tax audits by local authorities in the normal course of business on a range of tax matters in relation to corporate tax and transfer pricing. As at 30 September 2026, the Group has net current tax liabilities of $28.5m (FY2026: $16.2m) which include $20.4m (FY2026: $9.9m) of provisions for income tax uncertainties. There is a further $3.8m (FY2026: $1.9m) of accrued interest relating to these amounts recognised in other payables. The increase is due to tax exposures and related interest acquired with KST, against which an indemnification asset of $8.6m is recognised. This covers the sellers’ proportional share of the acquired exposures and is backed by funds held in escrow should the exposures crystallise. 5.
