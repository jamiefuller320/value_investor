# Morgan Sindall Group plc (MGNS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T18:11:13.011869+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Morgan Sindall Group is a UK-listed construction and regeneration group operating through five decentralised divisions — Partnership Housing, Mixed Use Partnerships, Fit Out, Construction and Infrastructure — with FY 2025 revenue of £5.0bn and over 8,500 employees. The quantitative screen rates the stock a **strong buy** (16/22 models, composite 76%, five factor families: cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk) on P/E 11.5×, P/B 2.7×, 3.7% yield, ROE 26.1%, net-cash balance sheet and Piotroski 8/9. Primary RNS filings confirm record FY 2025 and H1 2026 earnings, a £19.1–19.5bn secured plus preferred-bidder workload, and 20% dividend growth to 158p. The valuation hook is a statistically cheap, cash-rich compounder (18% ten-year adjusted PBT CAGR per management) trading below its 200-day moving average with neutral timing. The central debate is whether cash-generative Fit Out and public-sector Construction/Infrastructure can continue to offset housing-related capital absorption, partnership losses and eventual mean reversion in Fit Out margins.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MGNS offers an unusual combination of screen-level cheapness and contractor-quality fundamentals. The name passes Graham Enterprising, earnings yield, FCF yield, Lynch PEG, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Piotroski F-Score, Composite Value, Earnings Quality and Financial Health — a breadth that screens rarely achieve in UK industrials.

Filings substantiate the quality-and-cheapness alignment:

- **Earnings momentum:** FY 2025 adjusted PBT £232.6m (+35% YoY) on revenue £5,019m (+10%); adjusted EPS 370.0p (+33%). H1 2026 adjusted PBT £116.1m (+21%) on revenue £2,562m (+8%) — management's "record first half."
- **Balance sheet as competitive advantage:** Year-end net cash £531m (FY 2025), £418m at 30 Jun 2026; average daily net cash £368m (FY 2025) rising to £423m (H1 2026). Management explicitly positions net cash as a bidding differentiator for long-dated public-sector frameworks and partnerships.
- **Visibility:** Secured order book £12.0bn at FY 2025 (H1 2026: £12.2bn), with preferred-bidder work of £7.1bn (£7.3bn at H1 2026) — totals £19.1bn and £19.5bn respectively.
- **Cash returns:** FY 2025 total dividend 158p (+20%, cover 2.4× within the 2.0–2.5× policy); H1 2026 interim raised 10% to 55p. FY 2025 operating cash inflow £195.9m (87% of adjusted operating profit); free cash flow £161.5m (filing-aligned; screen FCF £170.7m).

The decentralised model lets Fit Out and Construction Services fund Partnership capital through the cycle — the structural feature separating MGNS from levered pure-play contractors. Screen failures (Graham Defensive, net-net, deep value, economic moat on ROA/margins, high dividend yield quartile) reflect P/B above deep-value thresholds and cyclical sector economics, not balance-sheet distress. The strong-buy signal is new (one week at signal, neutral timing, RSI ~42, price ~6% below 200-day SMA), so the thesis supports accumulation rather than maximum immediate sizing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and housing exposure.** Private housing demand remains subdued; Partnership Housing capital employed guided to £500m–£580m in FY 2026 with ROCE below 2025 (8% at H1 vs 10% prior year). Open-market completions fell 10% (FY 2025) and 23% (H1 2026). Mixed Use Partnerships expected small FY 2026 loss.

**Working capital.** Annual report marks working capital mismanagement risk as **increased**. Inventories rose £127m FY 2025 and a further £90m in H1 2026; net working capital swung from −£104m to +£30m.

**Building safety / regulatory.** Provisions £51.8m at 30 Jun 2026; Developers Pledge obligations live; MHCLG reimbursements may start H2 2026. Contingent liabilities on bonds, guarantees and claims disclosed with insurance mitigants.

**Fit Out normalisation.** FY 2025 op profit £139.9m well above medium-term band; FY 2026 expected below 2025 — key earnings risk.

**Cyber.** Risk status **increased** in annual report despite ISO27001 and MOD certification.

**Governance (minor).** Responsible Business Committee dissolved; CFO role expanded from Apr 2026. No going-concern qualification.

**Pension gap.** No material DB risk in extracts; full pension note not available — unverified.
