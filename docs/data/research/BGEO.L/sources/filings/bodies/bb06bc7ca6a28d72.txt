Macroeconomic developments: Georgia Sustained growth momentum Georgia’s economy maintained strong growth momentum in the first nine months of 2026, with real GDP expanding by 7.4% y-o-y, according to preliminary data, driven primarily by services. With growth broadly in line with our expectations, we maintain our full-year real GDP growth forecast of 7.5%. Our positive outlook is supported by resilient household consumption and sustained external inflows, and is further reinforced by the IMF’s upward revision of its 2026 growth forecast for Georgia from 6.5% to 7.2% in October. While elevated global energy prices have contributed to higher domestic inflation, economic activity has remained resilient, supported by diversified external inflows and prudent macroeconomic policies. Meanwhile, the Middle Corridor’s growing strategic importance offers additional growth opportunities for Georgia. Resilient external flows In 3Q26, external inflows remained resilient. Merchandise exports rose by 18.8% y-o-y, driven by higher global commodity prices, while imports increased more moderately by 10.6%. International tourism revenues also returned to growth, increasing by 11.9% y-o-y following a modest contraction in the previous quarter amid air travel disruptions in the Middle East. Meanwhile, inbound money transfers declined by 4.6% y-o-y as an EU-sanctioned Russian remittance operator suspended operations in several countries, including Georgia. Notably, transfers from other major source countries, including the US, EU and Israel, continued to grow. Appreciating GEL and record-high international reserves In the first nine months of 2026, the Georgian Lari (GEL) appreciated by 3.3% against the US dollar. During the same period, it gained 6.9% against the Euro and 5.4% against the Pound Sterling. The appreciation was underpinned by strong external inflows, continued deposit de-dollarisation, and rising non-resident holdings of Georgian government securities, with their share reaching 12.9% at end-September, up from 3.0% at end-2025. The National Bank of Georgia (NBG) continued its foreign currency purchases in 3Q26, lifting gross international reserves to a record high of USD 8.2 billion at the end of September 2026 (up 50.5% y-o-y). We expect the GEL to remain broadly stable in the medium term, supported by sound macroeconomic fundamentals and prudent policies. Elevated inflation and moderately tight monetary policy Headline inflation remained elevated at 5.6% in September 2026, primarily driven by higher fuel prices, while core inflation was more moderate at 3.5%, closer to the NBG’s 3% target. Headline inflation eased slightly from 5.8% in June, and we expect it to average 5.3% in 2026 before gradually returning to the target in 2027. The NBG has kept the refinancing rate unchanged at 8.25% since its 25-bp hike in May. We expect monetary policy to remain moderately tight through the remainder of 2026. Strong fiscal discipline Georgia’s fiscal position remained sound, supported by sustained economic activity. The fiscal deficit is expected to remain at 1.4% of GDP in 2026, broadly unchanged from 2025 and 0.6 pp below the budgeted level, reflecting stronger-than-expected economic growth and lower-than-planned capital expenditure. For 2027, the deficit is budgeted at 2.5% of GDP, consistent with maintaining fiscal sustainability. Government debt is expected to decline to below 32% of GDP by end-2026, with growing share of domestic debt in total government borrowings. Healthy bank lending and continued de-dollarisation The banking sector continues to demonstrate sound fundamentals, ample liquidity, and strong capitalisation. Lending growth remained broadly in line with nominal economic activity, with the loan portfolio expanding by 16.9% y-o-y in 3Q26 on a cc basis, following growth of 14.5% in the previous quarter. Business and consumer lending continued to drive credit expansion. Loan dollarisation declined to 41.5% at end-September 2026, down 0.2pp quarter-on-quarter. Deposit dollarisation also fell by 0.9pp over the same period to 43.0%. More information on the Georgian economy and financial sector can be found at Galt & Taggart , the Group’s investment banking and brokerage subsidiary. Macroeconomic developments: Armenia Resilient growth Economic growth in Armenia remained resilient in 3Q26, following real GDP growth of 6.0% y-o-y in the first half of the year. Strong domestic demand and the rapid re-routing of trade flows have moderated the adverse impact of recent trade tensions with Russia. We maintain our full-year 2026 real GDP growth forecast of 5.5%, supported by continued growth in services, ongoing fiscal stimulus, and the recent launch of operations at a new gold mine. The evolving geopolitical landscape in the broader region, alongside Armenia's recalibrating relationship with Russia, presents some downside risks to the near-term growth outlook. Nevertheless, the Armenian economy has remained resilient, supported by diversified external inflows, prudent macroeconomic policies, and trade liberalisation with the EU. The government remains committed to maintaining economic cooperation with Russia and to continuing Armenia’s participation in the Eurasian Economic Union. The medium-term growth outlook also carries significant upside potential from the historic 2025 peace accord with Azerbaijan and improving relations with Türkiye. Strong Dram Armenian merchandise exports fell by 11.6% y-o-y in 3Q26 amid lower re-exports and Russian import restrictions, while imports grew by 15.5%, driven by strong domestic demand. During the same period, international tourism arrivals fell by 13.5% y-o-y, driven by a decline in visitors from Russia. In contrast, remittances continued to grow strongly, increasing by 16.1% y-o-y. The Armenian Dram (AMD) continued to appreciate, supported by strong foreign investment and external borrowing in the mining and IT sectors, as well as external borrowing, despite weaker current account inflows. The AMD gained 4.9% against the US dollar in the first nine months of 2026. Over the same period, it gained 1.4% against the GEL. The Central Bank of Armenia (CBA) continued its foreign exchange purchases, increasing gross international reserves to a record high of USD 6.7 billion at end-September 2026 (up 55.3% y-o-y). Tighter monetary policy despite easing inflation Inflation eased in 3Q26, as rising fuel prices were offset by moderating food prices. Headline CPI inflation declined to 4.3% y-o-y in September 2026, from 5.1% in June. Meanwhile, lower inflation in non-food goods and services suggests that inflation expectations remain contained. We expect headline inflation to average 4.5% in 2026, before returning to the CBA’s 3% target next year. Despite lower headline inflation, the CBA raised its policy rate by 25 bp in September 2026 to 6.75%, citing stronger domestic and external demand as a source of increased inflationary risks. We do not rule out a further rate hike before the end of 2026. Continued fiscal expansion Fiscal policy is expected to remain growth-supportive in 2026, with the fiscal deficit projected to widen to 4.0% of GDP from 3.7% in 2025. Despite higher public spending, strong tax revenue growth is expected to keep government debt broadly stable, at 46.5% of GDP at end-2026, down slightly from 46.7% at end-2025. The Ministry of Finance targets a reduction in the fiscal deficit to below 3% of GDP by 2028. Armenia’s fiscal framework is also supported by the ongoing IMF Stand-By Arrangement. Sound banking sector The Armenian banking sector remained sound, supported by strong capital and liquidity buffers. Bank lending grew by an estimated 25.4% y-o-y in 3Q26 on a cc basis, following growth of 22.1% in the previous quarter. Consumer and business lending continued to drive credit expansion, with corporate lending particularly strong, reflecting rising funding needs for large-scale projects amid Armenia’s increasing openness to foreign investment. This has also contributed to higher loan dollarisation, up to 37.2% at end-September 2026, from 36.1% at end-June. Business Division results The Group results are presented by the following Business Divisions: 1) Georgian Financial Services (GFS), 2) Armenian Financial Services (AFS), and 3) Other Businesses. Performance highlights • GFS net operating income grew by 15.1% y-o-y in 3Q26 and 15.1% y-o-y in 9M26, driven in both periods by strong net interest income generation complemented by solid net fee and commission income. Q-o-q, net operating income was up 4.1%, with net interest income as the primary driver, supported by a positive contribution from net foreign currency gains and net fee and commission income. • Net interest income grew 15.2% y-o-y in 3Q26 and 17.1% y-o-y in 9M26, with NIM expanding 40 bps y-o-y in both periods to 6.6% and 6.4%, respectively, reflecting a methodology adjustment, whereby mandatory reserves are excluded from interest-earning assets in the NIM calculation; Adjusting for this, NIM was up 20 bps y-o-y in 3Q26, reflecting a higher share of loans in interest-earning assets mix. On a q-o-q basis, underlying NIM was flat, as a 10-bps reduction in loan yield to 12.7% was offset by a 10-bps decline in the cost of funds to 5.2%. • Net fee and commission income grew 31.9% y-o-y in 3Q26 and 27.2% y-o-y in 9M26, partly reflecting arrangements with international payment systems, including rate changes and sign-on benefits. Excluding these effects, net fee and commission income grew 17.4% y-o-y in 3Q26, driven mainly by settlement operations alongside continued growth in acquiring volumes. Renegotiated terms with international payment systems also had a significant positive impact on 4Q25, creating a high base for the y-o-y comparison in 4Q26; however, on a normalised basis, we expect underlying y-o-y growth in net fee and commission income in 4Q26 to be broadly in line with that of the current quarter. • Net foreign currency gains were broadly flat y-o-y in 3Q26 and declined 3.9% y-o-y in 9M26, reflecting a more stable currency environment and increased market competition. • Operating expenses increased 9.3% y-o-y in 3Q26 and 13.2% y-o-y in 9M26, driven by higher staff costs, mainly reflecting salary increases. On a q-o-q basis, operating expenses decreased by 3.4%, against an elevated 2Q26 base that included heightened marketing and Investor Relations activities, an accelerated recognition of previously granted unvested share-based awards following the voluntary departure of an executive, and the seasonal phasing of certain expenses. On an underlying basis, operating expenses were flat q-o-q. • Portfolio quality remained sound. The cost of credit risk ratio was 0.7% in 3Q26 and 0.6% in 9M26, compared with 0.6% and 0.5% in the respective prior year periods, and 0.5% in 2Q26. The y-o-y increase was mainly driven by normalisation of ECL in Retail with underlying portfolio performance remaining stable and robust, partially offset by strong performance in CIB. On a q-o-q basis, the increase was due to low base in 2Q26 driven by strong recoveries in CIB and methodological changes positively impacting ECL in SME. • NPL ratio decreased to 1.9% as at 30 September 2026, from 2.3% a year earlier and 2.1% in the prior quarter, reflecting continued improvement in underlying asset quality.
