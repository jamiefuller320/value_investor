H1 FY2027 STRATEGIC UPDATE UK Consumer As the UK rail industry prepares for the changes that GBR may bring, Trainline's focus has been on deepening the advantages that matter most to customers. In the first half, we made significant progress in helping customers find value, navigate disruption and travel with greater confidence, and in adding new products that offer value and flexibility. Railcard growth was a highlight in the half. We grew active digital railcard users in our App by 26% 6 to over three million, a rate that was more than three times faster than the rest of the railcard market 10 . This was driven by investment in surfacing prompts for customers to buy or renew a railcard at high-intent moments in the booking journey. Trainline now represents 36% of all railcard users in the UK 11 , up three percentage points year-on-year. Critically, railcard growth is not just about Trainline's share. We are helping to grow the railcard market itself. For example, Network Railcards, which offer savings across London and the South-East, are now being adopted by a broader audience, as we make it easier for customers to discover, purchase and use them. Railcards save customers an average of £175 per year, and by making that saving more accessible, we are actively shifting customer perception of rail as an affordable choice. We also continued to build traction with younger audiences, with Trainline digital railcards now representing 47% of all 16 to 30 year-old railcard users in the UK 12 . Holding a railcard in the Trainline app also creates a powerful loyalty dynamic for Trainline. Railcard holders transact four times more often than non-holders 13 , making them among our most valuable customers. As we grow this cohort, the compounding effect on frequency, lifetime value and platform engagement is substantial. It is also a competitive advantage that is difficult for competitors to replicate, as we are the leading retailer selling and integrating the digital railcard and the ticket in one app. What further differentiates Trainline is our suite of travel and disruption features, which support customers throughout their journey. The scale of our customer base and the depth of our data make these features very hard for competitors to replicate. In H1 we continued to add to these features including the launch of AI-powered Arrival Predictions, giving customers personalised real-time intelligence on whether their train is likely to arrive on time, as well as Auto Find My Train, which gives customers personalised live journey updates on their phone without searching for their train. We rolled out a new personalised home screen as well as a map-view interface, showing the live location of a customer's train to all users, whilst Voice AI now handles UK refund calls, giving customers faster resolution for simple queries. Customer engagement with our travel and disruption features grew in the half. Now 91% of eligible travellers see disruption features, double the level at the start of the half, and they return to engage with them month after month. More than half of eligible customers who see a prompt now claim Delay Repay, almost double the rate in January. This follows changes we made to help customers understand when they are entitled to compensation and to submit a claim. Alongside these product innovations, we have accelerated AI investment across the business to enable us to move faster. AI is assisting our people, with tools rolled out across the business and an agent platform live in our data business division, with AI completing certain workflows. We are seeing promising early wins including our lead and cycle time across the business is down 10% versus last year. In the half, we continued to add unique products that offer customers flexibility and value. Ancillary revenue grew 22% year-on-year, reflecting this progress. We launched Trainline Flex, giving customers the ability to combine the cheapest Advance Single ticket with cancel-for-any-reason insurance, increasing flexibility for customers on the best value tickets. We are also building a range of offers and partnerships that give customers value at the moment of booking, whilst enabling Trainline to develop larger, deeper commercial partnerships. This included an expanded partnership with PayPal, giving customers relevant, timely offers from a partner they already trust. Trainline's digital pay-as-you-go (DPAYG) trial with East Midlands Railway was extended for a further 12 months to September 2027, following strong performance and positive feedback from Rail Delivery Group. The extension includes broader customer types, enhanced fraud detection and a commercial arrangement that reflects the value of Trainline's technology. Trainline remains the most trusted brand in UK rail retailing, with our brand consideration significantly ahead of competitors. The combination of deep product investment, consistent brand building and a scaled customer base drives strong trust that competitors struggle to replicate. In H1 we continued to invest in building brand recognition across a range of audiences. We created partnerships to engage family audiences through Yoto Player, a podcast series for families travelling with children on trains, and younger audiences, through targeted campaigns across social media. Against a backdrop of a regulated fare freeze, the period saw headwinds from ongoing fare reform, including changes to refund policies, and the phased roll-out of single leg pricing, with the latter contributing to a lower average transaction value, partly offset by increased transaction volumes. Whilst we also continue to face competition, including from Train Operating Companies (TOC) self-preferencing, we delivered a resilient performance in the UK, reflecting the strength of our customer relationships and the continued relevance of our proposition. GBR The regulatory framework underpinning the future market and the establishment of Great British Railways (GBR) continues to develop. The Railways Bill is currently progressing through Parliament and is expected to be passed imminently. On 9 October, the Office of Rail and Road (ORR) published its draft Retail Code of Practice, alongside associated policy frameworks. The publication is an important step forward for UK rail retail. For the first time, there will be a regulatory framework governing how GBR interacts with all retail market participants. The draft confirms fair, transparent, non-discriminatory access to fares, products, systems and data, which is a positive foundation for a fair and open retail market. The proposed framework in relation to funding of GBR states GBR will need to act in a way which does not exclude competitors, distort the online retail market or abuse its position. However, Trainline believes that the proposed Code and frameworks require further clarity to deliver fully on this commitment. In particular, we are seeking to strengthen the mechanisms and oversight to ensure equivalence of treatment with GBR Online Retail, as is precedent in other sectors where state backed entities operate in the market. Trainline will continue to engage actively in the consultation process and maintains an assertive stance. In late 2025, the UK Government announced a preliminary market engagement exercise to seek supplier insight ahead of a planned procurement of a website and app to deliver GBR Online Retail. Trainline has engaged with this exercise, but the full procurement process, initially expected to begin in early 2026, has not yet started and we are still awaiting details. Update on CMA Investigation Trainline operates across multiple regulatory frameworks, each with its own requirements for the transparency and presentation of rail fares and fees. Rail tickets are inherently complex and Trainline’s booking flow has always sought to simplify these fares for customers and help them choose the right option for their needs. Booking fees are calculated on the total value of the basket and vary according to the choices customers make during the booking journey. They are also subject to a maximum cap. Following receipt of a letter from the CMA in November 2025, we have been proactively engaging with both the CMA and the ORR to work through the requirements around the presentation of fees and ensure we meet our obligations across each regulatory framework. Following the announcement by the CMA of an investigation into the presentation of fees in August 2026, we continue to engage proactively with the CMA and other relevant regulators, looking for a swift resolution that ensures customers have clear and transparent information and maintains a straightforward booking experience. International Consumer International Consumer reached a significant milestone in the period, with all our markets turning profitable for the first time 5 . This includes Spain which demonstrates that following the initial investment phase, an aggregation model can quickly reach scale and generate profit. The European opportunity is substantial, and we are well placed to capture it. While the half presented some headwinds, including extreme summer heat, and the impact on consumer sentiment from the tragic rail accidents in Spain, we remain confident in our long-term structural positioning. A highlight of the half was continued double-digit net ticket sales growth on the South-East France high-speed network. This comes more than a year following Trenitalia’s introduction of services between Paris and Marseille and further demonstrates the opportunity presented by aggregation. Underpinning this performance, and in line with the UK, is our French railcard strategy, which is central to our model for driving acquisition, activation, and long-term customer lifetime value. Railcard sales grew over 70% year-on-year and we launched new product features to promote railcard sales and improve engagement. Ancillary revenues also grew strongly in International in the half, driven by hotel and insurance products, showing the potential to build these revenue streams beyond the UK. Alongside this, Trainline remains the number one cited rail app in Google AI Overview across each of our core European markets, excluding France. As customers increasingly turn to generative search to plan and book travel, being the default recommendation at that moment of intent is a significant and growing advantage. France and Italy together are expected to represent around €10 billion of competitive high-speed rail revenue by 2030 14 , and the case for aggregation in both markets remains compelling. The timeline for Italian liberalisation has moved back by approximately one year, with SNCF now expected to launch domestic services connecting Turin to Naples and Turin to Venice from mid 2028. While this is later than we had anticipated, our preparation continues and our playbook is well developed. In France, Velvet is on track for a summer 2028 launch, following receipt of its railway operating licence in September 2026, with several new entrant carrier brands planning to launch thereafter. The liberalisation of the Channel Tunnel is another tangible opportunity. Virgin Trains is advancing with plans for its 2030 launch, having secured ORR approval for track access. Meanwhile, Trenitalia has recently signed an agreement for the supply of 19 high-speed trains ahead of its planned launch in 2029. Channel Tunnel liberalisation should give customers more choice, and we see this as a meaningful opportunity for Trainline. Our ability to bring trains and tickets from multiple carriers together in a single, trusted retail experience positions us well as new operators enter the market. Foreign travel remains a large opportunity, with a total addressable market estimated at around €5 billion 14 . Trainline's proposition is well matched to the customer, with foreign travellers needing all trains and tickets in one place, in a retail experience they can trust. In H1, net ticket sales were soft, driven by geopolitical uncertainty impacting inbound travel into Europe. In the half, we also continued to expand our supply coverage, with new routes in Ireland going live. Each new market extends the breadth of our network and strengthens the proposition for both domestic and foreign travel customers. The foundations are in place. As liberalisation accelerates, Trainline is well positioned to convert that structural advantage into sustained profitable growth. European Regulation The European Commission published its formal "EU Passenger Package" proposal in May 2026, aimed at making cross-border rail travel simpler, more transparent and more passenger-friendly. The proposals include measures to ensure incumbent operators provide access to timetables, fares and real-time data on a fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory (FRAND) basis, by improving third-party access to rail content, as well as making third-party inventory available through their own digital channels. FRAND conditions also include instruments to guarantee a viable commission for online platforms such as Trainline. The proposals are subject to negotiation in the European Parliament and Council, with implementation likely to be phased over several years. Trainline Solutions Business travel sales through our own branded channels and our B2B Distribution business account for the majority of Trainline Solutions and remain our strategic focus. Through B2B Distribution, we help large travel management companies (TMCs) retail train tickets to their business travel customers across multiple geographies and carriers through a single API connection. B2B Distribution is a highly scalable, low marginal cost route to growth, and we continue to see significant white space ahead. B2B Distribution net ticket sales grew 22% year-on-year in H1, with international continuing to grow at a faster rate, up 45%. International sales are on track to overtake the UK business by year end, reflecting the increasing diversification of our client base, platform leverage and increasing brand awareness across Europe. We are focused on large TMCs, which are sticky clients with high switching costs, average customer tenure of around seven years, and a strong tendency to consolidate global rail operations with a single partner. We secured four major TMC wins in H1 including the largest operators in the Spanish and German TMC market. Capital allocation framework and share buyback programme The Group’s capital allocation framework is as follows: Trainline’s primary use of capital is to invest behind its strategic priorities – including enhancing the customer experience and building demand for rail travel - to drive organic growth and deliver attractive and sustainable rates of return. The Group may supplement that with inorganic investment, should it help accelerate delivery of the Group’s strategic growth priorities. Trainline will also continue to manage debt leverage, including retaining a prudent and appropriate level of liquidity headroom should unforeseen circumstances arise. Any surplus capital thereafter may be returned to shareholders, including through the repurchase of Trainline’s shares. In line with Trainline’s capital allocation framework, on 11 September 2026, Trainline announced a new share buyback programme of up to £100 million. The new programme commenced on 15 September, following completion of the Group’s prior buyback programme, and is expected to run over the subsequent 12-month period. As at 31 October 2026, the Company had bought back and cancelled £6 million of shares under the new programme and £356 million in total (28% of issued share capital 9 ) since launching its first buyback programme in September 2023. General information (continued) Adjusted EBITDA The Group believes that adjusted EBITDA is a meaningful measure of the Group’s operating performance and debt servicing ability without regard to amortisation and depreciation methods as well as share-based payment charges which can differ significantly. Adjusted EBITDA is calculated as profit after tax before net financing income/(expense), tax, depreciation and amortisation, exceptional items and share-based payment charges. Exceptional items are excluded as management believes their nature could distort trends in the Group’s underlying earnings. This is because they are often one-off in nature or not related to underlying trade. Share-based payment charges are also excluded as they can fluctuate significantly period-on-period. Adjusted earnings Adjusted earnings are a measure used by the Group to monitor the underlying performance of the business, excluding certain non-cash and exceptional items. Adjusted earnings is calculated as profit after tax with share-based payments charged in administrative expenses, exceptional items and amortisation of acquired intangibles added back, together with the current and deferred tax impact of these adjustments also added back. Exceptional items are excluded as management believes their nature could distort trends in the Group’s underlying earnings. Share-based payment charges are also excluded as they can fluctuate significantly period-on-period and are a non-cash charge to the business. Amortisation of acquired intangibles is a non-cash accounting adjustment relating to previous acquisitions and is not linked to the ongoing trade of the Group. Net Debt Net debt is a measure used by the Group to measure the overall debt position after taking into account cash held by the Group. Net debt represents aggregate amount of loans and borrowings as disclosed in Note 9 (excluding lease liabilities and accrued interest on bank loans) and associated directly attributable transaction costs after taking into account cash held by the Group. Following an amendment to the revolving credit facility agreement, the covenant calculation was updated in H1 FY2027 to exclude lease liabilities from net debt as management believe this is a more accurate reflection of the Group’s borrowings. Lease liabilities were included in net debt in prior periods. Notes (continued) Adjusted free cash flow The Group uses adjusted free cash flow as a supplementary measure of liquidity. The Group defines adjusted free cash flow as cash generated from operating activities after adding back cash exceptional items and one-off cash items. Cash flows in relation to the purchase of property, plant and equipment and intangible assets, excluding those acquired through business combinations or trade and asset purchases, and cash flows in relation to taxes, interest, lease payments and treasury share purchases are also deducted. One-off cash items in the period relate to the purchase of property, plant and equipment for new office leases. CMA investigation On 18 August 2026, the Competition and Markets Authority (“CMA”) opened an investigation into the Group’s compliance with consumer protection law, specifically in relation to the presentation of mandatory booking fees. The CMA's announcement of 19 August stated that, at this stage, no finding has been made, and it should not be assumed that the Group has infringed consumer protection law. The Group considers that a present obligation may exist as a result of this investigation. Whilst there is considerable uncertainty over the final outcome of the investigation, the Group has at this stage made a judgement that it is more likely than not that an outflow may be required. Accordingly, the Group has recognised a provision of £3.7 million as at 31 August 2026, representing management’s current best estimate of the outflow, including associated legal fees, that may be required to close the matter. The CMA have indicated that the next update on the case will be in January 2027. The balance is classified as current on the basis the expected date of any potential outflow will be within the next 12 months. Recognition of this provision, and its quantum, are accounting judgements made solely to comply with the Group’s financial reporting obligations. They do not constitute, and should not be construed as, any admission, acceptance or acknowledgement by the Group of liability, wrongdoing or infringement of consumer protection law, nor any agreement with the CMA as to the existence, nature or extent of any infringement or as to the amount of any fine or redress that in theory might ultimately arise. The ultimate outcome of the investigation remains highly uncertain and is very difficult to predict with precision. The regime under which the investigation has been opened is new, having come into force in April 2025, and there is very limited precedent to guide an assessment of likely outcomes. Possible outcomes range from: closure of the investigation or a final decision that there was no infringement, either of which would involve no finding of infringement, fine or redress; through to acceptance of undertakings, which involves no finding of infringement or fine, and could be with or without redress; to a settlement or a final decision that there was an infringement, either of which could involve a fine or redress or both.
