## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Shell plc screens as a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (P/E ~9.8, P/B ~1.4, yield ~3.6%, ROE ~14%, debt/equity ~40%), but the screen’s adjusted overlay is Hold owing to divergent statutory, adjusted and free-cash-flow bases. Filings show a cyclical major with strong 2026 earnings momentum—H1 2026 income attributable to shareholders of $16.5bn and adjusted earnings of $16.8bn—against a softer FY 2025 base (adjusted earnings down 22% to $18.5bn; CFFO down 22% to $42.9bn). Valuation appeal rests on cash generation, progressive dividends and active buybacks, tempered by commodity cyclicality, Middle East/Qatar disruption, the pending ~$13.6bn ARC acquisition, and rising net debt through FY 2025. Key debate: whether current elevated earnings and distributions are sustainable through the cycle, or largely a geopolitical price spike masking structural transition and reserve-replacement pressure.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Shell offers a large-cap integrated energy franchise trading below typical market multiples, with a balance sheet that management stresses remains investment-grade and a capital-allocation framework targeting 40–50% of cash flow from operations (CFFO) to shareholders via dividends and buybacks.

The quantitative screen passes 12 of 22 models across five factor families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk), with full data quality (20/20 metrics). That aligns with filing evidence of durable cash economics: FY 2025 free cash flow of $26.1bn despite lower realised prices, and H1 2026 free cash flow of $20.5bn on stronger trading, refining and price realisation. Adjusted ROACE reached 12.4% in Q2 2026 versus 9.4% for FY 2025.

Business quality is supported by portfolio simplification (UK upstream Adura JV, Jiffy Lube divestment, Sprng Energy sale agreed), $5.8bn cumulative structural cost reductions since 2022 (per H1 2026 filing), and LNG/upstream scale. The screen’s earnings-yield and low-P/E-plus-yield passes are consistent with statutory EPS of $3.03 in FY 2025 and H1 2026 basic EPS of $2.94.

Caveats weaken pure “deep value” conviction: adjusted earnings fell in FY 2025; SEC proved reserves replacement was approximately −40% (73% excluding M&A); renewables and chemicals segments remain margin-challenged; and the screen flags FCF basis mismatch (filing $23.9bn vs screen TTM $21.5bn). The adjusted screen signal is Hold—not a veto, but a reminder to size for cyclicality rather than treat current cash flows as normalised.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and commodity:** Earnings, CFFO and distributions remain highly oil/gas/refining-margin dependent. FY 2025 showed the downside: adjusted earnings −22% and FCF −34% on softer prices. H1 2026 upside partly reflects conflict-driven price volatility and trading gains that may not persist.

**Geopolitical and operational:** Qatar LNG production shut since early March 2026 following Ras Laffan attack (RNS, 19 Mar 2026); Q2/H1 filings cite Middle East conflict impacts on Qatari volumes. Ormen Lange gas output cut ~40% after compressor failure, outage extended to 2027 (*Shell (LSE:SHEL) Cuts Ormen Lange Output 40%…*, 12 Aug 2026). These are material to Integrated Gas and European gas supply.

**M&A and capital allocation:** ARC Resources acquisition (~$13.6bn equity value, ~$16.4bn enterprise value; shareholder approval ~99.5%, close expected Q3 2026) increases production growth ambition but adds integration, regulatory and balance-sheet deployment risk; buybacks were paused mid-programme for the deal.

**Leverage and working capital:** FY 2025 net debt rose $6.9bn to $45.7bn despite $26.1bn FCF, reflecting distributions and lease additions. Q1 2026 net debt spiked to $52.6bn on $11.2bn working capital outflow from commodity price volatility—a reminder that “headline” FCF can diverge sharply from distributable cash in dislocated markets.

**Reserves and impairments:** Negative SEC reserves replacement in FY 2025; ongoing impairments in Marketing (Rotterdam biofuels), Renewables ($565m H1 2026) and Chemicals. UK Energy Profits Levy charge of $536m in FY 2025 Upstream identified items.

**Transition and regulatory:** Filings reference Pillar Two tax, emission certificate/biofuel programme cash flows, and climate-transition portfolio choices (renewable capacity under construction down; Atlantic Shores withdrawal cited in FY 2025). Form 20-F risk framework (referenced in RNS cautionary statements) highlights climate regulation, litigation and long-dated decommissioning obligations (~$27bn provisions).

**Associates:** Raizen restructuring may require Shell capital injection of BRL 3.5bn (~$685m) with ownership dilution (H1 2026 interim notes).

**Going concern:** H1 2026 interim accounts confirm going concern through 31 Dec 2027 after stress-testing commodity prices and principal risks; no qualification flagged.

**Governance:** Audit and Risk Committee chair transition—Ann Godbehere steps down 1 Aug 2026; Holly Keller Koeppel appointed (RNS *Directorate change*, 30 Jul 2026). Routine, not a red flag.
