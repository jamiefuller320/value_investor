# Bellway p.l.c. (BWY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:02:22.621223+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Bellway p.l.c. (BWY.L) screens as a buy on cheapness, dividend quality, GARP and financial health (P/B 0.7, P/E 15.9, yield 3.4%, D/E ~5%), yet trades at a cyclical trough in returns rather than distressed fundamentals. The investment case rests on a well-capitalised balance sheet turning to net cash (£157.7m at FY26 year-end per the August 2026 trading update), disciplined capital return (£150m buyback plus a further £50m tranche), and volume recovery from the FY24 trough (7,654 completions) to 9,695 in FY26. The central debate is whether sub-1.0x book and a 2.5x underlying dividend cover adequately compensate for structurally lower margins (~10% vs mid-teens ambition), a £506.5m legacy building-safety provision (interim filing), and renewed mortgage-rate sensitivity. Filings confirm operational resilience but not a re-rating catalyst; the screen’s FCF basis mismatch (filing −£66.9m vs TTM +£104.9m on Yahoo) warrants caution on cash-quality overlays.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Bellway offers a classic cyclical deep-value setup: the shares trade below tangible book (screen P/B 0.7) after an earnings reset, while five quantitative models pass—Schloss Low P/B, Neff PEGY (PEGY 0.05), Dividend Growth, Dreman Contrarian and Financial Health—with four of four metric families satisfied and high data quality (18/20). Business quality is better than the depressed ROE (4.5% on screen) implies: underlying ROCE was 8.9% in H1 FY26 (interim results), the land bank stands at ~94,000 plots, and Bellway retains five-star HBF customer satisfaction. Management is prioritising capital efficiency—adjusted operating cashflow exceeded £850m in FY26 (August trading update) versus £638.9m in FY25—funding dividends, buybacks and legacy remediation without stressing leverage (adjusted gearing under 5% at FY26 year-end). Volume is recovering (+10.8% completions YoY to 9,695) and ASP is stable-to-up (£324k, mix-driven, not price inflation). The screen’s adjusted “hold” on negative filing FCF reflects working-capital timing in a land-heavy model, not balance-sheet fragility; net cash at year-end supports the dividend-and-buyback pillar of the thesis. Against peers, Bellway presents as a quality operator at a distressed-sector multiple rather than a distressed operator.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and demand.** Trading updates (June and August 2026) flag moderation in reservations after mortgage-rate rises from April 2026; incentives ~5% of ASP. Forward order book value fell 21% YoY at FY26 year-end. CEO cites geopolitical uncertainty (Middle East) and domestic political unpredictability. The interim going-concern note stress-tests a severe scenario (private completions −50% vs FY22 peak, ASP −10%) and confirms covenant headroom on £530m committed facilities—but this underscores inherent cyclicality.

**Legacy building safety and regulatory.** Interim filing: £506.5m remaining provision; £10.7m H1 adjusting charge; SRT/Pact/Scottish Accord obligations; contingent liability language warns estimates may increase as investigations continue. Residential Property Developer Tax (4%) adds to the effective rate (~29%). CMA market-investigation exceptional costs (£0.3m H1; £15.4m in FY25 per interim notes). RPDT and remediation cash calls compete with shareholder returns.

**Margin and returns.** Underlying operating margin stuck near 10% (FY26 guidance ~10%, vs mid-teens long-term target in interim). Underlying ROCE 8.9% (H1 FY26)—well below FY23 peaks. Bulk sales mix supports volume but dilutes margin.

**Governance / strategy.** Unsolicited all-share approach for Crest Nicholson (June 2024 RNS in index, rejected by target board) indicates M&A appetite but also integration/execution risk if revived. Ashberry brand phase-out over three to four years adds transition risk.

**Pension.** Retirement benefit asset £2.1m at 31 January 2026 (interim)—immaterial.

**Liquidity.** Not a near-term concern: net cash at FY26 year-end, £400m revolving facility to 2030, USPP £130m. Land creditors £289.6m at HY add to adjusted gearing (10.3%).
