John Tutte Chair 12 October 2026 Chief Executive's Market and Operational Review Market Customer demand during the autumn of 2025 was affected by uncertainty ahead of the Government's Budget, consistent with the pattern experienced in the prior financial year. Trading improved during the early part of the spring selling season but moderated from April as increased economic uncertainty and the conflict in the Middle East drove further inflationary pressures and higher mortgage rates, which weighed on consumer confidence and affordability. To support affordability and maintain sales momentum in a more challenging market, the use of targeted incentives increased to average around 5% (2025: 4.1%), resulting in some moderation in underlying selling prices. These pressures are more pronounced in the South of England, where higher house prices and mortgage requirements increase customer sensitivity to borrowing costs. The private reservation rate decreased by 5.8% to an average of 131 per week (2025: 139). In line with our strategy, bulk sales made a good contribution during the year, with the private reservation rate per outlet per week at 0.55 (2025: 0.57). The private reservation rate excluding bulk sales was 0.49 (2025: 0.52). The overall reservation rate, including social homes, was 3.5% lower at 165 per week (2025: 171) and the cancellation rate remained low at 12% (2025: 13%). The availability of competitively priced mortgage products provided some support to customer demand during the year, although interest rate volatility affected buyer confidence and affordability remained constrained, particularly for customers requiring higher loan-to-value mortgages. Against this backdrop, we welcome the Government's proposed Your First Home scheme, which has the potential to provide substantial support for first-time buyers and help address the deposit and affordability constraints faced by many prospective homeowners. We look forward to further detail on the scope, design and timing of its introduction. Prod uction and cost control Build cost inflation was at low single-digit levels during the first half of the financial year but increased in the second half, principally reflecting greater pressure on material and energy costs amid heightened geopolitical and supply chain uncertainty. The Group has received price increase requests from suppliers and subcontractors but has continued to negotiate robustly, limiting the impact on agreed rates. Product availability across the Group is currently good, and our procurement arrangements retain flexibility to benefit from any reduction in input costs. Looking ahead, ongoing volatility in global energy prices and the risk of supply chain disruption could lead to further cost increases. While the extent of any impact remains uncertain, we currently anticipate overall build cost inflation of around 3% to 4% in FY27. We are strengthening cost control and operational efficiency through greater Group-wide standardisation. During the year, we finalised the Bellway Collection, our new portfolio of standard house types, which accommodates both traditional and timber-frame construction methods and provides the flexibility to meet the requirements of the Future Homes Standard. Supported by centralised design, procurement and site layout reviews, the Bellway Collection will improve consistency, simplify delivery and help mitigate build cost pressures. As part of our long-term growth strategy, we are increasing the use of sustainably sourced timber frame construction. The Group is targeting an increase in timber frame use to around 30% of housing output by 2030 (2026: 20%). This will deliver operational, financial and environmental benefits through shorter build times, improved work-in-progress ('WIP') efficiency and lower supply chain carbon emissions. Bellway Home Space, our proprietary timber frame manufacturing facility, opened in January 2026 and began supplying frames to our divisions for housing completions from the second half of FY26. The planned increase in timber frame output will be delivered primarily through this facility. We are confident that this investment will support sustainable volume growth, improve capital efficiency and contribute to a higher underlying return on equity ('RoE'). Refresh of the Bellway brand Our consistent focus on providing high-quality homes and service for our customers has resulted in Bellway retaining its position as a five-star 4 homebuilder for the tenth consecutive year. Positive customer recognition is key to our ongoing success and, as reflected by the investment in our new range of house types, we are improving the design of our homes to meet the changing needs and expectations of our customers. To support our strategic priorities of driving growth and efficiencies across the business, we have also refreshed the Bellway brand. The redesigned brand has been successfully launched with a new digital-first approach to simplify the customer journey, attract a broader range of buyers to our outlets and enhance revenue opportunities, including customer options and upgrades. As part of the brand refresh process, and following a detailed review, we decided to phase out the Ashberry brand. Following the introduction of our refreshed Bellway brand, new Bellway Collection house types and enhanced digital customer experience, maintaining an additional brand representing a relatively small proportion of Group output is no longer the most efficient use of resources. We do not anticipate any impact on our plans for outlet growth over the medium term, with the transition to be phased over the next three to four years as existing and new outlets gradually become part of the main Bellway brand. Customer demand remains constrained by affordability pressures, with mortgage interest rates above prior-year levels, while wider economic uncertainty continues to affect homebuyer decision-making. While the Government's proposed Your First Home scheme has the potential to provide meaningful support for first-time buyer demand, the period ahead of its launch may influence the timing of purchasing decisions among some prospective customers. In the nine weeks since 1 August, the private reservation rate per outlet per week, including bulk sales, was 0.50 (1 August to 5 October 2025: 0.51). The private reservation rate excluding bulk sales was 0.45 (2025: 0.48). Given the lower opening order book and continued affordability pressures, volume output is expected to reduce in FY27, and the Group is targeting total completions of between 9,000 and 9,400 homes (2026: 9,695). This will include an increased contribution from bulk sales (2026: 615 homes) to support volume delivery and cash generation, while maintaining financial discipline. Build cost inflation, elevated sales incentives and a higher proportion of bulk sale completions are expected to result in a further reduction in the underlying operating margin in FY27. Management actions are underway to further strengthen cost control and operational efficiency, although these will not fully offset the near-term margin pressures. Notwithstanding current industry headwinds, Bellway's strong balance sheet, high-quality land bank and operational capabilities provide the resilience to navigate near-term uncertainty. Our focus on disciplined cost control and operational efficiency will help mitigate current pressures, while selective land investment and the accelerated trade-out of lower-margin sites provide a strong platform for improving margins and returns beyond FY27. With a nationwide divisional presence and significant operational capacity, the Group is well-positioned to increase volume output and deliver higher profits over the medium term as market conditions improve. SRT and associated review Bellway continues to act responsibly with regards to building and resident safety, and this is reflected by the significant resource and funding the Group has committed to remediate its legacy apartments. In March 2023 the Group signed the SRT with MHCLG. Under the terms of the SRT, developers have agreed to identify and remediate, life-critical fire safety defects in residential buildings over 11 metres in height that they have developed or refurbished since April 1992. The Group contractually committed to remediate its legacy buildings in both Wales and Scotland by signing the Pact with The Welsh Ministers (the 'Pact') in May 2023 and the Scottish Safer Buildings Accord in July 2023. Signing the SRT has led to improved clarity on the standards required for internal and external remediation, including Publicly Available Specification ('PAS') 9980:2022, which is the code of practice for Fire Risk Appraisals of External Wall construction ('FRAEW'). Buildings are deemed to be assessed under the requirements of the SRT when a qualifying assessment has been approved by the MHCLG. This requires the completion of both a FRAEW and a Fire Safety Assessment ('FSA'). Due to the change in legislation establishing a retrospective legal obligation for structural defects in dwellings, this is seen as a highly unusual event and is the primary reason the structural defects provision should be treated as an adjusting item. As previously reported, the Group has identified a structural defect relating to the reinforced concrete frame at an historical high-rise apartment scheme in Greenwich, London and a mid-rise apartment scheme in Slough. The increase in the limitation period means Bellway has a legal obligation for undertaking the remedial works at both the Greenwich and Slough sites. During the year, an independent design was received for the Slough scheme. The design included modelling which indicated that the structural defect is limited to a single area of the building. As a result, the remediation strategy is now expected to be significantly less invasive than previously anticipated, leading to a reduction in the estimated cost. The revised cost estimate is based on a third-party tender and reflects management's expected scope of works. Contingent liabilities On 30 October 2025 the Competition and Markets Authority ('CMA') made a decision to close its investigation into information sharing practices in the sector following binding commitments from certain housebuilders and a requirement for the housebuilders to work with industry bodies to develop and publish industry-wide UK competition law guidance for the wider sector. The CMA did not find that the Competition Act 1998 had been infringed, and the acceptance of commitments did not involve any admission of wrongdoing by the housebuilders. An application to commence a proposed collective class action was filed in the Competition Appeal Tribunal on 24 June 2026 against seven major housebuilders, including Bellway. The application alleges that exchanges of competitively sensitive information breached competition law, affected competition and resulted in higher prices for certain purchasers of new-build homes. The application states it is informed by the CMA decision referred to above. The proposed collective action remains at a very early stage and, as a result, it is not yet possible to assess the likely outcome.
