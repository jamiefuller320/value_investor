# 4imprint Group plc (FOUR.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T07:33:28.446883+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
4imprint Group plc (FOUR.L) is a US-focused direct marketer of promotional products, listed in London but reporting in US dollars. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy (11/22 models, composite 55%, sector-relative 63%, all five factor families passed). Filings show a resilient, cash-generative franchise: FY2025 revenue fell 2% to $1.35bn but operating margin held at 10.8%, with $132.8m cash and no drawn debt. The valuation hook is quality at a modest multiple — screen metrics include P/E 16.3, 4.1% yield, ROE 70.1% and FCF yield 6.1% — against a business that has historically gained share through cycles. The central debate is whether tariff-related margin pressure and weak new-customer acquisition (-12% in 2025) are cyclical trough effects or a structural slowdown in a maturing US promotional-products market; H1 2026 adjusted PBT fell 12% as tariffs bit, though management guides full-year adjusted PBT of ~$130m.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, 4imprint offers a rare combination: high reported returns on capital, strong free cash conversion, and a balance sheet that is typically ungeared with net negative working capital — characteristics that align with the screen’s passes on FCF Yield, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Dividend Growth, Piotroski F-Score and Financial Health.

The business model is structurally attractive. Roughly 98% of revenue is North America; suppliers drop-ship imprinted goods, keeping inventory low and cash conversion high (109% of operating profit in FY2025). Existing-customer retention is strong — flat order counts in 2025 despite a soft market — and the brand-led marketing mix (revenue per marketing dollar ~$7.86 in 2025) has improved materially versus pre-pandemic norms. Management maintained the regular dividend at 240.0c per share in the downturn, consistent with policy to at least hold the payout in weaker periods, and returned excess cash via a 250.0c special dividend in June 2025.

The screen’s buy signal is supported by business quality rather than aggressive growth: revenue growth is modest (-2% FY2025; +1% H1 2026), but profitability and cash generation remain robust. Cheapness and dividend metrics look credible against filing-based FCF of $128.0m (FY2025) versus the screen’s lower TTM figure ($88.4m), which reflects a known FCF basis mismatch flagged in the screening snapshot. At ~16x earnings with a 4%+ yield and net cash, the market appears to price cyclical uncertainty rather than balance-sheet or franchise risk — a profile consistent with accumulate rather than pass.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / macro:** Filings flag macroeconomic conditions as an increased risk. US tariff policy is the dominant near-term issue: ~60% of revenue relates to China-sourced products (H1 2025 disclosure). Tariff costs phased in during H1 2026 and compressed gross margin by ~130bp. Promotional products demand tracks business sentiment; ASI industry data cited in H1 2025 showed market revenue declining in Q1–Q2 2025. New-customer acquisition remains weak (-12% FY2025; improving but still negative H1 2026).

**Competitive:** Intense fragmentation with risk from AI-enabled search disruption, buying groups, and private-equity-backed entrants. Brand and marketing flexibility are mitigants, but search-engine algorithm changes and agentic AI are explicitly flagged as evolving threats.

**Operational / supply chain:** Drop-ship model concentrates reliance on Tier 1 suppliers and parcel carriers; facility concentration in Oshkosh/Appleton, Wisconsin. Cyber threat risk trend is increasing (H1 2026 interim update).

**Governance / key person:** Orderly succession — Paul Forman became Chair (16 March 2026) replacing Paul Moody; Michelle Brukwicki became CFO (1 May 2025) replacing David Seekings. No red flags in filings, but dual leadership transitions warrant monitoring.

**Accounting / tax:** H1 2026 uncertain tax treatments created $5.1m adjusting items; carrying amounts may change materially. Revenue recognition as principal (gross revenue) is a critical judgment — appropriate for the drop-ship model but worth noting.

**Pension:** Legacy UK DB scheme winding up; low residual risk once complete.

**Liquidity / leverage:** Low risk near-term. Cash $132.8m (FY2025), no drawn debt, undrawn facilities, going concern confirmed. Dividend policy ($142.8m paid in FY2025 including special) reduces retained equity and contributes to optically high ROE (70.1% screen); this is capital structure, not necessarily operating leverage.

**Screen-specific:** Negative revenue growth (-3.1% per screen) and FCF definition divergence (filing $127m vs screen TTM $88.4m) warrant caution on mechanical yield metrics.
