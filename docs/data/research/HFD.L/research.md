# Halfords Group plc (HFD.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T06:37:14.212589+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Halfords is a UK motoring and cycling retailer transitioning toward a higher-margin, services-led model (Autocentres, Fusion garages, Halfords Motoring Club), with FY26 showing the strongest operational momentum in several years. The quantitative screen flags a **buy** on cheapness (P/B ~1.0), cash generation, dividend growth, and earnings quality, though timing suggests waiting for a pullback (RSI ~61, above 200-day MA). Filings show underlying profit recovery from £38.4m (FY25) to £45.4m (FY26, 52-week basis) and gross margin at a decade high of 52.8%, but reported earnings remain distorted by FY25’s £49.1m goodwill impairment and ongoing non-underlying charges. The central debate is whether operational self-help and services mix can sustain mid-single-digit underlying growth through a soft consumer backdrop, or whether lease-heavy balance sheet, cyclical exposure, and goodwill/intangibles cap re-rating.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Halfords offers a tangible-asset-heavy UK franchise trading near book with a covered dividend (FY26 total 9.0p; ~3.7% yield per screen) and improving underlying returns (ROCE 14.2% vs cost of capital 10.6%, per FY26 preliminary results). The screen passes four families—**cheapness, quality, dividend, risk**—via Schloss Low P/B, FCF Yield, Dividend Growth, Piotroski F-Score, and Earnings Quality, with composite score 65% versus 55% sector-relative.

Business quality is better than headline ROE (6.4% per screen) implies: over half of revenue is now service-related, LfL sales accelerated to +4.8% in FY26, and Autocentres ex-Avayler operating profit rose more than 20%. Management’s “Optimise” phase (category management, garage utilisation, Better Buying) is delivering measurable margin expansion (+210bps group gross margin YoY). Net cash excluding IFRS 16 leases (£11.2m at FY26 year-end on a 52-week basis) and a £180m RCF extended to April 2029 support balance-sheet resilience—consistent with the screen’s risk-family pass, albeit lease-adjusted leverage remains material.

The screen’s “wait for pullback” timing overlay is relevant: shares re-rated after FY26 results (Proactive Investors, 25 Jun 2026) and FTSE 250 reinclusion (effective 4 Aug 2026), so entry discipline matters despite the fundamental buy signal.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / macro:** UK consumer discretionary exposure; management flags sensitivity to Middle East conflict effects on sentiment/spending from H2 2026 (FY26 preliminary results). Cycling recovery aided by weather; durability untested in downturn.

**Competitive:** Fragmented tyres market, online price competition in commoditised motoring products; services moat (advice, fitting, garages) is real but not impregnable.

**Accounting / goodwill:** FY25 **£49.1m goodwill impairment** highlights sensitivity of £355.7m goodwill to discount rates. Tangible equity is thin relative to market cap at ~1.0x P/B. Non-underlying items persist (WMS implementation, closure costs, acquired-intangible amortisation).

**Leverage / liquidity:** IFRS 16 lease liabilities **~£248m**; total net debt including leases **£236.7m** at Mar 2026 (52-wk basis). Net cash ex-leases is positive but modest. **£180m committed RCF** (extended to Apr 2029); interim going-concern review cites covenant compliance under stress scenarios—positive, but lease and working-capital demands (negative working capital, inventory build) warrant monitoring.

**Operational:** Avayler SaaS remains loss-making (**£3.4m** operating loss, revenue £2.1m in FY26). Warehouse system issues cost **£3.1m** in HY26 (resolved, but third DC migration ongoing).

**Governance:** Chair transition: **Keith Williams** steps down at Sep 2026 AGM; **Jock Lennox** appointed Chair from 10 Sep 2026 (directorate change RNS, 25 Jun 2026). CEO **Henry Birch** (appointed Nov 2025) executing “Fit for the Future” strategy—execution risk on relatively new leadership team.

**Regulatory:** E-bike safety scrutiny and industry accreditation (Carrera range expansion)—reputational opportunity but also compliance exposure.
