Strategic update We are making good progress with our strategy to create a focused, lean and cash generative business. With the completion of the sale of Catalyst Technologies and the acquisition of Cormetech, we have fundamentally reshaped JM’s portfolio around our leading capabilities in PGM chemistry and emissions control, and made significant returns to shareholders. We have strong core businesses – Clean Air and PGM Services – with leading positions in durable markets, alongside growth opportunities that leverage our core technology. We remain committed to delivering greater cash generation and materially enhanced shareholder returns. Clean Air – a leading global autocatalyst player delivering material margin improvement In Clean Air, we aim to be a long-term partner providing world-leading technology to help our customers reduce harmful emissions. Our strategic priority is to maintain our leading position in diesel, secure a profitable share in gasoline including hybrids, whilst continuing to drive efficiencies. In the half, we delivered an underlying operating margin improvement of 180 basis points, to 14.2% and continue to drive efficiency across our operations. For 2027/28, Clean Air (including Clean Air Solutions) is expected to deliver sales of more than £2 billion (of which over 95% is already won) and an improved operating margin of 16 to 18%. Clean Air Solutions and Cormetech (“Clean Power”): creating a global leader in high growth, stationary emissions control Johnson Matthey completed the acquisition of Cormetech in July 2026 for an enterprise value of $360 million, representing a 10.3x acquisition multiple based on Cormetech’s expected 2026 EBITDA as at May 2026¹. The combination of Johnson Matthey’s Clean Air Solutions business with Cormetech creates a large scale leader in the high growth stationary emissions control market, with a strong presence in the US power generation market, a diverse client base and a highly complementary portfolio of products. On an annualised basis, the combined business is now expected to achieve sales in excess of £250 million (previously in excess of £200 million) and an operating margin of at least mid-teens in 2026/27. With the combined strong management team in place, the focus is successful integration, leveraging our strong market position – particularly in the structurally growing US data centre market – and delivering synergies. In the two months since completion, we have already secured our first joint business win with a leading manufacturer of heat recovery steam generators. Together, Cormetech’s SCR² catalysts and JM’s oxidation catalysts will be supplied into the emissions control system for gas turbines used in data centres. This demonstrates the potential for our combined offering and strong commercial proposition, reflected in a c.$350 million orderbook³ which underpins sales to 31 st March 2028. We continue to expect annualised run-rate EBITDA synergies of at least $20 million by 2030. PGM Services – a world leader in PGMs PGM Services is a world leader in PGMs. Our leading capabilities in R&D, product manufacture, refining and metal management provide a fully circular business model that underpins the group. We are transforming our refining operations and investing in a new, world-class PGM refinery in the UK. This will drive efficiencies, improve operational resilience and support improved working capital management. The project continues to progress through its fit-out and commissioning phase, with the refinery expected to come online in early 2028 in line with the optimal ramp up schedule. The current refinery will run in parallel to enable continuity of operations and a smooth transition. Our guidance for 2027/28 is unchanged. We expect PGM Services to generate sales in excess of £450 million, with an operating margin of around 30%¹. Beyond this, underpinned by our new refinery and growth in PGM Products, we expect PGM Services to deliver at least low single digit CAGR in underlying operating profit over the medium to long-term. Whilst the green hydrogen market has slowed, we are maintaining long-term growth optionality. Environment, health and safety (EHS) – A major work-related EHS event, could result from operational or project-related activities, process safety failures, asset integrity issues or regulatory non-compliance, threatening JM’s people, operations, product portfolios and reputation. Failure to manage metal commitments – JM’s use and movement of high-value precious metals exposes the Group to risks relating to metal availability, trading income volatility, theft, security and control weaknesses. These risks may be heightened by volatile metal prices and varying operational models across the business. 5. Geopolitical, economic and market volatility – JM’s global footprint exposes the Group to geopolitical, macroeconomic and market volatility, including conflicts, trade restrictions, sanctions and economic instability in key markets. Innovation to drive business growth – A failure to develop, scale and commercialise competitive, cost efficient products and technical services, that align with evolving customer needs and market demands. Operational asset failure – Failure of one or more critical operational assets could disrupt JM’s supply chains, operational performance and reputation. Supply chain resilience – JM relies on a global network of suppliers and intermediaries for critical materials and services, some of which are highly specialised with limited alternative sources. Talent, culture and engagement – The success of JM’s strategy depends on the ability to attract, retain and engage a skilled and motivated workforce, supported by effective leadership and a strong, aligned culture. Organisational change, reduced capacity and evolving ways of working may increase pressure on employees and leaders, creating a risk that change fatigue, lower engagement or loss of key talent could constrain successful strategy execution. 10. Transformation delivery – JM's ability to achieve its strategic objectives may be impacted by delays, execution challenges, resource constraints or ineffective change management affecting the delivery of the transformation programmes, resulting in slower realisation of business benefits, reduced competitiveness and organisational inefficiencies.
