# Pollen Street Group Limited (POLN.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T00:56:43.385634+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Pollen Street Group is a FTSE 250 alternative asset manager combining a growing Private Equity and Private Credit franchise (Asset Manager) with a balance-sheet Investment Company that co-invests alongside limited partners. The quantitative screen flags a strong buy on cheapness (P/E 8.9, P/B 0.85), dividend yield (6.9%), GARP and risk metrics, with 12 of 22 models passing and a 74% composite score. Primary filings show accelerating fundamentals: FY2025 profit after tax rose 14% to £56.6m, fee-paying AUM grew 32% to £5.2bn, and Q1 2026 total AUM reached £8.4bn after Private Credit Fund IV closed at £2.5bn. The valuation hook is a listed alt-manager trading below book with a progressive dividend (58.0p FY2025, +8%) and an active buyback programme, whilst management argues the share price “significantly undervalues” the platform. The key debate is whether recurring management-fee growth and resilient ~10% investment-company returns outweigh sector-wide private-credit anxiety, carried-interest dilution to employees, and the cyclical/market sensitivity of listed fund managers.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy rating aligns with a business that is delivering genuine operating momentum rather than a distressed deep-value trap. Pollen Street passes four value families—cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk—with debt/equity at 34%, ROE 9.6%, and FCF of £46.7m (Yahoo, consistent with filing cash-flow commentary). Fundamentally, the model is improving: Asset Manager revenue is increasingly recurring (management fees up 26% to £69.9m in FY2025 per the Full Year 2025 Trading Update), fee-paying AUM compounding (32% YoY to £5.2bn), and the Investment Company generating stable income on net investment assets (£32.9m; underlying return 10.6%). FY2025 EPS of 93.7p grew 19%, aided by buybacks, whilst the 58.0p dividend represents a well-covered cash return policy. For a value investor, POLN offers a rare combination of sub-9x earnings, sub-1x book, and a 6.9% yield on a platform targeting £10bn total AUM (currently £8.4bn post-Q1 2026). The screen’s “wait for pullback” timing note (RSI ~46, price below 200-day MA) is consistent with recent listed-alt-manager weakness rather than deteriorating company fundamentals. Business quality is supported by closed-end fund structures (no LP redemption risk), granular credit portfolios, and successful FY2025 exits including the Shawbrook IPO.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Sector and cyclical:** The FY2025 report explicitly flags investor nervousness around private credit—BDC discounts, defaults, fraud incidents, and competitive pressure as banks re-enter lending markets. Listed alternative asset managers faced a “more challenging backdrop” in 2026 from AI-related growth-stock sell-offs and private-credit caution, compounded by geopolitical risk (Iran conflict cited). These are cyclical/sentiment risks not captured by static value screens.

**Credit and investment:** Principal risks include investment underperformance, credit impairment on balance-sheet and fund assets, interest-rate and currency exposure, and idiosyncratic loan-portfolio events. The filing stresses robust underwriting but acknowledges higher-for-longer rates and reduced exit liquidity could affect valuations and fundraising.

**Regulatory and conduct:** Conduct and regulatory risk is flagged as material—market abuse, supervisory fines, and reputational damage could impair fundraising. Evolving RI/climate disclosure requirements add compliance cost.

**Governance / key person:** The Board reduced the Group’s carried-interest share in Private Credit Fund IV from 25% to 17% to reward team members—a strategic retention decision with no near-term P&L impact per management, but it dilutes long-term shareholder participation in performance fees. Board turnover is elevated: new Chair (Lynn Fordham), new CFO (Crispin Goldsmith, January 2025), new NED/Audit Chair (Robert Ohrenstein), and Jim Coyle retiring after nine years. Lynn Fordham’s concurrent NED role at Czechoslovak Group (January 2026) warrants monitoring for time/commitment conflicts.

**Leverage and liquidity:** Net debt-to-gross investment assets at 35% is stable but meaningful; viability testing identified potential liquidity pressures in extreme scenarios, albeit with mitigants. Investment gains/losses on securities (£43.8m loss in FY2025 cash flow per Yahoo) reflect fair-value volatility in the portfolio.

**Competitive / structural:** AI disruption poses emerging risk to software-exposed Private Equity holdings (partially mitigated by “systems of record” focus). Fundraising fraud risk and fee-compression pressure in competitive private markets are explicitly noted.

**Going concern / covenants (filing language):** Directors confirm going concern is appropriate; debt covenants not anticipated to breach even under stressed scenarios including no new fundraising and material impairments.
