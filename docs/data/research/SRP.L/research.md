# Serco Group plc (SRP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T06:38:37.548248+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Serco Group plc is a UK-listed government services contractor (~50,000 staff) pivoting toward defence, justice & immigration, and citizen services, with roughly two-thirds of recent order intake in defence. The quantitative screen rates the name a **buy** on cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk families (composite 61%; FCF yield, Neff PEGY, dividend growth and earnings quality models passed), though an earnings-basis overlay adjusts the signal to **hold** where statutory and underlying EPS trends diverge. Filings show a business generating strong cash, low covenant leverage (~0.7–0.75× adjusted net debt/EBITDA), a record £12.8bn pipeline and reiterated 2026 guidance for ~£5.0bn revenue and ~£300m underlying operating profit. The central debate is whether defence-led growth and margin progression (~6%) justify a mid-teens P/E, or whether immigration-policy exposure, US procurement timing and the gap between statutory and adjusted earnings cap upside — especially with RSI near 72 suggesting neutral timing.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Serco offers a rare combination in UK outsourcing: visible contracted revenue (£14.6bn order book), disciplined capital return (£540m of buybacks since 2021; dividend +8–10% recent steps), and screen-friendly cash economics. The screen’s **FCF Yield** pass aligns with filing-reported free cash flow of **£219m** in FY2025 (112% trading cash conversion) and FY2026 guidance of ~**£160m**, against adjusted net debt of **£206m** at year-end and leverage well below the 1–2× target band. **Neff PEGY** (PEGY 0.09; growth 10.5% per screen) and **Dividend Growth** (full-year dividend 4.50p in 2025, +8%; interim 1.60p in H1 2026, +10%) reflect an earnings and cash trajectory that management expects to accelerate: underlying operating profit guided from **£272m** (2025) to **~£300m** (2026), with H1 2026 already at **£157m** on a **6.2%** margin — a 10-year high per company commentary.

Business quality has improved materially since the 2024 Asia Pacific goodwill impairment (**£114.5m**; Australian immigration rebid loss). Contract retention exceeds **90%** (95% in H1 2026), book-to-bill was **114%** in 2025 and **100%** in H1 2026, and the MT&S acquisition (**£242m** EV, May 2025) deepens US defence credentials. The screen’s **Earnings Quality** pass is partially offset by its own overlay flagging statutory/adjusted divergence — reported diluted EPS was **14.07p** in 2025 versus underlying **16.93p**, and statutory net income collapsed to **£44m** in 2024 before recovering to **£146m** in 2025 (Yahoo fallback for statutory series only where filing bodies emphasise underlying metrics). Net: the quantitative case is cash-and-growth driven; filing evidence supports operational improvement, but value investors should anchor on underlying measures and treat statutory volatility as a diligence item, not a disqualifier.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and political:** Revenue is overwhelmingly government-linked. UK immigration policy directly affects the Group’s largest contract and temporary-accommodation demand; management guides further immigration revenue reductions in 2026. Outsourcing of justice and asylum services remains politically sensitive (HMP Dovegate rebid won, but sector scrutiny persists). US Government shutdowns and procurement delays slowed North American awards in 2025–H1 2026 (**£3.2bn** awaiting adjudication per H1 RNS).

**Contract and rebid:** ~**£1.8bn** annual revenue from contracts requiring extension/rebid before end-2028 (~40% of revenue). Win rates are strong (82–83% on rebids/extensions over two years), but concentration in UK MoD, Home Office and US federal programmes creates customer-concentration risk.

**Accounting and earnings quality:** The screen’s earnings-basis overlay (statutory vs underlying divergence) is substantiated by filings: 2024’s **£114.5m** goodwill impairment and 2025’s **£17.3m** Australian deferred tax derecognition drove reported/adjusted gaps. **£25.4m** provision for EHC acquisition contingent liability (H1 2026 note) and **£192.7m** total provisions (employee, property dilapidations, onerous contracts, claims) warrant monitoring. Tangible book value is negative (goodwill/intangibles **~£1.09bn** at Dec 2025 per filing balance sheet; Yahoo reports tangible book **-£218m** — consistent directionally).

**Operational/geographic:** Asia Pacific margins compressed to **2.2%** in H1 2026 following immigration exit; Middle East revenue down **25%** with regional instability noted. North America CEO transition (Michael LaRouche departing) adds key-person risk during a procurement recovery phase.

**Liquidity and covenants:** Going-concern assessments (FY2025 and H1 2026) conclude adequate headroom to March/August 2027; reverse stress tests require implausible **60%** bid failure plus **175–200bp** margin erosion to breach covenants (limit **3.5×** leverage). Performance bonds and guarantees **£211.3m** (Jun 2026 contingent liabilities note). Not a near-term liquidity concern, but lease growth tied to asylum housing bears watching if policy shifts.

**Governance:** Orderly CFO succession (Nigel Crossley retired March 2026; Mark Reid appointed) — no red flag, but new finance leadership during a defence-acquisition integration phase.
