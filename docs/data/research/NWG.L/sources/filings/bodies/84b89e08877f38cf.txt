Capital and risk management Capital, liquidity and funding risk Introduction NatWest Group takes a comprehensive approach to the management of capital, liquidity and funding, underpinned by frameworks, risk appetite and policies, to manage and mitigate capital, liquidity and funding risks. The framework ensures the tools and capability are in place to facilitate the management and mitigation of risk ensuring that NatWest Group operates within its regulatory requirements and risk appetite. Key developments since 31 December 2025 CET1 ratio 13.5% (2025 - 14.0%) The CET1 ratio decreased by 50 basis points to 13.5% due to a £7.2 billion increase in RWAs partially offset by an immaterial increase in CET1 capital. The CET1 capital increase was mainly driven by an attributable profit to ordinary shareholders of £3.6 billion (net of ordinary interim dividend paid) offset by an increase in regulatory deductions following the acquisition of Evelyn Partners of £2.7 billion and a foreseeable ordinary dividend accrual of £1.3 billion. RWAs £200.5bn (2025 - £193.3bn) Total RWAs increased by £7.2 billion to £200.5 billion reflecting: ● a net increase in credit risk RWAs of £5.9 billion, primarily driven by lending growth, movements in risk metrics and an increase from the acquisition of Evelyn Partners. These increases were partially offset by the benefits of RWA management actions and decreases in CRD IV model updates; ● an increase in operational risk RWAs of £0.7 billion driven by the acquisition of Evelyn Partners; ● an increase in market risk RWAs of £0.4 billion, driven by the incremental risk charge (IRC) reflecting changes in government bond positions and increased stressed (SVaR) based RWAs related to client trading activity in the currency options business; ● an increase in counterparty credit risk RWAs of £0.2 billion, primarily due to an update to the approach to determining collateral liquidity in securities financing transactions and CRD IV model updates. ● UK leverage ratio 4.6% (2025 - 4.8%) The leverage ratio decreased by 20 basis points to 4.6% due to a £40.6 billion increase in leverage exposure partially offset by a £0.6 billion increase in Tier 1 capital. The key driver of the leverage exposure movement was an increase in other financial assets. MREL ratio 30.0% (2025 - 31.9%) The Minimum Requirements of own funds and Eligible Liabilities (MREL) ratio decreased by 190 basis points to 30.0% driven by a £7.2 billion increase in RWAs and a £1.5 billion decrease in MREL. MREL decreased to £60.1 billion driven by a £1.5 billion decrease in senior unsecured debt and an immaterial decrease in eligible Own Funds. The decrease in senior unsecured debt was mainly driven by redemptions totalling £2.4 billion partially offset by new issuances totalling £1.5 billion. There were offsetting moves in eligible Own Funds due to the issuance of a £0.5 billion Additional Tier 1 instrument and a $0.8 billion subordinated debt Tier 2 instrument, and the redemption of a £1.0 billion Tier 2 instrument. Liquidity portfolio £223.0bn (2025 - £237.9bn) The liquidity portfolio decreased by £14.9 billion to £223.0 billion compared with Q4 2025. Primary liquidity decreased by £4.0 billion to £153.2 billion, driven by lending growth partly offset by issuance and increased deposits. Secondary liquidity decreased by £10.9 billion due to reduced collateral at the Bank of England. LCR average 137% (2025 - 147%) The average Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) decreased by 10 percentage points to 137% during 2026, driven by lending growth and changes to outflow assumptions partly offset by issuance and increased deposits. NSFR average 131% (2025 - 135%) The average Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) decreased by 4 percentage points to 131% during 2026 driven by lending growth partly offset by issuance and increased deposits.
