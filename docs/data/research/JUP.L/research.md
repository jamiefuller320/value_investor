# Jupiter Fund Management Plc (JUP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T07:31:17.076696+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Jupiter Fund Management Plc (JUP.L) screens as a strong buy on deep value metrics (P/E 8.5×, P/B 0.9×, 3.7% yield, 12% ROE, 17/22 models passed), but the investment case rests on an operational inflection: the first calendar year of positive net flows since 2017, improving investment performance, and accretive scale from the CCLA acquisition. Filings show FY2025 underlying profit before tax of £138.3m and H1 2026 momentum (AUM £73.7bn, underlying PBT +67% YoY, cost:income 77%), yet earnings quality remains skewed by performance fees (£120.3m in FY2025). The stock trades below book with net debt minimal and active capital return (dividends, buybacks), but goodwill at £523.6m (~39% of H1 2026 assets) and CCLA integration risk temper conviction. Key debate: whether sustained organic flows and a path to a 70% cost:income ratio can re-rate a cyclical, fee-dependent asset manager, or whether performance-fee volatility and fee-margin dilution cap the upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags JUP.L across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families (composite 74%, sector-relative 70%, 20/20 data quality). For a value investor, the hook is a UK active asset manager trading at a discount to tangible book (P/B 0.9×) with improving fundamentals that screens do not fully capture in trailing multiples.

Business quality is strengthening on filing evidence. FY2025 delivered £1.3bn of net inflows—the first positive calendar year since 2017—versus £10.3bn outflows in 2024. AUM rose 19% to £54.0bn; H1 2026 AUM reached £73.7bn (+36%), supported by £0.7bn H1 net flows and the CCLA deal. Investment performance has improved materially: 68% of mutual fund AUM above peer median over three years at FY2025 (vs 61% in 2024), rising to 77% for pre-existing Jupiter funds at H1 2026. Management is executing on cost discipline (non-compensation costs to £89m at FY2025 year-end; cost:income improved from 82% to 77% in H1 2026) and a medium-term 70% target.

Capital allocation supports the value case. The Group redeemed £50m Tier 2 subordinated debt in April 2025, maintains an undrawn £100m revolving credit facility, and held regulatory capital of £244m at H1 2026 (>3× requirement). Shareholder returns include a 50% payout of pre-performance-fee underlying EPS, a FY2025 special distribution linked to performance fees, and a £30m/3% buyback programme launched April 2026 (8.8m shares cancelled by 17 July 2026 per interim filing).

The screen’s strong-buy signal aligns with a genuine turnaround narrative—flows, performance, scale, and balance-sheet strength—but earnings-quality concerns (performance-fee dependence, goodwill weighting) warrant accumulation rather than maximum conviction.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Regulatory and compliance: Principal risks unchanged from FY2025 annual report include regulatory, investment performance, technology/information security, outsourcing, financial, and people risks. Provisions for claims/regulatory action rose to £3.2m at H1 2026 (from £0.6m at year-end) per interim notes. Asset management remains subject to FCA oversight and evolving conduct rules.

Cyclical and market sensitivity: Revenue is overwhelmingly AUM-linked; geopolitical volatility in Q2 2026 affected client sentiment despite positive net flows. Performance fees are lumpy (£120.3m FY2025 vs £8.9m H1 2026; £120.3m was 28% of FY2025 net revenue). Excluding performance fees, FY2025 underlying PBT fell to £62.2m from £79.0m in 2024—a reminder that the core franchise was still under revenue pressure despite headline growth.

CCLA integration and acquisition risk: £100.3m cash consideration completed February 2026; £0.7bn H1 net outflows from CCLA strategies were expected but bear monitoring. CCLA mutual fund performance remains weak (44% above peer median over three years at H1 2026). Synergy target raised to £17m (from £16m) but integration costs, £3.5m property impairment, and £15.3m H1 exceptional items highlight execution risk. Goodwill increased to £523.6m; intangibles from CCLA total £51.9m.

Competitive and structural: Average Group fee margin fell to 60bps (H1 2026) from 65bps (FY2025) due to CCLA’s lower-margin book. Active managers face persistent passive competition and fee compression; Jupiter’s turnaround is not yet fully reflected in ex-performance-fee earnings.

Governance and key person: CEO Matthew Beesley (appointed 2024) is driving strategy; investment performance remains dependent on key portfolio managers. No material governance red flags in available filings.

Balance sheet and liquidity: Net leverage is low and regulatory capital is strong, but goodwill plus intangibles represent a large share of equity. The £100m RCF is undrawn; no covenant concerns disclosed. Pension liabilities appear reduced versus prior years (Yahoo fallback shows non-current pension plans in 2022–2023 but not prominently at FY2025—verify in full annual report).

Litigation/contingencies: Interim notes reference provisions for claims arising in the normal course; no quantified material litigation disclosed beyond the £3.2m provision.
