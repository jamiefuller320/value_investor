IAG Q3 results 2026
Strong revenue performance; full year operating margin guidance maintained
Luis Gallego, IAG Chief Executive Officer, said:
“IAG has delivered another resilient quarter. Our strong yield performance in all our markets reflects robust ongoing customer 
demand for travel for our airline brands. For the quarter we have achieved an operating margin before exceptional items of 17.7%, 
despite the very high price of jet fuel and a number of one-off disruption events, clearly demonstrating the quality of our business. 
“With our significant free cash flow generation, underpinned by a very strong balance sheet, we remain committed to delivering for 
our shareholders: we have announced a 10% increase in the interim dividend per share and have commenced the final tranche of our 
€1.5 billion share buyback programme.
“As a result of the actions we are taking the outlook for the rest of the year, as well as the start of next year, is positive. We are on 
track to deliver a full year operating margin before exceptional items of at least 12%, within our through-the-cycle target range, and 
to deliver further returns to shareholders.”
Highlights
• Strong revenue growth driven by long-haul yields across all of our main markets, Very strong performance in the North and South 
Atlantic. Positive revenue trends continuing for the fourth quarter and into 2027
• Resilient third quarter operating profit before exceptional items of €1,726 million at an operating margin of 17.7%. Strong pricing 
was offset by lower capacity, the impact of one-off disruption events and in particular by higher jet fuel prices 
• Each of our airlines has performed well in the quarter, with individual operating margins between 14.4% and 23.8%
• IAG Loyalty continues to grow its profit, highlighting the quality and value of this business to the Group
• Taking action to maintain strong profitability and free cash flow: through disciplined capacity allocation, revenue growth and cost 
savings
• Very strong balance sheet: net leverage at 30 September 2026 of 0.6x
• 10% increase in the interim dividend to €0.053 per share (2025: €0.048 per share)
• Final tranche of €500 million of the €1.5 billion share buyback programme has now commenced; due to complete by the end of 
February 2027
• On track to deliver another year of world-class operating margins, profits, free cash flow and shareholder returns
Q3 financial performance
• Revenue in the third quarter increased by 4.5% to €9,744 million. Passenger revenue increased by 3.8%; Cargo revenue increased 
by 3.9% as we targeted premium traffic and pricing. Other revenue increased by 11.3%, as IAG Loyalty delivered another good 
quarter of growth.
• Non-fuel unit cost increased by 3.4%, impacted by disruption during the quarter, including major Air Traffic Control outages, partly 
mitigated by the ongoing benefit of cost-saving transformation initiatives. Our total fuel cost increased by 30.4% in the third 
quarter, net of the benefit of hedging.
• IAG Loyalty increased its profit by 8% to £152 million, at a margin of 18.9%.
• Operating profit before exceptional items in Q3 decreased to €1,726 million (2025: €2,053 million) as revenue growth was not 
sufficient to offset the impact of disruption and high fuel prices. Our year-to-date (nine months) operating margin before 
exceptional items of 13.5% demonstrates the quality and resilience of our business.

Trading
• We have increased unit revenue across all of our geographic markets, underpinned by strong demand for travel and the 
attractiveness of our schedules, network and airline brands.
• The North Atlantic has been particularly strong in the quarter, with unit revenue increasing by 11.0%, with capacity broadly flat 
(0.2%). All of our airlines reported positive unit revenue. British Airways grew capacity by 2.2% and increased its unit revenue by 
12.0% (and by 12.6% at constant currency). Iberia continues to deliver profitable growth through its A321XLRs, as it increased ASKs 
by 11.5% and unit revenue increased by 4.2%. Aer Lingus increased its North Atlantic capacity by 1.1% and unit revenues by 0.7%.  
• The South Atlantic continues to perform well, as ongoing strong demand has delivered an increase in unit revenue of 2.3% with 
capacity growing by 5.4%. Iberia increased capacity by 4.8% (including its new XLR routes to Fortaleza and Recife) and unit 
revenue increased by 1.9%, whilst British Airways also increased unit revenue by 5.0% on capacity growth of 2.8%.  
• The European market has been more challenging due to high levels of competitor capacity growth. We have reduced capacity by 
1.2% mainly at Iberia and Vueling to protect profitability. Vueling reduced capacity by 5.2% but increased its unit revenue by 1.2%, 
whilst Iberia reduced capacity by 3.0% and unit revenue declined by 1.6%. British Airways has again performed well as capacity 
increased by 1.0% and unit revenue by 2.5%, reflecting its strong brand and positioning.  
• Domestic Spain has also been competitive and our airlines have performed relatively well due to their strong positions in core 
markets. Vueling increased capacity by 7.3% in its core markets and Iberia increased capacity by 18.1% as it reallocated capacity 
from the Middle East, which put pressure on yields due to the much-reduced selling window. Overall unit revenue in the region 
was broadly flat, increasing by 0.1%.
• Our trading in the rest of the world was strong. In the AMESA region capacity decreased by 19.0% as flights no longer operated to 
many Gulf destinations. In particular British Airways has been affected by the challenges of redeploying aircraft and crews which 
has affected absolute profit as well as profitability. Unit revenue in the AMESA region increased by 13.2%, helped by business 
customers now flying from North America via London. In Asia Pacific, capacity increased by 9.3% through network changes to 
support traffic no longer flying via the Middle East, with strong demand driving an increase in unit revenue of 3.8%.

Delivering for our shareholders
We are announcing an interim dividend of €0.053 per share, an increase of 10% year on year which reflects a broadly inflationary 
increase in the amount of the payment as well as the benefit of the share buyback programme. As announced last year we continue 
to expect to pay around half of our total annual dividend as an interim and around half as a final dividend. 
We have also commenced the final €500 million tranche of our €1.5 billion share buyback programme. This is scheduled to complete 
in February 2027, ahead of our Full Year 2026 results announcement.  
Outlook
• Commercial strength: we are continuing to deliver strong passenger yields and revenue growth, led by the long-haul business, 
demonstrating the attractiveness of our network and brands. We continue to expect good revenue growth, with 85% of expected 
forward bookings for the fourth quarter now secured. Bookings for the first quarter of 2027 are currently strong.
• Resilient performance despite short-term headwinds on profitability: significantly higher fuel cost and operational disruption 
reduced third quarter profitability to 17.7%. We continue to expect to deliver an operating margin before exceptional items in Full 
Year 2026 of at least 12%, within our through-the-cycle target range of 12% to 15%, demonstrating the quality of the Group’s 
businesses and resilience of our earnings streams.
• Financial strength: our strong balance sheet, with net leverage of 0.6x at 30 September 2026, and significant free cash flow 
supports ongoing shareholder returns, including the increase in the interim dividend and ongoing €1.5 billion share buyback 
programme, as well as expected future dividends and excess cash returns.

Full Year 2026 modelling assumptions
• Full year capacity (ASK) is now expected to be slightly down compared to Full Year 2025, driven by the absence of Middle East 
capacity, the impact of disruption events to date across our airlines and our disciplined, proactive capacity management as we 
focus on profitable flying in a seasonally quieter final quarter.
• We now expect Full Year 2026 non-fuel unit cost to increase by around 1% as a result of the impact of the disruption on the Group 
over the last few months, as well as the impact from lower flown capacity than planned.
• Total fuel cost is expected to be €8.9 billion for the full year, based on the jet fuel curve and foreign exchange rates at 3 November 
2026. We are currently 45% hedged for 2027.
• Capital expenditure for Full Year 2026 is now expected to be €3.1 billion. We now expect 12 aircraft to be delivered in 2026 
(compared to 16 guided at half year results) due to a delay to three Boeing 737 deliveries at Vueling and one Airbus A321neo for 
British Airways.

FINANCIAL REVIEW for the nine months to 30 September 2026
IAG capacity
In the first nine months of 2026, passenger capacity operated, measured in available seat kilometres (ASKs), fell by 0.2% versus the 
same period in 2025. The capacity operated was lower than the original plan, which was for growth of approximately 3%, with the 
reduction driven by cancellations due to the conflict in the Middle East, together with aircraft availability linked to engine issues and 
disruption related to air traffic control disruption, including from the UK’s National Air Traffic Services (NATS).

The conflict in the Middle East, particularly from 28 February 2026 onwards, led to the Group’s airlines promptly suspending flights 
to destinations in the immediately affected area, including Abu Dhabi, Amman, Bahrain, Doha, Dubai and Tel Aviv, with Jeddah 
suspended from 24 April. The Middle East represents approximately 3% of the Group’s capacity. British Airways redirected capacity 
to South Asia, Africa and Asia Pacific, including additional flying to destinations such as Bangkok, Bengaluru, Delhi, Malé (Maldives), 
Nairobi and Singapore. Iberia redeployed capacity previously flying to the Middle East to Japan, Latin America, the North Atlantic 
and the Spanish Islands. In recent months, the Group has developed various plans to resume flights to destinations in the Middle 
East, with a smaller schedule than previously, but the continued conflict in the region has meant that capacity restoration has been 
postponed, or has been on a smaller scale than envisaged. As a result of these delays, where possible, the Group has redeployed 
capacity to alternative destinations in other regions, based on demand.

financing requirements. Consequently, the Directors have a reasonable expectation that the Group has sufficient liquidity to continue 
in operational existence over the going concern period, and hence continue to adopt the going concern basis of preparation for this 
report.

Fuel hedging
The Group seeks to reduce the impact of volatile commodity prices by hedging prices in advance. The Group’s fuel hedging policy is 
designed to provide flexibility to respond to both significant unexpected reductions in travel demand or capacity and/or material or 
sudden changes in jet fuel prices. The policy allows for differentiation within the Group, to match the nature of each operating 
company, and the use of option structures for a proportion of the hedging undertaken. The policy operates on a three-year rolling 
basis, with hedging of up to 75% of anticipated requirements in the near term, reducing steadily over the first two years down to 
20% for the final year. The policy also provides flexibility for low-cost airlines within the Group to adopt hedging of up to 80% in the 
near-term. In the near-term, the Group hedges its anticipated jet fuel exposure using a greater proportion of jet fuel derivatives. 
Further out, the Group uses more liquid proxy instruments, primarily Brent crude oil and gasoil, with positions progressively built up 
into jet fuel hedges, where possible, as maturity approaches.

IAG Loyalty VAT 
As previously disclosed, on 29 October 2024 His Majesty’s Revenue and Customs in the UK (HMRC) issued a decision asserting that 
VAT is payable at the standard rate of 20% upon issuance of Avios by IAG Loyalty from March 2018 onwards. This contrasts with 
IAG Loyalty’s historical approach of accounting for VAT depending on the nature of the redemption products for which Avios are 
redeemed, with the vast majority being flights that are zero-rated.
The Group has a hearing scheduled at the First-tier Tribunal (Tax) in the UK during the remainder of the fourth quarter of 2026, with 
a decision expected during 2027. To enable a hearing of the case at the First-tier Tribunal (Tax), without admission of liability, the 
Group in prior periods paid €516 million to HMRC for periods prior to its decision, net of amounts recovered as input VAT for certain 
of its subsidiaries, which is expected to be refunded if the matter is resolved in the Group’s favour. Accordingly, these amounts have 
been recorded as a non-current asset on the Balance sheet.
For payments made to HMRC for periods subsequent to its decision on 29 October 2024, a proportion of the payments made 
reduce the amounts that would previously have been recognised within Deferred revenue in the Balance sheet upon issuance of the 
Avios and subsequently within Passenger revenue and Other revenue in the Income statement when the Avios are redeemed. These 
payments amount to €122 million at 30 September 2026 and are disclosed as a contingent asset.
The Directors are satisfied that it is not probable that an adverse outcome will eventuate, and accordingly, the Group continues to 
consider at 30 September 2026, and through to the date of this report, that the €516 million non-current asset, recognised as a result 
of the payment to HMRC, is recoverable.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
The Group has continued to maintain its processes to identify, assess, and manage risks under the Group’s Enterprise Risk 
Management (ERM) framework. The principal risks and uncertainties affecting the Group are detailed in the Risk management and 
principal risk factors section of the 2025 Annual report and accounts and these remain relevant.
The IAG Board monitors the risk landscape and challenges management on its plans to address and adapt to changes that influence 
or impact the Group’s performance, including the Group’s businesses’ response to significant geopolitical events such as the 
outbreak of war or market shocks, elevated fuel price or supply pressures, volatility in financial markets and friction in trade flows, 
disruption in operations and the Group’s supply chain, and agentic AI and AI tools and other emerging technologies that could 
transform or disrupt the business or the aviation industry.
In assessing the Group’s principal risks, the Board has considered the risk environment including:
• status of the negotiations between the US and Iran, and its impact to ongoing market volatility and macro-economic uncertainty, 
fuel pricing and operational and customer disruption, including airspace restrictions;
• access to financial markets and hedging mitigations, with increasing inflation and pressure on interest rates;
• energy price increases and security over jet fuel supply from the intermittent closure of key supply lanes;
• monitoring the trade and economic effects, outcomes to markets and demand resulting from central bank or governmental policy 
decisions that may reduce customer confidence in key customer segments or markets;
• resilience to the cyber threat environment, further heightened by geopolitical conflicts, with the acceleration in agentic AI to gain 
unauthorised access to systems or data within the Group’s businesses or its supply chains;
• operational, IT and technical resilience across its airlines, response plans, customer support and disruption management tools to 
adapt to disruption, particularly caused by more prolonged weather events or air traffic control (ATC) restrictions or outages;
• status of negotiations with unions to secure sustainable collective agreements and business change;
• increased friction impacting the flow of goods and creating further supply chain delays in aircraft maintenance, especially for 
engines and components;
• approvals processes for airport expansion and proposed costs for London and other hub airports;
• mandates for the use of synthetic sustainable jet fuel and carbon schemes, and relative competitive distortion, particularly for EU 
and UK aviation; and
• managing the cost base and any new inflationary effects and managing AI and agentic AI adoption and transformational change at 
pace.
No new principal risks were identified through the risk management discussions and assessments in the year to date. Where further 
action has been required, the Board has considered potential mitigations, and, where appropriate or feasible, the Group has 
implemented or confirmed plans that would address those risks or retain them within the Board’s determined Group risk appetite.
From the risks identified in the 2025 Annual report and accounts, given the current environment, the main risks that continue to be a 
key area of focus, due to their potential implications for the Group, are outlined below.
Critical third parties in the supply chain
The aviation sector is affected by its reliance on a fragmented global supply chain which is currently facing the additional stresses of 
inflationary pressures, regulatory approval and delayed manufacturing timelines for new fleet and friction in global trade driven by 
geopolitical events and tariffs. Procurement specialists work with all critical suppliers to understand any potential disruption that 
could impact on the availability of new fleet, engines or critical goods or reliability of critical services. The Group is particularly 
focused on aircraft and engine manufacturers, given continued delays in fleet deliveries and engine availability and durability 
problems, to understand their recovery plans and ensure that the additional cost and complexity of delays is minimised or resilience 
to mitigate third party weaknesses is addressed and embedded. Airport infrastructure developments and the airlines’ dependency 
on airport planners in hub airports, to enable growth in a timely, cost effective manner or to minimise operational impacts, are 
assessed and proactively managed. Expansion plans for the London Heathrow airport could see the costs of inefficient infrastructure 
developments impacting on traffic and network, growth and fleet decisions.
Data and cybersecurity
Ongoing geopolitical conflicts, protectionist stances between regions and governments and the acceleration in agentic AI to gain 
unauthorised access to systems or data and to scale attacks sees increased exposure across all sectors particularly those with 
complex, interconnected supply chains. These can have a significant impact with uncertain recovery times or require limitations on 
operations to address and resolve or restore data loss. The Group is further developing its security defences and extending 
monitoring and recovery capabilities and that of its third parties to protect itself.
Economic, political and regulatory environment
Wider macroeconomic events continue to drive significant market uncertainty and volatility as well as financial factors including 
inflation, interest rates, fuel price or fuel shortage and foreign exchange rates. The status of the negotiations between the US and 
Iran and its impact on markets and demand, fuel price and security over fuel supply, as well as operations, remain under review. The 
tone of dialogue and political responses between countries to ongoing conflicts or relative policy position may cause operational 
disruption to the Group or its third parties as well as see shifts in investment, change in support for the aviation industry or increased 
costs. The Group monitors and assesses threats and potential impacts, particularly the effects of trading friction, barriers to trade or 
additional tariffs. The rise of populist governments and the trend of regulatory and policy disparity between key regions, or 
significant reversal in government policy approaches, may see further protectionism which could result in market or competitive 
distortion that increases costs to the Group’s airlines.
Operational and IT resilience
The operational environment of the Group’s airlines remains highly reliant on the ability to adapt operational and customer processes 
to external event changes, particularly with the increased severity and prolonged nature of weather events, air traffic control outages 
and restrictions, in addition to a volatile geopolitical environment creating short term operational and airspace disruptions. The 
Group continues with the delivery of significant IT infrastructure transformation to modernise and digitalise its IT estates, as well as 
securing increased technical resilience. The Group is affected by its reliance on the global supply chain and the impacts of delays in 
aircraft deliveries, scarcity of components and engines on aircraft turnarounds, aircraft on the ground and reduced aircraft utilisation. 
Its airlines are focused on minimising the impact of any unplanned outages or disruption to customers with additional resilience built 
into the airlines’ networks.

People, culture and employee relations
The Group relies on the successful agreement of sustainable collective bargaining arrangements to operate its airlines and continue 
to adapt to the competitive environment. Breakdown in the bargaining process may result in industrial or strike action impacting 
operations and disrupting customers and business performance. This can delay transformational change and create further stress 
across the business. Where agreements are open and there is a threat of unrest, the airlines engage in discussions with unions as well 
as other relevant bodies.
Sustainable aviation
The Group monitors and assesses the outcomes of EU and UK policy on carbon costs from mandates on the aviation sector. Current 
mandates and policy proposals could see an increase in competitive distortion rather than improve the competitiveness of EU and 
UK aviation, particularly from the costs of mandates for the use of synthetic sustainable jet fuel. The aviation industry has a 
significant reliance on the infrastructure and production of sustainable aviation fuels to meet government mandates. The costs of 
these mandates may impact the commercial viability of routes.
Transformation, innovation and AI
The Group has identified initiatives within its cost base to help offset inflationary price increases driven by geopolitical events and 
the additional costs of resilience and disruption management. Use of AI and agentic AI models provide solutions to operational 
bottlenecks or disruption events, optimise outcomes or re-engineer ways of working supported by data driven insights. The Group’s 
businesses are focused on securing skillsets and implementing organisational structures for future ways of working as AI tools and 
end to end domain transformation are embedded across the Group’s businesses. An AI governance framework with Group oversight 
is in place across AI enabled initiatives. The talent and cultural mindset needed to deliver the transformation, innovation and growth 
plans to meet the Group’s ambition will be critical to delivery success.
The Board and its sub committees have been appraised of regulatory, competitor and governmental responses on an ongoing basis.

ALTERNATIVE PERFORMANCE MEASURES DEFINITION AND RECONCILIATION
The performance of the Group is assessed using a number of alternative performance measures (APMs), some of which have been 
identified as key performance indicators of the Group. These measures are not defined under International Financial Reporting 
Standards (IFRS), should be considered in addition to IFRS measurements, may differ to definitions given by regulatory bodies 
applicable to the Group and may differ to similarly titled measures presented by other companies. They are used to measure the 
outcome of the Group’s strategy based on the Group’s strategic imperatives of: strengthening our core; driving earnings growth 
through asset-light businesses; and operating under a strengthened financial and sustainability framework.
During the nine months to 30 September 2026, the Group has made no changes to its pre-existing disclosures and treatments of 
APMs compared to those disclosed in the Annual report and accounts for the year to 31 December 2025.
The definition of each APM, together with a reconciliation to the nearest measure prepared in accordance with IFRS is presented 
below.
a Profit after tax before exceptional items
Exceptional items are those that in the Board’s and management’s view need to be separately disclosed by virtue of their size or 
incidence to supplement the understanding of the entity’s financial performance. The Management Committee of the Group uses 
financial performance on a pre-exceptional basis to evaluate operating performance and to make strategic, financial and operational 
decisions, and externally because it is widely used by security analysts and investors in evaluating the performance of the Group 
between reporting periods and against other companies.
There has been one exceptional item recorded in the nine months to 30 September 2026 (nine months to 30 September 2025:

d Gross and Net debt to EBITDA before exceptional items (KPI)
To supplement total borrowings as presented in accordance with IFRS, the Group reviews both Gross debt to EBITDA before 
exceptional items and Net debt to EBITDA before exceptional items to assess its level of gross and net debt in comparison to the 
underlying earnings generated by the Group in order to evaluate the underlying business performance of the Group. These measures 
are used to monitor the Group’s leverage and to assess financial headroom against internal and external analyst and investor 
benchmarks and their long-term industry expectations.
Gross debt is defined as long-term borrowings (both current and non-current). Net debt is defined as Gross debt, less cash, cash 
equivalents and current interest-bearing deposits.
EBITDA before exceptional items is defined as the rolling four quarters operating result before exceptional items, interest, taxation, 
depreciation, amortisation and impairment. 
The Group believes that this additional measure, which is used internally to assess the Group’s financial capacity, is useful to the 
users of the financial statements in helping them to see how the Group’s financial capacity has changed over the reporting period. It 
is a measure of the profitability of the Group and of the core operating cash flows generated by the business model.

e Results on a constant currency basis
Movements in foreign exchange rates impact the Group’s financial results. The IAG Board and Management Committee review the 
results, including revenue and operating costs at constant rates of exchange. These financial measures are calculated at constant 
rates of exchange based on a retranslation, at prior year exchange rates, of the current year’s results of the Group. Although the 
Board and Management Committee do not believe that these measures are a substitute for IFRS measures, the Board and 
Management Committee do believe that such results excluding the impact of currency fluctuations year-on-year provide additional 
useful information to investors regarding the Group’s operating performance on a constant currency basis. Accordingly, the financial 
measures at constant currency within the discussion of the Group Financial review should be read in conjunction with the 
information provided in the Group financial statements.
