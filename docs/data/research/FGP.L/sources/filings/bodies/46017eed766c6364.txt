With around 30,000 employees, we reported adjusted revenue of £1.72 billion and transported almost 1.5m passengers a day in FY 2026. We create solutions that reduce complexity, making travel smoother and life easier. Our businesses are at the heart of our communities and the essential services we provide are critical to delivering wider economic, social and environmental goals. Each of our divisions is a leader in its field: First Bus is one of the largest bus operators in the UK, serving more than 20% of the population in the UK with a fleet of c.6,000 buses and coaches, and carrying more than a million passengers a day. We operate a fleet of c.320 trains through two DfT contracted train operating companies: WCP (incorporating Avanti West Coast and West Coast Partnership Development) and GWR, and three Open Access routes (Hull Trains and two under the Lumo brand). Chief Executive Officer review On track to deliver full year earnings and material shareholder returns I am pleased to report further progress and continued delivery of our commitments in the first half of our 2027 financial year, leaving us on course to maintain FY 2027 adjusted earnings per share, in line with FY 2026. The work we have done to transform First Bus over the last few years and to manage the material headwinds experienced in prior years has driven further earnings growth in the division in H1 2027. In First Rail, earnings during the period reflect the transfer of SWR to the DfTO in May 2025 and the launch and ramp up of our new Stirling Open Access service. Group adjusted revenue has grown by over 20% compared to the prior year, to just over £1 billion. Group adjusted earnings per share has decreased, to 8.4p (H1 2026: 9.9p), with profit growth in First Bus offset by launch and ramp up costs and sustained price competition in Open Access rail. We have returned c.£72.3m to our shareholders via dividends and our ongoing £100m share buyback programme in H1 2027. The Board has declared an interim dividend of 2.3p (H1 2026: 2.2p) which will result in a dividend payment of c.£12m, to be paid on 29 December to shareholders on the register on 27 November. Looking ahead, as we continue to reposition the Group and thanks to our significant free cash generation, the Board has today announced a further £50m share buyback programme, following on from the current £100m programme, which we expect to complete in FY 2028. Focus on service delivery continues to drive growth Service delivery remains a key strategic priority across our businesses. Not only do reliable, sustainable services attract and grow passenger demand, they also position us to win value accretive contracts in key markets as the UK bus and rail industries continue to evolve. In First Bus, we have again improved our Net Promoter Score, from +15 in the prior year, to +25 in H1 2027 and in our franchise operations we continue to rank highly in performance tables. Our expertise and commitment to best-in-class service delivery have also been recognised with the award of the Sizewell C worker transport contract, where at peak construction an estimated 8,000 workers will be on site, new route contracts in London and more recently, two bus franchise contracts in West Yorkshire. In First Rail, we took over the operation of the London Overground concession in May 2026. We have a highly experienced team in place, working to restore and improve operational performance levels. Initiatives include a step change in driver recruitment and training to ensure all depots are fully staffed moving forwards. In Open Access, our East Coast Main Line operations continue to rank highly in customer satisfaction surveys and seat miles utilisation remains in line with H1 2026 at 68%. Launch of Stirling service brings the benefits of open access to the West Coast During H1 2027 we launched our new Lumo service on the West Coast Main Line, linking Stirling to London Euston. This is the first ever direct service to the capital for communities in Greenfaulds, Whifflet and Larbert and has already created around 100 new jobs. The service reported revenues of £6.3m for the half year, with seat miles utilisation of 45%, in line with the long-distance industry average. Lumo has established a strong brand, with attractive fares and an excellent track record of growing demand and we are confident that our Stirling service will continue to attract new passengers as the ramp up phase continues. We were disappointed that our revised application for a new Lumo service between London and Rochdale was declined by the Office of Rail and Road (ORR). Our proposal demonstrated a workable solution and strong economic benefits for passengers and communities in Manchester and the North West, alongside significant investment in UK public transport. Economic growth, regional regeneration and thriving local communities have returned to the forefront of public policy, with increasing recognition that improving lives requires investment not only in infrastructure, but also in the networks and opportunities that connect people to work, services and society. Against a backdrop of fiscal constraint and increasing transport subsidy, we firmly believe that open access should be part of Great British Railways’ solution to operating the railway effectively, with competition on a level playing field. Operated without government subsidy, open access services drive productivity, create jobs and bring significant benefits to passengers and communities including affordable fares, excellent customer service and greater choice, as well as a material contribution to the UK in taxes and infrastructure charges. Private sector investment in new, environmentally friendly fleets and improved connectivity is also vital for driving growth and reducing the burden on the UK taxpayer by slowing the long-term growth in public subsidy and delivering improved returns on infrastructure investment. Our strong balance sheet provides significant capital allocation optionality Alongside operational delivery in the first half, our cash conversion and balance sheet have remained robust, with free cash generation before acquisitions and returns of £36.0m and adjusted net debt of £186.0m at the end of the half. We now anticipate free cash generation over the three years to FY 2029 of over £500m, boosted by the c.£48m Mistral Data sale proceeds, the recent £17.5m Group Section Pension escrow return and the c.£55m cash inflow from the depot sales in West Yorkshire. This provides significant scope to deploy capital within a disciplined framework to grow and diversify our portfolio in attractive markets whilst continuing to deliver meaningful shareholder returns. On course to maintain full year earnings per share We are on course to maintain Group adjusted earnings per share in FY 2027, from a high-quality earnings base. Looking further ahead, in First Bus we have scope to grow earnings further, with a medium-term total adjusted operating profit margin target of 8-9% and a lower level of capital expenditure. In First Rail, our Open Access earnings will see further growth following the launch of our new Carmarthen service and the addition of ten-car operations on certain Lumo East Coast services in the summer of 2028. A clear path to continued sustainable growth and returns We enter the second half of our financial year with increased visibility following the confirmation of the transfer dates for our two remaining DfT TOCs and with a more sustainable earnings base, with around half of our revenues now contracted with some level of cost inflation protections. We will continue to reposition the Group to improve our cost base and operating model, including an anticipated c.£20m of cost savings and portfolio margin improvements in FY 2028, to continuously improve performance, increase earnings and grow in attractive UK bus and rail markets, supported by our strong balance sheet and highly cash generative business model. Underpinning this strategy is the Board's continued commitment to progressive dividends and the return of surplus cash to shareholders. I would like to conclude by thanking all our employees for their continued hard work and expertise that has contributed to another period of consistent delivery and progress. Together we are delivering the best possible services for our customers and creating a stronger, more resilient FirstGroup, well positioned to seize the many opportunities ahead. A strong performance driven by focus on continuous improvement Our results for H1 2027 reflect the work we have done to transform our business, deliver continuous improvements and successfully navigate the material headwinds we experienced in prior years. Revenue has increased to £750m (H1 2026: £703m) thanks to growth in all three of our businesses, Regional Bus, Business and Coach, and Franchising. Adjusted operating profit has grown by 18%, to £50.2m thanks to further yield and cost efficiencies, the completion of a depot sale in Wales and further growth in Business and Coach and in First Bus London. Service delivery remains a key priority Service delivery is a key focus across our operations, underpinned by our commitment to the safety of our customers, employees and all third parties interacting with our businesses. By delivering the best possible service we can improve customer satisfaction, encourage more people to use our services, support revenue growth and maximise performance incentives in our franchised businesses. It also positions us well to participate in further partnership, franchising and commercial opportunities. In Regional Bus, alongside our focus on employee engagement and support and driver recruitment programmes, we continue to implement data and behavioural led interventions to improve performance and punctuality. Revenue per mile improved to £5.69 in H1 2027 (H1 2026: £5.55) and we reduced our agency drivers by c.55% compared to the prior year. In our franchise operations in London and Rochdale, we continue to rank highly in the operator league tables. By making use of our rich customer data to identify targeted actions, we have also continued to improve customer experience in H1 2027. Our overall Net Promoter Score has increased further, to +25 (H1 2026: +15) tracking closely with improvements in reliability and punctuality. Customers continue to highlight improvements in driver friendliness, vehicle cleanliness and journey comfort. Ongoing investment in fleet modernisation, including through the rollout of electric vehicles, has further enhanced the customer experience and contributed positively to overall satisfaction scores. In H1 2027, Norfolk Suffolk Coast and Dorset achieved NPS scores of 46 and 43 respectively, supported by good punctuality and reliability. Leicester continued to be one of our best-performing operations, achieving an NPS score of 40 and industry-leading on-time performance of 92.4%. Passenger volumes have stabilised Alongside our focus on performance and customer experience to help grow passenger demand, we have continued to strengthen our marketing operations, bringing in specialist skill sets to deliver data-led pricing strategies and marketing campaigns that are more commercially focused and efficient. We are now largely through the decline in passenger volumes following the transition to the £3 fare cap in January 2025. Total volumes in Regional Bus, excluding our operations in Cornwall that were closed in February 2026, declined by 0.6% compared to the prior year, with a 2.4% increase in concessionary volumes offsetting a 2.4% decline in commercial volumes. The free bus travel for children scheme introduced across England in August drove a 47% increase in child tickets year on year, demonstrating how young person funding schemes can stimulate passenger growth and encourage lifelong bus use. The scheme has supported continued volume growth in the majority of our markets in England, and as customers returned from the summer holiday period, we have also seen improvements in our adult and student volumes. In Scotland, the expansion of the £2 fare cap pilot scheme to the Strathclyde region during September 2026 resulted in a 2% improvement in our commercial volumes in Glasgow compared to volumes prior to the cap being introduced and on our Glasgow Airport Express Service we have seen year on year passenger growth of 20%. Looking ahead, we welcomed the recent announcements regarding the re-introduction of the £2 fare cap in England from 1 January to 31 December 2027 and the extension of the £2 fare scheme in Scotland to Aberdeen and Aberdeenshire from April 2027. We look forward to working with the UK and Scottish Governments to deliver the schemes, and as we have with previous schemes, on the detail of the reimbursement mechanisms. Focus on cost control as industry-wide inflationary pressures remain Despite an increasing proportion of contracted revenues and fare schemes with some inflationary protection, an ongoing focus on cost efficiencies remains a priority. In H1 2027, revenues benefitted from fare increases of c.£15m and contracted revenue inflation of c.£9m that more than mitigated £16m of cost inflation. Costs during the period increased due to inflation by c.2.5%, mainly in wages, with a c.4% average increase in driver pay awards. In line with our focus on staggered, multi-year pay award settlements, we have now settled 76% of our driver pay awards with multi-year awards. We continue to manage our fuel and electricity costs with our two-year forward hedging programme. As at November 2026, c.83% of our fuel requirement for H2 2027 was hedged at 42.8p per litre and c.68% for FY 2028 at 43.3p per litre. Approximately 87% of the Group’s H2 2027 forecast floating rate electricity consumption is hedged at £79/MWh and c.75% for FY 2028 at £73/MWh. An important period for Business and Coach In Business and Coach, where we are actively working to grow and diversify, we have again grown earnings. Revenue in H1 2027 increased by 11% to £123m thanks to continued contract extensions, new contract awards and the contribution of our new businesses. In July, we were pleased to announce that our subsidiary, Specialist Passenger Solutions (SPS) had been awarded the contract to operate worker transport and site shuttle services for the Sizewell C nuclear power construction site. The contract has a term of up to five years from July 2026 and SPS will operate a modern, zero emission fleet of c.150 buses provided to SPS by Sizewell C Ltd. The new operation is also expected to create around 400 new jobs, including drivers, engineers and support staff and at peak construction it is estimated that nearly 8,000 workers will be working at Sizewell C. We look forward to making use of our extensive experience from our SPS operations at Hinkley Point C where we have successfully managed workforce transport for the UK’s largest construction site since 2016 and currently transport more than 12,000 people a day. We have also continued to expand our UK coach portfolio through a series of targeted acquisitions, including Eagle Coaches in Bristol, Wilfreda Beehive in Doncaster, Peace Coaches in Aberdeenshire and KB Coaches in Gloucestershire, combined with the expansion of services we operate on behalf of Flixbus. The acquired businesses represent annual revenues of c.£15.5m and adjusted operating profit of c.£3.1m and will further strengthen our regional presence, broaden our customer offering and contribute to our diversified coach business that has grown to around 640 coaches and more than 800 employees. Further growth ahead in First Bus London First Bus London contributed revenue of £173m in H1 2027 and remains on course to deliver full year revenue of c.£350m. We have won a number of new routes over the last few months, including the new Superloop 15 route out of our recently acquired Wandsworth depot, and retained a number of key routes out of our Shepherds Bush and Fulwell depots. We currently have 100% of our FY 2027 contracted revenues secured, 96% of FY 2028 and 81% for FY 2029. Leading in electrification, enabling adjacent revenue opportunities Thanks to our accelerated investment in electrification over the last few years, we have one of the largest zero emission bus fleets in the UK (c.28% of our fleet at the end of September), four fully and 20 substantially electrified depots. In London, 42% of our fleet is now electric and during H1 2027 we completed the installation of a gantry charging system at our Westbourne Park depot with the capacity to charge at least 150 electric buses. Our expertise in electrification has been recognised by both the South Yorkshire and West Yorkshire Combined Authorities who have engaged First Bus to support the electrification of two depots in each region ahead of the transition to franchising. Works include a comprehensive design, tendering and project management service. The electrification of our fleet and depots is also allowing us to start to unlock adjacent revenue streams including third party charging at our depots, capacity market trading and on-site battery storage. During H1 2027 we had third party charging available at 14 of our depots outside London and having successfully installed a one-megawatt battery energy storage facility at our Hoeford depot in August, we are expanding our partnership with Palmer Energy Technology to bring battery storage units to more of our sites. We have six live projects underway, including the installation of a 2.15 megawatt battery energy storage system in Aberdeen. Designed to house five or more bus battery-equivalent, these units will be used to participate in grid-based revenue streams by distributing power back into the grid at peak times. West Yorkshire asset sales and contracts unlock material value and bolster our franchise portfolio We continue to work alongside our regional partners to support the transition to franchising. We have now completed the sale of depots in South Yorkshire and Wales, and we recently announced an agreement with the West Yorkshire Combined Authority (WYCA) regarding the sale of five depots and 330 buses for £109m. Following the transition to franchising in West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire and Wales, our Regional Bus owned fleet is expected to reduce to c.2,900 vehicles from c.4,500 currently. An estimated c.300 vehicles that will not be used in franchising will be available to be cascaded to other depots, resulting in a c.£10m further deferral of medium-term capital expenditure and other related working capital. We were also delighted to have recently been awarded two bus franchise contracts in West Yorkshire. Following a period of mobilisation, the contracts in Bramley and Huddersfield, where we currently operate, are due to commence in May 2027 with a core five-year term with an option to extend for a further two years. WYCA will retain revenue risk and specify the service levels, with the Group responsible for the delivery of services. We anticipate combined annual revenues of c.£40m from the contracts with operating profit margins reflecting the more capital light franchise model, with one depot still partially operating with owned diesel vehicles. We look forward to drawing on our scale, expertise and strong operational track record to deliver high-quality, sustainable bus services in partnership with WYCA. An experienced partner to help bus networks grow and thrive We are a leading, highly experienced operator with a large, well-capitalised fleet and depot footprint, a strong focus on best-in-class service delivery and a proven track record of delivering quality services under both the franchise and partnership models. This positions us well to actively take part in future franchising and partnership opportunities as the regional bus market in England evolves. Looking ahead, an estimated £1.5bn of annualised revenues in regional bus will be competitively franchised over the next five years in regions that have already formally announced their intention to transition to the franchise model. This includes in South Yorkshire, West Yorkshire and Wales where we currently operate, representing annual revenues of c.£260m and in Liverpool and West Midlands where we do not currently operate. We are working alongside our local authority partners to support the transition to franchising, including through the sale of depots in South Yorkshire, Wales and West Yorkshire, and will actively participate in value accretive franchising opportunities where we can make use of our substantial assets and expertise. In Scotland, the First Minister has recently announced that the Scottish Government intends to bring Scotland's bus network under public control by 2031 through a combination of franchising and regulation. We welcome the aim to deliver better bus services and encourage more people to use public transport. The Scottish Government began its engagement on the ‘Better Buses’ legislation that was announced as part of the Plan for Government in September, and we will continue to work in partnership as proposals are worked up in further detail ahead of the introduction of legislation which we understand is set to be introduced to Holyrood in 2027. The Bill will then need to progress through the Parliamentary process before becoming law. Active partnerships with local and national stakeholders are essential for thriving local bus networks. Regardless of the model, our aim is to provide consistently high-quality service delivery and to encourage more people to use the bus. We will continue to adapt our business to deliver great value, to shape networks to suit where and when people want to travel, to serve communities and grow local economies in a sustainable way. Outlook We anticipate revenue of just over £1.5bn for FY 2027, with further progress in adjusted operating profit. We anticipate capital expenditure of c.£140m in FY 2027, net of c.£15m of co-funding. In FY 2028, our capital expenditure is expected to decrease to £80-90m following a period of accelerated decarbonisation investment. FY 2028 revenue and adjusted operating profit and margins in Regional Bus will reflect the transition to franchising, offset by a higher level of cash generation, in line with the move towards a more capital light model in these markets. Looking further ahead, as the UK bus market evolves, we will make use of our scale, expertise, substantial asset base, operational footprint and decarbonisation credentials to grow earnings further, with a medium-term total adjusted operating profit margin target of 8-9% and a lower level of capital expenditure. H1 2027 financial overview The division has reported total adjusted revenue of £260.9m in H1 2027 (H1 2026: £130.9m), which included revenue from the London Overground that the Group started to operate in May 2026. In Open Access, Hull Trains and Lumo delivered revenue of £60.2m, up 13% against the prior year, boosted by the new Stirling service that was fully operational in August 2026, contributing revenue of just over £6m during the period. In line with the Government’s announced policy to bring the National Rail Contracts into public ownership at the earliest possible opportunity, the DfTO took over the operation of SWR on 25 May 2025. Net attributable fees earned by the Group from the DfT TOCs in H1 2027 were £12.8m after the non-controlling interest of £1.6m. Mistral Data contributed adjusted operating profit of £1.7m in H1 2027 (H1 2026: £1.6m). The sale of Mistral Data was completed in August 2026 and will result in an adjusting profit item of £46.3m in FY 2027. The division’s adjusted operating profit of £67.0m was in line with the prior year (H1 2026: £66.6m). This included DfT TOC IFRS 16 accounting adjustments of £18m, following finalisation of the GWR National Rail Contract end date, which offset the transition of SWR, sustained price competition on the East Coast Main Line in Open Access and launch and ramp up costs of c.£8.0m incurred for the launch and ramp up of the new Stirling service. Additional costs in Open Access also included a scheduled c.£1.8m increase in Lumo’s East Coast infrastructure charge, now at its highest contracted level, having reached the fifth year of operation in October 2025. DfT TOC IFRS 16 lease liabilities recognised on the balance sheet at the end of H1 2027 were £124.8m (H1 2026: £828.1m). Open Access financial performance impacted by Stirling launch and ramp up costs and sustained price competition on East Coast Main Line H1 2027 has been another important period in Open Access, with the launch and ramp up of our new Lumo Stirling service. We have reported a 31% increase in total Open Access passenger journeys, to 1.87m during the period. H1 2027 seat miles utilisation of 68% in the East Coast Main Line operations remains well above the long-distance industry average of 45%. Lumo has successfully launched on the West Coast Main Line In May 2026 we launched our new Lumo service that was fully operational in August, linking Stirling to London Euston. The service calls at Larbert, Greenfaulds, Whifflet, Motherwell, Lockerbie, Carlisle, Preston, Crewe, Nuneaton and Milton Keynes. For Whifflet, Greenfaulds and Larbert it provides the first direct rail services to London. We have a new Lumo West Coast headquarters in Preston which serves as our main operational centre, creating local employment and entry points for people who may not have previously considered the rail sector. We have already created around 100 jobs, including Level 3 Train Driver apprenticeships, instructor roles, qualified driver positions and approximately 50 Customer Experience Ambassador roles across the route. Growing our Open Access reach, driving benefits for our customers and communities In the last year we have grown our Open Access capacity by c.50% and remain on course to nearly double our capacity over the next few years, underpinned by our c.£500m lease agreement with Hitachi for 14 new electric, battery or bi-mode trains. This follows the award of additional paths on the East Coast Main Line, the extension of some Lumo services to Glasgow, the launch of our new Stirling service. Looking ahead, FY 2029 will see further growth in capacity with the introduction of our new Carmarthen service in the summer of 2028 and ten-car services on certain Lumo East Coast services. During H1 2027 we continued to test how Open Access can add capacity in response to strong demand. In July 2026, Lumo operated a 10-coach service between Edinburgh, Newcastle and London, the highest capacity domestic intercity train operated in the UK that weekend. We were disappointed that our revised application for new Lumo service between London and Rochdale was declined by the ORR. We believed our proposal demonstrated a workable solution and strong economic benefits for passengers and communities in Manchester and the North West. We still have applications under consideration by the ORR, including expanding our existing Carmarthen track access rights to add new services from Paignton to London and Hereford to London, an extension of our current track access rights for the Stirling service to the late 2030s with the addition of five new, battery electric trains and for a new route between Cardiff and York via Birmingham, Derby and Sheffield from December 2028 to December 2033. Discussions on these applications continue with the ORR and Network Rail, supported by detailed business case and performance modelling conducted by our internal teams and third-party experts. Looking ahead, as Great British Railways is established, we firmly believe there is a continued role for private sector operators in the future railway, with competition on a level playing field. Operating without Government subsidy, Lumo and Hull Trains continue to demonstrate the unique contribution that open access rail can make to the UK’s transport network. By increasing connectivity between major cities, regional centres and underserved communities, combining commercial innovation with a strong customer focus, they are bringing significant benefits to passengers and local communities, as well as material benefits to HM Treasury. Mobilising the London Overground operation We took over the operation of the London Overground in May 2026 and now have a highly experienced team in place, working to restore and improve operational performance levels. Initiatives include a step change in driver recruitment and training to ensure all depots are fully staffed moving forwards. We are confident that our deep sector expertise and long-standing relationship with TfL, having operated London Trams since 2000 and the London Cableway since 2024, will allow us to strengthen performance and deliver a range of benefits across this vital network. Leveraging our expertise to unlock value in Rail Services H1 2027 has been a pivotal period in our Rail Services, with the sale of Mistral Data to Tracsis plc for c.£48m. We established Mistral Data to develop and provide complementary software solutions to the rail industry and grew revenues from c.£7m in FY 2022, to c.£13m in FY 2026, with operating profit of c.£4m. Revenues in our remaining Rail Services businesses, First Customer Contact (FCC) and First Rail Consultancy increased by 24% in H1 2027 and we continued to secure contract extensions and new external customers. In FCC, we have secured a DfT TOC contract extension and continued to develop our AI portfolio, tailored to our rail clients. First Rail Consultancy has continued to diversify its client portfolio beyond First Rail, with client wins including two major passenger rail operators. Work to support a strategically important freight programme for Network Rail has continued and work with a major airport on its Surface Access Strategy has been extended. Going forward, there is a significant pipeline of projects, particularly in rail operations and engineering. Supporting the transition of the DfT TOCs We now have confirmed dates for the transfer of both GWR and WCP to the DfTO, on 13 December 2026 and 7 March 2027 respectively. Our teams are working hard with the DfT and DfTO to ensure smooth and orderly transitions for customers, colleagues and stakeholders. We are very grateful to everyone at GWR and Avanti for their dedication, expertise and hard work. Their contribution has been central to the success and significant contribution they have made throughout the Group’s stewardship of these operations. Having been part of FirstGroup for 30 years, Great Western Railway (GWR) has built a strong track record of innovation, operational excellence and investment in its people. The company revived the historic Great Western Railway name in 2015, reinforcing its heritage while continuing to modernise the railway. GWR is recognised as one of the UK’s Top 100 Apprenticeship Employers and has achieved Platinum status in the Investors in People ‘We Invest in Apprentices’ accreditation. In 2026, it launched the UK railway’s first dedicated Security Operations Centre and introduced the UK’s first battery-only train in passenger service, demonstrating its commitment to safety, sustainability and innovation. This builds on significant fleet investment over the past decade, including the introduction of the Intercity Express Trains and the ongoing rollout of the refurbished Class 175 fleet. Avanti West Coast has established a strong track record of innovation, customer service and social impact. The operator has delivered significant fleet upgrades, including the introduction of the Evero trains and the £117m upgrade of its Pendolino fleet, while also enhancing the customer experience through the launch of the rail industry’s first Standard Premium travel class and the Club Avanti rewards programme, which now has more than one million members. Beyond its operational achievements, Avanti West Coast generates substantial economic and social value, delivering £470m of annual social value and contributing more than £5 billion to the UK economy. The company has also demonstrated its commitment to creating an inclusive workplace, earning bronze accreditation through the Inclusive Employers Standard. Looking ahead For the 2027 full year, in Open Access we anticipate revenues of c.£130m, with a lower level of Stirling ramp up losses in H2. Looking ahead, we will see a further increase in capacity in FY 2029 as our new Carmarthen service is expected to begin operations in the summer of 2028 and with the addition of ten-car operations on certain Lumo East Coast Main Line services. The total adjusted operating profit margin in Open Access is expected to progress to mid-teens following a c.two-year mobilisation and ramp up period in the Stirling and Carmarthen operations. The London Overground will add anticipated revenue of c.£300m in FY 2027, progressing to a mid-single digit operating profit as we improve performance over the next 12 months. Earnings in the DfT TOCs will reflect the transfer of GWR on 13 December 2026 and WCP on 7 March 2027, a c.£7m earnings reduction after tax and non-controlling interests. As the DfT TOCs transition, we anticipate a cash inflow of c.£70-80m, after any further reorganisation cash costs the Group may incur, from October 2026, with cash received from the management fees a year in arrears. Under the terms of the First Rail contractual agreements with the DfT, cash can only be distributed by the TOCs either up to the lower amount of their retained profits or the amount determined by prescribed liquidity ratios. The ring-fenced cash represents that which is not yet approved for distribution while prior year fees are being finalised, or the amount required to satisfy the liquidity ratios at the balance sheet date. The London Overground concession also holds ring-fenced cash insofar as it is available for use by the Overground operation, but not by the wider Group until it has been distributed by First Rail London. Interest rate risk Exposure to floating interest rates is managed to ensure that at least 50% (but at no time more than 100%) of the Group's pre-IFRS 16 gross debt is fixed rate for the medium term. Fuel and electricity price risk We use a progressive forward hedging programme to manage commodity risk. As at November 2026, 83% of our ‘at risk’ First Bus diesel requirements for H2 2027 was hedged at an average rate of 42.8p per litre, 68% of our requirements for the year to the end of March 2028 at 43.3p per litre, and 3% of our requirements for the year to the end of March 2029 at 48.1p per litre. We also have an electricity hedge programme in place, with 87% of our consumption (based on current consumption forecasts) hedged for H2 2027 at £79/MWh, c.75% for FY 2028 at £73/MWh and 55% for FY 2029 at £70/MWh. Foreign currency risk ‘Certain’ and ‘highly probable’ foreign currency transaction exposures (including fuel purchases for the UK divisions) may be hedged at the time the exposure arises for up to two years at specified levels, or longer if there is a very high degree of certainty. The Group does not hedge the translation of earnings into the Group reporting currency but accepts that reported Group earnings will fluctuate as exchange rates against pounds Sterling fluctuate for the currencies in which the Group does business, although this exposure is materially reduced following the sales of the North American divisions. During the year, the net cash generated in each currency may be converted by Group Treasury into pounds Sterling by way of spot transactions in order to keep the currency composition of net debt broadly constant.
