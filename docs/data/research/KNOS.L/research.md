# Kainos Group plc (KNOS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T06:48:31.562792+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Kainos is a UK-headquartered IT services and Workday-ecosystem software group whose FY26 results (year ended 31 March 2026) show a clear inflection: revenue up 17% to £431.1m, bookings up 32%, and backlog at a record £433.9m, following a difficult FY25 that included restructuring. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on quality, GARP, dividend and financial-health metrics (PEG 0.21, ROE 35.6%, 3.2% yield), though headline multiples (P/E 26.6, P/B 10.9) are not classic deep value. The central debate is whether Workday Products’ SaaS scaling (ARR £89.0m, targeting £100m by end-2026) and public-sector contract wins can sustain growth while adjusted margins recover from 16% (FY25: 18%) after a contractor-heavy delivery ramp. Filings confirm a debt-free balance sheet and strong cash conversion, but net cash fell to £89.1m after £90m of buybacks over 18 months.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy rating rests on a rare combination for UK mid-cap tech: **profitable growth at reasonable PEG**, **high returns on equity**, **progressive dividends**, and **balance-sheet strength**—all validated in primary filings rather than inferred.

**Business quality.** Kainos operates three complementary divisions: Digital Services (custom digital platforms, heavily public sector and healthcare), Workday Services (consulting partner), and Workday Products (subscription SaaS on Workday’s platform). FY26 revenue was diversified across commercial (47%), public (35%) and healthcare (18%) customers, with 86% of revenue from existing clients—evidence of recurring relationships the screen’s Economic Moat and Quality Value models likely capture.

**Quantitative alignment.** The screen passes Lynch PEG (PEG 0.21), Neff PEGY (0.06), Quality Value, Economic Moat, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Earnings Quality and Financial Health—five factor families in total. Reported growth metrics (screen: 128.9% earnings growth basis) align with FY26 statutory diluted EPS rising 24% to 35.1p and adjusted diluted EPS up 7% to 41.1p (Full Year Results, 18 May 2026). ROE of 35.6% and FCF of £49.7m (screening snapshot; filing operating cash flow £57.7m) support the Financial Health pass.

**Value investor hook.** This is not a low P/B asset play (P/B 10.9). The case is **GARP on a recovery**: FY25 saw revenue fall 4% to £367.2m and adjusted PBT drop 15% to £65.6m amid restructuring (£8.4m charge, ~7% headcount reduction per FY25 annual report extract); FY26 revenue re-accelerated 17% while adjusted PBT grew only 2% to £67.1m as margins compressed. If contractor usage normalises (management guides margin improvement in H2 FY27), earnings could grow faster than revenue—supporting the screen’s low PEG despite elevated absolute multiples.

**Capital allocation.** Progressive dividend (29.6p total, +4%) with 70% payout of adjusted profit after tax, plus disciplined buybacks (£90m returned over 18 months), signals shareholder orientation without leverage risk (no debt per FY26 balance sheet).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Margin and delivery execution.** FY26 adjusted margin fell to 16% as contractor costs rose to £18.5m (from £4.5m) and third-party supplier costs to £30.1m (from £14.7m). Management expects improvement as contractors convert to permanent staff by H2 FY27; failure to execute would leave profit lagging revenue growth.

**Public-sector and healthcare concentration.** ~53% of revenue from public and healthcare customers (FY26). Contract awards with Home Office, NHS England, DVSA and others create visibility but expose the group to UK government fiscal priorities, procurement cycles and political change—cyclical and regulatory risk not fully captured by quantitative screens.

**Workday platform dependency.** Workday Services and Products together represent 44% of FY26 revenue and are tied to Workday Inc.’s ecosystem, partner terms and the Built on Workday partnership (£7.8m annual fees per FY26 filing). Strategic partnership concentration is a competitive and customer-concentration risk.

**AI governance.** The Full Year Results reference “unsafe use of AI” as a principal risk; Responsible AI team being doubled. Public-sector AI deployment adds regulatory and reputational exposure.

**Pricing model evolution.** Workday Products charges by user numbers; filing notes AI may reduce headcount growth at customers, requiring pricing model adaptation—potential revenue-per-customer pressure.

**Customer satisfaction.** Net Promoter Score declined to 61 from 70 (still “excellent” by Bain thresholds, but directionally weaker).

**Key person transition.** Malachy Smith, who built Workday Products, transitions to an advisory role in June 2026; division leadership restructured with external hires—execution risk during ARR scale-up to £100m.

**Balance sheet erosion from capital returns.** Net assets fell from £138.0m to £100.5m; cash down £44.6m in FY26 despite 99% cash conversion. Not a liquidity crisis (still £89.1m net cash, no debt) but reduces buffer.

**Governance.** Non-executive Katie Davis (Remuneration Committee chair) steps down at September 2026 AGM after ~seven years (RNS, 28 April 2026). Routine succession, but RemCo chair turnover warrants monitoring.

**Short interest.** Citadel Advisors held short positions up to 0.62% (May–June 2026) but **no active short position** as of June 2026 per Investegate extract—not a current red flag.

**Going concern.** Parent interim filings prepared on going-concern basis (standard boilerplate); consolidated FY26 auditor report described as unqualified with no emphasis of matter (Full Year Results footnote)—no going-concern qualification identified in available extracts.

**Pension / litigation / covenants.** No material pension deficit, litigation or covenant language identified in available filing bodies; Annual Report 2026 RNS (24 July 2026) contained no risk disclosures—full statutory risk section not separately indexed.
