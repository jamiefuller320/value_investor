# Kainos Group plc (KNOS.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T11:19:01.534080+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Kainos is a UK-listed digital services and Workday-ecosystem software business whose FY26 results show a clear reacceleration: revenue rose 17% to £431.1m, bookings grew 32%, and contracted backlog reached £433.9m, with management subsequently raising FY27 guidance. The quantitative screen rates the name a **hold** (6/22 models; composite 41%, sector-relative 51%), reflecting strong quality and cash-generation signals offset by rich absolute valuation (P/E 34.5, P/B 14.2, FCF yield ~3.2%). For a value investor, the debate is whether durable franchise quality—Workday Products ARR at £89.0m, public-sector and NHS contract wins, debt-free balance sheet—justifies paying a growth multiple after a 36% premium to the 200-day moving average. Operational momentum supports the business case; valuation and margin normalisation remain the constraints on upgrading beyond hold.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The hold rating is appropriate for a value-oriented process. Kainos passes four screening families—cheapness, quality, GARP, and risk—via models such as Neff PEGY (PEGY 0.14, aided by a 2.4% yield), Quality Value, Economic Moat, Magic Formula, Earnings Quality, and Financial Health. Yet it fails 16 of 22 models, including Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Deep Value, FCF Yield, Buffett Quality (P/E too high), Lynch PEG (statutory growth ~2.8%), and Piotroski F-Score (6/9). That pattern describes a **quality compounder trading above classic value thresholds**, not a mispriced deep-value candidate.

Business quality is genuine. Filings show FY26 adjusted pre-tax profit of £67.1m on £431.1m revenue, 99% cash conversion, no debt, and FCF of approximately £49.7m (operating cash flow £57.7m less capex £8.0m per the FY26 RNS). Workday Products ARR grew 23% to £89.0m; Digital Services rebounded (+23% revenue) on NHS and UK government wins; Workday Services returned to growth (+9%). The August 2026 trading update guides FY27 revenue and adjusted PBT **comfortably ahead** of consensus (£509.3m revenue; £77.1m adjusted PBT).

However, adjusted profit margin compressed from 18% to 16% as contractor and third-party costs rose sharply (£18.5m and £30.1m respectively), and the group returned £55.7m via buybacks while net assets fell from £138.0m to £100.5m. ROE of 35.6% is elevated partly because equity has been reduced. Timing is neutral (RSI 68; price extended above the 200-day MA). A value investor can acknowledge franchise strength and backlog visibility without finding sufficient margin of safety at current multiples—a textbook **hold**: own for quality if already held; do not chase for deep-value entry.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Workday ecosystem dependency.** Workday Products and Workday Services together represent ~44% of FY26 revenue. The strategic partnership carries annual fees of approximately £7.8m (FY26 first full year). Product roadmap, pricing models, and go-to-market remain tied to Workday's platform decisions; leadership transition in Workday Products (Malachy Smith to advisory role; Derek Brown joining June 2026) adds execution risk during a scaling phase toward £100m ARR.

**Public-sector and healthcare concentration.** Filings attribute ~35% of revenue to public sector and ~18% to healthcare (FY26). Contract wins with NHS England, Home Office, and DVSA underpin growth but expose the group to UK fiscal cycles, procurement delays, and political shifts (e.g. NHS England reorganisation—management notes no disruption to awards to date).

**Margin and cost-structure pressure.** Adjusted margin fell 200bp to 16%. Contractor costs rose £14.0m year-on-year; third-party supplier costs £15.4m; employers' NI +£3.0m; performance bonuses +£11.5m. Management expects margin recovery as contractors are replaced with permanent hires in H2 FY27—this remains unproven in filings.

**Valuation and capital allocation.** Net assets fell 27% to £100.5m after £90.0m of buybacks over 18 months. High ROE partly reflects a shrinking equity base. At P/E 34.5 and EV/EBIT 25.4 (screen inputs), the stock fails deep-value and acquirer's-multiple screens; further buybacks at elevated prices may destroy per-share value if growth slows.

**AI governance and competitive risk.** FY26 results cite "unsafe use of AI" as a principal risk; the Responsible AI team is being doubled. AI-related Digital Services revenue (£45.8m, +11%) grew slower than the division overall—monetisation of the AI narrative is still emerging.

**Governance.** Katie Davis (Independent NED, Remuneration Committee chair) will step down at the September 2026 AGM after nearly seven years. Shruthi Chindalur joined as NED in September 2025. No material governance red flags in available filings.

**Other.** Receivables/accruals growth (+54%) warrants scrutiny. Citadel Advisors disclosed a short position (July 2026 news). Macro volatility acknowledged in trading updates; filings cite a debt-free balance sheet and strong backlog as mitigants. No pension deficit or going-concern issues identified in available bodies.
