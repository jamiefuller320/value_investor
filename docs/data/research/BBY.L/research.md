# Balfour Beatty plc (BBY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T06:52:48.831857+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Balfour Beatty is a UK-listed international infrastructure contractor with a transformed balance sheet, a record £22.9bn order book, and earnings momentum across UK power transmission, US buildings, and support services. The quantitative screen flags a **buy** on cheapness (FCF yield ~14.7%), quality (Piotroski pass, ROE 23.1%), dividend growth, and GARP (PEG ~0.15), though conviction is modest at 35% given the signal is new. H1 2026 results (12 Aug 2026) beat prior guidance: underlying profit from operations rose to £119m (+55% YoY) and full-year earnings-based PFO growth guidance was upgraded to low double digits. The central debate is whether exceptional net cash (~£1.6bn average, recourse basis) and infrastructure tailwinds (UK energy, defence, US data centres) justify a premium P/B (~3.7x) after the shares reached a record high on the interim release, versus cyclical contract risk, legacy US civils exposure, and ongoing Building Safety Act provisions.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, BBY offers an unusual combination: **contractor cyclicality with investment-grade-like liquidity**. The screen passes six of 22 models across five factor families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk), with full data quality (20/20 metrics). That alignment is rare in construction.

**Business quality supports the screen:**

- **Cash and capital return:** FY2025 average net cash (recourse) was £1,212m per the H1 2026 filing; H1 2026 average net cash reached **£1,616m**, with period-end recourse net cash of **£1,708m**. A **£200m** 2026 buyback is underway (£102m completed in H1); interim dividend raised **12%** to **4.7p**.
- **Order book visibility:** **£22.7bn** at FY2025 (Companies House 2025 ARA extract), **£22.9bn** at H1 2026 — roughly **2x** annual revenue, skewed to public sector and regulated clients (85% of UK Construction orders from public/regulated; 85% on target-cost or cost-plus terms per H1 filing).
- **Earnings recovery:** Underlying PFO from earnings-based businesses rose **42%** in H1 2026 to **£153m**; US Construction swung from an **£11m** H1 2025 loss to **£22m** profit; Support Services PFO margin expanded to **9.1%**.
- **Hidden asset:** Directors' valuation of the Infrastructure Investments portfolio held at **£1.1bn** (65% US-weighted), including military housing and student accommodation — not fully reflected in book value given intangible-heavy statutory accounts.

**Valuation hook:** At P/E ~16.5x on recovered earnings and FCF yield ~14.7% (screen; Yahoo FCF £607m for FY2025), the market prices BBY as a quality compounder rather than a distressed contractor — which matches the post-transformation profile under the "Build to Last" / "Evolve, Energise, Explore" strategy launched March 2026.

**Why buy vs. pass:** The screen's FCF and earnings-quality signals are corroborated by filing-level cash generation and order-book depth. The main pushback is not balance-sheet quality but **timing** (record share price, neutral RSI ~48) and **cyclical re-rating risk** if UK/US infrastructure spending slows.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / contract execution:** Construction remains inherently cyclical. US Civils (9% of US Construction revenue) suffered a significant H1 2025 write-down on a Texas highways JV; H1 2026 improved but final close-out and cost recoveries remain outstanding. UK transport large-scheme timing (e.g. Lower Thames Crossing) is uncertain per company disclosure.

**Regulatory / legacy liabilities:** H1 2026 non-underlying charge of **£9m** for Building Safety Act claims (provision increased following reassessments). BSA exposure is an ongoing tail risk for UK construction peers.

**US military housing:** Independent compliance monitorship of Balfour Beatty Communities concluded Jun 2026 — a positive milestone — but the segment incurred **£12m** pre-disposal operating losses in H1 from monitor and legal costs. Reputational and contractual risk in US government housing persists even post-monitorship.

**Governance / key person:** Planned CFO transition (Philip Harrison → Myles Westcott, effective post-AGM May 2026) and board refresh (Barbara Moorhouse stepping down; Melody Meyer and Sandra Stash appointed Jul 2026). CEO Philip Hoare (appointed Sep 2025) is early in tenure; transition risk is moderate given advisory overlap.

**Pension:** BBPF largely de-risked (surplus, journey plan, no further contributions expected), but **RPS triennial valuation** remains unresolved — a potential future cash call if funding deteriorates.

**Competitive:** UK infrastructure bidding is disciplined but competitive (Morgan Sindall, Kier, etc.). Order quality and selective bidding are explicit mitigants; 85% target-cost/cost-plus in UK Construction limits fixed-price blow-ups.

**Accounting / valuation:** High goodwill and intangibles (**£1,075m** per Yahoo FY2025) vs. tangible book **£68m** — common in contractors with acquisitions and concessions. Directors' valuation of Investments (**£1.1bn**) uses DCF/broker methods and can diver materially from IFRS carrying values.

**Liquidity / leverage:** Recourse net cash is a strength, not a red flag; non-recourse borrowings within Infrastructure Investments were **£487m** at H1 2026. Going concern assessment is clean; RCF undrawn.
