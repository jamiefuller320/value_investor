# Balfour Beatty plc (BBY.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-02T08:32:27.470092+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Balfour Beatty is a UK-listed international infrastructure group benefiting from a record £22.9bn order book, secular demand in UK energy, defence and transport, and a recovering US buildings franchise. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (6/22 models; filing-aligned FCF yield ~14.6%), though conviction is still low at 34% given the signal is new. Filings show accelerating momentum: FY2025 underlying profit from operations (PFO) rose to £252m on revenue of £10.8bn, and H1 2026 underlying PFO jumped 55% to £119m with guidance raised. The valuation hook is strong cash generation (recourse net cash £1.7bn at HY2026) and capital returns (14.0p FY2025 dividend, £200m 2026 buyback) against a mid-teens P/E. The key debate is whether margin improvement in US construction and power transmission is durable, or whether cyclical execution risk, legacy provisions and a thinly margin US civils book cap re-rating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, BBY offers a rare combination in UK construction: visible multi-year revenue from a high-quality order book, net cash rather than net debt, and shareholder returns funded by operations rather than leverage. The screen passes FCF Yield, Lynch PEG (0.22), Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Piotroski F-Score and Earnings Quality — five distinct quality/cash families alongside cheapness — which aligns with filing evidence of improving earnings quality: underlying EPS rose from 43.6p (FY2024) to 47.6p (FY2025) and reached 21.7p in H1 2026 alone (vs 14.4p prior-year half).

Business quality is better than sector stereotypes suggest. Roughly 85% of the UK construction order book is public-sector or regulated-industry work, and 85% is on target-cost or cost-plus terms — structures that limit fixed-price blow-ups. Support Services (power transmission) delivered a 9.1% PFO margin in H1 2026 on 10% revenue growth, reflecting recurring framework revenue. US Buildings (91% of US Construction revenue) is scaling profitably; US Construction returned to £22m PFO in H1 2026 from an £11m loss. The £1.1bn Infrastructure Investments portfolio (Directors' valuation) adds optionality, with US military housing monitorship concluded in June 2026 removing a drag on sentiment and costs.

Capital allocation reinforces the case: five consecutive years of buybacks, a 12% interim dividend increase to 4.7p, and £102m repurchased in H1 2026. At 17.0x P/E, 3.8x P/B and 1.6% yield (screen metrics), the market prices BBY as a cyclical mid-cap rather than an infrastructure compounder with £1.6bn+ average net cash — a gap the screen correctly flags.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Contract execution and cyclicality.** Construction remains inherently cyclical. US Civils (9% of US Construction revenue) produced only 0.9% group-level margin despite improvement; a Texas highways joint venture required a significant H1 2025 write-down, with final close-out and cost recoveries still pending. UK transport large-scheme timing (e.g. Lower Thames Crossing awaiting private finance) adds revenue visibility risk.

**Building Safety Act.** H1 2026 recognised a **£9m** increase to the Building Safety Act provision (non-underlying), following prior-year charges. UK residential exposure warrants monitoring.

**US military housing legacy.** The DOJ compliance monitorship concluded on 6 June 2026, but Infrastructure Investments still carried **£584m** of US military housing in the £1.1bn Directors' valuation. Monitor and legal costs drove a £12m pre-disposal operating loss in H1; management expects improvement in H2.

**Pension.** BBPF is in surplus on agreed terms, but RPS triennial valuation is unresolved. Surplus-sharing mechanics could require repayments if funding falls below thresholds.

**Governance and management transition.** Group CFO Philip Harrison stepped down on 7 May 2026 after 10+ years; Myles Westcott appointed. Non-executive Barbara Moorhouse departs 31 May 2026. Transitions appear orderly but add transition risk at a period of guidance upgrades.

**Macro and inflation.** The May 2026 trading update cites protection from "volatile macroeconomic backdrop and related inflationary pressures" via contract mix — an acknowledgment of the risk rather than immunity.

**Balance sheet / liquidity.** Going concern confirmed in H1 2026 after stress-testing; RCF refinanced to **£515m** (September 2031). Negative working capital of 19.1% of revenue is a feature of advance-payment contracts but concentrates counterparty and completion risk.

**Competitive.** UK construction is fragmented; order quality and selective bidding are management's stated edge, but margin pressure in Gammon (2.2% H1 margin vs 3.1% prior year) shows competition is not absent.
