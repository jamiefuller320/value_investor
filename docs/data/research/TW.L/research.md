# Taylor Wimpey plc (TW.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T17:32:24.002234+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Taylor Wimpey plc (TW.L) screens as a buy on deep value, dividend yield, GARP and balance-sheet metrics (13/22 models; composite 69%; P/E 11.8, P/B 0.7, yield 5.0%). The investment case rests on a net-cash housebuilder trading below tangible book (TNAV 117.6p per FY2025 results) with a large owned landbank (~77k plots) and a policy to return at least 7.5% of net assets annually. Adjusted operating profit has held near £416–421m across 2024–25 despite margin compression (10.9% vs 13.4% in 2023), but statutory profit fell to £100.4m after £243.8m of cladding and CMA exceptional charges. The central debate is whether discounted valuation and balance-sheet strength outweigh cyclical demand weakness, rising remediation cash needs (~£150m guided for 2026), and free cash flow that lagged distributions in FY2025 (£126.5m FCF vs ~£322m returned). No interim or trading-update filings are present in the research index; H1 2026 operational detail is therefore unverified from primary sources.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Taylor Wimpey offers a classic cyclical deep-value setup: the quantitative screen passes Graham Defensive, Schloss low P/B, deep value, earnings yield, high dividend yield and financial health models, with current ratio 4.99 and minimal leverage (net cash £342.6m at 31 December 2025; £600m undrawn RCF). Business quality indicators from filings are respectable rather than exceptional: five-star HBF customer service (4.24), industry-leading NHBC CQR (4.96), and completions growth to 11,229 (+6%) in 2025. Land cost embedded at ~12.7% of ASP supports medium-term margin recovery if volumes scale toward the 14,000-home target. The screen’s buy signal is supported by tangible asset backing and disciplined capital allocation history (over £2.8bn returned since 2018), but earnings quality is impaired by recurring cladding provisions and FCF that does not fully fund the stated distribution framework — the screen’s FCF basis overlay (filing £126.5m vs TTM £276.8m) flags this explicitly. At current prices slightly below the 200-day moving average (RSI ~46), timing is neutral rather than catalyst-driven.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical housing demand.** Order book at 1 March 2026 was £2,182m / 7,678 homes vs £2,283m / 8,097 homes prior year (FY2025 RNS). Net private sales rate 0.74/week; cancellation rate 14%. FY2026 volume and margin guidance embed softer pricing. Principal risks B (mortgage availability) and C (build costs) rated moderate residual.

**Cladding and regulatory remediation.** FY2025 exceptional cladding charge £225.8m (cavity barriers behind brickwork/render); cumulative provision utilisation £49.4m in year. Guided 2026 cash spend ~£150m. Building Safety Levy implementation deferred to October 2026 with mostly post-2029 cash impact per filing. Principal risk A (government policies/regulations) increased in 2025.

**Governance / competition.** CMA information-sharing investigation closed 30 October 2025 without infringement finding; Taylor Wimpey paid £15.8m toward Affordable Homes Programme plus fees (£18.0m exceptional). Voluntary commitments restrict certain information exchange.

**Distribution sustainability.** FY2025 FCF of £126.5m fell materially short of ~£322m returned; net cash fell £222m. Distribution policy unchanged at 7.5% of net assets or £250m minimum, but flexibility via buybacks may mask dividend pressure in downturns. Screen flags FCF yield and earnings quality failures.

**Margin and cost trajectory.** Adjusted margin down 260bps over two years; FY2026 guided lower again (~£400m on similar revenue base). Low single-digit build-cost inflation persists; employer NI and wage pressures cited in FY2024 filing.

**Going concern / viability (FY2025 RNS).** Directors confirm going concern with liquidity and covenant headroom; five-year viability stress test (10% volume decline, 10% price cut, £150m one-off cost in 2026) absorbed by £430m cash plus £600m RCF. No covenant breach language identified.

**Pension.** Funding surplus but IFRIC 14 balance-sheet liability; contingent PFP payments from 2029 — manageable near term, not immaterial long term.

**Data gap.** Absence of indexed interim/H1 2026 filing limits verification of recent distribution cuts and completion downgrades reported in news.
