# BP p.l.c. (BP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T06:42:47.245694+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
BP p.l.c. screens as a **buy** on cheapness, dividend yield (~4.8%), FCF yield, and quality metrics (P/E 20.2, P/B 1.9, D/E 95%), with neutral timing. The investment case rests on a cyclically elevated earnings window, a credible balance-sheet repair plan (net debt target $14–18bn by end-2027), and a ~4.8% dividend supported by operating cash flow. Reported IFRS earnings remain distorted by impairments and inventory effects — underlying replacement cost (RC) profit is the better operating lens. The central debate is whether new CEO Meg O’Neill’s “smaller, simpler, more profitable” reset can close the gap to Shell and peers, or whether governance upheaval (chair removed May 2026), suspended buybacks, and commodity cyclicality cap re-rating. Filing coverage is adequate on annuals and recent half-year results; formal interim filings are absent from the index.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags BP across five factor families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with 10 of 22 models passing and a composite score of 57% (sector-relative 56%). For a value investor, the hook is not headline P/E on reported earnings (which collapse in impairment years) but **cash-backed valuation**: underlying RC profit of $7.5bn in FY2025 (annual report) against net debt of $22.2bn, operating cash flow of $24.5bn, and a dividend that management continues to grow (2Q26 dividend raised 4% to 8.66 cents). At ~P/B 1.9 and yield ~4.8%, the market prices BP as a structurally challenged supermajor rather than an integrated cash generator — a gap the screen exploits.

Business quality is mixed but not absent. Upstream reliability exceeded 96% in 2025; 1H 2026 underlying RC profit of $8.9bn already exceeds full-year 2025 underlying RC ($7.5bn), driven by stronger refining margins and oil realisations. The $20bn divestment programme (Castrol 65% sale to Stonepeak for ~$6bn proceeds, Gelsenkirchen refinery, North Sea exit, Archaea Energy sale process) targets net debt of $14–18bn and structural cost savings of $6.5–7.5bn by 2027. If proceeds land and capex stays disciplined ($13.5–14.0bn guided for 2026), FCF should cover the dividend with room for deleveraging — consistent with FCF Yield and High Dividend Yield screen passes.

The screen’s low conviction score (35%, new signal) is appropriate: governance turnover, earnings volatility, and cyclical exposure mean the buy case is **conditional on execution**, not a deep-value floor.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Governance:** The board **removed Chair Albert Manifold with immediate effect** on 26 May 2026, citing “serious concerns” over governance standards, oversight, and conduct (RNS). Ian Tyler appointed interim chair; permanent succession underway. This follows a CEO transition (Meg O’Neill from 1 Apr 2026) and director departures in April 2026. Governance instability is a material overlay on a strategy reset already in flux.

**Cyclical / commodity:** ~2.2mmboe/d upstream production and large refining/trading exposure make earnings highly sensitive to oil, gas, and margin cycles. 1H 2026 strength partly reflects geopolitical disruption (Middle East) and elevated refining margins — potentially non-recurring. 1Q26 trading statement flagged a large working capital build; cash conversion can lag earnings in volatile markets.

**Leverage / capital allocation:** Net debt $22.3bn remains above the $14–18bn 2027 target. Buybacks suspended since early 2025; dividend growth continues (+4% in 2Q26). Gulf of America settlement payments ~**$1.6bn pre-tax in 2026** (per 2Q26 guidance). Hybrid bond redemptions and divestment proceeds help, but delivery of $8–9bn 2026 divestment proceeds (including ~$6bn Castrol) is not guaranteed pending regulatory approvals.

**Impairments / accounting:** Reported profit of $0.1bn in FY2025 vs underlying RC $7.5bn highlights recurring write-offs (transition businesses, exploration). 1H 2026 net impairments **$1.2bn**. Inventory holding gains/losses create quarter-to-quarter reported volatility.

**Operational:** 2Q26 upstream plant reliability fell to **92.4%** (from 95.7% in 1Q); tier 1/2 process safety events rose to 25 in 1H26 vs 15 in 1H25. Middle East disruption affects production guidance (3Q26: 2,100–2,250mboe/d).

**Pension / provisions:** Defined benefit pension net position is mixed (surplus $7.5bn vs deficit $4.1bn at 30 Jun 2026). Long-term provisions **$15.9bn** non-current. Gelsenkirchen sale transfers pension obligations to buyer (per Mar 2026 RNS).

**Regulatory / litigation:** Ongoing Gulf of America liabilities; UK North Sea exit and Castrol divestment subject to regulatory approval. Geopolitical exposure (Middle East operations, Iran conflict oil price spike noted in news flow).

**Strategy execution:** Pivot from “integrated energy company” to upstream/downstream simplification requires divesting Castrol, North Sea, Archaea, and multiple refineries while maintaining dividend — execution risk is high if commodity prices revert.

Going concern: Deloitte’s 3 Aug 2026 interim review found **no material going-concern uncertainties**.
