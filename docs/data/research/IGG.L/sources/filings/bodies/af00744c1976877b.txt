The event will set out the priorities arising from the strategic review announced on 19 March 2026, alongside its maintained medium-term guidance and an updated capital allocation framework. Further to the trading update of 2 October 2026, the Group is also providing product and divisional commentary on Q3 2026 trading. Breon Corcoran, CEO, said: “Trading, investing and entertainment are converging, and IG is well placed to capitalise on the opportunity. Today we will set out how we will deliver our next phase of growth - broadening and enriching our propositions, investing in our brands and increasing efficiency. These priorities build on our current plan, which has driven stronger active customer growth, and underpin my confidence that we will create significant shareholder value in the coming years.” Event details The Capital Markets Event will be held at UBS, 5 Broadgate, London EC2M 2QS from 14:00 UK time. Highlights • Vision: to build a leading international platform for active investors at the convergence of trading, investing and entertainment, a large, fast-growing market with strong structural tailwinds. • Priorities: to deliver the next phase of growth by broadening and enriching IG’s propositions, investing in our brands and increasing efficiency. • Outcomes of the strategic review: – The proposed redomicile of the Group to Jersey is expected to become effective in late 2026 or early 2027, subject to Court approval and other conditions. – IG has refreshed its organisational model around three divisions: IG Consumer, IG Securities and tastytrade. – The proposed acquisition of Underdog, a US fantasy sports and prediction markets operator, which is on track to complete in late 2026 or early 2027. 2026 guidance (unchanged from IG’s Q3 trading update issued on 2 October 2026): • Total revenue growth in a mid-single-digit per cent range year-on-year. • Group EBITDA margin in the low-40s per cent range, excluding approximately £30 million of non-recurring redomicile and restructuring costs, as well as expenses related to the acquisition of Underdog, which are contingent on closing. Medium-term guidance excluding Underdog (unchanged): • Organic total revenue growth of 10%+ CAGR, from a 2025 base of approximately £1,100 million. • Group EBITDA margin in the mid-40s per cent range. • This guidance reflects IG’s expectations for performance over time, not for any specific year. Pro forma Group guidance including Underdog (unchanged): • Underdog’s performance and strategy were covered at IG’s investor webinar on 8 October 2026. A recording is available at www.iggroup.com/investor-relations . • On completion, Underdog is expected to lift the combined Group’s medium-term revenue growth rate above IG’s standalone organic rate, driven by its stronger double-digit revenue CAGR. • The pro forma Group EBITDA margin would initially be below IG’s standalone margin and is expected to increase over the medium term as Underdog scales. Capital allocation framework (refined): • Balance sheet (refined): commitment to an investment grade credit rating. • Regular distributions (unchanged): a progressive ordinary dividend per share. • Inorganic investment (unchanged): disciplined M&A, delivering ROIC above the Group’s WACC in years three to five and adjusted EPS accretion by year three. • Additional distributions (unchanged): capital returned where not required for other priorities, with share buybacks expected to resume in 2027, subject to share price performance and other demands on capital. Key themes from today’s presentations IG Consumer • Over the past 21 months, the UK has grown active customers and net revenue around twice as fast as the rest of IG Consumer, driven by active-customer-led, multi-product growth. IG Consumer CEO, Michael Healy, is rolling this strategy out across the division’s other markets. • IG’s unified proposition, which brings trading, investing and crypto together on a single platform, is now live in the UK and will be rolled out across IG Consumer. Financial prediction markets will launch in Q4 2026 for UK professional clients. IG Consumer has also applied for a UK gambling licence and expects to broaden its markets to include politics, entertainment and sports in early 2027. • Multi-product customers are more valuable and stickier, generating around three times the average revenue per user of single-product customers, with marketing paybacks under six months and customer value over the first 36 months of their lifetime around four times acquisition cost. • AI-first servicing is reducing cost to serve, funding continued investment in product and brand. IG Securities • Enhanced market-making measures introduced in H2 2025, spanning pricing, risk management and hedging, have lifted return to volume (“RtV”), or the conversion of gross customer fee and commission income into net trading revenue, to an average of approximately 80%. Q3 2026’s RtV of 70% was within the modelled range, and the Group expects RtV to be structurally higher over time. • IG Securities is evolving from an OTC derivatives-led business into a more diversified institutional business spanning liquidity provision, prime brokerage and white label, delivered by specialist quantitative talent and built on modern trading technology. tastytrade • tastytrade has a profitable, cash-generative position in a fast-growing US options market, with room to accelerate growth by broadening its appeal to younger, mobile-first and multi-product customers. • tastytrade is developing from a niche options platform into a broader platform for active traders, including an AI-native, mobile-first app, widening its addressable market. Q3 2026 performance The Group’s Q3 trading update on 2 October 2026 covered total revenue, net trading revenue and other key performance indicators, including active customers and first trades. IG today provides its usual quarterly disclosure of performance by product and division. Group total revenue was £240.5 million, down 14% year-on-year and 21% quarter-on-quarter, within which net trading revenue of £212.3 million declined 15% year-on-year and 23% quarter-on-quarter. Stock trading and investments revenue declined 31% year-on-year. This reflected softer market conditions and reduced trading activity, including lower volumes in international stocks, as customers responded to concerns over technology and AI valuations, alongside caution ahead of potential changes to capital gains tax in the forthcoming UK Government Budget.
