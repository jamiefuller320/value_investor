The trial met its primary endpoint, demonstrating a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) as assessed by an Independent Review Committee (IRC) versus chemotherapy. In , reduced the risk of disease progression or death by 67% compared with chemotherapy, after a median of 7.1 months (HR, 0.33; 95% CI, 0.20-0.55; p<0.0001). Median PFS was 5.5 months with versus 1.4 months with chemotherapy. The results were presented during a Presidential Symposium session at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Congress 2026 and will support a regulatory submission by Hansoh in China. Hesham Abdullah, Senior Vice President, Global Head of Oncology, R&D, GSK said, “This is Hansoh’s second positive phase III study for in China and adds to a growing body of evidence across multiple solid tumours. For patients with relapsed osteosarcoma, where treatment options are extremely limited, these findings are particularly important. The results will help inform GSK's global development programme, including our ongoing phase II trial in advanced osteosarcoma and soft tissue sarcoma." In the trial, safety findings were consistent with prior findings in this tumour type. Grade 3 or higher adverse events (TRAEs) occurred in 93% of patients receiving at the 12 mg/kg dose and 83% in those receiving chemotherapy. The most common Grade 3 or higher TRAEs in both arms were haematologic events. TRAEs led to discontinuation in 4% of patients receiving and 2% of patients receiving chemotherapy. About osteosarcoma Osteosarcoma is a rare cancer with a global incidence of approximately 3.4 cases per million people per year. 1 It mainly affects children and young adults and is the most common primary bone cancer, accounting for 20-40% of all bone cancers. 2 Following chemotherapy, treatment options for patients with relapsed osteosarcoma are severely limited, with no clear standard of care available. 3 After patients progress on two prior lines of treatment, options become even more limited, with no approved therapies. About risvutatug rezetecan is a novel investigational conjugate targeting the B7-H3 protein, which is highly expressed in more than 10 solid tumour types, supporting GSK’s ambition to develop it across more than 40 indications by 2040. More than 1,000 patients have received as part of GSK’s global EMBOLD clinical trial programme, which includes phase III trials in small cell lung cancer ( ) and metastatic prostate cancer (mCRPC), with additional upcoming phase III trials planned in SCLC and mCRPC. has received several global regulatory designations to date, such as orphan drug designations in the US, Japan and the EU for SCLC; Breakthrough Therapy Designation in the US and Priority Medicines (PRIME) Designation from the EMA for relapsed or refractory ; and Breakthrough Therapy Designation in the US for relapsed or refractory osteosarcoma, among others. About is Hansoh Pharma’s multicentre, randomised, phase III trial evaluating compared with chemotherapy (gemcitabine and docetaxel) in patients in China with osteosarcoma who had progressed or relapsed after at least two prior lines of systemic therapy. Patients were randomised 1:1 to receive 12.0 mg/kg every 3 weeks on day 1 or gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² on days 1 and 8 plus docetaxel 75 mg/m² on day 8 every 3 weeks. While an 8 mg/kg dose was used in the trial in lung cancer, a higher 12 mg/kg dose was selected for this trial to account for potential difficulty in penetrating bone tissue. The primary endpoint is Independent Review Committee (IRC)-assessed progression-free survival. Secondary endpoints are investigator-assessed overall survival, investigator-assessed PFS and safety, as well as objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate and, duration of response (DOR). About EMBOLD EMBOLD is a phase Ib/II study evaluating the efficacy and safety of in global participants with previously treated unresectable advanced or metastatic sarcomas. The trial is designed to characterise the of and inform the optimal dose in adolescents and adults who have either osteosarcoma or soft tissue sarcoma. The primary endpoints are PFS and confirmed ORR; secondary endpoints include safety, DOR and outcome measures.
