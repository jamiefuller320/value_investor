Issued: 23 October 2026, London UK GSK presents positive data for in ovarian cancer ● 62% confirmed objective response rate and 12.2-month median duration of response in phase Ib trial ● 11.1-month median progression-free survival in a setting ● Five pivotal phase III studies now underway across gynaecological cancers GSK plc (LSE/NYSE: GSK) today announced updated data from the global phase I trial for mocertatug rezetecan ( ), an investigational B7-H4-targeted conjugate (ADC). Results showed clinically meaningful and durable responses at >13 months , building on initial data in ovarian cancer (PROC) announced earlier this year. These data will be presented in a oral session at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Congress 2026 in Madrid, Spain. [1] achieved a confirmed objective response rate (cORR) of 62% (n=28/45; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 46.5-76.2%), with a median duration of response (DoR) of 12.2 months (95% CI: 7.0-12.7) at the selected phase III dose of 5.8 mg/kg. Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 11.1 months (95% CI: 8.3-13.9). 1 Hesham Abdullah, Senior Vice President, Global Head Oncology, R&D, GSK , said: “Current treatment options for patients with ovarian cancer are limited and achieving durable responses remains a challenge. These promising results for show that treatment responses and durability are maintained over time in many patients, alongside encouraging progression-free survival and a manageable safety profile. We have accelerated the phase III development programme across gynaecological cancers, with five pivotal studies now underway.” Safety results remain consistent with previously published data. About The clinical trial is a , , phase I study evaluating the safety, tolerability and efficacy of injection in patients with ovarian cancer (PROC) or advanced/recurrent endometrial cancer (EC). Phase Ia assessed up to four dose levels in patients with advanced solid tumours, with intravenous administration every three weeks until progression or toxicity. In the phase Ib dose expansion, patients with PROC or EC (1-3 prior lines of therapy) were randomised to three or two dose levels, respectively. At data , a total of 136 PROC patients were enrolled in . At the selected phase III dose evaluated in phase Ib, the most common adverse events in PROC were gastrointestinal and haematological, consistent with treatments in this class. adverse events led to dose interruptions in 51% of patients with PROC and dose reductions in 47%. Primary endpoints included: incidence of toxicity in phase Ia and confirmed objective response rate (cORR) by investigator per RECIST 1.1 in phase Ib. Patients with EC treated at the selected phase III dose continue to be followed, with data expected to be presented in the future. It is composed of a fully human monoclonal antibody covalently linked to a topoisomerase inhibitor payload and has a ratio (DAR) of 6.
