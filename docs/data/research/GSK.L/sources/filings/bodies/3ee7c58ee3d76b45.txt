Issued: 18 October 2026, London UK GSK presents first global data for in small cell lung cancer ● 61% overall response rate in phase Ib trial ● Results build on positive pivotal data in Chinese patients ● Data supports accelerated development, including in ongoing global phase III trial in GSK plc (LSE/NYSE: GSK) today announced the first global data for risvutatug rezetecan ( ), its investigational B7-H3-targeted conjugate (ADC), in patients with relapsed small cell lung cancer ( ). Results from the phase Ib portion of the EMBOLD trial showed positive activity and a manageable safety profile, adding to recently reported positive phase III data in patients with SCLC in China. 1 In the trial, achieved a confirmed objective response rate (ORR) of 61% among evaluable patients at the 8 mg/kg dose (n=11/18; 95% CI: 35.7-82.7), compared with 32% at the 6 mg/kg dose (n=7/22; 95% CI: 13.9-54.9). These findings will be presented in a poster session at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Congress 2026 in Madrid, Spain. Along with data from Hansoh’s phase III study in SCLC, this data supported selection of the 8 mg/kg dose currently being evaluated in GSK’s global phase III EMBOLD study. Hesham Abdullah, Senior Vice President, Global Head of Oncology, R&D, GSK said , “Today’s first global data for build on the statistically significant overall survival benefit recently reported in Hansoh’s pivotal Chinese trial. As the data for continues to grow, we remain focused on accelerating development in small cell lung cancer, where patients often relapse quickly and urgently need more treatment options.” The safety profile was manageable across both dose levels at a median of 100.5 days. Grade 3 or higher adverse events (TRAEs) were observed in 42% of patients receiving 8 mg/kg and 15% of those receiving 6 mg/kg, with haematological events being the most common. The overall rate of interstitial lung disease (ILD) was low (2%; 1 out of 50 patients) and the case was moderate (grade 2). Hye Ryun Kim, MD, PhD, Division of Medical Oncology, Yonsei Cancer Center, and a study investigator in the EMBOLD study, said, “ small cell lung cancer remains one of the most aggressive cancers we treat, with survival rates typically in the single digits. Patients whose disease relapses often have limited options, and current therapies can be difficult to tolerate. The response rates and manageable safety profile observed with are encouraging and support its ongoing development in this setting.” These data will be presented alongside a broader set of findings from the EMBOLD programme at ESMO 2026, including global results from EMBOLD , an analysis of ILD across the early clinical programme, and the design of the global phase III EMBOLD study. evidence from the RESONANCE study further demonstrates the complexity of treatment after relapse in and highlights the absence of a clear treatment standard in later lines of therapy. Together, these findings contribute to the growing body of evidence for and reflect GSK’s focus on advancing targeted medicines where significant patient need and scientific opportunity remain. About EMBOLD EMBOLD (NCT06551142) is a global, phase Ia/b study evaluating the safety, tolerability and clinical activity of monotherapy in adult patients with advanced or metastatic solid tumours. In the phase Ib portion of the study, patients with relapsed (2L+) small cell lung cancer were randomised to receive 6 mg/kg or 8 mg/kg intravenously every three weeks until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, loss to or death. The analysis presented at ESMO 2026 included 50 patients who had received a median of two prior therapies. The study population included patients with prior topoisomerase-1 inhibitor or tarlatamab exposure, as well as patients with brain or liver metastases. Enrolment in the phase Ib portion of the trial was still ongoing at data , and longer will be needed to further characterise the durability of response. About risvutatug rezetecan is a novel investigational conjugate targeting the B7-H3 protein, which is highly expressed in more than 10 solid tumour types, supporting GSK’s ambition to develop it across more than 40 indications by 2040.
