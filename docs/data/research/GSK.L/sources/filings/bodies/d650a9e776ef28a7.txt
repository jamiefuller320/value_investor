Issued: 13 November 2026, London UK Bexsero (4CMenB) receives positive CHMP opinion for Meningitis B booster for 10+ year olds ● Recommendation based on data showing immune memory established following vaccination in infancy can be boosted up to 19 years later 1 ● Once approved, the booster could help vaccination programmes maintain protection into adolescence, when disease incidence reaches a second peak 2 GSK plc (LSE/NYSE: GSK) today announced the Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) of the European Medicines Agency (EMA) has recommended updating the Bexsero label to include a single booster dose for individuals aged 10 years and older who were previously vaccinated at least two years earlier with Bexsero . The recommendation is supported by data showing that immune memory established following primary vaccination in infancy can be boosted with a single dose up to 19 years later 1 . Sanjay Gurunathan, Senior Vice President, R&D Vaccines & Infectious Diseases, GSK, said: “As the first generation of children vaccinated against MenB in infancy reaches adolescence, today’s positive CHMP opinion supports maintaining protection with a single booster dose during a second period in life of increased disease risk. If adopted, adolescent boosters could help reduce the overall burden of Invasive Meningococcal Disease (IMD) in countries with established infant MenB programmes”. While Invasive Meningococcal Disease (IMD) is uncommon, it can progress rapidly and become 3 . Disease incidence is highest in infants and young children, with a second peak in adolescents and young adults 2 . Adolescents are more likely to catch or pass on these bacteria because they often spend more time socialising, live closely with others, and may share personal items 4 . In Europe, serogroup B is the leading cause of invasive meningococcal disease in all age groups under 65 years 2 . The CHMP’s positive opinion is based on data from , a Phase IIIb open label, study which assessed the immune response and safety of a single booster dose of Bexsero in healthy individuals aged 10-20 years who previously completed their primary vaccination series with Bexsero . The study met its primary endpoint. The safety and reactogenicity of a single booster dose is consistent with the known safety profile of Bexsero in this age group. About is a Phase IIIb, open label, study assessing the immune response and safety of a single dose of Bexsero in healthy children aged 10-20 years. The study enrolled 312 participants across Finland, Italy, Spain and the UK, including 234 who had received Bexsero during infancy and 78 who had not previously received a meningococcal B vaccine. The primary endpoint was to assess the superiority of the immune response to a single booster dose in previously vaccinated participants compared with a first dose in adolescents who hadn’t received a MenB vaccine in the past. About invasive meningococcal disease (IMD) IMD is an uncommon but serious infection caused by Neisseria meningitidis that can present as meningitis and/or septicaemia (blood poisoning). Although anyone can develop the disease, incidence is highest among infants and young children, with a second peak in adolescents and young adults 2 . IMD can progress rapidly and may result in death or complications despite treatment. 5,6 About Bexsero (4CMenB) Bexsero is registered in 61 countries for the prevention of Invasive Meningococcal Disease (IMD) caused by Neisseria meningitidis serogroup B. In Europe, it is included in 15 national and/or regional immunisation programmes for infants, and 3 national immunisation programmes for adolescents, with additional infant vaccination programmes funded at a regional level in some countries 7 . Around 140 million doses of Bexsero have been distributed globally since 2015 8 .
