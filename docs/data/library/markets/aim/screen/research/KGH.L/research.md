# Knights Group Holdings plc (KGH.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T17:55:04.642200+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Knights Group Holdings plc is a UK AIM-listed consolidator of regional law firms, pursuing scale through organic growth and bolt-on acquisitions. The quantitative screen rates the stock a **buy**, driven chiefly by free-cash-flow yield (16.1%), low PEG (0.14), dividend growth, and earnings-quality metrics — though headline P/E (35.1x) and ROE (4.5%) look expensive on reported earnings. Yahoo data show revenue rising from £125.6m (FY2022) to £162.0m (FY2025), with FY2026 news citing record revenue above £200m and ~28% growth, partly acquisition-led. The central debate is whether strong cash generation and roll-up momentum justify a leveraged, goodwill-heavy balance sheet (negative tangible book, net debt up to £64.8m), or whether integration risk, recurring restructuring charges, and thin primary-filing evidence warrant caution despite the screen signal.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, the case rests on **cash over accounting earnings**. The screen passes four factor families — cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk — with six of 22 models clearing, including FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, and Earnings Quality. Operating cash flow has been consistently positive (£12.2m–£25.3m, FY2022–FY2025 per Yahoo), supporting a 3.2% dividend yield with rising payouts (£3.9m in FY2025 vs £1.2m in FY2022). The roll-up model has delivered top-line compounding: revenue CAGR roughly 8–9% on Yahoo FY2022–FY2025 figures, with news flow suggesting an acceleration to ~£207m in FY2026.

Business quality is **mixed rather than moat-like**. Knights operates in fragmented UK legal services — a sector with local brand loyalty but limited pricing power and partner-retention risk. The group’s strategy (national platform, shared services, acquisition pipeline; Law Society Gazette, Sep 2025, cites ambition toward 50 offices) aligns with a proven UK consolidator playbook, but the screen **fails** Graham, deep-value, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Financial Health, and Piotroski F-Score (4/9). That profile fits a **GARP/cash-yield** name more than a classic deep-value or quality compounder. The buy rating is therefore best read as “cheap on cash and growth-adjusted metrics, expensive on reported earnings and balance-sheet quality” — attractive if FY2026 momentum sustains and acquisition integration stays disciplined, less so if leverage and goodwill become binding constraints.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Evidence gap (primary):** With no filing bodies, standard risk disclosures — going concern, bank covenants, pension obligations, contingent liabilities, SRA regulatory matters — **cannot be assessed** from this pack. Verify-before-trade is essential.

**Balance sheet and acquisition risk:**
- Negative tangible book and goodwill at 38% of assets create impairment sensitivity if acquired practices underperform or key partners depart.
- Net debt nearly doubled YoY (£35.2m → £64.8m); FY2025 debt-funded M&A increases refinancing and covenant risk in a higher-rate environment (context only: UK 10y proxy ~4.7% in macro pack).
- Recurring restructuring/M&A charges (£4.3m–£6.0m annually, FY2023–FY2025) suggest integration costs are structural, not one-off.

**Screen red flags:**
- Piotroski F-Score **4/9** — fails on ROA improvement, leverage decline, current-ratio improvement, gross margin, and asset turnover.
- Failed models include Financial Health, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, and Deep Value — inconsistent with a high-conviction quality compounder.
- Headline P/E 35.1x on reported earnings; growth metric (246%) likely distorted by base effects from FY2022 loss.

**Sector and operational:**
- UK legal services face competitive pressure, fee transparency, and cyclicality tied to property, corporate, and insolvency workstreams.
- Partner retention and culture integration are critical in law-firm roll-ups; turnover is not visible without filings.
- Large receivables/WIP balance (£59.9m other receivables) raises working-capital and revenue-recognition questions.
- AIM listing: lighter ongoing disclosure vs Main Market; less analyst coverage increases information asymmetry.

**Governance:** No adverse news identified, but CEO remuneration was discussed in trade press (simplywall.st, 18 Oct 2025) — compensation alignment cannot be judged without the remuneration report.
