BUSINESS REVIEW Total revenue was up 10.4%, and up 6.4% on a like-for-like basis. Whilst not immune to the cost headwinds that face our industry, Young’s profit conversion has been outstanding, and we delivered another record profit for the first half. Total pub EBITDA was up 14.0% to £88.2 million (2025: £77.4 million); adjusted operating profit was up 14.7% to £46.7 million (2025: £40.7 million), with operating margins growing further at 16.0% (2025: 15.4%). The strong conversion continued throughout the P&L, with adjusted profit before tax up 19.3% to £37.1 million (2025: £31.1 million). The period started well, with a dry Easter weekend and strong May bank holidays before building into one of the sunniest, warmest summers on record. Our pubs with gardens and riverside locations performed particularly strongly, delivering like-for-like sales growth of 7.5%. This underlines the advantage of our high-quality estate and the benefits of consistent investment in attractive outdoor spaces. The excellent weather also coincided nicely with the FIFA World Cup. England match days delivered increased sales of approximately £4.8 million, and total like-for-like sales across the five-week tournament increased by approximately £6.8 million, with non-England match days seeing a 5.3% increase in like-for-like sales. Category performance The excellent weather and great sporting events during the summer, unlocked significant volume and value growth across our drink sales, which were up 11.7% in total and 7.9% on a like-for-like basis. The sales strategy for the FIFA World Cup focused on early visibility to capture pre-booked revenue, partnering with several drinks suppliers to deliver highly targeted pre-match events to bring in our consumers early and delivering speed of service during the key times. The FIFA World Cup-driven drinks growth ensured lager was the strongest performing draught category, with sales up 20% year-on-year. Naturally, we have also continued to look at ways to support and invigorate the category outside of one-off sporting events. Launched in May as a Young’s exclusive, Jubel Lime has quickly become a firm favourite with customers, commanding a 5.9% share of total draught sales in the 60 pubs it’s listed in. Cask ale remains deeply rooted in Young’s heritage and in September, to celebrate our 195 th anniversary, we gave a nod to that heritage by rebrewing Ram Rod. This 4.5% ABV special bitter returned on draught as a limited batch, made available to 36 Young’s pubs. Wimbledon tennis fortnight continues to be a huge success for our local South West London pubs. This year, Young’s partnered with Stella Artois through a series of large-scale marketing activations. At the Dog & Fox, they delivered the premium ‘Stella Artois Wimbledon White Can’ experience, which featured an in-store shopping experience, terrace takeovers, and a dedicated Stella Coach House. Additionally, Young’s pubs exclusively launched the limited-edition Stella Artois Strawberries and Cream lager at the key Wimbledon pubs. Not surprisingly, given the great summer weather, cocktails continued to grow in popularity across Young’s pubs. Our refreshed Spritz summer cocktail menu grew in value by 29.6% year-on-year. Aperol Spritz has maintained the top spot, with over 37% of the category, although Hugo Spritz grew share to 27%. Several new summer cocktails were introduced, including the Raspberry Honeybee, which features Beesou honey aperitif. Beesou is a purpose-driven brand built around environmental responsibility and proudly donates 10% of its profits directly to Bees for Development , an international charity supporting ethical beekeeping and biodiversity. In September we rounded out the period with ‘Negroni Month’, which was a huge success, rolling out a dedicated five-twist bespoke Negroni menu across 18 of our flagship pubs. Food sales in total were up 8.7% with like-for-like sales up 3.7%. Our food strategy remains centred on quality, seasonality and local flexibility, with entrepreneurial general managers and chefs encouraged to tailor menus and events to their individual pubs. This has been particularly powerful in driving growth with the exceptional summer of warm weather and major sporting events. In the spring, we continued the roll out of the Pizza Shack to a select number of pubs, providing an alternative to our Burger Shack and ultimately delivering additional sales across the summer period, particularly around the FIFA World Cup matches. After last year’s significant food inflation, our Executive Chef team, working with our suppliers, have supported the pubs in mitigating the food inflation this year, although the pressure on costs remains for the second half of the year. Our Young’s Rooms have had a good start to the year, with total revenue up 6.6%, and an increase in RevPar (revenue per available room) of £3.85 to £96.93. Our AI integrated revenue management tool, implemented last year, continues to develop our understanding of the optimum rates strategy across our estate, supporting the revenue growth for the period. We continued to invest selectively in our pubs with rooms, enhancing bedroom quality and positioning the business to benefit from continued demand for premium, characterful accommodation in London and the South of England. Investment During the period, we invested a total of £45.2 million, inclusive of £29.1 million for Cubitt House (including the repayment of acquired debt). We were pleased to complete the strategic acquisition of Cubitt House. This landmark addition encompasses nine iconic, food-led leasehold pubs with 16 boutique bedrooms situated across London’s most affluent West London neighbourhoods, including prime locations in Mayfair, Chelsea, Marylebone, Belgravia, and Notting Hill. Eight of the pubs are fully operational, while the ninth, The Duke of Connaught (Belgravia), is currently under development. This acquisition perfectly anchors our proven strategy of selectively expanding our London presence through well-invested premium pubs. An approach that is core to our continued strong performance is the commitment to maintaining a premium estate, and our strong financial position has enabled us to invest a total of £16.1 million across our existing estate in the first six months of the year. We delivered major pub investments at the Windmill (Mayfair), The Greyhound (Carshalton), The Bell (Fetcham), The Cock & Bottle (Notting Hill) and The Lamb (Bloomsbury), together with starting a major bedroom refresh at Alexander Pope (Twickenham). Young’s continuously reviews its estate to recycle capital efficiently, disposing of five non-core pubs during the period for total proceeds of £6.1 million.
