Strong investment performance across several of our principal franchises, combined with growing client demand for differentiated specialist strategies, drove both substantial growth in assets under management (AuM) and a marked improvement in flows. AuM increased by 43% over the six months, from £30.6bn at 31 March 2026 to £43.7bn at 30 September 2026. Investment performance and market movements contributed £9.5bn, while net inflows were £3.6bn from gross inflows of £9.6bn. This compares with net outflows of £690m in the same period last year and net inflows of £902m (excluding corporate actions and fund closures) for FY26 as a whole. Technology remained the principal driver of flows, reflecting strong investment performance and sustained demand for strategies exposed to the structural development of AI. Our client base is also becoming more international: clients outside the UK now account for around half of AuM, up from 45% in March, with Asia the fastest-growing region. Importantly, there were also positive contributions from Healthcare (including Polar Capital Biotechnology Fund), Smart Energy, Japan Value, and Polar Capital Global Absolute Return Fund. The period was not without volatility. In the second quarter, £1.2bn of net inflows were more than offset by £2.4bn of adverse market movements and investment performance, and AuM fell from £45.0bn at 30 June 2026 to £43.7bn at the period end. This is a reminder of the sensitivity of our AuM to markets, particularly given the increased scale of our Technology franchise. Investment performance Investment performance remains the foundation of our business and the most important driver of sustainable long-term growth. Across our UCITS fund range, representing approximately 76% of Group AuM, 78% of AuM ranked in the top two quartiles of the relevant Lipper peer group over one year, 83% over three years, 90% over five years, and 100% since inception at 30 September 2026. Against benchmark, 65% of UCITS AuM had outperformed over one year, 82% over three years, 79% over five years, and 96% since inception. Technology and AI remained important contributors, alongside strong relative performance from Smart Energy, Japan Value and UK Value. Performance has not been uniformly strong. Our Polar Capital Biotechnology and Healthcare Discovery Funds underperformed significantly, with August and September particularly difficult. A sharp de-grossing of small and mid-cap biotechnology stocks, alongside rising US Treasury yields, penalised fundamentally driven quality growth portfolios, while lower-quality and heavily shorted stocks rallied. The team has made no material changes to positioning and remains confident in the fundamentals of its holdings. Elsewhere, Polar Capital Emerging Market Stars, Global Insurance and European ex-UK Income Funds lagged their benchmark. Our approach remains consistent: preserve genuine investment independence while maintaining clear performance expectations, constructive challenge, and accountability for delivering good client outcomes. AuM and fund flows Group AuM increased by £13.1bn during the period, to £43.7bn at 30 September 2026. Net flows were positive in both quarters, at approximately £2.5bn in the first and £1.2bn in the second. Second-quarter net inflows alone exceeded those for the whole of FY26 (£902m), despite a more volatile market. While this represented a moderation from an exceptionally strong first quarter, driven by early-period Technology demand, the consistency of positive flows was encouraging. Open-ended funds generated net inflows of £3.5bn and segregated mandates £154m, while investment trusts recorded modest net outflows of £48m, principally through share buyback activity. Technology strategies generated approximately £3.8bn of net inflows, including £3.1bn into Polar Capital Global Technology Fund and £0.7bn into Polar Capital Artificial Intelligence Fund. Healthcare contributed approximately £0.6bn, including £0.4bn into Polar Capital Biotechnology Fund, while the Polar Capital Smart Energy Fund contributed approximately £0.3bn. Polar Capital Global Absolute Return and Japan Value Funds also recorded positive flows. Outflows were more concentrated: the Emerging Markets and Asia team recorded approximately £0.9bn of net outflows, principally from Polar Capital Emerging Market Stars Fund. Around half of this reflected a single client halving its holding as it moved to a more benchmark-aware approach through passive exposure, while retaining a significant investment in the strategy. Global Insurance recorded approximately £0.2bn of net outflows and UK Value £40m. Demand for our Technology strategies has been exceptional, reflecting the team’s long-term performance record. As with any period of strong sector demand, a proportion of these flows may prove tactical and more sensitive to a correction or change in sentiment. Our priority is therefore not simply to maximise near-term flows, but to convert gross demand into durable and increasingly diversified net inflows. Client engagement remained particularly strong across Europe and Asia, where we are building momentum with regional banks and global financial institutions. The US remains a significant opportunity, but progress has been more limited, constrained by current product performance and a relatively narrow range of US-relevant strategies. Investment trusts Investment trusts remain an important component of the Group, representing c21% of Group AuM. Investment trust AuM increased from £6.6bn to £9.3bn over the period, driven by £2.7bn of investment performance and market movements. Net outflows of £48m reflected share buybacks of £79.3m by Polar Capital Technology Trust and £6.5m by Polar Capital Global Financials Trust, partly offset by share issuance of £37.6m by Polar Capital Global Healthcare Trust, as UK-domiciled investors adopted a more cautious outlook. We continue to work closely with the trust Boards to support long-term shareholder outcomes, including through discount management, clear communication, and strong investment performance. Given the increase in core profitability during the period, the Board has declared an interim dividend of 18.0p per share (January 2026: 14.0p), payable in January 2027. Strategy – from intent to execution Last year we set out our ambition to scale through differentiation and focus , built around four priorities. The focus has now shifted from defining the strategy to executing it and, while it is early, we are seeing tangible progress. In Investment and Product , we are concentrating resources where we have a clear investment edge and a credible path to scale, assessing new teams and extension strategies selectively, and exploring new structures, including active ETFs, that could broaden our addressable market. In Distribution , the priority is to broaden the client base and the sources of flow, as described above. In People, Culture and Technology , we must preserve the investment independence at the heart of our boutique model, while continuing to build depth within our investment teams. We have strengthened our software and data engineering capability, remaining focused on moving further practical AI use cases from pilot into controlled everyday use. In Financial Discipline and the Operating Model , strong growth gives us the opportunity to invest, but not a reason to relax discipline. We continue to apply clear commercial hurdles to new products, teams, and initiatives, and to pursue operating leverage through simplification and automation where appropriate, so that AuM can grow without a proportional increase in headcount and cost. Outlook The first half demonstrated the potential of the business when investment performance, differentiated product and client demand align; however, we should not become complacent. Flows remain concentrated in some of our largest franchises, market movements can have a significant impact on AuM, and performance in parts of the range requires improvement. Investor allocations also remain sensitive to market volatility, geopolitics, and changing risk appetite. AuM at 13 November 2026 was £38.2bn. The reduction since the period end principally reflects weaker markets, particularly in Technology, illustrating the sensitivity noted above; net flows remained positive at £207.2m. We have a strong balance sheet and a growing pipeline of client engagement. The structural pressures facing traditional active management have not disappeared, but clients continue to seek differentiated strategies, proven specialist expertise, and access to long-term structural growth opportunities. That is the environment in which Polar Capital should be well placed to compete. Our strategy remains straightforward: scale where we have a genuine edge, provide support where improvement is required, broaden selectively, and maintain financial discipline. If we execute consistently, I believe we can convert the momentum of the first half into sustainable long-term growth for clients and shareholders.
