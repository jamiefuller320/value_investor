## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
M.P. Evans Group PLC is a London-listed, Indonesia-focused integrated palm-oil producer whose estates and mills benefit directly from commodity prices and operational leverage. The quantitative screen rates the name **Strong Buy** (16/22 models, 80% composite): cheap on earnings and FCF, high ROE (~19.8%), solid dividend (~3.5%), and a Piotroski F-Score of 8/9. Yahoo-sourced FY2025 figures show revenue of **£371.0m**, net income of **£111.2m**, net cash of **£86.3m**, and **zero debt** — a sharp improvement on FY2024. The valuation hook is a sub-11x P/E on record earnings, ~£112m FCF, and an active buyback, against a business with tangible plantation assets. The key debate is whether record profitability and H1 2026 volume growth are sustainable, or whether Indonesian export policy and palm-oil price mean reversion will compress multiples — a risk the screen’s “Financial Health” failure may understate.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MPE combines **asset-backed cheapness** with **operating quality** in a way that aligns with five passed screen families: cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk.

**Cheapness and cash generation.** The screen passes Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Acquirer’s Multiple, and Dreman Contrarian. Headline metrics: **P/E 10.8**, **P/B 2.0**, **FCF ~£86.9m** (screen; Yahoo FY2025 FCF **£112.5m**), and **current ratio 3.62**. Failed deep-value screens (Graham Net-Net, Schloss Low P/B) reflect plantation-heavy balance sheets rather than distressed balance-sheet value — consistent with a quality compounder, not a net-net.

**Quality and capital discipline.** ROE **19.8%**, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Magic Formula, and Piotroski **8/9** (only asset turnover failed). Yahoo shows net income rising from **£52.5m (2023)** → **£87.9m (2024)** → **£111.2m (2025)**, with gross margin expanding as revenue grew **£307m → £371m** over 2023–25. FY2025 **total debt was repaid to zero** (from **£33.0m** in 2024); **£45.1m** debt repayment and **£38.7m** dividends were funded from **£137.1m** operating cash flow.

**Shareholder returns.** Dividend Growth screen passed; yield **3.5%**. Ordinary shares fell from **53.3m (2024)** to **52.2m (2025)**, with news confirming ongoing AIM buybacks through July 2026 (~1,600–1,650p). Management is returning cash while reducing the share count — supportive for per-share value.

**Business quality.** Vertically integrated Indonesian palm-oil production (estates → mills → CPO) gives cost control and volume leverage. H1 2026 trading updates (news, not filed bodies) report a **14% FFB harvest increase**, suggesting the earnings step-up has operational backing, not price alone.

**Why Strong Buy, with caveats.** The screen’s **conviction score is only 47%** (“new”, one week at signal, improving). That fits a commodity-linked name where screens capture trailing cheapness but not forward policy or price risk. Thesis holds if palm-oil prices stay firm and Indonesian regulation does not materially constrain exports; the screen’s quality and FCF metrics suggest the market may still be pricing mean reversion too aggressively.

**Governance and listing.** AIM-listed with less stringent ongoing disclosure than Main Market; **no filing bodies** limits governance assessment (board changes, related parties, auditor emphasis-of-matter).

**Screen failures worth noting.** **Financial Health** and **High Dividend Yield** failed — yield at 3.5% is solid but not deep-income; health scoring may flag commodity volatility or sector norms. **Lynch PEG** failed, consistent with cyclical earnings complicating growth-adjusted valuation.
