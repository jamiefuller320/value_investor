## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Fincantieri (FCT.MI) screens as a buy on cash-generation quality and improving fundamentals rather than classic deep value: four of 22 models pass (FCF Yield, Piotroski F-Score 8/9, Composite Value, Earnings Quality), with a 71% composite score versus 66% sector-relative. The investment case rests on a sharp earnings recovery from 2022–23 losses, a defence and underwater pivot under the 2026–2030 industrial plan, and strong recent order momentum — but headline valuation (P/E 23.8, P/B 4.4) is not cheap, and the balance sheet remains stretched. Primary regulatory filings were not retrieved in this research pack, so all financial figures below fall back to Yahoo (`financials_annual.json`); that gap limits verification of covenants, pensions, and going-concern language. The central debate is whether record H1 2026 margins and €600m underwater M&A translate into durable FCF and deleveraging, or whether dilution, negative tangible equity, and weak liquidity cap upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Fincantieri offers a quality-recovery profile rather than a Graham-style bargain. Yahoo data show revenue rising from €7.35bn (2022) to €8.92bn (2025), with net income swinging from a €309m loss (2022) to €123m profit (2025); operating cash flow reached €596m in 2025 versus €445m in 2024. That trajectory aligns with the screen’s quality and earnings-quality passes and an F-Score of 8/9 (positive net income, OCF > net income, declining leverage, improving gross margin and asset turnover).

The quantitative hook is cash economics: the screen flags FCF yield at 23.8% and FCF of ~€1.15bn, but Yahoo reports 2025 free cash flow of only €204m — a material discrepancy that must be reconciled before treating FCF Yield as a durable edge. On Yahoo figures alone, FCF improved modestly year-on-year (€204m vs €181m) while operating cash flow accelerated sharply, suggesting working-capital release rather than a step-change in underlying FCF.

What the screen does not capture is strategic repositioning: news flow points to record H1 2026 profits, cruise-led margin expansion, €11bn of new orders in four months (May 2025), and a €600m push into underwater/defence assets (July 2026). Management targets ~€500m net profit by 2030 and net debt zero by 2032, with dividends from 2028 — a long-dated value realisation path that fits a patient accumulator but not a pure asset-based or dividend value mandate. Failed screen models (Graham Defensive, Deep Value, Financial Health, High Dividend Yield) correctly flag rich multiples, no dividend, high debt/equity (123.5%), and a current ratio below 1.0 (0.88).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Balance sheet and liquidity:** High leverage (debt/equity 123.5% per screen), current ratio below 1.0, and negative working capital of €869m are the primary financial red flags. Without filing text, covenant headroom, maturity profile, and going-concern assessments cannot be verified — a significant verify-before-trade gap.

**Tangible equity and M&A:** Negative tangible book (€54m deficit) after goodwill/intangible growth to €1.05bn signals acquisition-driven balance-sheet risk. July 2026 news reports ~€600m invested across four underwater businesses; integration and impairment risk are elevated.

**Capital structure / dilution:** February 2026 saw a ~10% capital placement via accelerated bookbuild; May 2026 incentive-plan share issuance (~2m shares); June 2026 buyback closed at 1m shares — net dilutive. No dividend (failed High Dividend Yield screen); management guides dividend resumption only from 2028.

**Governance:** April 2026 coverage (“Strano caso a Fincantieri l’ex manager vende azioni poco prima del crollo”, la Repubblica, 28 April 2026) flags insider selling ahead of a share-price fall — worth monitoring though not adjudicated here.

**Cyclical and segment risk:** Q2 2026 earnings commentary (Yahoo/yfinance, 29 July 2026) notes defence revenue declines alongside cruise strength — segment mix shift creates earnings volatility. Shipbuilding remains contract-lumpy and working-capital intensive.

**Valuation:** P/E 23.8, P/B 4.4, EV/EBIT 35.9 (failed Acquirer’s Multiple) — the name is not statistically cheap; the screen buy rests on quality/FCF metrics that may be overstated if the FCF discrepancy persists.

**Pension / provisions:** Long-term provisions €274m and pension lines exist on Yahoo balance sheet, but without annual-report notes the funding status and IAS 19 sensitivities are unknown.
