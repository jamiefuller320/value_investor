# Azimut Holding S.p.A. (AZM.MI) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T11:54:33.399664+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Azimut Holding is a Milan-listed independent asset and wealth manager that the quantitative screen rates as a **buy** (14/22 models; composite 62.7%), driven by cheapness (P/E 9.9, P/B 2.3), a 5.5% dividend yield, high reported ROE (29.3%), and strong free-cash-flow generation in FY2025. The investment case rests on sustained net inflows—headlines cite record organic growth (€4.6bn in Q1 2026; €32bn for FY2025)—and a pivot toward higher-margin private markets and international expansion, including a majority stake in Brazil’s Unifinance (January 2026). The central debate is whether current multiples adequately discount Italian regulatory scrutiny (Bank of Italy findings drove a ~15% sell-off in November 2025), structurally weak balance-sheet liquidity (current ratio 0.53), and earnings normalisation after FY2024’s peak. Primary regulatory filings are absent from the research pack; all financial figures below fall back to Yahoo unless noted.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Azimut presents a rare combination of asset-manager growth, capital-light balance-sheet deleveraging, and income. The screen passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with models including Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Magic Formula, Buffett Quality, and High Dividend Yield. Debt/equity of ~4% and FCF of €554m (Yahoo, FY2025) support the risk-family pass, whilst ROE near 30% and a sub-10× P/E align with quality and GARP screens.

Business quality, as inferred from news flow rather than filing bodies, appears robust: management reaffirmed full-year guidance on the Q1 2026 call (1 August 2026), and FY2025 recurring net profit reportedly rose ~20% alongside record net inflows. Revenue has compounded from €1.21bn (2023) to €1.41bn (2025), with operating margins consistently above 44% of revenue. The screen fails on deep-value criteria (P/B 2.3 > 1.0), Graham Defensive (P/B too rich), Lynch PEG (earnings growth −0.07%), Piotroski F-Score (5/9), and Financial Health (weak liquidity)—appropriate guardrails rather than outright disqualifiers for a growing, dividend-paying asset manager.

The buy case is therefore a **GARP-plus-yield** play on a compounding AUM franchise, not a net-net or deep-value bargain. Quantitative cheapness is partially offset by a strong multi-year share-price rally (Yahoo commentary cites ~45–124% returns over one to three years), meaning the margin of safety is valuation-relative rather than absolute.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory:** The most material event in the past year was a ~15% share-price fall following Bank of Italy (*Bankitalia*) findings (Investing.com, 13 November 2025; corroborated by Borsainside sell-off coverage same date). Filing bodies containing the precise nature of findings, remediation requirements, or capital impact are unavailable; this remains a live governance and regulatory overhang.

**Liquidity and balance-sheet structure:** Current ratio 0.53 and persistently negative working capital (−€1.1bn) failed the Financial Health screen. For an asset manager, this may reflect business-model mechanics (client assets/liabilities) rather than distress—but without statutory accounts or going-concern language, the risk cannot be dismissed. No covenant or contingency text is available from filing bodies.

**Earnings quality and momentum:** Piotroski F-Score 5/9 flags deteriorating ROA trend, rising leverage (though absolute debt is low), declining current ratio, and gross-margin pressure. Reported earnings growth is −0.07%, failing Lynch PEG. FY2025 net income declined despite revenue growth.

**Capital markets and cyclicality:** AUM, performance fees, and net inflows are market-sensitive. The €70m private placement with CDP and Mediocredito Centrale (February 2026) suggests ongoing funding needs for growth/private-markets expansion—worth monitoring against equity dilution and return on invested capital.

**M&A integration:** The Unifinance acquisition (Brazil, UHNW wealth, January 2026) and broader international push add execution and emerging-market regulatory risk.

**Valuation:** P/B 2.3 and a strong multi-year rally (Yahoo, May 2026: +45% one-year, +124% three-year) limit deep-value margin of safety. Mediobanca reportedly called the stock undervalued (September 2025), but that is sell-side opinion, not a filing.

**Pension:** Yahoo shows non-current pension obligations of €5.5m (2025)—immaterial on face value, unverified in primary filings.
