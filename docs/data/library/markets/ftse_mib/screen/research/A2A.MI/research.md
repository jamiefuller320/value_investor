# A2A S.p.A. (A2A.MI) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T17:31:19.410572+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
A2A is a Lombardy-based multi-utility (power generation, distribution, waste, water, district heating) trading at roughly 9.8× earnings and 1.4× book with a ~4.4% dividend yield — a profile that passes nine of 22 quantitative value models. The investment case rests on a regulated-and-contracted earnings base, a rising dividend (€0.104/share for 2026, +4% per press coverage), heavy capex into renewables and grids, and optional upside from data-centre power demand. The central debate is whether headline cheapness adequately compensates for a 13% decline in reported net income in FY2025 (Yahoo), elevated leverage (~€5.2bn net debt), and Italian regulatory/energy-market exposure. Primary regulatory filings were not retrieved in this research pack; financial analysis below relies on Yahoo fallback data and news headlines, limiting filing-level verification of covenants, contingencies, and going-concern language.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen rates A2A a **Strong Buy** (composite 82%, sector-relative 78%), with nine models passing across **cheapness, dividend, and risk** families: Earnings Yield (E/P 10.2%), FCF Yield (7.6%), Low P/E + High Yield, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality. Data quality is full (20/20 metrics). At P/E 9.8, P/B 1.4, ROE 11.5%, and yield 4.4%, the stock screens as inexpensive relative to European utilities peers while still returning cash to shareholders via dividends and modest buybacks.

Business quality is mixed rather than pristine. A2A operates essential infrastructure with partial municipal ownership (Brescia/Milan footprint), giving strategic backing but also political overlay — evidenced by local-body share sales (Gamec/Palazzo Frizzoni) in 2026 press. Revenue grew to €13.7bn in FY2025 (Yahoo), and news reports FY2025 MOL (operating profit) of €2.24bn with €1.7bn of investments — consistent with a company reinvesting aggressively into the energy transition. The screen's risk-family pass and declining net debt (€5.55bn → €5.15bn, Yahoo) support balance-sheet manageability, though the stock fails quality/moat models (Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Graham Defensive) and Piotroski F-Score (6/9), flagging deteriorating ROA, gross margin, and current-ratio trends. For a value investor, the hook is a cash-generative utility at a discount, with dividend growth and buybacks providing a floor; the screen's "improving" one-week trend is too early to treat as established conviction (conviction score 30%, stability label "new").

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and market:** Italian utility returns are set by ARERA/regulatory frameworks; FY2025 results commentary (Yahoo earnings-call summaries, Mar/May 2026) cites regulatory and energy-market headwinds. The November 2025 strategic plan triggered a ~6% share fall on weaker near-term forecasts (Investing.com, 12 Nov 2025), indicating the market prices execution risk on the industrial plan.

**Earnings quality:** Net income declined in FY2025 despite revenue growth; recurring unusual charges (~€126m) and write-offs appear in each of the last four years (Yahoo). Piotroski components failing: ROA trend, gross margin trend, current-ratio trend — consistent with margin pressure amid rising capex and working-capital swings (receivables increased €878m in FY2025 OCF bridge).

**Leverage and pensions:** Net debt ~€5.2bn with €7.3bn gross debt; pension obligations €196m (Yahoo). Covenant terms unavailable without filings.

**Governance / ownership:** Municipally influenced shareholder base creates strategic support but also political risk — local entities selling stakes (Bergamonews, Apr–May 2026) and CEO Carnevali stating the municipal quota is "strategic" with no planned sales (Il Sole 24 ORE, 18 Jun 2026). M&A speculation (A2A–ERG merger rumours, Feb 2026) was followed by a company clarification denying indiscrezioni — event risk remains in consolidated Italian utilities.

**M&A / capital allocation:** FY2024 included €1.31bn business purchases (Yahoo investing cash flow); FY2025 saw €48m purchases offset by €445m disposals. Inorganic history adds integration risk.

**Screen failures:** Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, High Dividend Yield (despite 4.4% headline yield — likely threshold/growth criteria), Financial Health, and Piotroski — signalling the name is **cheap but not high-quality** on classical screens.

**Filing gap itself:** Absence of primary filings is a research red flag for verify-before-trade workflows; contingent liabilities and going-concern language cannot be assessed.
