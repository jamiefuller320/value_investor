# Infrastrutture Wireless Italiane S.p.A. (INW.MI) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T17:41:08.729279+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Infrastrutture Wireless Italiane (INWIT) is Italy’s dominant listed wireless-tower operator, offering a defensive infrastructure asset with recurring lease revenues, strong operating cash conversion, and an headline dividend yield of 8.7%. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy (8/22 models; composite 67%), driven chiefly by FCF yield, dividend metrics, Piotroski quality (8/9), and contrarian/value composites. The valuation hook is a mid-teens P/E on a business generating roughly €518m of free cash flow against a ~8.9% FCF yield, with management continuing to return capital via dividends and buybacks. The central debate is whether the yield is sustainable: 2025 cash dividends (~€675m) exceeded FCF (~€518m), net debt rose to €4.2bn, and the market reacted sharply to long-term guidance cuts and Master Service Agreement (MSA) disputes with anchor tenants. Primary regulatory filings were not retrieved in this research pack, limiting verification of covenants, contingencies, and dividend policy — a material gap for a leveraged income story.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, INWIT presents a classic “yield plus asset” case that aligns with several passing screen families: cheapness (FCF Yield, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value), quality (Piotroski F-Score 8/9, Earnings Quality), dividend (High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth), GARP (Neff PEGY), and risk. Business quality appears solid on operating metrics: revenue grew from €853m (2022) to €1,077m (2025), EBITDA from €774m to €972m, and net income from €293m to €362m (all figures from Yahoo fallback — see Financial Review). Operating cash flow has consistently exceeded reported net income (Piotroski “OCF > net income” pass), and gross margins remain high (~98%), consistent with a tower-co model where cost of revenue is minimal.

The screen’s dividend and FCF-yield signals are the primary valuation anchor. At P/E 16.3, P/B 1.6, and ROE 9.1%, the stock is not deep value on book (tangible book is deeply negative due to ~€6.2bn of goodwill), but it screens as inexpensive on cash-return metrics. Management has been an active capital returner: share count fell from ~997m (2024) to ~920m (2025), and the FY2025 dividend was raised 7.5% to €0.5543 per share (Il Giornale d’Italia, 30 April 2026). For income-oriented value investors, the combination of infra-like cash flows, visible payout growth, and contrarian positioning after a sharp share-price correction supports the screen’s buy signal — provided leverage and tenant-contract risks are acceptable.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory / tenant-contract:** Berenberg turned cautious on INWIT “after MSA disputes” (MarketScreener Italia, 18 May 2026). MSAs govern lease pricing and colocation terms with major Italian mobile operators; disputes can compress long-term revenue visibility — consistent with the November 2025 long-term guidance cut that triggered an ~8% share fall (Investing.com, 11 November 2025).

**Cyclical / sector:** Italian telecom consolidation and operator capex cycles can affect tenancy ratios and upgrade pricing. Morningstar (5 December 2025) listed INWIT among European names receiving the largest fair-value cuts post-quarterly results, signalling analyst reassessment of growth assumptions.

**Leverage and dividend sustainability:** Net debt rose ~€630m in 2025 alone; interest cover is narrowing as finance costs climb. Dividends exceeded FCF in 2025 — a red flag for income investors unless funded by disposals, debt, or one-offs not visible in this pack. Negative tangible equity (~€3.0bn) limits balance-sheet flexibility in a downturn.

**Governance / disclosure:** No filing bodies means going-concern language, litigation contingencies, related-party transactions, and debt covenants could not be reviewed. This is a structural research gap, not a confirmed absence of risk.

**Market / sentiment:** Investing.com (3 June 2026) reported INWIT was flagged as overvalued before a ~39% price decline; coverage is skewed toward price commentary rather than fundamental disclosures.

**Screen failures worth noting:** The name fails Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Magic Formula, and Financial Health models — suggesting the buy case rests on yield/FCF/contrarian factors rather than classic quality-at-a-reasonable-price or balance-sheet strength screens.
