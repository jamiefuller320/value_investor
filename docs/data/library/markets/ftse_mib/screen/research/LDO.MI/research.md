## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Leonardo S.p.a. (LDO.MI) is a state-linked Italian aerospace, defence, and security group benefiting from European rearmament, a multi-year order backlog, and visible deleveraging. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy (6/22 models; composite 56%), driven chiefly by quality, GARP, and earnings-quality signals—not classic deep value. At P/E 23.3, P/B 3.1, and dividend yield 1.2%, the valuation hook is growth-at-a-reasonable-quality multiple (PEGY 0.37; Piotroski F-Score 9/9), not asset-based cheapness. The central debate is whether defence demand, GCAP/space optionality, and balance-sheet repair justify a premium multiple, or whether cyclicality, negative working capital, heavy intangibles, and governance friction cap upside. Primary regulatory filing bodies are absent from the research pack; financial trends below rely on Yahoo fallback data and news headlines, which limits verification depth.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, Leonardo is a buy on business quality and improving fundamentals rather than on screen cheapness. The model passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—but fails Graham-style, deep value, FCF yield, and high-yield screens, signalling a compounder/GARP profile within Industrials rather than a distressed or net-net opportunity.

Business quality aligns with the screen: ROE 13.1%, perfect Piotroski F-Score (positive earnings, OCF exceeding net income, declining leverage, improving margins and asset turnover), and passes on Magic Formula, Earnings Quality, Dividend Growth, and Neff PEGY (0.37). Reported free cash flow of approximately €1.28bn (screen metric) and Yahoo annual FCF rising from €390m (2022) to €734m (2025) support the earnings-quality case, though working-capital intensity remains a watchpoint.

Operationally, the defence upcycle provides a durable demand backdrop. News flow points to Q1 2026 orders of €9.0bn (+31% y/y), revenue €4.4bn (+7%), and EBITA €281m (+33%), with 2026 guidance confirmed; net debt has fallen materially on Yahoo data (€2.53bn in 2022 to €643m in 2025). A share buyback programme (launched May 2026; 880,000 shares repurchased in July 2026 at an average €46.87) signals management confidence. The screen’s low conviction score (35%; signal only one week old, “improving”) warrants patience, but the combination of backlog growth, deleveraging, and quality metrics supports accumulation for investors accepting a quality-at-a-fair-price framing rather than deep-value margin of safety.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Filing verification gap.** Absence of annual and interim filing bodies is the principal research limitation. Pension funding, litigation provisions, related-party transactions, and going-concern assessments cannot be confirmed from primary regulatory text.

**Balance sheet and cash conversion.** Negative working capital (€489m), rising inventory (€6.16bn), and Q1 2026 FOCF of €-411m (news headline) suggest cash conversion lags reported earnings in periods of order growth. The screen fails FCF Yield and several cash-based value models.

**Intangibles and goodwill.** Tangible book value (€1.01bn) is small relative to equity (€9.56bn) and total assets (€34.5bn), leaving earnings vulnerable to impairment if programme assumptions weaken.

**Pension and provisions.** Yahoo shows combined pension/post-retirement balances of roughly €984m (current plus non-current) and long-term provisions €627m. Magnitude and funding status require filing confirmation.

**Governance and state influence.** The Italian Ministry of Economy and Finance indicated Francesco Macrì as chairman (Corriere di Arezzo, 10 Apr 2026); the AGM approved FY2025 accounts and installed Macrì as president (leonardo.com, 7 May 2026). Critical shareholders questioned the board at the AGM (Banca Etica, 7 May 2026). Outgoing CEO Roberto Cingolani stated performance could justify a more ambitious 2026 outlook (WSJ, 6 May 2026), while Jefferies reduced its rating citing CEO transition uncertainty (MarketScreener, 21 Apr 2026). State-linked governance adds political and strategic risk.

**Cyclical and ESG/reputational risk.** Defence demand is cyclical and budget-dependent; sector pullbacks have hit the share price (e.g. October 2025 headlines on ceasefire-related selling). Civil-society legal action against Leonardo and the Italian state was reported (Arci, 20 Nov 2025). Export controls, programme delays (e.g. GCAP), and European space consolidation (Airbus–Leonardo–Thales merger push; Yahoo, 23 Jun 2026) add execution and regulatory uncertainty.

**Valuation.** At P/E 23.3 and P/B 3.1, the stock is not statistically cheap; a miss on order conversion or margin would compress the multiple quickly.
