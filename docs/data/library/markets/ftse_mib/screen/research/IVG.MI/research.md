# Iveco Group N.V. (IVG.MI) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:00:57.033029+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Iveco Group N.V. screens as a buy primarily on asset and cash-flow cheapness—P/B ~0.9, EV/EBITDA ~8.0, and a reported FCF yield above 20%—rather than on earnings quality or balance-sheet strength. The company is mid-pivot: it completed the sale of its Defence business to Leonardo (with an linked extraordinary dividend) and is refocusing on commercial vehicles, buses, powertrain, and financial services, with electrification partnerships (Microvast) and reported strategic interest from Tata Motors in its CV franchise. The central debate is whether sub-book valuation and one-off proceeds compensate for cyclical revenue decline, compressed operating margins, volatile free cash flow, and a leveraged capital structure. Primary regulatory filings were not retrieved in this research pack, so conviction rests on secondary data and must be treated as provisional.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, the quantitative case is coherent if one accepts a cyclical, asset-heavy industrial trading below tangible replacement value. The screen passes all four metric families—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—with standout passes on Deep Value, FCF Yield, Lynch/Neff PEG variants, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality (9 of 22 models). Headline metrics from the screen: P/B 0.94, EV/EBITDA 8.0, FCF yield 20.3%, dividend yield 2.4%, and trailing FCF of €752m, set against a low ROE of 3.1% and a elevated headline P/E of 36.6x that reflects trough/normalised earnings rather than peak cycle.

Business quality is mixed. Iveco operates in structurally important but cyclical end-markets (heavy trucks, buses, off-highway powertrain) where electrification capex and R&D are rising. Portfolio simplification—exit from Defence, potential Tata Motors linkage for global CV scale—could unlock strategic value not captured by backward-looking screens. However, the screen fails quality and health checks (Piotroski F-Score 5/9, Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Buffett Quality, Financial Health, Economic Moat), signalling that the buy case is a cyclical/asset-value play, not a compounder. The improving, one-week-old buy signal (conviction 34%, stability: new) warrants patience rather than aggressive sizing until filing-backed verification is available.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and operational:** Revenue and operating profit are on a two-year downtrend; Q1 2026 continuing operations were loss-making (Yahoo). Commercial vehicle demand in Europe remains sensitive to freight activity, interest rates, and emission-regulation costs. Gross margin compression and elevated restructuring charges (€32m in 2025 per Yahoo) suggest ongoing cost pressure.

**Cash flow and leverage:** FCF halved in 2025; net debt of €3.0bn against equity of €2.76bn implies meaningful financial risk if the cycle deteriorates further. Total debt €6.1bn and interest expense €229m (2025) consume a material share of EBIT. Screen failure on Financial Health and Piotroski (5/9—ROA, current ratio, gross margin, and asset turnover all deteriorating) aligns with this.

**Quality and returns:** ROE 3.1% and failed quality screens (Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Quality Value) indicate no durable moat at current returns. Headline P/E 36.6x on reported earnings is not classic deep-value on an earnings basis; the case rests on book/FCF metrics that may normalise lower in a prolonged downturn.

**Structural / strategic:** Post-Defence Iveco is a purer CV play but loses a formerly diversified earnings stream. Electrification requires sustained R&D and capex with uncertain payback timing. Tata Motors linkage (June 2026 news) could be transformative or merely speculative until confirmed in regulatory filings.

**Governance and pensions:** Pension obligations €397m (non-current) and long-term provisions €1,584m warrant scrutiny; without filing bodies, adequacy of funding and any covenant linkage to net debt cannot be assessed.

**Research pack limitations:** Zero primary filings means no access to going-concern statements, litigation contingencies, related-party transactions, or segment-level margin bridges. This is the single largest red flag for a verify-before-trade workflow.
