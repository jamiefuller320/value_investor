## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Amplifon is the world’s largest hearing-care retailer, operating a global network of audiology shops under owned and franchise formats. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy on cash-flow cheapness (FCF yield ~10.6%), a low Neff PEGY (0.17), dividend growth, and earnings quality, despite failing most quality and classic value models. The valuation hook is a sharp de-rating after a 2025 earnings reset—net income fell to €91.3 million on flat revenue—while operating cash flow and free cash flow remained robust at €415 million and €296 million respectively. The central debate is whether this is a cash-generative franchise at an inflection (Fit4Growth margin plan, UK exit, organic Q4 recovery) or a leverage-heavy roll-up facing integration risk from the proposed €2.6 billion GN Hearing acquisition. **Critical data gap:** indexed regulatory filings belong to Ampeak Energy Limited (UK AIM), not Amplifon; financial analysis below relies on Yahoo fallback data and must be verified against Borsa Italiana / company filings before trading.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, the screen’s buy case rests on a disconnect between reported earnings (P/E 40.7, ROE 5.7%) and underlying cash economics. Amplifon passes four of four signal families—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—driven primarily by FCF Yield and Neff PEGY, with Dividend Growth and Earnings Quality as supporting passes. Operating cash flow has exceeded net income consistently (OCF €415 million vs. net income €91 million in FY2025), suggesting the earnings screen understates economic cash generation and supporting the Earnings Quality pass.

The business quality case is mixed. Amplifon operates a scalable retail audiology model with recurring demand tailwinds from ageing populations and rising hearing-aid penetration, and management is pursuing vertical integration via GN Hearing (manufacturing + retail). However, 16 of 22 screen models fail, including Graham, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Piotroski F-Score (5/9), and Financial Health—flagging elevated leverage (debt/equity 118%), weak liquidity (current ratio 0.61), thin ROE, and rich headline multiples. The stock’s ~49% one-year decline (per news coverage) has compressed the PEGY ratio, creating a contrarian entry point if Fit4Growth delivers margin recovery and GN Hearing integration succeeds. Value investors should treat this as a **cash-flow special situation** rather than a classic quality compounder: the screen identifies cheap cash, not cheap earnings.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and liquidity:** Debt/equity ~118% and current ratio 0.61 failed Financial Health and multiple quality screens. Negative tangible equity and heavy goodwill (€1,928 million) create impairment risk if GN Hearing integration or organic growth disappoints. FY2025 buybacks (€108 million) plus dividends (€65 million) alongside rising debt warrant scrutiny of capital allocation discipline.

**GN Hearing acquisition (€2.6 billion / DKK 17 billion):** Announced March 2026; described by management as the most transformative deal in company history. Market reaction was sceptical (“Amplifon’s Big Bet Has Investors Hearing Alarm Bells,” 17 March 2026). Integration, antitrust approval, financing structure (cash + shares to GN Store Nord), and synergy delivery are unverified without primary filing detail. Completion targeted end-2026.

**Earnings trajectory:** Three-year net income decline and FY2025 margin compression suggest operational headwinds beyond FX—competitive pricing, reimbursement pressure, or market saturation in core geographies cannot be assessed from available filings.

**Governance / data quality:** The filing index maps to the wrong legal entity (Ampeak Energy), undermining confidence in the research pack’s regulatory coverage for AMP.MI. This is a material verify-before-trade flag.

**Filing language unavailable:** No Amplifon going-concern, contingency, covenant, or pension disclosures are accessible from indexed bodies. Yahoo shows non-current pension liabilities €12.5 million (immaterial relative to debt).

**Competitive:** Hearing-aid retail is consolidating; vertical integration via GN Hearing may confer advantages but also concentrates manufacturing and channel risk.
