DEAR SHAREHOLDERS, 
We continued to make good progress in implementing 
our strategic priorities to strengthen our business and 
generate value for our shareholders in 2025. In a market 
environment that remains challenging, we managed our 
expenses with a clear focus on overall operational effi-
ciency, further streamlined our organization and portfo-
lio, de-risked our development pipeline, and achieved 
significant success in the leasing business. 
The result is a strong operational performance with sta-
ble income despite a large volume of income-producing 
property sales, and a focused, prime office portfolio that 
is better positioned today than 12 months ago. Good over-
all results led to a consolidated net income of €184m and 
return on equity of 7.2%, contributing to a total account-
ing return of 43% since the end of 2018. 
Increased occupancy and stable income despite 
property sales 
Proactive asset management with teams on the ground 
in our local markets allowed us to quickly respond to 
tenant demands and market developments, increasing oc-
cupancy and maintaining stable income despite shrink-
ing the overall size of the portfolio due to sales: 
– Improved occupancy from 93% to 95% yoy.
– Completed c. 190.100 sqm of leasing at rents c. 2%
above the ex

– Red uced landlord non-recoverables, maintained stable

non-core in

– Exceeded FFO I guidance achieving full year figure of
€118.5m.
Improved portf

88% of our portfolio has a sustainability certificate 
(DGNB, LEED, BREEAM, incl. properties in certification 
process), up from 84% at the end of 2024. The rate of 
properties meeting EU Taxonomy Substantial Contribu-
tion or Do No Significant Harm to Climate Change Miti-
gation criteria (in terms of energy efficiency class) stood 
at 83% as at the reporting date. Thanks to an increase in 
the green electricity share in property operations in 2025, 
the CO2 emissions intensity of our portfolio (Scope 1, 2 
and 3.13) fell by 29% compared to the previous year, put-
ting it 76% below the CRREM benchmark value. In Sep-

Large sales volume profitably closed 
Our long-term investment strategy pursues a clear focus 
on large, high-quality Class A office properties in prime 
inner-city locations. In line with this strategy, we take ad-
vantage of market liquidity to sell non-strategic assets as 
well as monetize future profit where no further value cre-
ation exists. Against this background, we target to sell 
properties that are non-core in terms of asset class, loca-
tion, building quality, age, or value creation potential, 
continuously optimizing the quality of the property port-
folio. In 2025, we have made the following progress in 
streamlining our portfolio:  
– Closed the sale of 16 non-core assets (incl. one JV) total-
ing c. €539m of gross sales volume at an average pre-
mium to book value.
– Sales included our last property in Serbia (market exit),
a hotel property in Berlin, two parking garages, and sev-
eral plots with residential, logistics, or mixed use.
– As a result of these sales, the German share of the in-
vestment portfolio rose to 73% and the office share to
98%.
– As part of our decarbonization strategy, we have sold
our last property with oil heating.
– Closed the sale of two more properties in Q1 2026 total-
ing c. €130m of gross sales volume.
Through active portfolio management, we have in-
creased the value of our property assets since 2018 from 
€4.5bn to €4.7bn, while reducing the underlying rental 
area from 1.4m sqm to around 0.7m sqm. Following these 
sales, our portfolio has improved in terms of quality, 
profitability, geographic footprint, and sector. With the 
proceeds from these sales we have multiple options, in-
cluding general corporate purposes, accretive investment 
in our prime portfolio, repayment of debt, buyback of 
shares, the distribution of dividends, and external invest-
ment should attractive opportunities arise. 
Organizational streamlining  
Simultaneously while improving the underlying portfo-
lio quality, we made good progress in further simplifying 
and increasing the efficiency of our business: 
– Outsourcing of our property management subsidiary
DRG in Germany (in which CA Immo held a non-con-
trolling 49% stake), focusing the internal capabilities on
value-add activities across the entire value chain.
– Reduced complexity and improved cost structures
through consolidation of roles and outsourcing of non-
core services that can be bought in for better service at
lower cost.
– Reduced administration expenses, as well as lower ex-
ternal legal and consulting fees.
– Shrunk exposure in non-controlling minority invest-
ments and joint ventures.
Mainly due to the reduction in personnel expenses and 
lower legal and consulting costs, indirect expenses have 
been reduced by a further 8% yoy. 
Maintained strong balance sheet and good access to 
external capital 
Our balance sheet remains solid, with an equity ratio of 
47.1%, net LTV of 34.5%, and an adequate liquidity posi-
tion maintained throughout the year. At the end of 2025, 
we refinanced our sustainability-oriented revolving 
credit facility of €150m for at least another three years, 
thereby achieving long-term positive effects by managing 
upcoming maturities at an early stage, and maintaining a 
strong and diversified capital structure. 
Return of excess capital to shareholders 
One of our key corporate priorities, in addition to focus-
ing on profitability and simplifying the business, is re-
turn of capital to shareholders. Since 2018 we have re-
turned c. €1.5bn of capital back to shareholders through a 
combination of dividends and share buybacks, helping 
drive a total accounting return of 43% between the end of 
2018 and today and outperforming the EPRA benchmark 
and an equal weight index of peers. Despite this return of 
capital, our business is larger (in GAV) and more resilient

STRATEGIC PRIORITIES 
We aim to maintain a proactive, disciplined approach to 
our activities as an investor, manager, and developer of 
modern prime office. In view of the continuously devel-
oping macro and market conditions, we will continue to 
focus on securing and increasing our competitiveness 
and resilience. With this aim, we continue to prioritize 
the following strategic objectives: 
Focus on profitability 
– Drive performance through prime portfolio quality and
less dilution from low returning assets.
– Reduce vacancy and capture rent reversion.
– Continued operational efficiency and improved margins
via process improvement and structural cost savings.
Accelerate non-core disposals
– Increase critical mass and economies of scale in strate-
gic core markets of Berlin and Munich.
– Dispose assets that don’t fit minimum quality require-
ments in terms of location, specification, technology,
certification, sustainability, and earnings potential.
– Potential further market exits in the course of continu-
ous portfolio optimization.
Accretive re-investment and organic growth 
– Organic growth through continued profitable develop-
ment.
– Offset declining earnings from sales with profitable de-
velopment income / gain.
Continued simplification of the operational platform 
– Continued outsourcing of non-value add activities.
– Structural consolidation as geographic footprint
shrinks.
Maintain strong balance sheet and financial ratios 
– Continuously optimize capital structure, address up-
coming maturities well in advance.
– Maintain <=50% gross LTV, and investment grade fi-
nancial policy.
Return excess capital to shareholders 
– Distribute proceeds from non-core sales via dividends.
– Share buyback program.
Select external investment 
– Maintain critical mass, grow asset base in key markets
(Berlin, Munich) through select external investment.
– Focus on higher value add development / redevelop-
ment opportunities.
OUTLOOK 2026 
Market environment 
The recent military escalation in the Middle East, par-
ticularly the tense developments surrounding Iran and 
potential restrictions on oil and gas shipments through 
the Strait of Hormuz, has led to significantly higher en-
ergy prices and volatile reactions in the capital markets. 
There are limited immediate impacts on CA Immo; how-
ever, potential indirect effects—such as on economic 
growth, inflation rates, and financing costs —cannot cur-
rently be estimated due to the uncertain short-, medium-, 
and long-term outlook. 
As a result of these events, the market outlook for 2026 
is marked by a high degree of uncertainty not only for the 
global economy but also for European real estate markets.  
Against this backdrop, we are focusing on navigating the 
economic cycle and mitigating potential external shocks 
as effectively as possible by continuing to focus on our 
strategic priorities and maintaining our prudent risk 
management policy.

plans, which are measured at fair value. The net item from pension obligations is presented as a provision, comprising 
the present value of the obligations less the fair value of the plan asset.

Revenue recognition according to IFRS 15 
Revenues are to be recognised in according with IFRS 15, when a performance obligation is fulfilled by transfer-
ring an agreed good or service to the customer. An asset is deemed to be transferred when the customer gains control of 
that asset. Control over a good or a service is transferred at a specific point in time if the obligation is not satisfied over 
a period of time. If one of the following criteria is met, the performance obligation is fulfilled over a period of time: 
a) the customer simultaneously receives and consumes all of the benefits provided by the entity as the entity per-
forms; 
b) the entity’s performance creates or enhances an asset that the customer controls as the asset is created; or 
c) the entity’s performance does not create an asset with an alternative use to the entity and the entity has an enforce-
able claim of payment for performance completed to date 
If a performance obligation is met over a period of time, according to IFRS 15, the contract related transaction price as 
well as contract performance and acquisition costs must be recognised as revenues or expenses, in accordance with the 
performace progress as at balance sheet date. The cost-to-cost method is used in CA Immo for the ongoing monitoring 
of construction projects and is a reliable method for determining the progress of the service performance. Thereby, to 
determine the performance progress, the ratio of the contract respectively construction costs incurred up to balance 
sheet date to the estimated total contract costs, respectively construction costs (cost-to-cost method) is applied.
