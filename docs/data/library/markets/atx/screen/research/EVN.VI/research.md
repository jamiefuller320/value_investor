## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, EVN offers a classic **asset-backed utility discount**: the screen’s Schloss low-P/B, earnings-yield, and Dreman contrarian models pass, supported by a balance sheet with ~€6.3bn common equity, net debt ~€1.1bn, and interest coverage that appears comfortable on Yahoo figures (EBIT ~€629m vs interest expense ~€54m). Leverage is conservative relative to peers, and the dividend family passes with ~3.2% yield — not top-quartile, but adequate alongside low multiples.

Business quality, as inferred from Yahoo only, is **adequate but not exceptional**: ROE ~8.1% fails Buffett/Moat screens; Piotroski F-Score is 6/9 (ROA and leverage trends not improving); statutory earnings growth ~46.5% likely reflects base effects rather than sustainable momentum (EPS fell from €2.97 in FY2023 to €2.45 in FY2025). Revenue has stabilised at ~€3.0bn after falling from the FY2022–23 spike (~€3.8–4.1bn), consistent with a post-crisis normalisation rather than structural decline.

The buy case rests on **valuation margin of safety** (below book, ~10× earnings) in a defensive sector, with operational cash flow (~€935m) demonstrating underlying cash generation despite capex absorption. It is **not** a deep-quality compounder: FCF-yield, earnings-quality, and acquirer’s-multiple models fail. A value investor would accumulate on the screen signal but size for regulatory/governance and capex-cycle risk, and treat FY2025’s collapsed FCF as a diligence priority once filings are available.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory:** EVN operates in Austrian (and international, e.g. Bulgaria) regulated energy and environmental-services markets. Allowed returns, tariff resets, and network-investment frameworks drive earnings and capex; none of this can be verified from the empty filing pack. Deferred revenue (~€786m) and provisions (~€368m) point to long-dated regulatory and decommissioning obligations that require annual-report note disclosure.

**Cyclical / commodity:** Revenue normalised sharply from FY2022–23 peaks (~€4.1bn → ~€3.0bn), showing residual commodity and trading exposure despite the regulated core. FY2022’s negative FCF and write-offs illustrate earnings volatility in stress scenarios.

**Governance:** Lower Austria historically holds a **majority stake** (commonly cited ~51%; not re-verified in this pack). Majority public-sector ownership raises minority-shareholder risks around dividend policy, related-party transactions, and strategic asset sales (FY2025 assets held for sale ~€606m warrants filing scrutiny).

**Pension / provisions:** Long-term provisions €368m and current pension-related liabilities ~€13m appear on the Yahoo balance sheet; funding status and discount-rate sensitivity are **unknown** without filing notes.

**Liquidity / leverage:** Net debt/EBITDA ~1.1× (Yahoo) is moderate, but **current ratio ~1.15** is tight for a Graham Defensive profile, and FY2025 FCF collapse raises questions about self-funded capex and dividends if investment stays elevated.

**Accounting / earnings quality:** Screen flags weak free-cash conversion; large investment portfolio (~€2.9bn AFS securities) and discontinued-ops swings add complexity. Piotroski score 6/9 with deteriorating ROA/leverage trends. No primary filing to assess impairment history (FY2022–24 had repeated write-offs per Yahoo).

**Competitive:** Regional utility competition is limited in networks, but generation and international operations face market and political risk (Bulgaria exposure referenced in public domain; **not confirmed in this source pack**).

**Evidence risk:** Zero news articles and zero filings in the research corpus — unusually thin for an ATX large-cap.
