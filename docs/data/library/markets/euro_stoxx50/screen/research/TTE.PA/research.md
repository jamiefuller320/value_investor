# TotalEnergies SE (TTE.PA) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:20:21.141955+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
TotalEnergies SE is a globally integrated energy major transitioning toward a 50% gas / 30% oil / 20% electricity-and-molecules mix by 2030, while returning substantial capital through dividends and buybacks. The quantitative screen flags the stock as a strong buy on cheapness (P/E 10.8, P/B 1.5, yield 4.8%), quality (ROE 12.5%, earnings quality passes), dividend growth, GARP, and moderate leverage (D/E 51%). FY2025 filings confirm durable cash generation ($27.3bn operating cash flow) despite earnings normalisation from the 2023 commodity peak, supporting the value case. The central debate is whether current valuation adequately compensates for cyclical earnings decline, energy-transition impairment risk, and geopolitical exposure (Russia/Yamal LNG, Mozambique LNG force majeure), versus the LNG-led growth pipeline and shareholder-return policy.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy rating rests on five converging value families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—all passing with full data coverage (20/20 metrics). At P/E 10.8 and P/B 1.5, TotalEnergies trades below typical integrated-major multiples despite a balance sheet that remains investment-grade in character: $117.5bn equity on $291.1bn assets, financial debt of $49.0bn against $26.2bn cash (Form 20-F FY2025). The company passes classic value screens (Graham Enterprising, earnings yield, FCF yield, Dreman contrarian, Magic Formula, acquirer’s multiple) and dividend screens (high yield, dividend growth), indicating the market prices in excessive cyclical pessimism relative to underlying cash economics.

Business quality supports the screen rather than contradicting it. TotalEnergies is among the most integrated European majors, with scale across E&P, LNG, refining-chemicals, marketing, and growing integrated power (VSB wind/solar acquisition, US power plants). FY2025 net income (TotalEnergies share) of $13.1bn and EPS of $5.78, while down 39% from the 2023 peak ($21.4bn / $8.67), still generated $10.4bn of free cash flow (operating cash flow $27.3bn less capex $17.0bn) and funded $8.1bn of dividends plus $7.5bn of share repurchases. That capital-allocation discipline—returning roughly 150% of FCF while investing in LNG and renewables—appeals directly to value investors seeking income plus optionality on gas/LNG demand.

The screen’s risk family pass (D/E 51%) aligns with filing evidence: net leverage rose in 2025 as financial debt increased to $49.0bn from $43.5bn, but the company remains far from distress territory and received unqualified audit opinions on both financial statements and internal controls (Form 20-F FY2024 and FY2025). For a value investor, TotalEnergies offers a rare combination of double-digit earnings yield, near-5% dividend yield with growth, active buybacks, and a credible LNG growth narrative—at a price that embeds mid-cycle, not peak-cycle, assumptions.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and earnings trajectory.** FY2025 net income fell for a second consecutive year (−17% YoY after −26% in FY2024). The screen’s cheap multiples partly reflect this downtrend; a prolonged oil/gas price downturn would further compress earnings and FCF, straining the dividend-plus-buyback policy.

**Energy transition and impairments.** Filings embed long-term Brent convergence toward IEA NZE pricing ($25.8/bbl by 2050), with sensitivity tests at −10%/−20% price scenarios and $200/t CO₂ costs. Management acknowledges that “additional impairment charges” may arise as transition assumptions evolve. FY2025 impairments of $250m were lower than 2024 but remain a live risk for long-dated upstream assets.

**Geopolitical and project-specific exposures (filing language).**
- **Russia/Ukraine:** Russian assets were fully impaired in 2022 except Yamal LNG (~$5.2bn equity-accounted investment per FY2024 auditor commentary). FY2025 filing states an impairment test on Yamal LNG found no charge, but warns that “activities in Russia… could be affected” by future sanctions.
- **Mozambique LNG:** Force majeure declared since April 2021 following security deterioration in Cabo Delgado; personnel withdrawn. Auditors assess asset values “in the context of force majeure” and assumptions on project restart—timeline uncertainty persists.
- **Arctic LNG 2:** 10% stake fully impaired in 2022; deconsolidated from equity method since Dec 2023 due to US sanctions.

**Leverage trend.** Financial debt rose to $49.0bn in 2025 while the company returned $15.6bn via dividends and buybacks against $10.4bn FCF. This is not acute distress, but rising net debt during an earnings downcycle warrants monitoring.

**Governance and pension.** Employee benefit obligations of $2.0bn (FY2025) and long-term provisions of $17.3bn represent material off-balance-sheet-style commitments. No material governance red flags surfaced in the filing extracts reviewed.

**Screen limitations.** The quantitative risk pass does not capture project delays (e.g., Saudi refinery delays referenced in news), volume declines, or LNG margin volatility. Timing data (RSI, vs SMA200) is insufficient in the screen snapshot.
