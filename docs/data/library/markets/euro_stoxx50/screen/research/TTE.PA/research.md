# TotalEnergies SE (TTE.PA) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T14:49:24.113487+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
TotalEnergies is a globally integrated energy major trading at a modest multiple (screen P/E 10.8, P/B 1.5, yield 4.8%) despite a diversified upstream, LNG, refining/trading and power portfolio and a capital-return framework that combines dividends and buybacks. FY2025 filing data show earnings and cash generation below the 2022–23 commodity peak, but the balance sheet remains investment-grade in character: $117.5bn equity, ~$34bn net debt (Yahoo; filing-derived below) and $26.2bn cash at year-end. The central debate is whether current-cycle strength—evident in Q2 2026 results and pre-release indicators (Brent ~$104/b, strong refining)—sustains shareholder returns, or whether normalising commodity prices, transition capex and geopolitical disruption (Middle East production, Russia/Yamal LNG, Mozambique LNG) compress the value case that the quantitative screen flags.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy rating rests on a rare combination for a Euro Stoxx 50 energy name: **cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk** families all passing (5/4 families; conviction 79%, stable four weeks). Key metrics—P/E 10.8, P/B 1.5, 4.8% yield, ROE 12.5%, debt/equity 51%—align with classic value screens (Graham Enterprising, earnings/FCF yield, Lynch/Neff PEG, Magic Formula, Dreman contrarian, dividend growth).

Business quality supports the signal. TotalEnergies is among the most integrated European majors: Exploration & Production, Integrated LNG, Refining & Chemicals/trading, Marketing, and a growing Integrated Power footprint (EPH flexible-generation deal, VSB renewables acquisition per FY2025 20-F). Management prioritises **cash over volume**, with FY2025 organic capex of $17.0bn against $27.3bn operating cash flow (Form 20-F 2025). Capital returns are material: $8.1bn dividends and $7.7bn buybacks in 2025 (20-F equity note), with Q2 2026 news confirming a **5.9% interim dividend increase** to €0.90/share and a **$1.5bn Q3 buyback**.

For a value investor, the hook is a **double-digit earnings yield on normalised earnings** plus a shareholder-return policy that has persisted through the down-cycle in reported net income (TE share fell from $21.4bn in 2023 to $13.1bn in 2025, yet distributions continued). Recent Q2 2026 operational momentum (production ~2.4 Mboe/d, elevated Brent and European refining margins) suggests near-term earnings power may exceed the trailing screen inputs, though that cyclical uplift is precisely what screens underweight.

**Impairments and transition assumptions (20-F narrative)**  
FY2025 upstream/LNG impairments were **$250m** (TE share) versus **$982m** in 2024. Impairment testing uses long-run oil prices converging to IEA NZE ($25.7/b in 2050) and CO₂ at $100/t (escalating 2% p.a. beyond 2031). Yamal LNG equity-accounted investment carried at **$5,835m** (31 Dec 2025); Russian assets otherwise fully impaired since 2022. Auditors flagged reserve estimates and impairment judgements as critical audit matters—standard for the sector but material to book value.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Commodity and cycle risk.** Screen cheapness partly reflects normalising oil/gas/refining margins. FY2023–25 earnings fell ~39% (TE share) while the share price screen still reads cheap—implying the market discounts sustainability of returns. Q2 2026 strength (elevated Brent, refining) may prove mean-reverting.

**Geopolitical and project risk (filing language).**  
- **Russia/Ukraine:** 20-F states Yamal LNG ($5.8bn carrying value) could be affected by future sanctions; Novatek/Arctic LNG 2 stakes deconsolidated/impaired.  
- **Mozambique LNG:** 20-F (FY2024) references ongoing **force majeure** since personnel withdrawal (Apr 2021); FY2025 20-F audit matter list de-emphasises Mozambique but project restart timing remains uncertain.  
- **Middle East:** Q2 2026 indicators cite ~210 kboe/d production impact and unlifted volumes recognised at sub-$70/b—near-term earnings distortion risk.

**Energy transition and litigation.** Paris Judicial Court (Jun 2026 filing body) dismissed claims to cap oil/gas production under France’s duty-of-vigilance law but ordered Scope 3 inclusion in the vigilance plan—a partial win with ongoing reputational/regulatory overhang. U.S. pension-fund divestment talk (May 2026 news, offshore wind exit) signals ESG friction not captured by financial screens. Impairment sensitivity to IEA NZE pricing remains a long-dated book-value risk.

**Operational/trading.** Q2 2026 guidance flags **Integrated LNG underperformance in gas trading** after Q1 outperformance—earnings volatility in a key growth segment.

**Balance-sheet drift.** Net debt increased ~$7bn in FY2025 despite strong OCF; worth monitoring against the two-point gearing improvement guided for Q2 2026 (absolute gearing level not stated in available filing body).

**Pensions and contingencies.** Employee benefits **$2.0bn** (20-F); long-term provisions **$13.5bn** (non-current). No “going concern” qualification in auditor reports. Covenant detail not present in available body extracts—**not verified in this pack**.

**Governance/capital structure.** Annual employee capital increase (up to 18m shares at €62, May 2026 filing) is modest (0.8% cap) but recurrent dilution channel.
