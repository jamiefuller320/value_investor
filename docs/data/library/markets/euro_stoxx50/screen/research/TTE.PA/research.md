## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
TotalEnergies SE is a globally integrated energy major (E&P, LNG, refining-marketing, growing power) returning substantial capital via dividends and buybacks. The quantitative screen rates the name **strong buy** (14/22 models, composite 82%): cheapness (P/E 10.8, P/B 1.5, yield 4.8%), quality (ROE 12.5%), dividend growth, GARP, and moderate leverage (D/E 51%) all pass. Yahoo-sourced financials (no primary filing bodies in corpus) show earnings normalising from the 2023 peak but still cash-generative: FY2025 net income $13.1bn, operating cash flow $27.3bn, FCF ~$10.4bn. The central debate is whether depressed multiples adequately compensate for cyclical earnings decline, rising net debt, and transition/geopolitical risk, versus a Q2 2026 earnings rebound (news: $5.4bn net income, dividend +5.9%, $1.5bn buyback) and LNG-led growth.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal rests on five converging families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with full metric coverage (20/20, data quality 1.0) and persistent conviction (79%, four weeks stable). At P/E 10.8 and P/B 1.5, the market prices mid-cycle pessimism into a business that still passes classic value screens (Graham Enterprising, earnings yield, FCF yield, Magic Formula, acquirer’s multiple, Dreman contrarian) and income screens (high dividend yield, dividend growth).

Business quality supports rather than contradicts the screen. TotalEnergies operates at scale across the value chain; FY2025 Yahoo figures show revenue $182.3bn, EBITDA $38.9bn, and ROE 12.5% despite a second year of earnings compression (net income down from $15.8bn in 2024 and $21.4bn in 2023). FCF of ~$10.4bn (operating cash flow $27.3bn less capex $17.0bn) funds an ~4.8% yield and active buybacks ($7.7bn repurchased in 2025), appealing to value investors seeking income plus LNG/gas optionality.

The risk-family pass (D/E 51%) aligns with balance-sheet data: stockholders’ equity $114.9bn on $291.1bn assets, total debt $60.1bn, cash $24.2bn (Yahoo, FY2025). Leverage has risen—net debt $34.0bn in 2025 vs $23.3bn in 2024—but remains manageable relative to cash generation. For a value investor, TotalEnergies offers double-digit earnings yield, near-5% dividend yield with recent growth, and buyback support at multiples that embed normalisation, not peak-cycle, assumptions.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Data limitation.** Without filing bodies, going-concern language, contingency disclosures, covenant terms, and auditor critical audit matters cannot be verified from primary sources. Risks below draw on financial trends and news; filing-specific language is unavailable.

**Cyclical earnings trajectory.** FY2025 net income fell for a second consecutive year (−17% YoY after −26% in 2024). Cheap multiples partly reflect this downtrend; a prolonged oil/gas price downturn would further compress earnings and FCF, straining the dividend-plus-buyback policy.

**Leverage and distribution policy.** Net debt rose to $34.0bn in 2025 (Yahoo) while the company returned ~$15.8bn via dividends and buybacks against $10.4bn FCF. Not acute distress at current cash-flow levels, but rising leverage during an earnings downcycle warrants monitoring.

**Volume and margin mix.** “TotalEnergies Q2 Earnings & Sales Miss Estimates as Lower Volumes Hurt” (2026-07-24) flags production/volume weakness despite strong absolute profits. LNG margin volatility and weaker gas performance were cited in Q2 2026 coverage.

**Energy transition.** News references low-carbon growth strategy (“TotalEnergies outlines low-carbon growth…”, 2026-06-26) and the company’s 2030 mix target (50% gas / 30% oil / 20% electricity-molecules, from prior research context). Transition capex and long-dated asset repricing are not quantifiable from available filings.

**Geopolitical and project risk.** Broader Middle East tension appears in market news (2026-07-24 global equity inflow article). Insurers cutting premiums for projects outside the Middle East (2026-07-23) underscores regional risk rotation. Saudi refinery delays noted (“TotalEnergies Stock Still Looks Cheap On Saudi Refinery Delays”, 2026-06-30). Russia/Mozambique/Yamal LNG exposures referenced in prior research cannot be confirmed without filing bodies.

**Governance and pension.** Employee benefits $2.0bn (Yahoo FY2025). No governance red flags in news manifest; no management changes reported.

**Screen limitations.** The quantitative risk pass does not capture project delays, volume declines, or timing (RSI and vs-SMA200 are null in the screen snapshot).
