# TotalEnergies SE (TTE.PA) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-04T07:42:36.995452+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
TotalEnergies is a globally integrated energy major trading at a conventional value discount (P/E 11.3, yield 4.6%, P/B 1.6) while operating a diversified upstream–LNG–downstream–power portfolio with active capital returns. The quantitative screen rates the name **buy** (13/22 models; all five factor families passed). Interim filings show a sharp H1 2026 earnings and cash-flow inflection on higher commodity prices and refining strength, with gearing improving to 13.1%. The central debate is whether current cash generation and shareholder returns (rising dividends, sustained buybacks) adequately compensate for cyclical commodity exposure, Middle East production disruption, and transition-related regulatory and litigation overhang.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The buy screen rests on a coherent value stack: **cheapness** (Graham Enterprising, earnings yield, FCF yield, acquirer's multiple), **income** (high dividend yield, dividend growth, Neff PEGY), **quality-of-cash** (earnings quality, magic formula), and **GARP** (Lynch PEG ~0.11 on ~1% model earnings growth). At 48% debt/equity and ROE 14.5%, leverage is moderate for the sector; returns are adequate though not moat-grade (economic moat and Buffett quality models fail on ROE/margin thresholds).

Business quality supports rather than contradicts the screen. Per the Universal Registration Document 2025 (IR allowlist filing body), FY2025 CFFO was $27.8bn, adjusted net income (TotalEnergies share) $15.6bn, ROACE 12.6%, and hydrocarbon production 2,529 kboe/d. H1 2026 results (half-year financial report, July 2026) accelerated: adjusted net income $11.4bn (+47% YoY), CFFO $18.4bn (+35%), adjusted EBITDA $25.7bn (+27%), with Refining & Chemicals adjusted net operating income up nearly fivefold to $3.4bn. Management confirmed a €0.90/share interim dividend (+5.9%) and a $1.5bn Q3 buyback.

For a value investor, the hook is twofold: (1) statistically cheap equity with persistent screen conviction (five weeks at buy, composite 56%, stable trend); (2) a business that converts commodity cycles into tangible distributions while reshaping the portfolio (EPH flexible power, Shell European onshore renewables, Namibia Orange Basin entry, Arctic LNG 2 exit). Screen quality-family passes are credible on cash conversion and dividend record, tempered by weak liquidity (current ratio ~0.97) and a sub-threshold Piotroski F-Score (5/9).

### Balance sheet and liquidity

URD gearing metrics indicate manageable leverage; H1 2026 improvement to 13.1% confirms deleveraging amid higher prices. The screen flags weak liquidity (current ratio ~0.97; Graham Defensive fail)—consistent with negative working capital typical of integrated oils but a genuine constraint in a commodity collapse. Employee benefit liabilities were ~$2.0bn at FY2025 year-end per **Yahoo fallback**; the 20-F body references pension plan methodology but does not provide an extractable funded-status figure in this pack.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and operational:** Revenue, earnings, and CFFO remain highly sensitive to Brent, refining margins, and LNG spreads. H1 2026 strength partly reflects elevated prices (H1 Brent ~$92/b per half-year report vs ~$72/b prior year); mean reversion would compress the value case quickly. Middle East conflict reduced reported production (−2% YoY H1); Q2 guidance flagged unlifted production accounting effects and prior LNG trading underperformance.

**Regulatory and litigation:** The Paris Judicial Court dismissed NGO/City of Paris claims to halt new oil and gas projects (June 2026 filing body) but required Scope 3 inclusion in the duty-of-vigilance plan; TotalEnergies filed an appeal (27 July 2026). Climate litigation remains a standing board agenda item per URD. EU sanctions compliance on Russia is structural: Arctic LNG 2 exit (August 2026 news) with ~$1.3bn reimbursement rights subject to sanctions—recovery uncertain.

**Geopolitical and portfolio:** Expansion into higher-risk jurisdictions (UAE gas, Namibia PEL 83 entry, September 2026) adds exploration optionality but reputational and security risk alongside Middle East operational exposure.

**Financial structure:** Gearing rose sharply in FY2025 before H1 2026 improvement; lease-adjusted gearing was 19.7% at year-end. Screen failures on financial health and current ratio warrant caution in stress scenarios. URD notes $2–3bn seasonal working-capital build in Q1—consistent with Q1 2026 negative FCF quarter per **Yahoo quarterly cashflow fallback**.

**Transition/strategy:** Portfolio pivot (Shell European renewables acquisition, EPH gas-to-power, selective renewables divestitures) creates execution and valuation debate; May 2026 reporting flagged potential U.S. pension fund divestment over offshore wind strategy—an ESG/governance overhang not captured by quantitative screens.

**Going concern / covenants:** No going-concern qualification appears in available filing bodies. The 20-F references contingent liabilities and pension obligations in truncated notes; specific covenant thresholds are not extractable from this pack.
