ETTelecom Desk ETTelecom Published On Mar 6, 2026 at 01:30 PM IST Read by 100 Professionals Select ETTelecom as Preferred Choice on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook NEW DELHI: Finnish telecom gear maker Nokia is scaling down its investments in venture funds, saying it no longer sees them as “strategic” to its business. Nokia, in its annual report for 2025, said that it has concluded a strategic review of its venture fund investment activities, and as a result, it “no longer views broad-based venture fund investments as having a strategic role and has initiated a process to scale down these investments”. Consequently, the company has also moved the financial reporting of venture fund investments from “operating activities” to “financial income”. Advt As of December 2025, the fair value of the venture fund investments and similar investments equaled 857 million euros (~₹9,118 crore), compared to 865 million euros as of December 2024. Nokia has also dialed down its investment commitment to 221 million euros as of December 2025, compared to 306 million euros as of December 2024. “As a limited partner in venture funds, Nokia is committed to capital contributions and entitled to cash distributions according to the respective partnership agreements and underlying fund activities,” the vendor said in its annual report released on Thursday. The Espoo, Finland-headquartered mobile network gear vendor stated that it makes financing commitments to several unlisted venture funds that make technology-related investments. At present, most of these investments are managed by the global venture capital (VC) firm NGP Capital , which backs startups and entrepreneurs in the data infrastructure, artificial intelligence (AI), semiconductors, and space domains. Under chief executive Justin Hotard, who took charge in February last year, Nokia has undertaken several measures, including a business reorganisation into two broad units – Network Infrastructure and Mobile Infrastructure – to take a lead in artificial intelligence (AI)-native networks and the sixth-generation (6G) technology. Advt Hotard has also been bullish about pursuing data centre opportunities for Nokia amid the AI boom, which is attracting multi-billion-dollar investments from Big Tech players and telecom companies.
