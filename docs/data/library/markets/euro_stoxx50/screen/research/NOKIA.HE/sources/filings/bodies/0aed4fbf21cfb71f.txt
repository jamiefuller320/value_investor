By MBN Staff MBN staff Mon, 10/21/2024 09:53 0 min read Leer en Español DIA READ ARTICLE Ready to read Reading... 1x Nokia's decision to cut 2,350 jobs, including 2,000 in China and 350 in Europe, aligns with a broader trend of layoffs in the tech industry as companies aim to reduce costs amid declining revenues. Despite the layoffs, Nokia reported increased operating profits, indicating a focus on cost reductions while preserving research and development efforts. This decision affects nearly one-fifth of Nokia's workforce in China, reports Reuters. However, while a spokesperson for Nokia confirmed the 350 job cuts in Europe, they did not provide details about the layoffs in China. According to the company's annual report, Nokia employed 10,400 people in China and 37,400 in Europe as of December 2023. These layoffs are part of a broader strategy announced last year, which aimed to reduce its workforce by up to 14,000 positions to achieve cost savings between US$868 million and US$1.3 billion by 2026. This decision is likely the result of Nokia's business in China being adversely affected in 2019, when western countries began restricting Huawei from operating in their markets. This affected Huawei's ability to conduct business internationally, resulting in reduced contracts from Chinese telecommunications operators for both Nokia and its competitor, Ericsson, according to Reuters. In 2019, Nokia derived 27% of its net sales from China; this figure has plummeted to below 6% in the latest quarter. Despite the job cuts, Nokia reported a 9% increase in third-quarter operating profit, primarily due to its ongoing cost-reduction efforts. However, net sales fell short of analyst expectations, resulting in a 4% decline in the company’s stock price. A spokesperson stated that Nokia has already realized US$542.15 million in gross savings from its cost-cutting measures. Pekka Lundmark, CEO, Nokia, emphasized the company’s commitment to maintaining its research and development output during this transition, saying: “I am happy with the pace of cost reduction. We are actually a bit ahead of the schedule that we had.” As of now, Nokia employs over 78,500 individuals, having reduced its workforce from an initial total of approximately 86,000. The company is focused on reaching a headcount of between 72,000 and 77,000 by 2026. These layoffs coincide with an ongoing trend in the technology industry , as companies like Samsung, Cisco, Intel, and Meta are also laying off employees to streamline operations and reduce costs.
