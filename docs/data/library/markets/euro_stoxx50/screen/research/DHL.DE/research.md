# Deutsche Post AG (DHL.DE) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T18:38:03.574951+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Deutsche Post DHL Group offers a globally diversified logistics franchise trading at modest multiples (P/E 18.5, P/B 2.7, dividend yield 3.4%) despite delivering FY2025 EBIT of €6.1bn and an 8% increase in basic EPS to €3.09. The quantitative screen flags strong value and quality characteristics — eight models passed across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families, with 70% conviction over four stable weeks. The central debate is whether cyclical freight weakness, rising net debt and a still-muted macro backdrop cap upside after a strong share-price run, or whether Strategy 2030 cost actions (Fit for Growth contributed >€600m EBIT in FY2025) and an upgraded 2026 outlook (Group EBIT now expected to exceed €6.5bn following Q2) justify accumulation at current levels. Primary filing coverage is solid for FY2025 annual data but thin on interim bodies; treat H1/H2 2026 trajectory as headline-driven until half-year filings arrive.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy rating aligns with a business that combines scale, cash generation and shareholder returns. DHL Group is the world’s leading time-definite express operator (#1 TDI), a top-three air/ocean forwarder, #1 contract logistics provider and #1 parcel operator in Germany — a portfolio that produced Group ROIC of 13.9% (+20bp yoy) in FY2025 despite revenue falling 1.6% to €82.9bn on currency effects.

For a value investor, the hook is valuation against normalized earning power: at P/E 18.5 and P/B 2.7, the market prices in cyclical caution while FY2025 EBIT grew 3.7% to €6,103m and FCF excluding net M&A rose 8% to €3,201m. The screen’s passes on Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Piotroski F-Score (8/9 family coverage), Composite Value and Earnings Quality reflect a combination of reasonable price, 15.6% ROE, 3.4% yield and durable operating cash conversion (FCF conversion ~91% of consolidated net profit in FY2025 per the Q4 presentation).

Management is actively returning capital — €1.4bn share buybacks in 2025 under a €6bn programme through end-2026, plus a proposed dividend increase to €1.90 — while investing in GDP+ verticals (Life Sciences & Healthcare, New Energy, e-commerce, AI/datacentres). The Q2 2026 guidance raise (EQS-Adhoc, 7 July 2026) suggests the cost-and-yield playbook is gaining traction faster than the cautious March 2026 initial outlook implied. Structural optionality from the planned Post & Parcel Germany legal carve-down (AGM vote 5 May 2026; expected effective H2 2026) could further clarify the DHL growth story, though execution risk remains.

Revenue remains below the 2022 peak (€94.4bn), reflecting post-pandemic normalisation and FX headwinds, but FY2025 marked an inflection: EBIT rose for the first time since 2022, return on sales improved 40bp to 7.4%, and basic EPS grew 8% yoy. Reported FCF fell 22% to €2,295m on higher net M&A outflows (€906m, including Life Sciences acquisitions); excluding M&A, FCF rose 8% to €3,201m — the metric management steers to.

**FY2025 divisional performance (Q4 presentation)**

- **Express:** EBIT €3,162m; margin 13.5% excl. non-recurring items (+70bp). Six consecutive quarters of yoy EBIT improvement; yield management (+3% net price change) offset TDI volume decline.
- **Global Forwarding, Freight:** EBIT €756m; Q4 EBIT €163m (-36% yoy). Gross profit pressured by freight-rate declines (ocean GP/TEU -16%, air GP/t -5% in Q4). €84m Cost of Change in FY2025.
- **Supply Chain:** EBIT €1,161m; margin 6.5% (+50bp). New contract signings total contract value €7.5bn.
- **eCommerce:** EBIT €379m. UK Evri deconsolidation and Iberia portfolio optimisation weigh on reported revenue; European B2C parcel growth +9.9% like-for-like.
- **Post & Parcel Germany:** EBIT €1,032m — back above the €1bn mid-term target; mail structural decline partly offset by parcel growth and pricing.

Group EBIT excluding non-recurring items was €6,229m (+7.1% yoy); reported EBIT €6,103m after €126m net negative one-offs (Cost of Change, M&A, legal provisions).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and trade exposure.** The 2025 annual report editorial describes global trade volatility “greater than at any time in at least two decades,” citing geopolitical tensions, tariffs and regulatory friction — particularly on China–US lanes. Global Forwarding, Freight remains the most cyclically exposed division; Q4 gross-profit declines on lower market rates (-16% ocean GP/TEU) drove a 36% yoy EBIT drop. Forward-looking statements in both filings caution that actual results may differ materially from projections.

**Leverage trend.** Net debt has risen every year since 2022 (€15.9bn → €21.5bn) and net gearing reached 48.7%. Equity ratio compression (34.6% → 31.9%) partly reflects buybacks and dividend outflows against a flat-to-down revenue base. No covenant language appears in available body extracts; this remains unverified.

**Pension and lease obligations.** Pension and post-retirement liabilities are material on the balance sheet (Yahoo fallback: ~€4.5bn combined current and non-current provisions). Capitalised lease obligations (~€14.8bn per Yahoo) add to total indebtedness beyond reported long-term debt of €9.0bn.

**Execution and restructuring.** Fit for Growth delivered >€600m EBIT but incurred €245m Cost of Change in FY2025 across divisions. The planned P&P legal carve-down (AGM 5 May 2026, effective H2 2026) introduces governance and transition risk. Board changes in 2025 (Tim Scharwath departure; Oscar de Bok moved to Forwarding; Hendrik Venter to Supply Chain) add management-transition uncertainty in the weakest division.

**Competitive and regulatory.** Forwarding competes with DSV and Kuehne+Nagel in a fragmented market with rate pressure. German mail faces structural letter-volume decline; regulatory pricing caps mail increases (10.6% headroom used in 2025, flat regulated mail pricing implied for 2026 per presentation). DHL eCommerce European revenue reportedly fell 11% (news, 6 May 2026) — segment-specific weakness not fully visible in Group aggregates.

**Governance / legal.** Q4 non-recurring items include €64m Express legal provisions and Group Functions legal alignment costs. Supervisory Board discussions referenced “potential risks from current lawsuits” (annual report). No going-concern qualification appears in available extracts; auditor issued unqualified opinion on FY2025 statements per Supervisory Board report.

**Screen limitations.** Timing signal is “insufficient_data”; RSI and price-vs-SMA200 are null. Recent share-price momentum (37–44% one-year rallies cited in news) may compress margin of safety despite screen metrics.
