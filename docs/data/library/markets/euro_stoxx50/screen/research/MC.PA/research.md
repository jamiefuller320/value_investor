# LVMH Moët Hennessy - Louis Vuitton, Société Européenne (MC.PA) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T14:50:12.323277+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
LVMH remains the benchmark luxury conglomerate, and the quantitative screen flags it as a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk metrics (P/E 23.0, P/B 3.7, yield 2.6%, ROE 16.2%, PEGY 0.09). FY2025 primary filings show reported revenue fell to €80.8bn (−5% reported, −1% organic) and profit from recurring operations to €17.8bn, but H2 organic growth turned positive (+1%) and operating free cash flow rose 8% to €11.3bn. The valuation hook is a quality compounder trading at a modest multiple after a multi-year de-rating, with cash generation and balance-sheet strength intact. The central debate is whether stabilising trends in Asia and Sephora offset ongoing pressure in Fashion & Leather Goods and cognac, and whether reported earnings decline (−13% net profit to €10.9bn) is cyclical trough or structural normalisation.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy signal rests on five factor families passing simultaneously — unusual breadth for a mega-cap luxury name. Neff PEGY (0.09) and Magic Formula passes suggest the market prices near-term earnings weakness (reported growth −1.4%) into a valuation that still embeds franchise quality; Buffett Quality and Financial Health passes align with filing evidence of a 22% recurring operating margin, €6.9bn net financial debt (down 26% YoY), and €11.3bn operating free cash flow. Dividend Growth support is corroborated by the proposed €13.00 per share dividend for 2025 (interim €5.50 paid December 2025; balance €7.50 due April 2026).

For a value investor, the case is not deep cyclical cheapness but “quality at a reasonable price” after de-rating: P/B 3.7 on a business generating 35% operating margin in Fashion & Leather Goods and consolidating beauty retail leadership via Sephora (+28% profit from recurring operations). The screen’s 40% conviction score (building, 2 weeks at signal) reflects this is an early-stage re-rating rather than a distressed bargain. Business quality — 75+ maisons, vertical retail (Sephora, DFS), and pricing power at Louis Vuitton and Loro Piana — supports the screen’s quality and risk passes despite top-line softness.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and demand.** FY2025 filings explicitly cite a disrupted geopolitical environment, trade tensions affecting China and US cognac demand, and a luxury slowdown since 2023 after exceptional prior years. Fashion & Leather Goods revenue remains −5% organic; Wines & Spirits profit −25%. Forward-looking language warns of an uncertain 2026 macro backdrop and references risk factors in the Universal Registration Document — that document is not in the source pack.

**China and brand reputation.** News coverage flags Louis Vuitton’s China IP/nationalism controversy (“Louis Vuitton’s China Dilemma,” 17 July 2026) and ongoing China exposure as a valuation debate (“Chinese Luxury Rebound Puts LVMH Valuation And China Exposure In Focus,” 3 June 2026). Filing bodies confirm rest-of-Asia improvement but do not quantify China separately.

**Governance and structure.** Family-controlled group (Bernard Arnault, Chairman/CEO); no governance red flags in filing bodies. Minority-interest purchase commitments (€6.3bn liability) create ongoing capital allocation complexity.

**Pension and covenants.** Filing bodies contain no going-concern, covenant, or contingency disclosures. The condensed balance sheet shows employee benefit obligation actuarial adjustments (€27m gain in equity, 2025) — immaterial at group level. No pension deficit or covenant breach language surfaced.

**Competitive.** Peer Richemont reported strong Q1 2026 jewellery sales (15 July 2026), lifting the luxury index including LVMH — suggesting share shifts within luxury rather than sector-wide collapse, but also that LVMH is not uniformly outperforming.

**Legal/regulatory.** “LVMH Faces Lawsuit Scrutiny Following Fair Value Debate” (simplywall.st, 2 July 2026) — details not in filings pack; treated as unverified headline risk.

**Filing gaps.** No interim release, no full annual report/URD, and no audit-report text beyond confirmation that procedures were carried out. Impairment/restructuring charges (€656m other operating expenses) are not disaggregated in available bodies.
