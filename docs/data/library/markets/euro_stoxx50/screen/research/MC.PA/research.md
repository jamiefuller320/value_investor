## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
LVMH is the world’s largest luxury conglomerate, spanning fashion and leather goods (Louis Vuitton, Dior), wines and spirits (Moët, Hennessy), watches and jewellery, perfumes and cosmetics, and selective retailing (Sephora). The quantitative screen rates MC.PA as a **buy** (6/22 models; composite score 58%; five factor families passed: cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk). After a multi-year de-rating — news sources cite share-price declines of roughly 26–38% — valuation has compressed to ~23× P/E, 3.7× P/B and a 2.6% yield, unusual for a franchise of this calibre. The central debate is whether the earnings trough visible in Yahoo-sourced data (three consecutive years of revenue and profit decline from the FY2023 peak) reflects a cyclical luxury slowdown — particularly in China and among aspirational consumers — or structural margin erosion. Primary regulatory filings are absent from the research pack, which materially limits audit-grade confidence despite a coherent value case.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and geographic exposure**
Luxury demand is highly correlated with Chinese consumer sentiment and global wealth creation. News coverage repeatedly flags China as the swing factor (*Chinese Luxury Rebound Puts LVMH Valuation And China Exposure In Focus*, 3 Jun 2026). More recently, *Louis Vuitton's China Dilemma: Protect the Monogram, Risk the Narrative* (17 Jul 2026) highlights IP and nationalism tensions that could affect brand perception in a key market. A delayed China recovery would extend the earnings trough visible in Yahoo data.

**Earnings momentum**
Three-year revenue and profit decline from peak is the most concrete red flag in the available data. Without interim filings, it is unclear whether deceleration has stabilised or is still deteriorating quarter-on-quarter.

**Inventory build**
FY2025 inventory of €22.7bn and a €1.3bn inventory working-capital drag suggest potential markdown or mix-shift risk in fashion/leather goods and cognac, though primary filing commentary is absent.

**Competitive dynamics**
Richemont’s Q1 sales beat (20% growth vs 11% forecast; *Cartier-owner Richemont's sales beat boosts luxury sector*, 15 Jul 2026) highlights divergent performance within luxury — jewellery outperforming while LVMH’s broader portfolio may be more exposed to aspirational softening. Hermès’s pricing power remains the benchmark LVMH must match.

**Brand and strategy execution**
*Dior Reset Puts Brand At Center Of LVMH Growth Questions* (3 Feb 2026, alternate news) signals creative and management turnover at a key growth engine. Dior’s performance is disproportionately important to group sentiment.

**Governance and legal**
*LVMH … Faces Lawsuit Scrutiny Following Fair Value Debate* (2 Jul 2026) — details are thin in the manifest, but any securities litigation warrants monitoring. The Arnault family’s controlling stake (~48% via Groupe Arnault) provides long-term alignment but limits minority influence.

**Primary filing gaps**
Without URD/AMF extracts, standard risk disclosures — related-party transactions, tax disputes, environmental liabilities, lease commitments beyond IFRS 16 totals, and going-concern statements — cannot be reviewed. This materially limits deep-dive confidence.

**Special items and impairments**
Yahoo data shows recurring restructuring charges and asset impairments (€720m FY2025). Filing language would clarify whether these are one-offs or structural.

**Macro and FX (colour only)**
Macro context shows EUR/USD at 1.1375 and Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,281 (24 Jul 2026). LVMH generates a majority of revenue outside the eurozone (historical company disclosure; not re-verified here). Euro strength and tourism flows affect reported growth; this is noted for context only and does not override the screen signal.
