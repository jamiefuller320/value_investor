# Telia Company AB (publ) (TELIA.ST) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T13:30:50.447761+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Telia is a Nordic–Baltic incumbent telecom transitioning from a multi-market conglomerate toward a focused infrastructure and connectivity operator, with portfolio simplification largely behind it. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy, driven chiefly by a high free-cash-flow yield (~12%), a 4.7% dividend yield with growth, a low Neff PEGY (0.07), and a solid Piotroski F-Score (7/9), offsetting rich headline multiples (P/E 35.2, P/B 3.5). Yahoo financials show revenue stabilising near SEK 81bn after divestitures, operating profit holding above SEK 10bn, and a step-up in FCF to SEK 12.6bn in FY2025 alongside net-debt reduction. The central debate is whether cash-generation quality and deleveraging justify a premium multiple after a strong one-year share-price rally, or whether leverage, negative tangible equity, and Nordic competitive/regulatory pressure cap upside. Primary filing verification is unavailable in this pack, limiting conviction on covenants, pensions, and segment detail.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, Telia offers a **cash-yield and income** angle rather than a classic deep-value or Graham-style entry. The screen passes five factor families—cheapness (FCF yield), quality (Piotroski, earnings quality), dividend (yield and growth), GARP (PEGY), and risk—while failing strict low-P/E, net-net, moat, and financial-health screens because of elevated P/E, high debt-to-equity (169%), and a sub-1.0 current ratio (0.92).

Business quality, as inferable from Yahoo and news flow, has improved post-restructuring: continuing-operations net income rose from SEK 4.9bn (2024) to SEK 5.8bn (2025); operating cash flow increased from SEK 21.2bn to SEK 27.6bn; FCF doubled from SEK 7.3bn to SEK 12.6bn, aided by working-capital release and lower net capex intensity relative to prior years. Net debt fell from SEK 77.1bn (2023) to SEK 53.9bn (2025), consistent with the screen’s observation of declining leverage in Piotroski components. Dividends have been held at roughly SEK 7.9bn annually (2024–2025), supporting the ~4.7% yield at current prices.

The buy case rests on **durable Nordic cash flows**, a ** shareholder-return policy**, and **valuation that screens cheap on cash rather than earnings** (earnings yield 2.8%, EV/EBIT 25.2—both fail traditional value hurdles). Statutory earnings growth of 13.2% and Neff PEGY of 0.07 suggest the market may still be normalising earnings after the 2022 impairment cycle (FY2022 net loss SEK 14.6bn, including large write-offs). Q1 2026 Yahoo quarterly data (revenue SEK 19.97bn, operating income SEK 3.08bn, EPS SEK 0.42) align with news commentary of a solid start to 2026, though interim filing bodies are not available here to reconcile segment performance.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and liquidity:** High debt-to-equity and a current ratio below 1.0 failed the screen’s Financial Health and several quality models. Net debt has fallen, but total debt remains SEK 83bn with substantial lease and derivative liabilities (derivative liabilities SEK 4.0bn non-current, FY2025 Yahoo). Without filing bodies, covenant terms and maturity walls cannot be assessed.

**Pension and provisions:** Defined pension benefit SEK 9.1bn (FY2025) and long-term provisions SEK 7.3bn create off-balance-sheet-style commitments typical of European incumbents. Pension cash flows (Yahoo: pension expense SEK 2.4bn in 2025 operating adjustments) warrant scrutiny in the next annual report.

**Competitive and regulatory:** Nordic telecom markets face persistent price competition, MVNO pressure, and heavy 5G/fibre capex requirements. News flow (Zacks industry pieces, May and Nov 2025) flags margin pressure and competition across European wireless. Finland and Norway are repeatedly cited as weak spots in earnings-call summaries—geographic concentration risk within an already regional footprint.

**Accounting and earnings quality:** FY2025 reported net income (SEK 3.53bn) is well below continuing-operations profit (SEK 5.83bn) due to discontinued-operation losses; prior-year comparables include large discontinued gains (2024: +SEK 2.86bn). The screen passes Earnings Quality and Piotroski, but ROE (10.6%) remains below quality-moat thresholds, and ROA/gross-margin Piotroski components failed—suggesting margin and asset-efficiency pressure persists.

**Valuation after rally:** Syndicated commentary questions whether recent gains fully price optimism (“Is Telia Company (OM:TELIA) Now Pricing In Too Much Optimism After Its 1-Year Rally?”, 30 Apr 2026; “Telia (OM:TELIA) Valuation: How Recent Gains Stack Up Against Analyst Expectations”, 19 Nov 2025). P/E 35.2 and P/B 3.5 offer limited margin of safety on earnings-based metrics.

**Governance / strategic:** Portfolio simplification reduces complexity but leaves execution risk on remaining markets. The Brookfield–KTH sovereign-AI MOU (24 Jun 2026) is early-stage; capital intensity and returns are unproven.

**Filing-language risks:** Not assessable—no regulatory body text available for going-concern, litigation contingencies, or regulatory fines.
