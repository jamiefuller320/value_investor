## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
AB SKF is a global bearings and industrial technology group rated **Strong Buy** by the quantitative screen (7/22 models; composite 79%), chiefly on GARP, dividend growth, Piotroski financial health, and risk metrics rather than deep-value cheapness. The valuation hook is a cyclical recovery narrative: headline P/E of 26.5x and PEG of 0.18 embed sharp earnings rebound off a weak 2025 base, while balance-sheet deleveraging and a ~3.0% dividend yield support the income case. The central debate is whether 2025’s revenue and margin compression (partly offset by divestiture gains) marks a trough or a structural reset, given ROE of only 8.3% and FCF yield of 2.6%. **Primary regulatory filings are absent from the source pack**, so this memo relies on Yahoo financials and must be treated as verify-before-trade.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy signal is driven by five factor families—cheapness (relative), quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—not by Graham-style absolute cheapness. SKF passes Lynch PEG (0.18), Neff PEGY (0.06), Dividend Growth, Piotroski F-Score (7/9), Composite Value, Earnings Quality, and Financial Health; it fails deep-value screens on P/B (2.1×), earnings yield (3.8%), FCF yield (2.6%), and ROE (8.3%).

For a value-oriented GARP investor, the case rests on three pillars visible in the Yahoo data (no filing confirmation):

1. **Earnings inflection.** Statutory earnings growth of 145% in the screen reflects rebound from a depressed 2025 (diluted EPS SEK 8.62 vs SEK 14.22 in 2024). Early 2026 quarterly data in Yahoo shows Q1 net income of SEK 1,627m and EPS SEK 3.57 on revenue of SEK 21,873m—materially stronger than weak 2025 quarterly prints—supporting the GARP pass.

2. **Balance-sheet and cash quality.** Piotroski 7/9 confirms positive net income, OCF exceeding net income, declining leverage, improving current ratio (2.12), and no dilution. Net debt fell from SEK 8,090m (2022) to SEK 3,105m (2025); total debt reduced from SEK 20,042m to SEK 14,984m. Operating cash flow remained robust at SEK 8,392m in 2025 despite the earnings dip.

3. **Dividend continuity.** Dividend Growth model pass aligns with sustained cash returns: dividends paid SEK 3,613m in 2025, covered by FCF of SEK 4,567m and OCF of SEK 8,392m. Yield ~3.0% is respectable though not top-quartile.

Business quality is implied by SKF’s global scale (revenue SEK 91.6bn in 2025) and R&D spend (~SEK 3.4bn), but ROE of 8.3% and thin gross margins (~27%) sit below moat-quality thresholds—the screen correctly flags this by failing Buffett Quality and Economic Moat models. The thesis is therefore **quality cyclical at a reasonable growth-adjusted price**, not a deep-value net-net or high-yield compounder.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical exposure.** Revenue and margins peaked in 2023 (SEK 103.9bn) before two consecutive down years. Industrial bearings demand tracks auto, wind, and general manufacturing cycles; the screen’s cyclical overlay did not trigger, but the 2025 earnings reset is consistent with end-market softness or destocking rather than a one-off accounting item alone.

**Pension and employee liabilities.** Combined pension provisions of ~SEK 10.7bn represent a meaningful off-balance-sheet-style commitment relative to equity of SEK 55.7bn. Cash-flow statements show recurring pension/employee benefit cash effects (SEK 724m expense item in 2025 CFO bridge).

**Returns and valuation risk.** ROE 8.3%, FCF yield 2.6%, and EV/EBIT ~18.9× (per screen) leave little margin for error if the 2026 recovery stalls. The stock fails Graham, Deep Value, Acquirer’s Multiple, and Economic Moat models—confirming it is **not** statistically cheap on absolute metrics.

**2025 earnings quality.** A substantial portion of 2025 pretax profit relied on special items (SEK 993m) and divestiture proceeds; underlying operating profit fell more sharply than headline net income suggests. Piotroski failed on ROA improvement and gross-margin improvement—consistent with deteriorating underlying profitability.

**Leverage and liquidity.** Deleveraging is positive, but gross debt remains SEK 14.98bn. Current ratio 2.12 is adequate; no covenant or going-concern language is available without filing bodies.

**Governance / disclosure.** Absence of Swedish primary filings (annual report, interim report) in the research pack is itself a red flag for verify-before-trade workflows; no RNS/SEC body text to assess litigation, contingencies, or related-party matters.

**Competitive.** Global bearings is consolidated (SKF, Schaeffler, NSK, Timken) but price-competitive; R&D intensity is high (SEK 3.4bn) yet gross margins remain mid-20s%, limiting pricing power evidence from available data.
