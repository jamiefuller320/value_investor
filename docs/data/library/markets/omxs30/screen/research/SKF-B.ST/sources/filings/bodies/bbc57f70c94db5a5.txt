We delivered 4.3% organic growth and 
increased adjusted operating margin by 0.9

supported by strong commercial execution.  
We are also bringing forward our BSEK 2 annual

Growth in selected markets supports improved margin
Ahead of the planned listing of SKF Vertevo on Nasdaq 
Stockholm on 1 December 2026, the Automotive business is 
reported as Discontinued operations from Q3. Looking at our 
Continuing operations, i.e. our industrial business, organic 
sales growth was 4.3%, driven by both volume and solid price/
mix. We saw higher demand in selected OEM markets, including 
drives connected to data centers, infrastructure, as well as 
security and defence. 
Bearing Solutions grew by 2.8%, with continued growth in 
India and Southeast Asia and further improvement in the OEM 
market in the Americas. Development was broadly unchanged 
in Europe, Middle East and Africa, as well as in China and 
Northeast Asia. Specialized Industrial Solutions (SIS) contin -
ued its strong performance where organic sales increased by 
8.3%, growing across all business units and particularly in 
Aerospace and Magnetic Solutions. It´s worth noting that in 
Magnetic Solutions, we continue to strengthen our position by 
expanding production capacity and increasing automation to 
capture structural growth. 
I’m pleased that the industrial business improved its 
adjusted operating margin, compared to the same quarter last 
year, to 16.3%, reflecting continued strong commercial execu-
tion, particularly in SIS. Rightsizing savings of approximately 
MSEK 350, corresponding to an annual pace of about BSEK 1.7, 
more than offset negative separation-related synergies. The 
CEO Statement
rightsizing program is progressing well, and we now expect to 
reach the targeted annual run-rate savings of approximately 
BSEK 2 by mid-2027 compared to end-2027 as previously  
communicated. 
We continue to actively work with our cost and commercial 
execution to mitigate continued cost inflation in input costs and 
logistics. Tariff-related costs were again largely compensated. 
Investments in our transformation initiatives to harmonize and 
modernize our IT landscape proceeded as planned. Support 
production to the automotive business in Q3 was non-material. 
Currency effects provided a minor tailwind in Q3.
Cash flow from operations increased year-over-year to  
BSEK 2.1, supported by the improved operating result and 
partly offset by an increase in inventory for components and 
materials, primarily within SIS to support continued growth.

Preparing for two focused companies
The approval by our shareholders of the distribution of the 
shares in SKF Vertevo marked an important milestone ahead of 
the planned listing on 1 December 2026. Another milestone will 
be SKF Vertevo’s Capital Markets Day (CMD) on 17 November. 
In Q3, they delivered a solid performance with an improved 
adjusted operating margin of 4.6% despite challenging market 
conditions where organic sales declined by –4.0%.
As the separation progresses, we have updated the classifi -
cation of some items affecting comparability (IAC) between 
separation and footprint optimization activities. The total 
expected level of IAC remains unchanged from our CMD 2025 
communication, and this has no impact on our path towards 
achieving our long-term industrial business margin target. 
Additional production lines transferred to the Automotive 
 business will decrease the need for industrial footprint optimi -
zation with BSEK 0.5, while IAC related to the separation will 
increase by the same amount, totaling approximately BSEK 3.  
 Out of SKF Vertevo’s total production, a few production lines 
remain to be transferred into SKF Vertevo plants during 2027. 
This means that contract manufacturing to SKF Vertevo will 
start to decrease from Q2 2027. Thereafter, it is expected to 
decrease significantly over the medium term.
Outlook
We expect organic sales for SKF Group’s continuing operations 
to strengthen somewhat in Q4, year-over-year. This as we see a 
similar market environment as we had in Q3. However, market 
volatility remains high which amplifies overall uncertainty.

SKF has a longstanding track record on understanding and reducing its environmental  
and  climate impact and started already in 2000 to set targets and report on carbon dioxide 
 emissions. In 2020, the target of decarbonizing own operations by 2030 was launched and  
in 2021 SKF’s target of net-zero greenhouse gas emissions for the full value chain by 2050  
was set. Both targets have been approved by the Science Based Targets Initiative. 
The four strategic levers to decarbonize manufacturing operations by 2030 are energy and 
operational efficiency improvements, as well as switching to renewable energy sources and 
electrification of fossil fuel applications. This covers both scope 1 direct emissions as well  
as scope 2 indirect emissions.
During the last reported quarter (Q2), total Scope 1 and 2 emissions were further reduced, 
well ahead of the target trajectory. Scope 1 emissions continue to decline, and the increased 
use of renewable electricity is contributing to further reductions in Scope 2. In 2026, the main 
increases in renewable electricity sourcing are coming from China. The impact on emissions 
from energy consumption related to production variations was limited, with the main develop -
ments driven by increased sourcing of renewable electricity.

NOTE 1  Accounting principles
The consolidated financial statements of the SKF Group were 
prepared in accordance with International Financial Reporting 
Standards (IFRS) as adopted by the EU. The interim report was 
prepared in accordance with IAS 34 Interim Financial Reporting. 
Disclosures as required by IAS 34 p. 16 A are provided in the 
notes to the financial statements as well as in other parts of the 
interim report. The financial statements of the Parent Company 
were prepared in accordance with the “Annual Accounts Act” 
and the RFR 2 “Accounting for legal entities”. SKF Group and 
the Parent Company applied the same accounting principles 
and methods of computation in the interim financial statements 
as compared with the latest annual report. IASB issued several 
amended accounting standards that were endorsed by EU, 
effective date 1 January 2026. None of these have a material 
effect on the SKF Group’s financial statements.
Pillar II income taxes legislation was effective from  
1 January 2024. Under the legislation, the Parent Company  
will be required to pay top-up tax on profit of its subsidiaries 
that are taxed at an effective tax rate of less than 15%.  
No top-up tax has been included in the financial statements for 
the third quarter 2026. SKF Group has analyzed the financial 
figures and concluded that the Group is not expecting any 
additional material top-up tax during 2026. The Group will 
 continue to assess the impact of Pillar II income taxes 
 legislation on its future financial  performance.
 Valuation principles and classifications of the financial 
instruments, as described in SKF Annual Report 2025, have 
been consistently applied throughout the reporting period. 
There are no major changes in fair value during the period.
In September 2024, SKF announced that the company was 
preparing for the separation and distribution of its Automotive 
business. On 7 September 2026 the SKF Board of Directors 
decided to propose to the SKF shareholders to distribute the 
shares in the wholly-owned subsidiary SKF Vertevo AB to the 
shareholders of AB SKF. The decision was taken at the 
Extraordinary General Meeting on 1 October 2026 and SKF 
Vertevo AB is expected to be listed on Nasdaq Stockholm on  
1 December 2026.

 distribution as per September 2026 and is accounted for under 
the applicable principles for assets/liabilities held for distribu -
tion and discontinued operations under IFRS 5 Non-current 
assets held for sale and discontinued operations.

reported as discontinued operations in the consolidated 
income statement. The consolidated income statement for 
comparative  periods has been restated accordingly. The

NOTE 3  Risks and uncertainties in the    business
SKF operates in many different industries and geographical 
areas. As a result, the Group is exposed to various types of 
risks. SKF appreciates that there are risks associated with the 
macro environment such as the geopolitical landscape, the 
state of global markets and significant industry and techno -
logical shifts. There are also business risks including supply 
chain disruptions, information and cybersecurity threats, and 
challenges in attracting talent in a competitive labour market. 
Additionally, there are legal and compliance risks arising from 
the increased regulatory demands and internal governance and 
coordination within the Group as well as ongoing regulatory 
investigations and processes. 
The SKF Group’s operations are also exposed to various 
types of financial risks; market risks (being currency risk, 
 interest rate risk and other price risks), liquidity risks and credit 
risks. Further information on the risks and how SKF works to 
mitigate them is found in SKF’s latest Annual Report. Specific 
risks related to the separation of the Automotive business are 
described in the information brochure published by SKF on  
7 September. Both of the above-mentioned reports are availa -
ble on www.skf.com/group/investors . 
The financial position of the Parent Company is dependent 
on the financial position and development of the subsidiaries. 
A general decline in the demand for the products and services 
provided by the Group could mean lower residual profits and 
lower dividend income for the Parent Company, as well as a 
need for writing down values of the shares in the subsidiaries.

Net sales
The Automotive-segment reported net sales of MSEK 5,993 
(6,178). The organic sales decline of –4.0% was driven by a weak 
market demand environment. Market conditions in light vehicles 
remain difficult in Europe and in the quarter demand declined 
across all geo graphies. In commercial vehicles, organic sales 
grew in the Americas as well as in China and Northeast Asia. 
Currency effects impacted net sales positively by 1.3%.
Adjusted operating profit
The adjusted operating profit for the third quarter was 
MSEK 273 (208), with a corresponding adjusted operating  
margin of 4.6% (3.4%). The improvement was mainly driven by a 
positive price/mix reflecting a shift in sales towards the vehicle 
aftermarket, as OEM volumes declined. Furthermore, currency 
effects had a positive impact on the adjusted operating profit. 
This compensated for wage inflation and higher logistics and 
material costs as well as lower volumes.
