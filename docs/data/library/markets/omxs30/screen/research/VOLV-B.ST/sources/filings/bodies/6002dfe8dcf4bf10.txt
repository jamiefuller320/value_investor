The positive development in the Volvo Group's performance 
continued in Q3. We delivered net sales of SEK 119.5 billion, with 
an organic sales growth of 7%. Vehicle sales increased by 8% and 
service sales grew by 5% organically. We maintained good 
profitability, with adjusted operating income of SEK 14.7 billion 
(11.7) and an adjusted operating margin of 12.3% (10.6), 
supported by dedicated work across the Group. Operating cash 
flow in the Industrial Operations improved to SEK 7.2 billion.
The gradual cyclical recovery in the truck and construction 
equipment markets continued in both Europe and North America, 
supported by the need to replace aging trucks and machines. 
Customer activity is on good levels, with high utilization of 
vehicles and machines supporting demand for services. Our 
service business continued to benefit from both higher volumes 
and positive price realization. Inflationary pressure remains, and 
we are working to gradually offset higher costs through pricing 
and operational efficiency.
In our truck business, we continued to build on the positive 
momentum in North America. Both Volvo Trucks and Mack Trucks 
continued to strengthen their market positions, supported by our 
renewed and highly competitive product ranges. In Europe, 
demand remained at a good level, with Volvo Trucks maintaining 
its position as market leader in heavy-duty trucks. Capacity 
utilization in our European production system was high, and we 
are gradually increasing production capacity in North America on 
the back of the good order intake during the year. Total truck order 
intake increased by 26% and deliveries increased by 11%. Net 
sales in our truck business amounted to SEK 81.6 billion (74.2), 
with both vehicle and service sales growing by 8% organically. 
The adjusted operating margin improved to 11.1% (9.1).
The strength and width of our product offer were clearly 
demonstrated during the quarter. At IAA Transportation in 
Hanover, Germany, in September, both Volvo Trucks and Renault 
Trucks presented highly competitive lineups, spanning electric and 
increasingly efficient conventional solutions. We are particularly 
proud that Volvo Trucks’ new heavy-duty electric range was 
awarded International Truck of the Year 2027 and that the new 
Renault Trucks Trafic Van E-Tech electric was named International 
Van of the Year 2027. In North America, both Volvo and Mack 
Trucks continue their major product renewals. Across our brands, 
we have new product and service offers on the market that 
improve our customers’ productivity, uptime and energy efficiency 
while reducing emissions. 
We also reached another important milestone in autonomous 
transport. Volvo Autonomous Solutions and Waabi started their 
first customer operation together, with the Volvo VNL 
Autonomous now transporting goods on the Dallas–Houston 
corridor in Texas. This is an important step in our journey toward 
commercializing autonomous freight solutions at scale.
Volvo Construction Equipment continued to perform well. 
Demand for machines was supported by infrastructure invest-
ments and data center construction in North America. Activity 
across the major European markets was generally good, although 
Germany has yet to show a material improvement. Machine sales 
grew by 13% organically. Customer activity and machine 
utilization remained at good levels, supporting the service 
business, with service sales growing by 5% organically. Net sales 
amounted to SEK 19.9 billion (18.9), with an adjusted operating 
margin of 16.1% (14.4).
Volvo Buses continued to show resilience in underlying earnings 
despite lower volumes. Net sales amounted to SEK 5.8 billion 
(6.0) and the adjusted operating margin amounted to 7.7% 
(12.6).
Volvo Penta continued to show strength, supported by good 
demand in marine commercial and power generation. Data 
centers are an increasingly important part of the business, 
representing 32% of the value of the order book at the end of the 
quarter. Net sales amounted to SEK 5.3 billion (5.0), and the 
adjusted operating margin was 21.1% (18.6).
In Volvo Financial Services, portfolio performance remained 
stable, reflecting the current business environment and the 
financial health of our customers. Adjusted operating income 
amounted to SEK 1,102 M (1,029).
While market conditions differ between regions and segments, 
our focus remains on what we can impact: staying close to our 
customers and delivering on our order backlog, maintaining cost 
and commercial discipline, while investing in the products and 
technologies that strengthen our long-term competitiveness. 
Together with our flexible business model and growing service 
business, this gives us a solid foundation for navigating changes in 
market demand and continuing to deliver good performance 
through the business cycle.
Martin Lundstedt
President and CEO

Volvo Group, Daimler Truck and Toyota Motor Corporation 
signed binding agreement for Toyota to join cellcentric
On July 27, it was announced that Volvo Group, Daimler Truck 
AG, cellcentric and Toyota Motor Corporation had signed a 
binding agreement for Toyota to join as an equal partner and 
shareholder in cellcentric, with each owner to hold a third each. 
Completion of the transaction is conditional upon obtaining 
regulatory approvals. Through the collaboration, the parties intend 
to strengthen cellcentric’s position as a leading developer and 
manufacturer of fuel cell systems for heavy-duty commercial 
applications.
Volvo Group announced energy park initiative as next step in 
Mariestad
On September 10, Volvo Group announced the next step in the 
Mariestad expansion process with plans for an energy park near 
the intended battery cell production site. The energy park will be 
developed along two parallel tracks: a large-scale battery storage 
facility and a future test bed for innovative energy solutions. 
Initially, the park is expected to provide approximately 70 
MW/260 MWh of battery storage capacity, making it one of the 
largest energy parks in Sweden.
Industry leaders scale full value chain for hydrogen mobility 
across Europe
On September 15, at IAA Transportation, Volvo Group, Daimler 
Truck, Toyota Motor Corporation, Bosch, Air Liquide, 
TotalEnergies, TEAL Mobility, and MB Energy unveiled their plans 
to accelerate the rollout of hydrogen-powered vehicles across 
Europe. For the first time in Europe, the German ecosystem has 
put in place all the conditions for a scalable deployment of 
hydrogen trucks by 2030, driven by the German authorities and 
European industrial leaders along the value chain. Building on the 
German deployment model as a practical baseline, scaling these 
solutions across the continent calls for aligned support from 
national governments and the European Commission to ensure 
long-term energy resilience and competitiveness.

 4 IMPORTANT EVENTS
Net sales
In Q3 2026, the Volvo Group’s net sales increased by 8% to SEK 
119,525 M compared with SEK 110,692 M in the same quarter 
the preceding year. Net sales increased in all regions except Asia. 
The decrease in Asia is explained by the divestment of SDLG in 
2025.
The organic sales growth was 7%, with vehicle sales increasing 
by 8% and service sales increasing by 5%.
Operating income
In Q3 2026, adjusted operating income amounted to SEK 
14,693 M (11,707), corresponding to an adjusted operating 
margin of 12.3% (10.6). Compared with Q3 2025, adjusted 
operating income was positively impacted by a stronger service 
business, IEEPA tariff refunds, price realization and increased 
volumes, which were partially offset by a negative market and 
customer mix as well as higher material and freight costs.
During Q3 2026, the net U.S. tariff impact was SEK 0.2 billion 
positive, as IEEPA tariff refunds of SEK 1.2 billion more than 
offset U.S. tariff costs. During the same period in the prior year, 
net tariff costs were SEK 0.5 billion.
Compared with Q3 2025, currency movements had a positive 
impact of SEK 970 M.
In Q3 2026, costs totaling SEK 339 M relating to claims 
arising from the European Commission’s 2016 antitrust settle-
ment decision were excluded from adjusted operating income. The 
provision previously recognized for these claims has been fully 
utilized in Q3. At this stage it is not possible to make a reliable 
estimate of the total liability that could arise from such proceed-
ings given the complexity of the claims and the different (and in 
some cases relatively early) stages to which national proceedings 
have progressed. Going forward, costs relating to these claims will 
be recognized in the Income Statement as incurred, and the costs 
will be excluded from adjusted operating income. In Q3 2025, a 
positive effect of SEK 811 M from the divestment of SDLG was 
excluded from adjusted operating income. For more information 
on adjusted operating income, please see Note 6.
Reported operating income amounted to SEK 14,354 M 
(12,517).
Financial items
In Q3 2026, interest income was SEK 369 M (380), whereas 
interest expenses amounted to SEK -454 M (-451).
Other financial income and expenses amounted to SEK -171 M 
(-310). The change is primarily due to revaluation effects of 
financial assets and liabilities.
Income taxes
In Q3 2026, income taxes amounted to SEK -3,330 M (-4,554). 
The effective tax rate was 23.6% (37.5).  The high tax rate in Q3 
2025 was mainly related to the taxable gain from the sale of the 
SDLG shares in China. Excluding the SDLG divestment, the 
effective tax rate in Q3 2025 was 23.5%.
Income for the period and earnings per share
In Q3 2026, income for the period amounted to SEK 10,768 M 
(7,583). Earnings per share amounted to SEK 5.29 (3.71).

 8 BUSINESS SEGMENT OVERVIEW
Market development
In Q3, both the European and North American truck market had a 
good development on the back of solid replacement demand, 
supported by an elevated age of the installed truck population, 
good fleet utilization and improved profitability among many 
customers. European demand is broad-based across markets, and 
heavy-duty truck registrations through September increased by 
8% compared with the same period in 2025. In North America, 
retail sales declined by 6% through September because of a slow 
start to the year.   
The Brazilian truck market declined by 4% compared with the 
prior year due to high interest rates, U.S. tariffs and increased 
energy costs. Governmental interest-rate subsidies in the Move 
Brazil program have supported demand in 2026, but funds have 
been consumed and no more funding has been presented ahead 
of the election in October.  
Demand in India continued to grow supported by steady freight 
and mining activity, investments in infrastructure and healthy 
replacement demand. As of September, the Indian truck market 
grew by 28% compared with the prior year. 
The Chinese market has been supported by a governmental 
subsidy program to stimulate transition into battery electric 
vehicles and lower level emission engines. Year to date the 
Chinese market grew 2% compared with the prior year. 
Orders and deliveries
In Q3, total net order intake increased by 26% year over year and 
reached 46,918 trucks and deliveries increased by 11% to 
49,611 units. Heavy-duty trucks net orders increased by 29% and 
deliveries increased by 11%.
In Europe, order intake increased by 22% to 25,989 units, with 
orders for heavy- and medium-duty trucks increasing by 25% and 
orders for light-duty trucks increasing by 13%. Total deliveries in 
Europe increased by 4% to 23,158 trucks, with heavy- and 
medium-duty trucks increasing by 3% and light-duty trucks 
increasing by 10%. Volvo's European heavy-duty market share 
through September reached 19.5% (19.3) and Renault Trucks' 
market share reached 9.1% (9.3). Both Volvo and Renault Trucks 
experienced more competition in the battery-electric heavy-duty 
truck market and Volvo's market share declined to 14.1% (27.6) 
and Renault Trucks' to 8.7% (17.0).
Order intake in North America increased by 18% to 11,150 
trucks. In the beginning of the quarter, production slots for 2026 
were almost sold out. Order books for 2027 were opened at the 
beginning of September. Deliveries in North America increased by 
42% to 13,683 trucks. Through September, Volvo's heavy-duty 
truck market share grew to 9.1% (8.3) and Mack's market share 
grew to 8.3% (7.3).  
In South America, order intake increased by 92% to 4,286 
trucks supported by the Move Brazil subsidy program and 
deliveries increased by 9% to 7,408 vehicles. In Brazil, Volvo 
retained its strong market position in heavy-duty trucks with a 
market share of 23.1% (23.1).
Order intake in Asia increased by 99% to 3,556 vehicles while 
deliveries declined by 9% to 3,388 vehicles. The strong increase 
in order intake is mainly explained by Turkey, where there were 
cancellations last year and good demand in Q3 this year. 
Order intake for fully-electric trucks increased by 163% to

IFRS 18 introduces defined categories and subtotals in the 
income statement, whereby income and expenses classified 
within the operating category are aggregated under the subtotal 
Operating profit. This subtotal is not directly comparable to the 
Volvo Group's historically reported Operating income, primarily 
because Share of profit/loss on investments in joint ventures and

Volvo Group considers these items to be an integral part of its 
operations, the additional subtotal Earnings before interest and 
taxes (EBIT) will be presented in the income statement and used 
as a performance measure going forward.
Operating cash flow for Industrial Operations will be presented 
separately from the formal cash flow statement, providing a 
reconciliation between Net cash from operating activities under 
IAS 7 and Operating cash flow before and after M&A.
IFRS 18 will be applied retrospectively. Accordingly,

and disclosed in a note in the Annual Report 2026.
NOTE 2 | RISKS AND KEY SOURCES OF ESTIMATION UNCERTAINTY
Each one of the Volvo Group's Business Areas, Divisions and 
Group Functions monitors and manages risks in its operations. In 
addition, the Volvo Group utilizes a centralized Enterprise Risk 
Management (ERM) reporting process, which is a systematic and 
structured framework for reporting and reviewing risk assess-
ments and mitigations as well as for follow-up on identified risks. 
The ERM process classifies Volvo Group risks into four categories:
Macro and market related risks – such as cyclical nature of the 
commercial vehicles industry, intense competition, political and 
social uncertainty, the introduction of tariffs as well as uncertain 
developments in global trade policies;
Operational risks – such as transformation and technology risk, 
new business models, risks related to supply chain and industrial 
operations, tariffs, cost inflation and price increases, information 
security and digital infrastructure, strategic transactions such as 
mergers and acquisitions, partnerships and divestments, residual 
value commitments as well as people and culture; 
Compliance risks – such as product and operational-related 
regulations, digital and data-related regulations, protection and 
maintenance of intangible assets, legal proceedings, corruption 
and competition law and human rights; and
Financial risks – such as insurance coverage, credit risk, tax risk, 
pension commitments, interest-rates and currency fluctuations, 
liquidity risk, as well as impairment on goodwill and other intangible 
assets.
For a more elaborate description of these risks, please refer to 
the section Risks and uncertainties on pages 60-67 in the Volvo 
Group Annual Report 2025.

Short-term risks, when applicable, are also described in the

Tariffs and trade policy shifts
Recent tariffs and other trade restrictions imposed or considered 
to be imposed by the U.S. and other countries have significantly 
increased uncertainty about trade conditions in markets where the 
Group is present, as well as in relation to global and regional 
supply chains. The situation is fast-changing and complex to 
assess, and future developments remain uncertain, including 
whether trade restrictions may impact the Group more severely 
than its main competitors. However, the introduction of tariffs, 
retaliatory tariffs or other trade restrictions on our vehicles, parts, 
and other products and materials could disrupt existing supply 
chains, impose additional costs on our business or that of our 
suppliers, create sudden disadvantages for Group operations 
compared to competitors having different supply chains, and 
could generally make our products more expensive for customers 
and/or less competitive.
Macro and market developments
Heightened international tension and conflicts, including the 
ongoing conflicts in the Middle East, as well as recent 
developments in global trade policies have also increased the risk 
of a broader economic slowdown. Such developments could 
negatively impact global demand and lead to increased costs for 
e.g. raw materials, components, transport and energy. A 
prolonged period of uncertainty in the market may also negatively 
affect investment levels and customer purchasing behavior, 
particularly in Group key markets. The Group will endeavor to 
adapt to changes in market conditions as they may evolve, but 
these developments could, individually or in combination, have a 
material adverse effect on the Group's business and financial 
performance.
