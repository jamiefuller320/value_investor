## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
NIBE Industrier AB is a Swedish industrial group spanning climate solutions (heat pumps), components, and stoves, with a quantitative screen rating of **buy** (5/22 models; composite 60%, sector-relative 56%). The case rests on earnings recovery after a weak 2024, a perfect Piotroski F-Score (9/9), strong 2025 free cash flow, and GARP-style metrics (PEGY 0.27; statutory earnings growth 28.8%), offset by rich headline valuation (P/E 32.2, P/B 2.4) and a goodwill-heavy balance sheet. Primary regulatory filings are absent from the research pack, so this memo relies on Yahoo financials and thin news coverage — figures should be treated as indicative until Swedish annual/interim reports are verified. The central debate is whether margin and cash-flow normalisation after the post-acquisition and Nordic housing downturn cycle justifies a premium multiple, or whether leverage, cyclical heat-pump demand, and intangible asset risk cap upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, NIBE qualifies less as deep value than as **quality-at-a-reasonable-growth-price** within the screen’s GARP and risk families. The quantitative engine passes Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Piotroski F-Score, Earnings Quality, and Financial Health — four of four model families (quality, dividend, GARP, risk) — while failing classic deep-value screens (Graham, Schloss, Deep Value, Acquirer’s Multiple) on rich P/E, P/B, and EV/EBIT. That pattern fits a compounder recovering from a trough rather than a statistically cheap stock.

Business quality signals are visible in the Yahoo data (fallback source; no filing bodies available). Net income rebounded from SEK 1.17bn in 2024 to SEK 2.28bn in 2025 (+94%), with diluted EPS rising from SEK 0.58 to SEK 1.13. Free cash flow strengthened sharply to SEK 3.24bn in 2025 from SEK 1.36bn in 2024, with operating cash flow of SEK 4.89bn. Piotroski components are uniformly positive — profitability, cash conversion, declining leverage, improving margins and asset turnover — suggesting operational repair rather than accounting deterioration alone. Statutory earnings growth of 28.8% and PEGY of 0.27 give the GARP pass intellectual coherence: the screen is buying normalised earnings momentum, not a low multiple.

The dividend family pass should be interpreted cautiously: the screen reports a 91.0% yield, which is inconsistent with NIBE’s known payout profile and likely reflects a data artefact (cash dividends paid of SEK 606m in 2025 versus implied yield math). Dividend Growth model passage may therefore overstate income appeal; verify payout ratio and yield from primary filings before sizing for income.

Overall, the buy signal is a **recovery-and-quality** call, not a contrarian valuation call. Conviction from the screen is still “building” (35%, two weeks at signal, stable trend).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical exposure:** Heat pumps and residential climate equipment remain tied to housing activity, energy subsidies, and interest rates. News coverage consistently flags Nordic market headwinds and currency impacts (Q3 2025, Q4 2025, Q1 2026 call summaries). The screen’s cyclical overlay did not trigger, but segment-level cyclicality (notably Stoves) is referenced repeatedly in headlines.

**Leverage and intangibles:** Net debt of SEK 14.9bn and debt/equity of 72% failed multiple conservative screens (Buffett Quality, Schloss, Graham Defensive). Goodwill of SEK 23.1bn against equity of SEK 30.3bn creates impairment risk if growth assumptions in acquired businesses (climate solutions roll-up strategy) prove optimistic. Tangible book near zero limits asset-backed downside protection.

**Earnings quality and special items:** 2024–2025 P&L includes restructuring/M&A charges and unusual items (e.g. SEK −172m unusual items in 2025; SEK +604m in 2024 per Yahoo normalisation fields). Without filing footnotes, recurring vs non-recurring earnings split is uncertain — the Earnings Quality model pass should not be treated as fully verified.

**Interest burden:** Interest expense was SEK 1.05bn in 2025 (vs SEK 1.42bn in 2024), still material relative to pretax income of SEK 3.16bn. Refinancing risk in a higher-rate environment is a latent concern; no covenant or going-concern language is available from filing bodies.

**Governance / key person:** NIBE is founder-influenced (Museum family); no specific governance red flags appear in the source pack, but this cannot be assessed without annual report board and related-party disclosures.

**Competitive:** European heat-pump market is crowded (Vaillant, Daikin, Bosch, etc.); NIBE’s organic growth claims in Q1 2026 news are positive but unverified against filing segment data.

**Data quality:** The 91.0% dividend yield in the screen snapshot is almost certainly erroneous and could distort dividend-model weighting. ROE of 7.8% reflects the 2024–2025 earnings trough and goodwill base — below quality thresholds on Buffett/Economic Moat screens.

**Regulatory:** No filing-sourced regulatory or litigation disclosures available; EU energy-efficiency regulation is a tailwind for heat pumps but subsidy policy changes remain a policy risk.
