## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Wienerberger AG is Europe’s largest brickmaker and a leading supplier of roof tiles and plastic pipe systems, trading at a material discount to book (P/B 0.8) with a near-5% dividend yield and strong free-cash-flow generation relative to market cap. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cheapness, dividend, GARP, and cash-flow metrics, tempered by weak reported ROE (2.2%), elevated leverage (debt/equity ~95%), and a statutory earnings decline of roughly 71% that depresses headline quality scores. The central debate is whether asset-backed valuation and resilient operating cash flow (€630m in FY2025 per Yahoo) adequately compensate for cyclical European residential construction weakness, margin compression in H1 2026, and a July 2026 profit-guidance cut. Primary regulatory filing bodies are absent from the research pack; all financial figures below fall back to Yahoo Finance data and should be verified against Wienerberger’s annual report and H1 2026 release before sizing a position.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy rating rests on five passing factor families—cheapness, quality (partial), dividend, GARP, and risk—driven chiefly by asset-based and cash-flow signals rather than earnings momentum.

**Valuation anchor.** At P/B 0.8 and FCF yield ~7.4%, the market prices Wienerberger below tangible book (Yahoo FY2025: tangible book €1,738m vs market-implied equity ~€2.2bn at current multiples) despite a franchise position in building materials across Central and Western Europe. The Neff PEGY model passes (PEGY 0.08), suggesting growth-adjusted yield looks attractive once the depressed earnings base is normalised.

**Cash generation vs. reported earnings.** Operating cash flow of €630m and FCF of €349m in FY2025 (Yahoo) comfortably exceed net income of €166m, indicating earnings are depressed by non-cash charges (impairments, depreciation) rather than a broken cash engine. The Piotroski F-Score of 8/9 corroborates improving balance-sheet and cash-flow trends—leverage declining, current ratio improving, no dilution—despite a failing gross-margin component.

**Income component.** A ~4.9% dividend yield passes the High Dividend Yield screen; FY2025 cash dividends of €104m (Yahoo) imply meaningful payout from FCF, though interim dividend coverage cannot be confirmed without filing bodies.

**What the screen does not capture.** Models requiring low P/E, high ROE, low leverage, or net-net criteria fail—reflecting cyclical trough earnings, post-2023 normalisation, and acquisition-funded balance sheet (€634m business purchases in FY2024 per Yahoo). For a value investor, the case is asset-and-FCF-led: buy a cyclically impaired but cash-generative market leader at a discount to replacement cost, with dividend income while waiting for European housing recovery. Conviction is moderate (screen conviction 42%, new signal) pending filing verification and clarity on H1 2026 guidance trajectory.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / demand.** Wienerberger is highly exposed to European residential construction, which remains under pressure from higher rates, affordability, and weather-related volume volatility (evident in Q1 2026). Margin squeeze despite revenue growth is a recurring news theme (Aug 2026). The July 2026 guidance cut signals management sees near-term earnings risk not fully reflected in trailing screen metrics.

**Leverage and financial health.** Debt/equity ~95% and net debt ~€1.4bn failed the Financial Health, Schloss, Buffett Quality, and Economic Moat screens. FY2024’s €634m acquisition spend increased goodwill and integration risk. Without filing bodies, covenant headroom, maturity profile, and going-concern language cannot be assessed—this is a material research gap.

**Pension obligations.** ~€367m in pension/post-retirement liabilities (Yahoo FY2025 balance sheet) represent a fixed charge in a cyclical downturn; filing-level discount-rate and funding disclosures are unavailable.

**Earnings quality / accounting.** Reported ROE of 2.2% reflects depressed earnings; repeated impairments (€49m FY2024, asset write-offs FY2025) and high goodwill/intangibles raise questions about acquisition accounting and future write-down risk. OCF exceeding net income is positive, but the screen’s Earnings Quality pass should be weighed against statutory earnings volatility (−71% growth metric).

**Inventory and working capital.** €1.3bn inventory and Q1 2026 working-capital drain (−€308m) flag potential obsolescence or demand mismatch if volumes remain soft.

**Governance / insider activity.** Managing Board share transaction by Gerhard Hanke reported 13 Aug 2026—routine disclosure, but no filing body to assess context.

**Competitive / structural.** European brick industry faces energy-cost sensitivity, overcapacity in some regions, and substitution toward alternative cladding; Wienerberger’s scale is an advantage but does not eliminate cyclicality.

**Research pack limitations.** Zero primary filings means contingencies, litigation, related-party transactions, and covenant terms are unverified.
