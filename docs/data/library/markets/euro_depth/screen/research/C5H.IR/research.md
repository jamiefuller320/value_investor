# Cairn Homes plc (C5H.IR) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-06T06:45:01.404748+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Cairn Homes is Ireland’s largest listed homebuilder, scaling output into a structurally undersupplied market with supportive government policy. The quantitative screen rates the name a **buy** (15/22 models; composite 68%; P/E 11.5, P/B 2.1, yield 3.6%, ROE 19.6%), supported by cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families all passing. Filings show accelerating fundamentals: FY2025 revenue of €944.6m (+10%) and H1 2026 revenue of €455.5m (+60%), with a record order book of 5,020 homes (€1.89bn) and upgraded FY26 guidance. The valuation hook is a low-teens earnings multiple on a business targeting c.17% ROE, progressive dividends and a new €50m buyback. The key debate is whether strong reported earnings will convert reliably to cash as inventory and WIP expand, and whether Irish housing policy and build-cost inflation remain supportive through the cycle.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Cairn offers a rare combination of **moderate valuation, demonstrable operating leverage and shareholder-return discipline** in a sector where peers often trade on thinner margins or heavier balance sheets.

**Quantitative alignment.** The screen passes Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Lynch PEG (statutory growth ~83%, Lynch PEG ~0.14), Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value and Financial Health. Debt/equity of 29% and current ratio above 5x support the risk-family pass. ROE of 19.6% on screen inputs exceeds FY25 reported ROE of 16.6%, suggesting either forward earnings momentum or metric timing differences — both consistent with H1 2026 acceleration.

**Business quality.** Filings describe a scaled platform: 1,139 unit completions in H1 2026 (+60% y/y), operating margin expansion to 16.4% (from 15.0%), and operating expenses at 4.8% of revenue versus 7.2% in H1 2025. Management prioritises first-time buyers and forward-fund social/affordable delivery, with ASP held to €393k (+1.6% y/y) against c.2.5% build-cost inflation — a credible affordability narrative. Landbank of c.18,000 wholly owned units, c.75% with planning permission or in process, underpins multi-year visibility.

**Capital allocation.** Total DPS rose 22% to 10.0c in FY25; interim DPS increased 10% to 4.5c for H1 2026; a €50m buyback commenced 2 September 2026. Net debt of €194.5m at 30 June 2026 (vs €307.4m at H1 2025) and liquidity of €304.1m indicate balance-sheet capacity without aggressive leverage (management targets debt/GAV c.20%).

**Where the screen is less convinced.** Failures on Deep Value (P/B 2.1), Graham Defensive, High Dividend Yield, Piotroski F-Score (4/9) and **Earnings Quality** (operating cash below earnings) warrant overlay caution rather than rejection — typical for inventory-intensive builders in growth phase.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and operational.** Housing demand, mortgage availability and Irish macro conditions drive volumes and ASP. H1 2026 gross margin fell 90bps on mix; build-cost inflation guided at c.2.5% with geopolitical supply-chain risk flagged in filings. Inventory of €1,176m (87% of total assets) carries **estimation uncertainty** on NRV, cost-to-complete and selling prices — no write-downs in H1 2026, but judgements are material.

**Regulatory and policy.** Growth thesis leans on Irish housing policy (300,000 homes by 2030, Croí Cónaithe, infrastructure funding). Planning reform, rent legislation and infrastructure delivery delays could affect pace and returns. Forward-fund and state-partner revenue (recognised over time) adds contract-execution risk.

**Leverage and liquidity.** Net debt/GAV targeted at c.20%; net debt rose to €194.5m at H1 2026 from €171.3m at year-end. All debt secured on group assets including €1,176m inventory; covenants complied at 30 June 2026. Contingent liability on construction bonds €27.2m (remote outflow per filing). PGIM loan note refinanced July 2026 (€42.5m to 2031).

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 operating cash €70.6m vs PAT €132.7m; screen fails Earnings Quality and Piotroski (OCF < net income, leverage not declining, asset turnover not improving). This is the principal quantitative–fundamental tension.

**Governance.** Orderly board refresh (Bernard Byrne chair from May 2025; Alan Ralph and Pat Farrell NED appointments 2026). Auditor rotation KPMG → Ernst & Young for FY2025. March 2026 secondary placing by selling shareholders (not a primary raise by the company). Defined-contribution pension costs only (€2.7m FY25); no material pension deficit disclosed.

**Going concern.** Directors and EY interim reviewer confirm going-concern basis; severe but plausible downside sensitivities applied; no material uncertainties identified in interim review.
