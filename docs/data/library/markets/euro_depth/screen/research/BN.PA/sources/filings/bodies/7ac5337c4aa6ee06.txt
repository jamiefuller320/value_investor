I. THIRD QUARTER 2026 SALES 
 
In Q3 2026, sales stood at €7,233m, up + 4.0% LFL. Volume/mix increased +0.4%, while price contributed +3.6%, 
reflecting disciplined pricing actions. On a reported basis, sales increased by +5.2%. This includes a positive impact 
from forex (+1.5%), linked to the appreciation of several currencies against the euro, notably the Chinese renminbi, 
the Mexican peso and the U.S. dollar. On the other hand,  scope had a negative effect of - 0.5% this quarter,  
reflecting the deconsolidation of the Dairy business in Argentina (since August 1st), partly offset by the consolidation 
of Huel (since September 1st) and the consolidation of the joint venture with Saputo Dairy Australia. In addition, the 
contribution from hyperinflation was +0.4%. 
Sales by operating segment 
 
In Q3 2026, EMEA LFL sales increased by +3.9%, with volume/mix at -0.4% and price at +4.3%. In Europe (+1.9% LFL) 
volume/mix was positive in EDP and Waters but negative in S pecialized Nutrition, resulting in total volume/mix of  
-1.3% while price was up +3.2%.  
In EMEA, EDP posted strong growth, notably in France, Spain and the UK, driven by the Danone, Alpro, Activia and 
High Protein brands. In Specialized Nutrition, IMF situation returned to normal in the Middle East, while the category 
continued to recover progressively  in Europe . The season was strong in W aters, notably with Volvic delivering 
double digit growth. 
In the Americas, LFL sales were up + 4.4%, with volume/mix at +0.7% and price  at +3.7%. Within the zone, 
North America delivered LFL sales growth of +2.4%, with volume/mix at -0.6% and price at +3.0%. 
Specialized Nutrition  delivered outstanding performance , driven by Aptamil, Neocate and Kate Farms. Waters 
posted strong growth with solid performance of evian in North America and improved momentum in Mexico.  
In EDP, High Protein continued to drive growth across the region. In North America, decisive actions are in place 
to regain momentum, focusing on step -changing execution and regaining innovation leadership through strong 
product pipeline beyond high protein. Effec tive immediately, new leadership has been appointed for the US 
business, reporting to Henri Bruxelles. 
In Asia Pacific, LFL sales increased by +3.6%, with volume/mix at +1.7% and price at +1.9%. Within the zone, China, 
North Asia & Oceania  (CNAO) posted LFL sales growth of + 0.1% with negative volume/mix of -1.6%, linked to 
Waters, and price at +1.7%.  
In Specialized Nutrition, CNAO delivered strong mid-single digit growth, driven by both IMF and Medical Nutrition. 
The rest of the APAC zone was very dynamic, particularly Indonesia and India. Waters was impacted by Mizone 
amid a poor season with  adverse weather conditions, prompting disciplined inventory management . Mizone 
decline resulted in approximately -1 point impact on Group LFL sales growth  in Q3. EDP continued to show strong 
momentum in Japan, with both Activia and Oikos.

III. SHARE BUYBACK 
 
Danone intends to buy back c.3.5 million of its own shares by the end of 2026, in order to offset the dilutive impacts 
resulting from the capital increases reserved to employees and the long -term incentive plans to be implemented 
in 2027. The repurchased shares will be allocated to employee shareholding plans. Details of the share buyback 
program are available in section 6 .2 of Danone’s 2025 Universal Registration Document, available on Danone’s 
website. 
 
IV. GOVERNANCE 
 
Vikram Agarwal, Chief Operations Officer, to retire from Danone  
 
After nearly four decades dedicated to Supply Chain and Operations, including five years at Danone, Vikram 
Agarwal decided to retire from Danone early 2027.  
During his time at Danone, Vikram has made a significant contribution to the company, helping strengthen and 
transform Danone’s Operations organization while leading teams with dedication, expertise and a constant focus 
on performance and excellence, at the same time evolving strategic relationships with our supply partners.  
Vikram will continue to lead Operations until early 2027 to ensure continuity and a smooth transition. Further 
information regarding his succession will be communicated in due course. 
 
V. RECENT MAJOR DEVELOPMENTS 
 
 September 21, 2026: Danone successfully issued a triple -tranche bond offering with a total amount of 
€1.5 billion, comprising:  
• a €500 million tranche of 18-month notes, carrying a floating coupon of 3-month Euribor +0.30%; 
• a €500 million tranche of 5-year notes, carrying a 4.0910 % coupon;

The settlement took place on September 28, 2026, and the bonds are listed on Euronext Paris.

balanced meal solutions. By combining the direct -to-consumer and community engagement capabilities of 
Huel with the scale and global reach of Danone, more consumers will have access to nutritionally complete 
and convenient, sustainable food. Huel is consolidated in Danone’s financial statements since September 1, 
2026. 
 August 3, 2026: Danone and Arcor have completed the creation of their joint venture to unlock new dairy 
opportunities in Argentina . This new partnership combines  Danone’s dairy business in Argentina (Danone 
Argentina SA), Mastellone Hermanos SA and Logistica La Serenísima. Danone now holds equal control of the 
joint venture with Arcor, and it is thus reflected since August 1, 2026, within ‘equity -accounted companies’ in 
Danone’s financial statements, based on the 50% shareholding. 
 
VI. IFRS STANDARDS AND ALTERNATIVE PERFORMANCE MEASURES NOT DEFINED BY IFRS 
 
IAS 29: impact on reported data 
 
Danone has applied IAS 29 in hyperinflationary countries, as defined in IFRS. Adoption of IAS 29 in hyperinflationary 
countries requires their non-monetary assets and liabilities and their income statement to be restated to reflect the 
changes in the general purchasing power of their functional currency, leading to a gain or loss on the net monetary 
position, included in the net income. Moreover, their financial statements are converted into euros using the closing 
exchange rate of the relevant period.

Definitions of geographical zones 
 
EMEA refers to European countries, Turkey, Middle East, Africa and CIS. 
 
The Americas refers to North America (the United States and Canada) and Latin America (including Mexico, Brazil, 
Argentina and Uruguay). 
 
APAC or Asia Pacific refers to China, Japan, Australia  and New Zealand  (former CNAO -  China, North Asia & 
Oceania - region) as well as the rest of Asia (including Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam and India).

Like-for-like changes in sales reflect Danone's organic performance and essentially exclude the impact of:  
 changes in consolidation scope, with indicators related to a given fiscal year calculated on the basis of 
the previous year's scope;  
 changes in applicable accounting principles;  
 changes in exchange rates, with both previous -year and current -year indicators calculated using the 
same exchange rate (the exchange rate used is a projected annual rate determined by Danone for the 
current year and applied to both previous and current years).  
Since January 1, 2023, all countries with hyperinflationary economies are taken into account in like-for-like changes 
as follows: sales growth in excess of around 26% per year (a three-year average at 26% would generally trigger the 
application of hyperinflationary accounting as defined in IFRS) is now excluded from the like -for-like sales growth 
calculation.

Recurring operating income is defined as Danone’s operating income excluding Other operating income and 
expenses. Other operating income and expenses comprise items that, because of their significant or unusual 
nature, cannot be viewed as inherent to Danone’s recurring activity and have limited predictive value, thus 
distorting the assessment of its recurring operating performance and its evolution. These mainly include: 
 capital gains and losses on disposals of businesses and fully consolidated companies; 
 under IAS 36, impairment charges on intangible assets with indefinite useful lives;  
 costs related to strategic restructuring operations or transformation plans; 
 costs related to major external growth transactions; 
 costs related to crises and major disputes; 
 in connection with IFRS 3 and IFRS 10, (i) acquisition costs related to acquisitions of companies resulting in 
control, (ii) revaluation gains or losses accounted for following a loss of control, and (iii) changes in earn -
outs subsequent to acquisitions resulting in control. 
Recurring operating margin is defined as the Recurring operating income over Sales ratio.
