## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
RELX PLC (REN.AS) is a high-quality information and analytics compounder—Elsevier, LexisNexis Risk, Legal, and Exhibitions—whose subscription economics, pricing power, and AI-enabled product rollout support durable mid-single-digit organic growth. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy (8/22 models; composite 55%; Lynch PEG 0.86 on ~24% earnings growth), driven by quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families rather than deep value. At ~20.8× P/E and 2.6% yield, the valuation hook is growth-at-a-reasonable-price after a sharp de-rating, not asset cheapness. The central debate is whether generative AI is a moat-widening tailwind (proprietary data, embedded workflows) or a long-run disintermediation risk to legal and STM publishing. Primary regulatory filings were not retrieved in this pack; financial trends below rely on Yahoo fallback data and should be verified against the 2025 annual report / Form 20-F and H1 2026 interim release before sizing.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, RELX fits the “quality at a fair price” bucket more than classic deep value. The screen passes eight models spanning cheapness (PEG frameworks), quality (Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Magic Formula, Earnings Quality), dividend (Dividend Growth), and risk (Piotroski F-Score 7/9). That profile aligns with a business generating high gross margins (~66%), rising free cash flow, and consistent capital return, rather than a distressed or net-net candidate.

Business quality is the anchor. RELX operates mission-critical databases and decision tools with high switching costs: STM journals and citation indices, legal research platforms, insurance underwriting analytics, and identity/fraud data. Revenue has compounded steadily (Yahoo fallback: $8.55bn in 2022 to $9.59bn in 2025), with net income rising from $1.63bn to $2.07bn over the same period. Operating cash flow reached $2.84bn and free cash flow $2.31bn in 2025 (Yahoo fallback). Management continues to deploy capital aggressively—$1.5bn of buybacks and $1.18bn of dividends in 2025 (Yahoo fallback)—under a stated £2.25bn repurchase programme (news, June 2025).

The screen’s buy signal is therefore coherent: PEG 0.86 and PEGY 0.07 suggest earnings growth is not fully priced, while quality and moat screens confirm franchise durability. What the screen correctly fails is Graham-style deep value and financial-health strictness—P/B 36.8, FCF yield 4.2%, current ratio 0.49, and elevated leverage metrics reflect an intangible-heavy balance sheet and negative working capital typical of subscription publishers, not a broken business. The opportunity is to accumulate a compounder after AI-related sentiment pressure, not to buy a statistically cheap asset.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Competitive / AI disruption.** The dominant market narrative over the past year is whether large language models erode RELX’s legal and STM publishing moats or accelerate product monetisation. Goldman Sachs initiated with Buy citing AI misplacement (27 Jun 2025); Morgan Stanley downgrade sent shares lower (7 May 2025, AskTraders). RELX’s response—LexisNexis Risk partnership with Cytora for AI underwriting (14 May 2025)—shows offensive positioning, but competitive intensity in legal tech and open-access STM pressure remain live risks.

**Leverage and liquidity.** Screen flags high debt-to-equity (~710× on Yahoo basis) and weak liquidity (current ratio 0.49). Low cash ($131m) against $1.54bn current debt maturities within a year creates refinancing dependency, though recurring cash flows mitigate near-term stress. Piotroski fails on “leverage declining” and “current ratio improving.” Capital return exceeding FCF in 2025 adds balance-sheet risk if growth slows.

**Intangible / accounting profile.** ~75% of assets are goodwill and intangibles; tangible book is deeply negative. Impairment risk is low historically but not zero if AI materially impairs STM or Legal segment economics. No filing-body impairment or going-concern language available to cite.

**Regulatory.** Risk and Legal segments face data-privacy, antitrust scrutiny of information monopolies, and sector-specific regulation (insurance, legal services). A greenwashing/retaliation suit was dismissed (Law360, 20 Oct 2025), but regulatory exposure in data-heavy businesses is ongoing.

**Governance / capital allocation.** Sustained buybacks at premium multiples shrink equity and amplify leverage metrics. PDMR share dealings are routine (6 Aug 2025). No governance red flags surfaced in available sources.

**Pension.** Defined benefit obligations ~$197m and non-current pension liabilities ~$154m (Yahoo 2025) are modest relative to EBITDA but unverified against filing footnotes.

**Cyclicality.** Exhibitions and certain Legal end-markets have cyclical elements, though the group screen did not flag cyclical exposure overlay.
