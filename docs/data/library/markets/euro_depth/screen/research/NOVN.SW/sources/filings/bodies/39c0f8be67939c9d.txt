Strategy Our focus Novartis is a “ ” innovative medicines company. We have a clear focus on four core therapeutic areas (cardiovascular- , immunology, neuroscience and oncology), with multiple significant and pipeline assets in each of these areas, that address high disease burden and have substantial growth potential. In addition to two established technology platforms (chemistry and biotherapeutics), three emerging platforms (gene & cell therapy, radioligand therapy and xRNA) are being prioritized for continued investment into new R&D capabilities and manufacturing scale. Geographically, we are focused on growing in our priority geographies – the US, China, Germany and Japan. Our priorities 1. Accelerate growth : Renewed attention to deliver medicines (NMEs) and focus on launch excellence, with a rich pipeline across our core therapeutic areas. 2. Deliver returns : Continuing to embed operational excellence and deliver improved financials. Novartis remains disciplined and shareholder-focused in our approach to capital allocation, with substantial cash generation and a strong capital structure supporting continued flexibility. 3. Strengthen foundations : Unleashing the power of our people, scaling data science and technology, and continuing to build trust with society. Results from ongoing trials and other highlights LOU064 (remibrutinib) Positive results from the Phase III and -2 trials showed oral remibrutinib met its primary endpoint in relapsing multiple sclerosis (RMS), significantly reducing annualized relapse rate (ARR) versus teriflunomide. Remibrutinib also showed superiority on all key secondary endpoints, including MRI lesion reduction, and a clinically meaningful delay in disability progression. The safety profile was favorable and consistent with remibrutinib in CSU, with no liver safety signal. Data were presented at MSToronto2026 in October. EWF980 ( ) In the Phase III HARBOR study, did not meet its primary endpoint of video hand opening time (vHOT) versus placebo in myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1). Evidence of clinical activity was observed in secondary endpoints and exploratory analyses. Novartis is evaluating the full HARBOR dataset and will engage with health authorities to determine the most appropriate development path. Data will be presented at an upcoming medical congress. TQJ230 (pelacarsen) In the Phase III HORIZON trial, pelacarsen did not meet its primary endpoint of reducing the risk of cardiovascular events versus placebo in patients with elevated Lp(a) and established cardiovascular disease (CVD), despite reducing Lp(a) levels in this study population. The findings provide important new evidence on the relationship between Lp(a) lowering and cardiovascular outcomes and may help inform future approaches to cardiovascular risk management. Data will be presented at an upcoming medical congress. Kisqali (ribociclib) The analysis of the Phase III NATALEE trial showed a clinically meaningful overall survival (OS) benefit with Kisqali plus endocrine therapy (ET) versus ET alone in the broadest HR+/HER2- early breast cancer (eBC) population at high risk of recurrence, reducing the risk of death by nearly 20%. Patients had been off Kisqali treatment for a median of 38 months, with clinical benefit continuing to deepen 5 after treatment completion. The safety profile remained consistent with previous analyses. Data were presented at ESMO 2026. LOU064 (remibrutinib) Novartis has discontinued the Phase III study of remibrutinib in generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) following a futility analysis, which indicated the treatment was unlikely to meet its efficacy objectives. No safety concerns were identified, and there is no impact on remibrutinib studies in other indications. Selected transactions Novartis completed the acquisition of Myricx Bio, strengthening the oncology pipeline with a new class of conjugates (ADCs), including two lead assets targeting B7-H3 and HER2, as well as a broader payload platform with potential impact across multiple solid tumor settings. Novartis exercised its exclusive option to acquire full global rights to Sironax's proprietary Brain Delivery Module (BDM) platform, a barrier crossing technology with the potential to transform the treatment of central nervous system diseases. Novartis entered into an exclusive global licensing agreement with BoomRay Pharmaceuticals for an undisclosed preclinical radioligand therapy asset, further strengthening its radioligand therapy portfolio. In October, Novartis entered into a strategic licensing and option agreement with Abogen Biosciences to advance therapeutics, including an exclusive worldwide license to ABO2203, a novel CD19xCD3 T-cell engager with the potential to transform the treatment landscape for autoimmune diseases. The transaction is subject to the completion of customary closing conditions. Capital structure and net debt Retaining a good balance between investment in the business, a strong capital structure, and attractive shareholder returns remains a priority. During the first nine months of 2026, Novartis repurchased 30.9 million shares for USD 4.7 billion on the SIX Swiss Exchange second trading line. These repurchases included 18.8 million shares (USD 2.8 billion) under the USD 10 billion share buyback announced in July 2025 (with up to USD 4.9 billion still to be executed). In addition, 12.1 million shares (USD 1.8 billion) were repurchased to mitigate the anticipated dilution related to participation plans of employees. Further, 2.0 million shares (USD 0.3 billion) were repurchased from employees. During the same period, USD 0.9 billion of compensation plan expenses were recognized to equity and 13.3 million shares were delivered to employees related to compensation plans from prior years. As a result, the total number of shares outstanding decreased by 19.6 million compared with December 31, 2025. These treasury share transactions resulted in an equity decrease of USD 4.1 billion and a cash outflow of USD 4.8 billion. Net debt increased to USD 37.4 billion at September 30, 2026, compared with USD 21.9 billion at December 31, 2025. The increase was mainly due to the free cash flow of USD 14.0 billion being more than offset by the net cash outflow for M&A, intangible assets transactions and other acquisitions of USD 16.7 billion, the USD 9.1 billion annual dividend payment and cash outflows for treasury share transactions of USD 4.8 billion.
