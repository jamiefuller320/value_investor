PRESS RELEASE 
 
 
 
 
Third quarter 2026 results 
 
TotalEnergies is leveraging once again its integrated business model  
and geographic diversification to post adjusted net income of $9.1 billion  
and cash flow of $12.3 billion, a sharp increase quarter-to-quarter 
 
• Gearing reached below 10% by the end of the third quarter 2026 
• New capital increase reserved for employees at a 20% discount on the share price 
• Interim dividend maintained at €0.90/share 
• $2.5 billion share buybacks in 4th quarter 2026 and in 1st quarter 2027 
 
 
   1
 
Paris, October 29, 2026 – The Board of Directors of TotalEnergies SE, chaired by CEO Patrick Pouyanné, 
met on October 28 , 202 6, to approve the 3rd quarter 202 6 financial statements. On the occasion, Patrick 
Pouyanné said: 
“In a high-price environment characterized by historic refining margin levels and a bull gas market, TotalEnergies is 
once again fully leveraging its integrated business model and its geographic diversification by posting an adjusted net 
income of $9.1 billion and cash flow of $12.3 billion for the third quarter, a sharp increase quarter-to-quarter.  
Oil & Gas production reached 2.474 Mboe/d in the third quarter, up more than 3% quarter-to-quarter, notably benefiting 
from a lower impact of the Middle East conflict, limited to only around 5% of the Company’s overall production over 
the quarter on average. Excluding the Middle East impact, production growth exceeds 4% year-on-year, supported by 
the ramp-up of projects launched since 2025. Furthermore, with the investment decisions taken this quarter for Cronos 
in Cyprus, Ima in Nigeria, Umm Shaif Gas Cap in the United Arab Emirates and Absheron Ph2 in Azerbaijan, all the 
projects contributing to achieving the production growth target of more than 3% per year by 2030 are now in execution 
phase. 
Exploration and Production posted adjusted net operating income of $3.2 billion and cash flow of $5.4 billion, reflecting 
production growth, sensitivity to declining oil prices and rising gas prices quarter-to-quarter. The Company completed 
its entry into PEL83 license holding the Mopane discovery and took part in three exploration discoveries in Norway 
(Grimsnes) and in Angola (Block 0 and Block 17 – Violeta 4). TotalEnergies once again demonstrates its leadership 
to control costs, with operating costs of $4.7/boe, anchored below $5/boe. 
The Integrated LNG segment achieved adjusted net operating income of $1.9 billion and cash flow of $1.7 billion in 
the third quarter of 2026, a very sharp increase quarter-to-quarter, underpinned by a 20% increase in average LNG 
price and the outperformance of LNG and gas trading activities in a bull market.

Downstream achieved a record quarter with adjusted net operating income of $4.1 billion and cash flow of $4.6 billion, 
up close to $2 billion compared to the previous quarter. The increase of nearly 10% refining throughput enabled the 
Company to fully capture historically high refining margins in Europe and worldwide, while oil trading activities 
outperformed at a level comparable with the two previous quarters. In this context of rising prices, the Company 
contributes to the security of supply for Europe by maximizing the use of its refineries and participating in diesel imports 
from the Middle East. TotalEnergies also announced enhanced protective measures for French consumers and 
economic players to face increasing diesel prices related to the Middle East conflict. 
Integrated Power posted adjusted net operating income of $0.6 billion and cash flow of nearly $0.8 billion, benefiting 
from an increase of more than 20% in net production in the quarter and the expected contribution from TTEP assets 
over a full quarter.  
Net investments amounted to $3.3 billion in the third quarter and $11.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, in line 
with the annual guidance of $15 billion. The gearing ratio now stands at 9.1%, an improvement of 4 percentage points 
compared to the second quarter, benefiting from free cash flow of more than $6 billion after payment of the dividend. 
The Board of Directors commended the very good operational performance of all business segments which enabled 
the Company to capture higher market prices. It authorized a new capital increase reserved for employees, who will 
benefit from a 20% discount on the share price. In line with the objective to return 40% of cash flow to shareholders, 
the Board confirmed a third interim dividend of €0.90/share for fiscal year 2026, up 5.9% compared to 2025, and it 
authorized share buybacks for cancellation of $2.5 billion for the fourth quarter as well as for the first quarter 2027.”

3.3 Production (14)* 
 
  
        
Hydrocarbon production was 2,474 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day in the third quarter of 2026, up 
more than 3% compared to the second quarter, benefiting from a gradual return-to-normal oil production in the 
Middle East. 
 
Hydrocarbon production is down 1% year-on-year primarily due to: 
• +4% from project start ‑ups and project ramp-ups, notably Mero‑4 and Lapa SW in Brazil, Anchor and 
Ballymore in the United States, Begonia and Clov Phase 3 in Angola , Mabruk in Libya  and Ratawi in 
Iraq, 
• +2% due to improved plant availability,  
• -2% due to the natural decline of fields, 
• -5% due to the impact of the conflict in the Middle East. 
 
Excluding the impact of the conflict in the Middle East, production would be up by more than 4% year-on-year.

GLOSSARY 
Acquisitions net of assets sales is a non-GAAP financial measure and its most directly comparable IFRS measure is Cash flow used 
in investing activities. Acquisitions net of assets sales refer to acquisitions minus assets sales (including other operations with non -
controlling interests). This indicator can be a valuable tool for decision makers, analysts and shareholders alike because it illustrates the 
allocation of cash flow used for growing the Company’s asset base via external growth opportunities. 
 
Adjusted EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation and Amortization) is a non-GAAP financial measure and its most directly 
comparable IFRS measure is Net Income. It refers to the adjusted earnings before depreciation, depletion and impairment of tangible and 
intangible assets and mineral interests, income tax expense and cost of net debt, i.e., all operating income and contribution o f equity 
affiliates to net income. This indicator can be a valuable tool for decision makers, analysts and shareholders alike to measure and compare 
the Company’s profitability with utility companies (energy sector). 
 
Adjusted net income (TotalEnergies share) is a non-GAAP financial measure and its most directly comparable IFRS measure is Net 
Income (TotalEnergies share). Adjusted Net Income (TotalEnergies share) refers to Net Income (TotalEnergies share) less adjus tment 
items to Net Income (TotalEnergies share ). Adjustment items are inventory valuation effect, effect of changes in fair value, and special 
items. This indicator can be a valuable tool for decision makers, analysts and shareholders alike to evaluate the Company’s o perating 
results and to understand its operating trends by removing the impact of non-operational results and special items. 
 
Adjusted net operating income  is a non -GAAP financial measure and its most directly comparable IFRS measure is Net Income. 
Adjusted Net Operating Income refers to Net Income before net cost of net debt, i.e., cost of net debt net of its tax effects, less adjustment 
items. Adjustment items are inventory valuation effect, effect of changes in fair value, and special items. Adjusted Net Operating Income 
can be a valuable tool for decision makers, analysts and shareholders alike to evaluate the Company’s operating results and 
understanding its operating trends, by removing the impact of non-operational results and special items and is used to evaluate the Return 
on Average Capital Employed (ROACE) as explained below. 
 
Capital Employed is a non-GAAP financial measure. They are calculated at replacement cost and refer to capital employed (balance 
sheet) less inventory valuations effect. Capital employed (balance sheet) refers to the sum of the following items: (i) Prope rty, plant and 
equipment, intangible assets, net, (ii) Investments & loans in equity affiliates, (iii) Other non -current assets, (iv) Working capital which is 
the sum of: Inventories, net, Accounts receivable, net, other current assets, Accounts payable, Other creditors and accrued liabilities, (v) 
Provisions and other non-current liabilities and (vi) Assets and liabilities classified as held for sale. Capital Employed can be a valuable 
tool for decision makers, analysts and shareholders alike to provide insight on the amount of capital investment used by the Company or 
its business segments to operate. Capital Employed is used to calculate the Return on Average Capital Employed (ROACE). 
 
Cash Flow From Operations excluding working capital (CFFO)  is a non-GAAP financial measure and its most directly comparable 
IFRS measure is Cash flow from operating activities. Cash Flow From Operations excluding working capital is defined as cash flow from

Integrated Power contracts, including capital gain from renewable projects sales and including organic loan repayments from e quity 
affiliates.
