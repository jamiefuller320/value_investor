Essity Aktiebolag (publ) Interim Report Quarter 3, 2026 1
CEO’s comments  
Essity’s development in the third quarter was 
characterized by stability and resilience in a 
challenging market. Profitability remained good 
despite intensifying cost headwinds, and cash flow 
was strong. Volumes were stable, and we 
strengthened our market positions in several strategic 
segments, demonstrating the competitiveness of our 
business. At the same time, the Group’s sales 
development was not at the level we are aiming for, 
and we continue our efforts to accelerate profitable 
growth and offset higher costs through price 
increases and efficiency improvements. Cost savings 
were delivered according to plan, investments in 
marketing and customer offerings are yielding results, 
and sales prices were higher than in the second 
quarter of this year. 
Demonstrated strength across several categories
Health & Medical delivered organic growth and good 
profitability during the quarter. Incontinence Products 
continued to grow, and the sales development in 
Medical Solutions was positive, with good growth in 
wound care. Sales in Personal Care also performed 
well, supported by continued high growth and 
increased market shares in Incontinence Products and 
Feminine Care. Consumer Tissue had lower sales due to 
lower volumes, primarily driven by optimization of the 
production footprint in Latin America, and price 
reductions in previous quarters. In Professional 
Hygiene, profitability remained solid, although volume 
development was not satisfactory. The decline was 
concentrated to the base assortment and smaller 
distributors which were more affected by the weaker 
market. However, we continued to grow in strategic 
products and increased sales to our larger distributors 
and customers. 
Product launches and price increases
During the quarter, we launched new and improved 
products across several categories to further 
strengthen our offering. For example, we expanded our 
range of compression garments with JOBST® Elvarex® 
Soft ready-to-wear, allowing us to reach patients more 
quickly. 
We continued to actively work on price increases to 
offset higher costs, and all business areas reported 
higher sales prices compared with the second quarter.
“Our focus is on translating 
strong market positions into 
profitable growth”
Another acquisition in feminine care
During the quarter, we took another step forward in our 
strategy to grow in attractive, value-creating categories 
by entering into an agreement to acquire Kenvue’s 
feminine care business in Brazil. The acquisition 
includes the market-leading brands Carefree, Sempre 
Livre and o.b., and strengthens Essity’s position in 
feminine care in Latin America. Brazil represents a 
significant growth opportunity in hygiene and health, 
where we already hold a leading position in 
incontinence products with TENA. This acquisition 
follows our previous feminine care acquisition in North 
America and represents another concrete step in the 
expansion of our feminine care business, where we 
have a proven track record of success and see good 
potential for profitable growth. The transaction is 
subject to regulatory approval and is expected to close 
in the second quarter of 2027.
Strategic review of Consumer Tissue
The strategic review of Consumer Tissue is proceeding 
according to plan. We have assessed the conditions for 
a separation and are now ready to move forward with 
evaluating the value creation potential of the different 
strategic alternatives - a separation or Consumer Tissue 
remaining part of Essity. The ambition is to identify the 
alternative that provides the best opportunity for both 
Consumer Tissue and Essity to reach their full potential 
and maximize long-term value creation.
Focus on higher growth
Our focus is on translating strong market positions into 
profitable growth. We continue to prioritize 
investments where we see the greatest opportunities to 
grow and create value, while improving efficiency and 
addressing increasing cost headwinds through pricing 
and cost measures. In parallel, we continue to develop 
our portfolio. We have clear priorities and will now 
ensure that our initiatives have a greater impact on the 
Group’s growth and profitability.
Ulrika Kolsrud 
President and CEO

Group
Net sales
Net sales increased 4.1% in the third quarter of 2026 
compared with the corresponding period a year ago 
and amounted to SEK 36,060m (34,638). The 
acquisition in Feminine Care in North America 
increased the Group's consolidated net sales by 1.9%.
Organic sales decreased 0.3%, primarily as a result of 
price reductions in previous quarters. Sales prices were 
higher in the quarter compared with the second quarter

positively and volumes were stable.
Growth was positive  in North America, with a high 
growth in both Health & Medical and Personal Care. In 
Europe and Latin America, organic sales were slightly 
lower, primarily due to reduced sales in Consumer 
Tissue.

Financial position
Net debt decreased SEK 1,591m compared with 
December 31, 2025 and amounted to SEK 24,952m. The 
decrease is mainly related to revaluation of pension 
assets and pension obligations. Net cash flow increased 
net debt by SEK 2,309m. The Group’s interest-bearing 
gross debt amounted to SEK 35,208m (35,187) at the 
end of the quarter. The average maturity period was 
2.6 years (3.6).
Compared with December 31, 2025, working capital 
increased to SEK 15,304m, mainly due to higher 
valuation of energy derivatives and increased trade 
receivables. Working capital amounted to 10.6% (9.2) of 
net sales.
Share buyback program
Between July 1 and September 30, 2026, Essity bought 
back 4,101,510 own Class B shares for a total amount of 
SEK 1,117m. The buybacks are part of the buyback 
program for SEK 3bn that Essity announced on April 22, 
2026. The buyback program extends from May 12, 2026 
until the 2027 Annual General Meeting at the latest. As 
of September 30, 2026, Essity’s holdings of own shares 
amounted to 7,458,875, corresponding to 1.1% of the 
total number of shares. Repurchased shares are 
expected to be canceled. The buybacks will be 
financed using cash flow from current operations after 
the ordinary dividend with the ambition to continue 
with share buybacks over time as a recurring part of 
Essity’s capital allocation.

Note 2 Risks and uncertainties
Processes for risk management
Essity’s Board determines the Group’s strategic direction based on recommendations 
from the Executive Management Team. Responsibility for the long-term, overall 
management of strategic risks corresponds to the company’s delegation structure, 
from the Board of Directors to the CEO and from the CEO to the Business Area 
Presidents. This means that most operational risks are managed by Essity’s business 
areas at the local level, but they are centrally coordinated when considered 
necessary. The tools used for coordination consist primarily of the business areas 
regular reporting and the annual strategy process, which includes risks and risk 
management. 
Essity’s financial risk management is centralized, as is its internal bank for financial 
transactions conducted by Group companies and the management of the Group’s 
energy risks. Financial risks are managed in accordance with the Group’s Finance 
Policy, which is adopted by Essity’s Board and, together with Essity’s Energy Risk 
Policy, provides a management framework. Risks are continuously compiled and 
monitored to ensure compliance with these guidelines. Essity has also centralized 
other risk management. 
Essity has a staff function for internal audit, which monitors compliance with the 
Group’s policies.
Essity’s risk exposure and risk management are described on pages 41–46 in the 2025 
Annual Report and in the sections under environmental information and social 
information on pages 58–60. No significant changes have taken place that have 
affected the reported risks. 
Risks in conjunction with company acquisitions are analyzed in the due diligence 
processes that Essity carries out prior to all acquisitions. In cases where acquisitions 
have been carried out that may affect the assessment of Essity’s risk exposure, these 
are described under the heading “Events during the quarter” in the interim or year-
end reports. 
Ongoing geopolitical tensions, such as the war in Iran and the conflicts in and around 
the Persian Gulf, contribute to increased economic uncertainty and rising commodity 
prices. The prices of oil and gas have increased significantly following the outbreak of 
the war. Changes in prices of commodities such as oil and gas may directly or 
indirectly affect the Group’s financial position and operating profit as a result of 
higher costs, price increases and reduced purchasing power among customers and 
consumers.

Note 7 Segment reporting
Effective January 1, 2026, Essity has implemented an organizational change, simplifying its structure and creating end-to-end accountability. This aims to further enhance 
customer and consumer focus, while increasing speed, agility and operational efficiency throughout the Group. The change facilitates increased strategic focus on the product 
categories and segments with the greatest potential for profitable growth. The new organization consists of the business areas Health & Medical, Personal Care, Consumer 
Tissue and Professional Hygiene, with clear overall responsibility in each business area for innovation, production, marketing and sales. The former Consumer Goods business 
area has been split into Personal Care and Consumer Tissue. Essity’s former global units Global Marketing & Innovation and Global Supply Chain have been integrated into the 
four business areas. The tables below show parts of the income statement broken down by operating segment: Health & Medical, Personal Care, Consumer Tissue and
