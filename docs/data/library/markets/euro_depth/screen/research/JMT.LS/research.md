## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Jerónimo Martins is a family-controlled Iberian food-retail group whose economic engine is Biedronka (Poland), supplemented by Pingo Doce (Portugal), Recheio (food service), Hebe, and Ara (Colombia). The quantitative screen rates the name a buy on cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk families — anchored by a ~6% FCF yield, 3.6% dividend yield, 20% ROE, and earnings-quality pass — despite failing deep-value and balance-sheet screens. At ~18× P/E and 3.7× P/B, the valuation hook is cash-generative scale at a modest premium to book, not a distressed entry. The central debate is whether Biedronka’s margin resilience and store rollout can offset Polish deflationary basket pressure, flat statutory earnings (-0.5% screen input), and IFRS 16-inflated leverage metrics that obscure a net-cash operating profile on a pre-lease basis. Primary filing coverage in the research pack stops at FY2022 ESEF extracts; recent H1 2026 trends must be triangulated from secondary sources.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, JMT offers a **quality compounder at a reasonable price** rather than a classic Graham deep-value ticket. The screen’s buy signal rests on four durable pillars:

1. **Cash and earnings quality** — FCF Yield and Earnings Quality models pass; Yahoo-sourced FY2025 operating cash flow was €2,425m against free cash flow of €1,368m, with TTM FCF of ~€1.19bn per the screen snapshot. OCF consistently exceeds net income, supporting the earnings-quality pass.

2. **Dividend + GARP overlay** — Dividend Growth and Neff PEGY (PEGY 0.05) pass despite near-flat statutory EPS growth, implying the market prices minimal growth expectations into a yield-supported multiple.

3. **Operational moat in discount food retail** — Biedronka is Poland’s leading hard-discount chain; scale purchasing, private label, and dense store network underpin ROE of 20% and Magic Formula pass (high returns on capital relative to price).

4. **Risk-adjusted balance sheet (economic view)** — The screen flags high debt/equity (179%) and weak current ratio (0.62), but ESEF filings show **net cash on a pre-IFRS 16 basis**: net debt/EBITDA of (0.9)× at FY2021 and FY2022, with unused credit lines and covenant compliance. Lease liabilities inflate headline leverage; the Risk family still passes.

What the screen does **not** capture: Poland revenue concentration, family governance, and the cyclicality of food retail margins in a deflationary basket environment — topics addressed below.

Peer context (euro_depth Consumer Defensive): JMT’s 5/22 model pass count aligns with buy-rated peers (TSCO.L, GRG.L, BAG.L at 8/22; DRX.L at 7/22). JMT uniquely combines FCF Yield + Dividend Growth + Magic Formula + Earnings Quality + Neff PEGY — a cash-yield + quality blend rather than a contrarian deep-value profile.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Poland / Biedronka concentration.** The group’s value driver is Biedronka. News flow (September 2026) references basket deflation and margin pressure at Biedronka despite a recent margin beat. FX exposure to the zloty is hedged economically (forwards per 2022 ESEF), but earnings translation risk remains.

**Competitive intensity.** Hard-discount retail in Poland faces Lidl, Aldi, and potential consolidation (Biedronka signalled interest in Carrefour Poland assets, March 2026). Margin defence requires continuous price investment.

**IFRS 16 leverage optics.** Capital lease obligations of €4.3bn (Yahoo FY2025) drive screen failures on Graham Enterprising, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Financial Health, and Economic Moat (ROA depressed by leased asset base). Economic net cash per filings is better than screen leverage suggests, but refinancing and lease-rate sensitivity matter — incremental lease discount rate rose to 5.29% at FY2022 (from 2.56% at FY2021) per ESEF.

**Liquidity presentation.** Negative working capital is structurally normal for food retail but creates screen failure on current ratio and Piotroski (leverage not declining, asset turnover not improving). Q1 2026 negative FCF warrants monitoring of seasonal patterns.

**Governance.** Family control (Soares dos Santos) is embedded in debt covenants (“maintenance of control by current shareholder”). Limited float and related-party dynamics are not fully assessable from this pack.

**Regulatory and litigation.** ESEF 2021 filings disclose tax litigation provisions and ongoing judicial processes (commercial, labour, regulatory). Polish and Portuguese retail regulation (pricing, labour, environmental) adds compliance cost.

**Macro / cyclical.** 2022 ESEF risk disclosure cites Ukraine conflict, energy crisis, inflation, and supply-chain disruption as material operating headwinds. No going-concern qualification; management expects to meet obligations from OCF and liquidity reserves.

**Earnings trajectory.** Statutory EPS growth of -0.5% (screen) and H1 2026 profitability below Q3 2025 peak suggest the easy post-COVID margin normalisation phase may be ending.

**Data quality.** Stale primary filings (latest indexed: FY2022) limit ability to verify recent margin bridges, segment splits, or auditor emphasis-of-matter paragraphs.
