CEO’s statement
The release of our third quarter 2026 results is 
overshadowed by the death of a colleague in 
what may be our first work-related fatality in 18 
years. Our thoughts go out to his family, 
colleagues, friends and others affected by this 
tragedy.  
Safety always comes first at Boliden, and I want 
to give credit to all my colleagues who report 
risks before they become incidents and adapt 
their workplaces and processes to mitigate 
identified risks. Courage to question 
established ways of working is often 
required in this respect and it is only 
together that we create an environment 
where this is part of our everyday work.   
Looking at the third quarter, we announced the acquisition of a controlling stake in Nexa, a zinc and 
silver-focused base metal producer with five mines and three smelters across Brazil and Peru. Once 
the transaction has been finalized, this will position us as a global leader within zinc, and we look 
forward to welcoming Nexa to the Boliden family. 
Our existing operations delivered stable production across both business areas. Among our mines, 
Somincor has really integrated well into Boliden and had a very strong production, while Kevitsa set 
a new record for milled volume and Garpenberg improved according to plan. At Aitik, mine 
production remained high but with higher waste rock dilution than initially planned. This has an 
impact on both the milled volume and average copper grade for the year, and we have updated 
Aitik’s guidance accordingly in this report. 
On the smelter side, Harjavalta set a new record for its nickel feed and the utilization of the new 
roaster at Odda increased significantly towards the end of the quarter, meaning that the ramp-up, 
while slower than expected, is now gathering pace. On the theme of investment projects coming to 
a close, Rönnskär’s new tankhouse is nearing completion and we look forward to soon being able 
to deliver copper cathodes “made in Sweden” once again.  
Finally, prices and terms continued to strengthen, with an increasing sulphuric acid price boosting 
by-product revenue from our smelters. There is a growing consensus that the copper price has a 
new floor, and this reinforces our already strong business case for Laver, the copper deposit for 
which the Swedish government in August upheld our mining concession. While a final investment 
decision will be made in a few years’ time following receipt of an environmental permit, we strongly 
believe in Laver’s potential to become a new benchmark open-pit mine with the world-class 
productivity and ESG performance that characterize our operations. Laver alone will deliver around 
10% of Europe’s domestic copper mine supply and will be crucial to our continent’s raw material 
sovereignty going forward.  
On that note, I am also happy to share that I have assumed the presidency of Euromines. This 
organization acts as the voice and representative body for the European metals and minerals 
mining industry, promoting responsible industry practices and advocating for the sector within 
European Union (EU) policymaking. In this role, I will continue to advocate for the conditions Europe 
needs to maintain a globally competitive mining sector: faster permitting processes, a coherent 
policy landscape, and investment in European mining.  
 
Mikael Staffas

Market developments   
Commodity markets were characterized by heightened geopolitical uncertainty and energy price 
volatility during the quarter.  
Higher energy costs renewed inflation concerns, shifting monetary policy expectations from easing 
towards tightening, with several major central banks raising interest rates. Rising bond yields, also 
reflecting concerns over government debt and fiscal sustainability, tightened financial conditions 
and increased risks to global growth and industrial demand. Against this background, supply 
constraints, trade policy uncertainty and demand from AI infrastructure and energy transition 
technologies supported parts of the metals market. 
Average metal prices and exchange rates 
 
Zinc 
Global demand for refined zinc increased by 4% versus the previous quarter reflecting seasonal 
variation but was down 3% compared to the third quarter of 2025. However, the longer-term 
average global rolling 12-month zinc consumption remained stable around 13.4 Mtonnes. Zinc 
metal premia rose in the US and Europe during the quarter as lower smelter output tightened 
regional supply, with additional support from resilient US demand.  
Global zinc concentrate production increased marginally compared to the previous quarter, 
remaining stable compared with the third quarter of 2025. Spot TCs for standard qualities 
continued a steep decline, ending the quarter at USD -130/tonne. However, the annual benchmark 
TC remains unchanged at USD 85/tonne.  
The average LME zinc price increased by 10% compared with the previous quarter and 35% 
compared with the third quarter of 2025. 
Zinc price  Global zinc demand, rolling 12 months 
 
 
 
Copper 
Worldwide consumption of refined copper decreased by 8% versus the previous quarter, gaining 
marginally compared with the third quarter of 2025. Whilst the longer-term trend reflects continued 
demand growth, the rolling 12-month average suggests stabilized demand around 28 Mtonnes 
over the past year.  
Concentrate production gained marginally against the previous quarter and the third quarter of 
2025. Spot treatment charges for standard grades continued to decline, reaching record low levels 
by the end of the quarter at USD -297/tonne, reflecting significant ongoing structural tightness in 
the concentrate market. Benchmark Chinese contracts remained at zero TC/RCs.  
The average LME copper price continued to rise, gaining 6% and 44% compared with the previous 
quarter and the third quarter of 2025 respectively.

Note 5. Breakdown of external income 
The sale of metal concentrates, metals, intermediate products and by-products is recognized upon 
delivery to the customer in accordance with the terms and conditions of sale, i.e. revenue is 
recognized when control passes to the purchaser. The Group’s metal concentrates are invoiced 
provisionally upon delivery. Definitive invoicing takes place once all relevant parameters 
(concentrate, quantity, metal content, impurity content and metal price) for the price setting period 
have been determined. 
Customers are invoiced for the Group’s metals and intermediate products upon delivery. The Group 
eliminates price risks associated with sales and purchases by hedging the difference between 
purchased and sold quantities on a daily basis. Customers are invoiced for by-products when 
control is transferred, which takes place upon delivery.

Mining concession for Laver upheld 
In August, the Swedish government announced that it had rejected the appeals against Boliden’s mining concession for Laver, a 
potential future mine that when operational could supply up to 10% of Europe’s domestic copper production. Boliden will now 
continue with the pre-feasibility study for Laver and will eventually apply for an environmental permit.  
“The Laver copper deposit has been of interest to us for more than 15 years and this is a major step forward. It has a strategic location 
in relation to essential infrastructure and the potential to achieve a favorable ore to waste rock ratio. When it comes to the 
environmental permit, there is sensitive nature and water bodies in the surroundings and we will of course design the mine with this in 
mind. Laver will bring around 1,300 direct and indirect job opportunities and we are cautious of both the benefits and challenges this 
will bring to the area. Therefore, we have already at this early stage-initiated dialogue with the Älvsbyn municipality around how 
Boliden can play an active part in building the community “, says Patrik Westerlund, Project Manager for the Laver project. 
Historically, the Laver deposit was mined by Boliden from 1938 until 1946 by appointment of the Swedish state. As per Boliden’s latest 
Mineral Resources and Mineral Reserves statement, it has indicated resources of 845,900 Ktonnes with a copper grade of 0.24%. An 
investment decision for Laver is expected around 2030 with operations that could potentially start around five years later.
