ANNUAL REPORT 2025 1
About this report
The 2025 Annual Report outlines the strategic priorities and key developments 
that shaped Adyen over the past year. It gives readers a clear view of our 
business, financial performance, and risk management, while offering deeper 
insight into the topics that guide our operations and support Adyen’s long-term 
growth.
The year in review summarizes key milestones of the year. Adyen at a glance 
gives a high-level overview of who we are and what we do as a company. In the 
rest of the Management Review we discuss our approach to key focus areas in 
our business, showing the steps we have taken this year to support our long-term 
success. Following this, the Corporate Governance chapter outlines the 
structures and policies that ensure effective oversight, transparency, and 
accountability within our organization. 
This year marks the second year of our Sustainability Statement, which sets out 
our material sustainability topics and the way these are identified, managed, and 
reported in accordance with the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive 
(CSRD) and the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS). Together, 
the Management Review,the Corporate Governance chapter and the 
Sustainability Statement constitute the Management Report. Finally, the 
Financial Statements provide a comprehensive account of Adyen’s financial 
position, performance, and cash flows over the reporting period.
To support navigation, readers can move between sections of the report using the 
buttons at the bottom of each page. These provide direct access to each chapter 
and allow for intuitive browsing throughout the document.

Message from the

Dear reader,
We are proud of the progress we made in 2025 as we continued executing 
against our long-term vision. Our focus remained consistent throughout the year: 
building a financial technology platform that enables our customers to operate 
with greater efficiency, resilience, and control as they scale globally.
Macroeconomic and geopolitical developments in the first half reshaped the 
operating environment for some of our customers, prompting them to reassess 
where and how they grow. Policy shifts, including changes to U.S. tariffs, 
accelerated the need to pivot to new regions. We supported our customers 
through these changes by enabling them to enter and scale in alternative markets 
with speed and confidence. Using our single platform and licensed infrastructure, 
they could access local payment rails and maintain consistent performance 
without reworking their setup.
As AI continued to be a central topic within the financial services industry, our 
customers were highly engaged in understanding its potential impact on their 
businesses. Our approach remains grounded: we build with our customers in mind 
and we apply AI where it meaningfully improves outcomes. By embedding 
intelligence directly into our infrastructure, rather than treating it as a standalone 
capability, we strengthen how payments, risk, and financial flows operate end 
to end.
The scale of our data, combined with our single tech stack and AI, is enabling a 
new level of performance across the platform. This is evident in the evolution of 
Dynamic Identification as a foundational layer. Built on our single stack and 
banking infrastructure, it draws on trillions of interactions across online and in-
store commerce to inform decisions in real time. As fraud grows more 
sophisticated, Dynamic Identification allows us to move beyond static checks and 
instead balance security, conversion, and customer experience with greater 
precision — supporting safe, scalable commerce.

Adyen at a glance
Adyen is the financial technology platform trusted by the world’s leading 
businesses. We simplify global commerce by replacing what is traditionally a 
fragmented, multi-party setup with a single platform built entirely in-house.
Our product offering spans both payments and financial services, all operated end-
to-end on our own infrastructure and banking licenses. This model gives us a level 
of control, reliability, and global consistency that sets us apart. It enables us to 
support enterprise and platform customers with complex, worldwide operations.
Our approach is pragmatic and customer-centric. We continuously evolve our 
platform and offerings to address long-term customer needs. We build products 
that work seamlessly across regions and benefit all customers rather than a single 
use case. This philosophy shapes how we innovate and how we partner with the 
businesses we serve. With 4,771 FTEs and 29 offices across 21 countries, our 
teams collaborate directly across time zones, guided by the Adyen Formula — 
our principles for decision-making that keep us building with speed, autonomy, 
and the long-term top of mind. 
We organize our commercial activities into three pillars. Digital supports online-
first enterprises such as subscription businesses, travel, and mobility. Unified 
Commerce connects online and in-person payments for global retailers, 
hospitality, and food & beverage groups. Platforms enables software providers to 
embed payments and financial services, including capital, issuing, and business 
accounts, into their offerings.
With this foundation, Adyen supports the full spectrum of how money moves for 
the world’s largest enterprises and the fastest-growing software platforms 
through a single, scalable solution.

Business strategy
The evolution of our foundation
From the beginning, we made a strategic choice: rather than build a business 
around the limitations of external providers, we would develop and operate the 
essential layers of the financial stack ourselves. That decision set us on a long-
term path to build a foundation that could support global commerce with the 
consistency, control, and speed required to scale.
The first step was our single global platform — we removed much of the 
fragmentation that typically characterizes global payment systems. This unified 
architecture allowed decisions to be made with full contextual awareness, 
improving authorization quality, reducing friction for legitimate shoppers, and 
simplifying operations for our customers. It also ensured that any improvement 
we make, from responding to new regulations to tackling emerging fraud 
patterns, takes effect everywhere, without reintegration work.
As our merchants grew, so did the need for predictable fund flows and consistent 
regulatory alignment across regions. This led to the next layer: acquiring our own 
banking licenses in the EU, US, and UK. Operating these licenses ourselves 
allows us to perform mission-critical activities without relying on partner banks, 
improving reliability and settlement speed. It also enables products such as 
issuing, bank accounts, and capital to function within the same unified 
environment, allowing our customers to unlock new revenue streams.
In 2025, we introduced the third layer to our foundation: Dynamic Identification. It 
establishes a behavioral trust layer based on real economic activity across our 
platform, allowing us to assess legitimacy in context rather than through isolated, 
point-in-time checks. The result is a smoother experience for trusted shoppers, 
fewer instances of hidden abuse, and stronger decision-making across 
onboarding, capital offering, and compliance. 
Each layer builds on the one before it. The single platform generates unified, high-
quality data. Our integrated banking infrastructure provides the environment to 
act on that data responsibly and consistently. And Dynamic Identification 
transforms it into evolving trust signals that grow stronger with scale. 
Together, they form a resilient foundation designed not only to support our 
customers today, but to prepare them for the next era of global commerce.
Our offering
As enterprise customers grow globally, the movement of money has become 
increasingly complex. Pay-ins, liquidity optimizations, and payouts often run 
across different banks, systems, and geographies, making it difficult to maintain 
visibility, reconcile transactions, or optimize liquidity. We address this by 
extending our platform beyond payments to manage the full lifecycle of funds for 
enterprise customers, using the same integrated foundation that powers our 
payments capabilities.
Intelligent Money Movement (IMM) brings money-in, money management, and 
money-out into one operating environment. By consolidating these activities, 
enterprise customers reduce their reliance on multiple banking partners and gain a 
clearer, real-time view of their global cash positions. This helps lower operational 
costs, frees up working capital that would otherwise sit idle, and improves 
financial predictability.
A key element is providing customers with secure accounts directly on our 
platform, enabling flexible settlement timing and tools to support liquidity when 
needed. From there, they can move funds efficiently to suppliers, partners, or end 
users, whether through fast global payouts or virtual cards for managing 
expenses. We are initially focusing this offering on industries where fund flows are 
particularly complex – such as retail marketplaces, travel, mobility, and insurance 
– in regions where we operate under our own banking licenses. Over time, we will 
extend the model more broadly.
This sits alongside our offering for platform customers, which already combines 
payments, accounts, issuing, and capital.  In 2025, these products continued to 
accelerate as platform customers increasingly relied on Adyen to power more of 
their operational and financial workflows. Embedded payments remained a strong 
fit as we partnered with winning platforms to reach the long tail of the market. For 
many of these customers, embedded payments is the first step to unlocking a 
broader suite of integrated financial services.
Over the last year, Adyen Issuing saw an 8x YoY increase in volume, driven by use 
cases such as expense management, disbursements, and controlled spend. 
Capital also scaled significantly, with loan volumes doubling over the year as 
platforms and enterprise customers leveraged embedded financing to support 
their users’ growth. Payouts maintained steady momentum with approximately 
40% YoY growth, enabling tens of billions of euros to move between Adyen and 
external banks each month. We also introduced CashOut this year, our newest 
financial product, which reached a run rate of several hundred million euros in 
annual volume within its first nine months, even while still in pilot phase.
Customer-driven innovation 
Our Adyen Uplift product suite solves a long-standing problem of balancing 
conversion, risk management, and cost. In 2025, routing optimization customers 
saw average scheme fee and interchange cost savings of 9.62%, a 42% reduction 
in false positives, and conversion improvements of up to 6% at the point of 
transaction. As usage deepens and more modules are adopted, performance 
continues to improve as decisions benefit from a growing, higher-quality data set.
Building on the success of the foundational modules introduced in 2024 – 
Optimize, Protect, Authenticate, and Tokenize – we expanded the suite in 2025 
with the pilot introduction of Personalize. Powered by Dynamic Identification, 
Personalize tailors the checkout experience for each shopper by automatically 
adjusting authentication methods and recommending the most cost-efficient 
payment options when they are likely to be accepted. This allows customers to 
lower payment costs without compromising conversion, all while ensuring 
shoppers experience a smooth and intuitive checkout. 
Ultimately, Adyen Uplift embodies our philosophy: deploying AI not as a feature, 
but as the engine that guarantees higher performance, foundational trust, and a 
definitive new standard for global commerce.
As intelligence deepens across our platform, we are also applying it to the rails on 
which payments run. In 2025, Intelligent Payment Routing (IPR) moved from pilot 
to full scale, seeing rapid adoption in its first full year. In the US, customers 
benefited from average cost savings of 9.2% for all IPR-evaluated transactions 
and an increase of +25 bps in authorization rates. We also brought IPR to 
Australia, where it now runs on local debit rails (Eftpos), serving 92 customers 
and providing AU$0.14 savings per transaction. Altogether, IPR delivered 
approximately $14.5 million in savings for our customers in 2025.

The future of commerce 
The foundation we have built also positions us for the next major shift in the 
industry: the rise of agentic commerce. Just as mobile transformed desktop and 
unified commerce reshaped retail, AI-driven agents are beginning to streamline 
the shopper journey from discovery to purchase. We see this as an emerging 
channel that will evolve steadily over the coming years, with early 
experimentation already underway across industries.
As with every fundamental change in commerce, our approach begins with our 
customers: we listen to their needs and build to address them. In 2025, across 
more than 100 conversations with global retailers, platforms, travel brands, and 
marketplaces, we saw strong engagement and thoughtful perspectives. Our 
merchants recognize the potential for new revenue streams and customer 
experiences, provided they retain control over their brand, data, and customer 
relationships. Delivering that control is exactly what our infrastructure is 
designed to do.
By extending our core capabilities – authentication, tokenization, risk intelligence, 
and now Dynamic Identification – into agent-led flows, we are building the 
safeguards, interoperability, and standards that keep merchants at the center of 
this new ecosystem. Our single platform acts as a neutral, unifying layer that 
ensures any agent can transact with any merchant safely, transparently, and on 
the merchant’s terms.
As we advance into this new era, we do so responsibly by favoring open 
standards over closed, proprietary approaches and actively contributing to 
industry initiatives such as the Agentic AI Foundation. We continue to embed 
ethical decision frameworks, strengthened sensitive data management, and 
sustainability considerations across our AI and infrastructure development. 
Internally, we automate where it genuinely improves workflows, while investing in 
the talent and capabilities that will drive the next evolution of our platform. 
In this way, agentic commerce becomes not a disruptive bypass of the traditional 
journey, but a trusted extension of it, powered by the same foundation that has 
guided our evolution for more than a decade.
Delivering in growth markets
The results of our long-term investments in high potential markets became even 
clearer this year. As digitization accelerated and regulatory demands increased 
across India, Japan, Mexico, and Brazil, businesses relied on us for direction.This 
reinforced our belief that sustainable growth comes from building for the long term.
2025 was a foundational year for Adyen in India. We expanded our local teams 
and infrastructure, and integrated UPI to support seamless real-time payments. 
We went live on our own acquiring rails with Visa, Mastercard, Amex, and RuPay 
— making us one of the first global fintechs to connect directly to India’s national 
card network. Additionally, we launched our cross-border license with a leading 
global software company. Our Bengaluru hub grew from 15 to over 60 people 
across key teams, strengthening our ability to build in India, for India.
In Japan, we supported customers through the March 3DS mandate by providing 
clear guidance and technical readiness. With a continued focus on deepening 
localization, we expanded into retail segments where our offering is a strong fit for 
the Japanese market and advanced our local payment method strategy. This 
included the launch of PayPay, Japan’s leading mobile wallet, further 
strengthening our ability to support local consumer preferences. We also saw 
early interest in Adyen Uplift, and began testing data center operations in major 
cities to bolster long-term regional capabilities.
In Latin America, progress in Brazil and Mexico reflected the strength of our 
market-specific strategy and execution. In Brazil, reliability and direct connectivity 
continued to drive growth. Building on our Pix integration, we added Pix Recurring 
and Pix Open Finance to support recurring and account-linked payments. In 
Mexico, Unified Commerce adoption increased, expanding our reach across 
Quick Service Restaurants, Mobility, and Consumer Electronics.
The groundwork laid over the past several years is now materializing across the 
business, visible in new customer wins, deeper localization, and rising momentum 
in these high-potential markets.
The team behind our growth 
Everything we achieved in 2025 is the direct result of our team. Our way of 
working has been shaped by the same principles that guided us at the start: hire 
exceptional people, give them ownership early, and enable them to move quickly 
within a clear long-term direction.
We maintain a consistently high bar because talented people want to work 
alongside others who challenge and elevate their thinking. Adyen is built to be the 
place for talented people — a company where you learn from those around you 
and can have a meaningful impact from day one. The idea is simple: when you join 
Adyen, you should feel like you are sitting next to some of the most exceptional 
people you’ve worked with. This expectation continues to shape our culture.
As we scale, our teams increasingly reflect a mix of long-tenured colleagues and 
experienced external hires. This balance combines deep institutional knowledge 
with external perspectives, supporting how we build, lead, and work together. It 
also reflects our ability to develop internal talent while selectively bringing in 
expertise as the organization grows.
A culture built on consistency and long-term orientation 
The core elements of our way of working – speed, autonomy, direct collaboration, 
and long-term thinking – have remained consistent over the years, even as the 
world around us has shifted. Rather than react to every external pendulum swing, 
we stay centered on building capabilities that compound over time and create 
durable value for our customers and team members.
Open and direct communication remains central to how we work. We emphasize 
in-person collaboration and face-to-face feedback because they accelerate 
alignment and strengthen teams, enabling fast decision-making. While we 
understand there are cultural nuances across our global offices, we view 
speaking directly as a practical skill that is often the most effective path to clarity. 
We encourage everyone to develop that skill early in their time at Adyen.
Our office-first approach reflects what we believe is best for teams. Senior 
colleagues showing up and sharing knowledge is a core part of how we support team 
members who are new or still developing. In-person work enables faster learning, 
deeper knowledge transfer, and a stronger sense of shared ownership. Being 
together also helps us sharpen ideas, solve problems faster, and connect better.

Accounting policy – Financial instruments
Classification
Financial assets
Adyen classifies its financial assets in the following measurement categories, those to be measured:
▪ subsequently at fair value through profit or loss (‘FVPL’), and 
▪ at amortized cost. 
The classification depends on Adyen’s business model for managing the financial assets and the contractual 
terms of the cash flows. 
Financial liabilities
Adyen initially classifies financial instruments as a liability or equity instrument based on the terms of the 
contractual arrangement, and subsequently reassesses the accounting treatment based on changes in 
circumstances. The derivative liabilities are classified as financial liabilities measured at fair value through 
profit or loss.
Recognition and measurement
Initial Recognition
At initial recognition, Adyen measures its financial instruments at its fair value plus/minus, in the case of a 
financial instrument not at FVPL, transaction costs that are directly attributable to the acquisition of the 
instrument.Transaction costs of financial instruments carried at FVPL are recognised in profit or loss.  
Subsequent Measurement
Financial assets at amortized cost 
Subsequent measurement of debt instruments depends on Adyen’s business model for managing the asset 
and the cash flow characteristics of the asset. Financial assets held within a business model whose objective 
is to hold the assets in order to collect contractual cash flows, where those cash flows represent solely 
payments of principal and interest, are measured at amortised cost.
Financial assets at fair value through profit or loss
Financial assets that do not meet the criteria for amortized cost or fair value through other comprehensive 
income (‘FVOCI’) are measured at FVPL. Any gain or loss is recognized in profit or loss and presented net 
within other financial results for the period in which it arises.
Financial liabilities 
Adyen’s financial liabilities are measured at amortized cost except for derivative liabilities, which are 
classified as financial liabilities measured at fair value through profit or loss.

(II) Processing fees: Fixed fee per transaction paid by merchants for the use of Adyen’s platform and

(III) Sale of goods: Adyen satisfies the performance obligation to deliver the ownership of the POS terminals

performance obligation to be distinct from its payment services. As a result, the revenues for the sale of POS 
terminals and related accessories are recognized at that point in time.

which are deemed other services - recognized at a point in time. Services transferred over time relate to the 
amortization of deferred revenue for services provided as part of the merchant contracts (note 2.3 and note 
2.4) and terminal services fees as part of the unified commerce offering.

Material Judgement – Principal versus agent for revenue out of settlement fees
Adyen contracts with third parties (financial institutions and network scheme providers) that provide 
services to enable Adyen’s payment processing and acquiring services to merchants, for which 
interchange and payment network fees are charged to Adyen ("pass-through settlement fees"). Adyen 
adopts a transparent pricing model and charges fees to merchants based on its own incurred costs 
(including interchange and payment network fees charged to merchants in a pass-through 
arrangement), plus a mark-up which is disclosed as "Settlement fees". 
Adyen applies its judgment in determining whether it has control of the full payment service inputs 
before the service is transferred to its merchants, and, in consequence, whether it is acting as agent or 
principal in relation to the settlement fees charged to merchants. Where Adyen is principal, it will 
present its acquiring mark-up and interchange and payment network fees passed on to merchants on a 
gross (principal) basis within revenue. Where Adyen is agent, it will present its acquiring mark-up on a 
gross (principal) basis, while the pass-through settlement fees will be presented within revenue on a net 
basis (i.e. not recognized gross in revenue and costs incurred from financial institutions).

General information
Introduction to the Sustainability Statement
At Adyen, we continue to strengthen and improve our sustainability reporting as 
the regulatory environment evolves. The European Sustainability Reporting 
Standards (ESRS), issued under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive 
(CSRD), provide a structured framework that supports us in reporting our 
sustainability performance and progress clearly and consistently, enabling 
informed decision-making and long-term value creation.
2024 was our first year of reporting in accordance with the CSRD, providing 
valuable learnings and helping us further develop our reporting processes. We 
continue to strengthen and refine our approach, recognizing that sustainability 
reporting is an iterative process and regulatory expectations continue to evolve. 
Details on progress made regarding each material sustainability matter can be 
found in the subsequent topic-specific sections of this Sustainability Statement. 
Our ongoing focus areas for enhancing the reporting process include:
▪ More granular and specific impacts, risks, and opportunities (IROs): As part 
of our 2025 double materiality assessment review, we improved the 
granularity of our IROs. This is to ensure that our reporting focuses on the 
matters most relevant and material to Adyen and its stakeholders. This 
refinement strengthens the connection between assessment outcomes and 
the way topics are presented in the 2025 Sustainability Statement. Further 
information is provided in the Double materiality assessment section of this 
Sustainability Statement.
▪ Focused effort on enhancing data quality and consistency: We focused 
efforts on strengthening our data collection processes to enhance accuracy 
and reliability of our disclosures. These efforts support more consistent 
reporting and provide a solid foundation for future improvements in 
sustainability disclosures.
▪ Strengthened alignment with ESRS standards to enhance reporting and 
comparability: Our reporting has been refined to further align with topical 
ESRS standards to ensure consistency with industry best practices and 
strengthen comparability. An example is the alignment of our Data privacy and 
data protection disclosures with ESRS S4 - Consumers and End-users. Refer 
to the Data privacy and data protection section of this Sustainability Statement 
for further information.
▪ Streamlining disclosures for clarity and comparability: We refined the way 
information is structured and presented across the Sustainability Statement, 
ensuring that disclosures are factual, clear, and easily comparable across 
reporting periods.
In our second year reporting in accordance with the ESRS framework, we’ve built 
a clearer picture of Adyen’s sustainability performance and increased company-
wide awareness. As our approach matures, we’re continuing to improve our 
practices and expand our coverage across the value chain.
As the regulatory landscape evolves –  including the expected adoption of the 
ESRS simplification standards – we will ensure our reporting keeps pace with 
emerging guidance, industry best practices, stakeholder expectations, and our 
strategic priorities. While the CSRD has not been transposed into Dutch law at the 
date of this Annual Report, we have aligned this statement with current ESRS 
standards and continue to monitor the EU’s proposed “Omnibus” simplification 
measures.
Basis of preparation
Adyen’s Sustainability Statement has been prepared in accordance with the 
CSRD, and the related reporting requirements set out in the ESRS as adopted by 
the European Commission and with the specifications established pursuant to 
Article 8(4) of  the EU Taxonomy Regulation. Refer to the EU Taxonomy report 
section for further information.
The scope of consolidation of this Sustainability Statement aligns with that of the 
Consolidated Financial Statements (refer to Financial Statements Note 1.1), 
ensuring consistency and completeness in our reporting . For a full list of entities 
included in scope of the Sustainability Statement, refer to note 34 ‘Company - 
Investments in consolidated subsidiaries on equity method’.
Our Sustainability Statement covers the sustainability IROs of our operations, 
including, where available, both upstream and downstream value chain IROs. 
This includes the environmental and social impact of our suppliers, partners, and 
customers, such as the carbon footprint of third-party service providers and the 
lifecycle impact of our in-person payment solutions. Where data gaps exist, we 
disclose them alongside plans for improvement. In this Sustainability Statement, 
we explain how and where the value chain has been considered alongside the 
relevant disclosures. Throughout this document, “sustainability” refers to 
environmental, social and governance topics that support long-term value 
creation and business resilience.
