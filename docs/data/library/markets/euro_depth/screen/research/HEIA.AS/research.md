## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Heineken N.V. screens as a buy on a blend of moderate valuation (P/E 17.5, P/B 2.1, yield 2.7%), cash-generative quality (Piotroski F-Score 7/9), and dividend/GARP characteristics, with sector-relative composite at 59%. ## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Heineken offers a recognisable global brand portfolio (Heineken, Amstel, Tiger, Cruzcampo, Murphy’s, alcohol-free 0.0) at a valuation that clears several classic screens without being deep-value cheap. The quantitative screen passes eight of 22 models across five families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — including Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Dividend Growth, Piotroski F-Score, and Earnings Quality. Key metrics sit in a “quality compounder at fair price” band rather than a distressed bargain: P/E below 25, P/B below 3, debt/equity at 95%, ROE 12.1%, and statutory earnings growth of 54% (reflecting the FY2024–FY2025 rebound).

Business quality is supported by durable cash conversion: operating cash flow of €5.0bn and free cash flow of €2.6bn in FY2025 (Yahoo), with FCF comfortably covering dividends (€1.3bn paid) and funding share buybacks (€701m). The board has an active €1.5bn buyback programme (second tranche announced February 2026), signalling capital-allocation discipline alongside a progressive dividend policy. Premiumisation and alcohol-free growth — Q1 2026 premium volume +5.8%, alcohol-free double-digit (Stock Titan, 22 Apr 2026) — align with industry volume headwinds and suggest pricing/mix can substitute for flat beer consumption.

The screen fails on deeper value and moat criteria (Graham Defensive, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Acquirer’s Multiple, Financial Health), which is consistent with a leveraged, goodwill-heavy balance sheet and ROE below 18%. That profile suits a GARP/value-quality buyer rather than a net-net or deep-value investor. Conviction on the screen signal is low (38%) and the signal is new (one week), so this is an early-stage opportunity requiring filing verification before sizing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and liquidity:** Total debt €19.3bn and D/E ~95% (Yahoo/screen); current ratio 0.93 and negative working capital. Without filing covenant language, covenant headroom is unknown — a verify-before-trade gap.

**Goodwill and acquisition risk:** €20bn of goodwill/intangibles against €18bn equity; tangible book is negative. Any impairment would hit reported equity and EPS; FY2024’s earnings collapse may partly reflect such charges, but this cannot be confirmed without annual report footnotes.

**Volume and cyclical exposure:** Three-year revenue decline despite premiumisation. Beer consumption is structurally pressured in developed markets; Americas weakness flagged in H1 2026 news. Screen cyclical overlay did not trigger, but sector dynamics remain a headwind.

**CEO succession:** Shareholders approved Rafael Oliveira as CEO and Executive Board Chair effective 1 October 2026 (11 Aug 2026). Prior CEO departure (January 2026) initially weighed on the share price; execution risk under new leadership is real for a company mid-restructuring.

**Litigation:** Greek court defeat (3 Jun 2026, simplywall.st) raises potential damages exposure; quantum not available in this pack.

**Pension:** €542m non-current pension/post-retirement liability (Yahoo FY2025) — modest relative to scale but worth monitoring in the annual report actuarial note (not retrieved).

**Competitive:** Global brewers (AB InBev, Carlsberg, local craft) and alcohol-free entrants compete on premium and zero-alcohol segments where Heineken is investing.

**Accounting / going concern:** No filing body extracts available; going-concern, contingency, or covenant language cannot be reviewed. This is a material research gap.
