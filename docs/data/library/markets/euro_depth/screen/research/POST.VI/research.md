## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Österreichische Post is Austria’s incumbent postal and parcel operator, with growing exposure to e-commerce logistics and a Bank 99 financial-services arm. The quantitative screen rates POST.VI a **buy** (7/22 models; composite 72%), driven chiefly by **FCF yield (~7.7%), dividend yield (~5.9%), and earnings-quality signals**, rather than deep Graham-style cheapness (P/E 24.0, P/B 3.1). The central debate is whether parcel and banking profits can offset **accelerating structural mail decline and new regulatory pressure**, while the balance sheet carries **high leverage and weak reported liquidity**. Primary filing coverage is absent from the research pack; financial conclusions rely on Yahoo data and headline news, limiting conviction.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, the case rests on **cash generation and income rather than asset-based deep value**. The screen passes four factor families—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—with standout models including FCF Yield, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality. At roughly **5.9% yield** and **7.7% FCF yield**, the market appears to price in mail erosion and regulatory uncertainty, leaving a spread between earnings power and valuation if parcel growth persists.

Business quality is mixed but not weak: revenue has expanded from **€2.52bn (2022)** to **€3.04bn (2025)** per Yahoo, with H1 2026 headlines citing revenue growth despite a difficult environment, strong parcel volumes, and Bank 99 profitability. ROE of **13.5%** is adequate but below quality-compound thresholds (Buffett Quality and Economic Moat models fail). The name is **not** a net-net or low-P/B story—Graham, Schloss, and Deep Value models all fail—so the buy case is a **yield-plus-FCF** play on a quasi-utility logistics franchise, not a classic distressed asset.

Screen conviction is low (**34%**, signal new for one week), and fifteen of twenty-two models fail, including Financial Health (debt/equity ~150%, current ratio ~0.37) and Piotroski F-Score (5/9). The thesis therefore requires acceptance of balance-sheet complexity—large negative working capital, lease-adjusted debt, and pension/provision lines—in exchange for a well-covered dividend if FCF normalises.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Structural mail decline.** H1 2026 earnings-call coverage cites accelerated mail erosion—the core legacy franchise is shrinking. Parcel growth must continue to outpace mail attrition; this is a secular, not cyclical, headwind.

**Regulatory risk.** The same H1 2026 call references new regulatory headwinds. Austrian postal incumbents face price-cap and universal-service obligations; without primary filings, the scope and financial impact are unquantified here.

**Leverage and liquidity.** Debt/equity ~150% (screen), total debt €837m against equity €726m, plus €367m lease liabilities. Current ratio **0.37**; Financial Health model fails. Negative working capital of **-€3.0bn** warrants filing-level review of deferred revenue, payables timing, and any factoring.

**Pension and provisions.** Long-term provisions of **€257m** and current pension-related lines suggest employee-benefit risk; Yahoo does not provide funded-status detail. Cannot assess from filings (absent).

**Earnings and FCF volatility.** Statutory earnings growth of **-76.1%** on the screen (TTM distortion likely from quarterly base effects). FCF was negative in 2022 and 2024; Q2 2026 FCF was **-€154m**. Dividend sustainability depends on normalised FCF, not peak 2025 working-capital release.

**Quality scores.** Piotroski F-Score **5/9** (fails): ROA not improving, leverage not declining, gross margin and asset turnover not improving. Buffett Quality and Economic Moat fail on ROE, margins, and leverage.

**Competitive pressure.** Peer context (Deutsche Post Q2 2026, simplywall.st, 18 Aug 2026) suggests robust parcel demand but full pricing in larger integrators; Austrian Post competes with DPD, DHL, and regional players in parcels.

**Governance / accounting.** No filing extracts to review related-party transactions, auditor emphasis-of-matter paragraphs, or contingent liabilities. This is a material research gap.

**Missing primary source verification.** Without annual or interim filing bodies, going-concern assurances, litigation reserves, and covenant compliance cannot be assessed from this pack.
