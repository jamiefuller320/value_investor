# Umicore SA (UMI.BR) — Research memo

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Umicore is a Belgian circular-materials group (battery cathode materials, catalysis, recycling) whose shares screen as a value buy on cheapness, quality, dividend continuity, and risk metrics after a severe multi-year de-rating. The quantitative hook is modest valuation (P/E 12.8, P/B 2.2, FCF yield 6.7%, 2.5% dividend yield) paired with a perfect Piotroski F-Score (9/9) and an earnings rebound in FY2025 following a €1.48bn net loss in FY2024 driven by large impairments. The central debate is whether FY2025 normalisation (revenue +30% to €19.4bn, net income €385m) marks a durable recovery in battery-materials economics or a cyclical bounce within a capital-intensive, EV-demand-sensitive franchise still digesting prior capex and write-downs. Primary regulatory filings are entirely absent from the research pack, limiting verification of segment trends, covenant language, and going-concern disclosures.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen flags Umicore as a buy across all four metric families — cheapness, quality, dividend, and risk — with high data completeness (20/20 metrics) and an improving one-week signal trend. For a value investor, the case rests on three pillars visible in secondary financial data.

First, **valuation reset after impairment**: the share price remains roughly 57% below five-year highs (per news coverage), yet FY2025 Yahoo data shows net income of €385m on revenue of €19.4bn, versus a €1.48bn loss in FY2024 when €959m of capital-asset impairments and €1.43bn of unusual charges dominated the P&L. At P/E 12.8 and P/B 2.2, the market may still be pricing residual scepticism from the write-down year rather than normalised earning power.

Second, **cash generation and balance-sheet repair**: FY2025 free cash flow recovered to €386m (Yahoo) from €287m in FY2024, with capex moderating to €325m from €582m. Net debt stood at €1.23bn against EBITDA of €1.17bn (~1.0x), and ROE of 18.6% (screen) reflects equity rebuilt from the FY2024 trough. Models passing FCF Yield, Acquirer's Multiple, Magic Formula, Earnings Quality, and Dividend Growth align with a business generating cash while paying a sustained dividend (€60m paid in FY2025, down from €192m in prior years — a prudent cut during the loss year).

Third, **quality momentum**: a 9/9 Piotroski score — positive net income, OCF exceeding earnings, improving ROA, declining leverage, improving current ratio, no dilution, and improving gross margin and asset turnover — signals operational inflection consistent with inventory and receivable normalisation after the 2022–2024 downturn. Umicore's strategic positioning in closed-loop battery materials and recycling offers long-duration optionality if EV adoption resumes, though the screen's failure on Economic Moat, Buffett Quality, and Financial Health models flags that moat durability and balance-sheet stress remain unresolved without filing-level detail.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and structural:** Revenue collapsed from €25.4bn (FY2022) to €14.9bn (FY2024) before rebounding — a ~42% peak-to-trough move indicative of severe battery-materials pricing and volume cyclicality. FY2025 recovery does not restore FY2022 profitability on comparable revenue, raising questions about permanent margin impairment in cathode materials.

**FY2024 impairments:** The €959m capital-asset impairment and €1.43bn unusual charges (Yahoo) suggest prior capex and asset values were materially overstated. Without filing bodies, the specific assets written down, trigger events, and any ongoing restructuring liabilities are unknown.

**Balance sheet and screen failures:** The quantitative screen failed Financial Health, Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Deep Value, and Economic Moat models — consistent with elevated leverage history, equity erosion, and uncertain moat. Net debt/EBITDA ~1.0x is manageable but pension obligations (€273m) and provisions (€226m + €107m current) add off-balance-sheet-style commitments not fully captured by simple leverage ratios.

**Capital intensity:** Cumulative capex exceeded €1.7bn across FY2023–FY2024 before moderating; €727m construction-in-progress at FY2025 year-end signals further spend ahead. FCF yield of 6.7% (screen) could compress if the battery-materials capex cycle re-accelerates.

**Governance and execution:** A May 2025 leadership refresh (new CFO Lily Liu, Chief Digital & Transformation Officer, two new Supervisory Board members) coincided with cost-saving plans referenced in news coverage — potentially positive, but also a signal of prior execution gaps during the downturn.

**Regulatory and competitive:** EV battery chemistry shifts (LFP adoption, localisation requirements in US/EU), Chinese cathode overcapacity, and critical-minerals sourcing rules represent structural headwinds not quantified in this pack. No filing language on contingencies, going concern, or covenant compliance is available for review.

**Evidence quality:** Zero primary filings and thin operational news coverage mean pension deficit trends, related-party transactions, and litigation reserves cannot be assessed from this research pack.
