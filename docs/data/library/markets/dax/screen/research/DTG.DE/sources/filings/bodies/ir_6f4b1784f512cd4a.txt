Structure and segments of the Daimler Truck Group
The Industrial Business comprises the vehicle segments Trucks North 
America, Mercedes-Benz Trucks, Trucks Asia, Daimler Buses and the 
reconciliation. Financial Services corresponds to the Financial 
Services segment. The eliminations of intra-Group transactions 
between the Industrial Business and Financial Services are 
generally allocated to the Industrial Business and are reported 
in the reconciliation. 
Effective January 01, 2025, Daimler Truck Group (hereinafter also 
referred to as “Daimler Truck” or “Group”) integrated its businesses 
in China and India from the Trucks Asia segment into the Mercedes-
Benz segment. This formed one global Mercedes-Benz Trucks 
segment. All other activities of the Trucks Asia segment, as well as 
the Trucks North America, Daimler Buses and Financial Services 
segments, are not affected by this reorganization. The figures for the 
2025 financial year and the restated year-on-year comparison are 
based on the new segment composition.
Daimler Truck AG, Mitsubishi Fuso Truck and Bus Corporation 
(Mitsubishi Fuso), Toyota Motor Corporation (Toyota) and Hino 
Motors Ltd. (Hino) signed definitive agreements on June 10, 2025, 
with respect to the planned integration of Mitsubishi Fuso and Hino. 
With the approval of the transaction of the Supervisory Board of 
Daimler Truck AG and Daimler Truck Holding AG, the criteria for 
classification as "assets and liabilities held for sale" and as 
"discontinued operations" were fulfilled on June 06, 2025.
Further explanations can be found in chapter E Profitability, liquidity 
and capital resources, and financial position and in E Note 4. 
Discontinued operations and assets and liabilities held for sale in the 
Notes to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

Terminology
This document contains terms such as “zero-emission (heavy-duty) vehicle” (abbreviated “ZEV”, “zero-emission vehicle”), “CO2e”, “CO2e-neutral”, “CO2e-neutral on the balance sheet” and “locally CO2e-free” or “CO2e-free in driving operation”. A “zero-emission heavy-duty 
vehicle” is according to Article 3 point (11) (a) of the Regulation (EU) 2024/1610 a vehicle without an internal combustion engine or with an internal combustion engine with emissions of no more than 3 g CO2/(tkm) or 1 g CO2/(pkm). “CO2e” stands for carbon dioxide 
equivalent and refers to the total amount of greenhouse gases released by a particular activity or process. It takes into account not only carbon dioxide, but also other greenhouse gases such as methane (CH4), nitrous oxide (N2O) and ozone (O3) by relating their climate impact 
to CO2. Since these gases have different effects on the climate, CO2e enables a holistic view of the climate effect of a particular activity. “CO2e-neutral” means that CO₂e emissions released into the atmosphere by a company’s activities are offset by a corresponding amount of 
CO2e. Offsetting can be achieved through various measures: reducing emissions, reducing energy consumption, switching to renewable energies, etc., or by storing or absorbing CO₂. “CO2e-neutral on the balance sheet” means that CO₂e emissions released are offset by 
compensation certificates and related projects. “Locally CO2e-free” or “CO2e-free in driving operation” means that no carbon or carbon dioxide equivalents (CO2e) is emitted from the vehicle into the immediate surroundings while driving. Unless otherwise indicated, the same 
understanding of terms is used in each case throughout the entire document.

A DECISIVE YEAR FOR DECARBONIZING TRANSPORT
As I took over as Chair of the Commercial Vehicle Board of the European Automobile 
Manufacturers' Association (“ACEA”) at the beginning of this year, I want to add the 
European industry perspective on decarbonizing transport. Trucks and buses keep the 
world moving – they are the backbone of logistics and public transport. With many of 
the world’s leading truck and bus manufacturers based in Europe, we have both the 
responsibility and the opportunity to lead.
We fully support the EU’s goal to decarbonize transport – and with dozens of different 
locally CO2-free truck and bus models in series production, our industry is delivering. 
But substantial market uptake will happen only when our customers can operate 
zero-emission vehicles as seamlessly and profitably as conventional vehicles today.
The EU Commission must take urgent action now to prevent manufacturers from 
having to pay penalties while the essential enabling conditions are simply not in place. 
The obligations for manufacturers must be aligned with the development of charging 
and hydrogen infrastructure networks and policy measures that support robust 
business cases for our customers, like CO₂‑based road charges in all EU Member 
States. 
2026 has to be the year Europe turns challenges into real progress, delivering 
pragmatic solutions that protect competitiveness while driving the decarbonization 
of transport.

Capital expenditure
The KPI for CapEx is the ratio of taxonomy-eligible or taxonomy-
aligned capital expenditure to total capital expenditure in accordance 
with the Taxonomy Regulation for the reporting year.
For the denominator, all additions to intangible assets and property, 
plant and equipment, equipment on operating leases, as well as 
additions to right-of-use assets in accordance with IFRS 16, including 
additions to the aforementioned assets in the context of business 
acquisitions, are included. The CapEx KPI for the Mitsubishi Fuso 
subgroup is included only until June 06, 2025. Acquired goodwill is 
not included.
To determine the numerator, the investments are analyzed to identify 
whether they are related to the production, leasing/financing of 
vehicles or Refurbishment and resale of used vehicles . 
In the 2025 reporting year, only the CapEx plan (CapEx B) will be 
taken into account for taxonomy alignment. The CapEx plan covers a 
timeframe of five years and the included investments can be 
exclusively assigned to activity 3.3 under the environmental objective 
Climate Change Mitigation. The capitalized research and 
development costs included relate to the development of Battery 
Electric Vehicles (BEV) and Fuel Cell Electric Vehicles (FCEV). 
Important milestones of the CapEx plan, such as the completion of 
major development projects, are regularly published on the Daimler 
Truck Group’s website. The CapEx plan includes expenditures in a 
range of €1,100 million to €1,300 million which are planned for the 
period from 2026 to 2030. During this timeframe, the criteria for 
taxonomy alignment are expected to be achieved. The change in the 
investment amounts within the specified range compared to the 
previous year is primarily attributable to the adjusted speed of 
transformation and the associated adjustments to the investment 
plans. Further information can be found in the chapter E Objectives 
and Strategy.

as discontinued operation of the Mitsubishi Fuso subgroup were

Operating expenditure
The KPI for operating expenditure is the ratio of taxonomy-eligible or 
taxonomy-aligned operating expenditure to the relevant direct, non-
capitalized costs (research and development, building renovation 
measures, short-term lease, maintenance and repair) in the reporting 
year.
The total operating expenditure from continuing operations to be 
included in the denominator comprise the volume pursuant to 
Appendix 1 of the Delegated Act on Article 8 of the Taxonomy 
Regulation. 
In the 2025 reporting year, only the OpEx Plan (OpEx B) will be taken 
into account for taxonomy alignment. The OpEx Plan is regarded as 
part of the CapEx Plan and fulfills the criteria set out there.

Credit ratings
The credit rating remained unchanged in 2025. Consequently, 
Daimler Truck continues to hold a solid investment‑grade rating, as 
presented in table ä B.21, from the rating agencies S&P Global

The A-/A-2 (long- and current) rating from S&P Global Ratings 
reflects the agency's assessment that Daimler Truck holds a leading 
market position in North America and a solid position in the 
fragmented markets of Europe and Latin America. The stable service 
and spare parts business, together with the Company’s defensive 
balance sheet structure, enhances resilience during periods of 
market volatility.
Moody’s A3 rating (long-term, Prime-2 short-term) for Daimler Truck 
reflects the considerable size of the Group as one of the world’s 
largest commercial vehicle manufacturers by revenue, its strong 
diversification, and its robust market positions in the American and 
European markets. Moody’s also highlighted Daimler Truck’s
