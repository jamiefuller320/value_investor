CHAIR OF THE MANAGEMENT BOARD
Fiscal year 2025 was once again marked by major challenges, including 
geopolitical tensions, wars and military conflicts in various regions. In 
addition, protectionist developments led to higher tariffs and trade barriers. 
The resulting economic uncertainty weakened consumer sentiment and 
industrial demand alike. These developments will continue to weigh on 
economic momentum in 2026. 
Despite these difficult conditions, we succeeded in moving Henkel forward 
in fiscal year 2025. We achieved or even exceeded key targets, continued to 
drive the transformation of our Company forward with determination, and 
underpinned our progress with solid financial numbers. 
The results for 2025 clearly demonstrate that we are on the right path with 
our strategy for sustainable, purposeful growth effectively positioning the 
Company for the future.  
Solid business performance and significant improvement in 
earnings  
Group sales in fiscal 2025 amounted to around 20.5 billion euros. This 
corresponds to a positive organic sales growth of 0.9 percent, i.e. excluding 
acquisitions and divestments and currency effects. Both of our business 
units, Adhesive Technologies and Consumer Brands, achieved organic sales 
growth in a challenging market environment.

In nominal terms, sales development was below the prior-year level. This 
was primarily due to strong negative currency effects, in particular the 
depreciation of the US dollar. Portfolio measures also had a negative impact, 
which was primarily attributable to the sale of the retail brands business in 
North America in the Consumer Brands business unit. 
Adjusted operating profit (adjusted EBIT) was slightly below the previous 
year’s level at around 3 billion euros due to significantly negative exchange 
rate effects. We made very good progress in terms of profitability. Adjusted 
return on sales (adjusted EBIT margin) rose to 14.8 percent, representing a 
gain of 0.5 percentage points versus prior year. Adjusted earnings per 
preferred share (EPS) amounted to 5.33 euros, which corresponds to an 
increase of around 4.7 percent at constant exchange rates. 
This significant improvement in earnings was achieved in both business 
units through further innovations, continued cost-saving measures and 
efficiency improvements in purchasing, production and the supply chain, as 
well as a positive business mix. In addition, the increase in the Consumer 
Brands business unit was driven by the completion of the integration 
process, continued savings and the further enhancement of the product 
portfolio. 
It is important to note that these excellent results were not achieved at the 
expense of investments in our brands and businesses. On the contrary, in 
the past fiscal year, we once again invested heavily in the future of both 
business units – in strengthening our brands, in innovation, and in 
sustainability and digitalization. We have thus further enhanced the high 
quality of our earnings statement. 
Free cash flow amounted to 1.85 billion euros. Our net financial position – 
despite payments for dividends and arising from the share buyback 
program – was positive and above the previous year’s level at 109 million 
euros.  
Based on our successful business and earnings development, as well as our 
strong financial foundation, we will – at the Annual General Meeting 
scheduled for April 27, 2026 – propose to you, our shareholders, a dividend 
increase of 1.5 percent compared to the previous year, amounting to 
2.07 euros per preferred share and 2.05 euros per ordinary share.  
This corresponds to a payout ratio of 37.9 percent, which is within our 
targeted range of 30 to 40 percent of net income after non-controlling 
interests and adjustment for exceptional items.  
I would like to thank all Henkel employees for their outstanding perfor-
mance. Through their teamwork and extraordinary commitment, we have 
once again successfully guided our Company through a challenging year.  
The dedication and motivation of our employees, driven by a shared 
purpose, our core values, and an inspiring culture, make us very confident – 
even in these challenging times – that Henkel is heading for a successful 
future. 
Major progress in the process of transformation 
Over recent years, we have fundamentally renewed our Company across 
many areas and have consistently pursued our strategic agenda for 
purposeful growth. A key factor in our long-term success is our willingness 
to continuously evolve and to actively shape change. We pursue holistic, 
profitable growth and aim to strengthen our results sustainably. To achieve 
this, it is necessary for us to realign ourselves time and again – and that is 
exactly what we are doing. This applies across all areas of our Company. In 
the following, I would like to explain what progress we have made and why 
these developments are so decisive for our future strength.

HENKEL ANNUAL REPORT 2025 
One of the most significant changes in Henkel’s recent history was the 
consolidation of our Consumer businesses into the Consumer Brands 
business unit, which we announced at the beginning of 2022. Since then, 
a great deal has happened. The new business unit has been fully 
operational since the beginning of 2023, and clear positive effects were 
already visible in its first year. In fiscal year 2025, we successfully 
completed the integration process. We completed the first phase in 2024, 
which primarily involved merging the sales and marketing teams. The 
second phase focused on further cost savings through optimizing the 
production and logistics network. Overall, we were able to slightly exceed 
the originally targeted annual savings of 525 million euros by the end of 
fiscal year 2025 across both phases. This successfully completed the 
integration of the Consumer Brands business unit – one year ahead of the 
original target. 
In fiscal year 2025, the Consumer Brands business unit generated sales of 
around 9.7 billion euros. This corresponds to an organic sales growth of 
0.3 percent. The increase was driven both by a strong pricing environment 
and by improved volume development in the second half of the year. 
Adjusted return on sales reached 14.5 percent, a significant increase of 
0.9 percentage points compared to the previous year. This strong improve-
ment in earnings was driven in particular by the continued enhancement 
and optimization of the product portfolio, i.e. the targeted increase in the 
added value of our products for consumers, as well as by savings from the 
creation of the integrated Consumer Brands business unit – primarily 
through the measures implemented in the second phase to optimize the 
production and logistics network. Increased volume sales of higher-margin 
products also had a positive impact. 
The importance of consolidating our Consumer businesses becomes partic-
ularly clear when looking at our performance over the past three years – that 
is, since the new business unit was established. In fiscal year 2022, the com-
bined adjusted return on sales of the related businesses stood at 8.3 percent. 
By the end of fiscal 2025, this figure had increased to 14.5 percent. This 
represents an improvement of 6.2 percentage points over the three years – 
a period which also saw significantly increasing investments in our brands 
and innovation capabilities within this business unit. 
This demonstrates that consolidating the previously separate Consumer 
businesses onto a single platform was the right decision. 
Today, we are vigorously aligning Consumer Brands toward strong brands 
and businesses that have high gross margins and leading positions in its 
markets and categories – hence the sale of our retail brands business in 
North America, which we successfully completed in spring 2025. The positive 
effects of focusing on high-growth and high-margin brands and businesses 
are readily apparent: The top ten brands within Consumer Brands, which 
accounted for nearly 60 percent of the revenue in 2025, posted very strong 
organic growth, along with a positive increase in volume.  
In a highly competitive consumer goods market, innovation is a key success 
factor for differentiation. Our approximately 1,000 research and development 
experts within Consumer Brands are working intensively on innovations that 
provide real added value to consumers. Fiscal 2025 saw us once again  
successfully launch numerous new products. One example is the new Creme 
Supreme hair colorant from Schwarzkopf. Concerns about potential hair 
damage are the most common reason why many consumers are reluctant 
to color their hair at home. Inspired by professional salon applications, we 
have therefore developed Creme Supreme, a new technology that 
strengthens and nourishes the hair structure through micro-bonding. These 
micro-bonds hold the hair fibers together more effectively and form a 
special protective layer around the hair, resulting in significantly less hair 
breakage.

The “House of Hair” concept also plays an important role in the development 
of innovations in the Hair category. As of March 2025, we have consolidated 
all areas of our hair cosmetics business in Hamburg at one site. This has 
created a creative campus dedicated to hair, where we combine everything 
that is decisive for the future of hair care under one roof: Research and devel-
opment, testing salons, marketing, and the Academy of Hair. Here, more 
than 300 experts work hand-in-hand to develop products that are not only 
innovative but also closely aligned with consumer needs. In addition to 
Hamburg, there are four other locations – Los Angeles, Tokyo, Shanghai, and 
Guadalajara. Each site plays an important role in the customer-oriented 
development of innovative products. 
To enable sustainable consumption and further contribute to the circular 
economy, we have continuously increased the proportion of recycled mate-
rials in the packaging of liquid detergents and products in the Hair category 
in Europe – for example, in well-known brands such as Persil, Weißer Riese, 
Spee and Gliss. In Europe, we use 45 percent recycled material for our 
consumer goods packaging. In North America, the recycled plastic content 
in the bottle body of the liquid hand soap brand Dial has been increased to 
100 percent. 
We also use ingredients that are based on sustainable raw materials.  
The overall very positive development of the Consumer Brands business unit 
proves that consolidating our consumer goods businesses into one entity, 
consistently optimizing the portfolio, and thus creating a strong platform 
for future profitable growth was the right decision for Henkel. 
The Adhesive Technologies business unit also posted a very positive business 
performance overall last year. We offer around 20,000 products and solu-
tions for some 100,000 customers worldwide in a variety of industrial 
segments. This gives us a unique position in the market – in terms of diver-
sity and depth, of our global presence and in terms of the expertise and 
competence of our teams, with their capability to develop tailored solutions 
for our customers and thus further strengthen our leading market position. 
The three business areas Mobility & Electronics, Packaging & Consumer 
Goods, and Craftsmen, Construction & Professional consistently align the 
Adhesive Technologies business unit with key future trends. These include, 
among others, electromobility and connectivity – that is, the increasing 
interconnection of systems – accompanied by a strong focus on sustainability 
and urbanization. 
In the past fiscal year, the Adhesive Technologies business unit generated 
sales of around 10.7 billion euros. This corresponds to good organic growth 
amounting to 1.5 percent. The increase in sales was driven particularly by 
volume growth, which accelerated once again in the second half of the year. 
Prices remained stable overall compared to the previous year. The main 
growth drivers throughout the year were the Electronics and Industry 
businesses.  
Adjusted return on sales increased slightly by approximately 0.1 percentage 
points compared to the prior year, reaching 16.7 percent. This improvement 
was driven on the one hand by positive volume growth and on the other by 
continued cost-saving and efficiency measures.  
Although Adhesive Technologies serves a diverse range of customers and 
applications, the automotive industry – especially the electromobility 
segment – was again a key focus of attention in the past year.   
Around 20 percent of sales in the Adhesive Technologies business unit is 
attributable to the automotive sector. Of this, approximately 20 percent 
relates to solutions for electric vehicles. Around 80 percent of sales in this 
segment are thus still generated by applications for vehicles with conventional 
powertrains. The automotive industry as a whole faced challenges in 2025, 
particularly in Germany. Nevertheless, our innovative solutions enabled us 
to win a greater number of tenders from manufacturers. In addition, we have 
an international presence and count numerous manufacturers in other parts 
of the world among our customers.

Innovation is a central success factor for our business. We therefore  
continue to invest consistently to position ourselves successfully in the 
competitive environment. A key focus is the targeted expansion of our 
network of state-of-the-art research and innovation centers.  
Such “Inspiration Centers” have been operating for some time in Düsseldorf, 
Mumbai in India, and New Jersey in the USA. In addition, in 2025 we opened 
our Inspiration Center in Shanghai for the Asia-Pacific region, with an 
investment of 60 million euros. More than 500 researchers and developers 
work there alongside customers from a wide range of industries. The  
construction of our first integrated innovation and technology center in 
Latin America, based in Jundiaí, Brazil, is also in progress and the facility is 
expected to open in early 2026.  
In Madison Heights, Michigan, USA, we also opened a new Battery Appli-
cation Center for North America. This further expands our capabilities in the 
development and application of advanced materials for batteries used in 
modern electric vehicles. This Battery Application Center complements the 
Battery Application and Test Center at our Inspiration Center in Düsseldorf 
and forms the starting point for a global network. 
The innovations and solutions developed within Adhesive Technologies 
contribute significantly to improving sustainability in our industrial  
customers’ production processes.  
For example, we work closely with various strategic partners along the value 
chain to bring alternative and more sustainable formulations onto the market. 
In this way, we reduce emissions in adhesives manufacturing, for instance 
through CO₂-reduced raw materials and the use of renewable energy in 
production. The improved sustainability profile of our adhesives, in turn, 
helps our industrial customers reduce the carbon footprint of their products. 
The expansion of the portfolio through acquisitions is an integral part of 
our growth agenda. In 2025, we strengthened the Adhesive Technologies 
business unit through the agreement to acquire ATP Adhesive Systems AG, 
headquartered in Switzerland. The group of companies is a leading provider 
of high-performance, water-based specialty tapes for a wide range of end 
markets, including automotive, electronics, medical, construction and the 
graphics industry. With around 700 employees, ATP has a strong presence 
in North America and Europe and expects sales of around 270 million euros 
in the 2025 fiscal year. The ATP Adhesive Systems portfolio offers an excellent 
strategic fit for our existing business and also aligns well with our sustaina-
bility goals, as more than 90 percent of its products use water-based technolo-
gies that have a lower carbon footprint. We aim to complete the transaction 
in the first half of 2026.

Responsibility for the environment, society, and future 
generations 
Sustainable business practices are firmly anchored in our values and shape 
both our purpose and our growth agenda. Our sustainability strategy for the 
Henkel Group provides the framework for this commitment. For several 
years now, we have focused on three key areas of action that guide our 
commitment – “Regenerative Planet,” “Thriving Communities,” and “Trusted 
Partners.”  
Within the focus area “Regenerative Planet,” we are consistently pursuing 
the goal of a climate-neutral and circular economy. We are progressing 
along a clearly defined net-zero roadmap, under which we aim to reduce 
our absolute greenhouse gas emissions across Scopes 1, 2, and 3 by 90 percent 
by 2045, in line with the evidence-related criteria of the Science Based 
Targets initiative (SBTi).  
Our net-zero target is a key milestone and provides important momentum 
across the entire Company. A particular focus is on close collaboration with 
our suppliers, as a large share of emissions arises along the upstream and 
downstream value chain. Through initiatives such as “Climate Connect,” we 
support our partners in reducing emissions, improving data quality, and 
jointly making progress toward net zero. In April 2026, we will also present 
our expanded sustainability ambition with new targets and commitments to 
further strengthen our contribution to a sustainable future.  
Within the focus area “Thriving Communities,” we have once again lived up 
to our aspiration to engage in society beyond our business activities. The 
voluntary and pro-bono engagement of our employees is at the heart of our 
social commitment and an integral part of our corporate culture. We firmly 
believe that personal engagement can change lives. As early as 1998, we 
launched the volunteer initiative “MIT: Make an Impact on Tomorrow,” making 
Henkel one of the first German companies to establish voluntary social 
engagement as a key element of its sustainability activities. Since then, 
increasing numbers of our employees, retirees, and teams have become 
involved in volunteer work. Henkel supports this engagement in a variety of 
ways – through financial or product donations and through paid leave for 
volunteering activities. In the past year as well, many of our employees 
around the world were actively involved in social projects. They supported 
the construction and renovation of homes for people in need, promoted 
sustainable palm oil cultivation, and helped disadvantaged young people 
gain a foothold in professional life. 
Full details on our activities and progress in the area of sustainability can be 
found in our sustainability statement, which is included for the first time this 
year as an integrated part of the combined management report in this 
annual report. 
Artificial intelligence as an opportunity 
In 2025, the rapid advances in the field of artificial intelligence (AI) were the 
dominant topic in digitalization. For us, the use of AI goes far beyond a 
purely technical issue. It is a strategic decision. In addition to increasing 
efficiency, AI offers the opportunity to fundamentally rethink business models. 
Artificial intelligence will have a significant influence on how we operate in 
the future.  
At Henkel, AI has been an integral part of our digital strategy for several 
years now. We actively implement AI technologies in practical applications 
across both our industrial and our consumer goods businesses.  
In 2025, we launched our first generative, AI-supported TV commercial in 
Germany, thereby bringing Persil’s iconic White Lady into a new era. This 
positions us as a pioneer in the use of state-of-the-art technologies for 
brand storytelling. 
AI is also used, for example, in the automation of processes in our adhesives 
development laboratories, and the virtual networking of our global production 
sites. In battery manufacturing, we use AI-supported simulation and inno-
vative debonding solutions to help our customers reduce development 
times and costs, as well as optimize performance.

Financial and capital management 
The financing of the Group is centrally managed by Henkel AG & Co. KGaA. 
Funds are, as a general rule, obtained centrally and distributed within the 
Group. Our financial management is based on the financial ratios defined in 
our financial strategy (see table of key financial ratios on page 106). We pursue 
a conservative and flexible investment and borrowings policy with a balanced 
investment and financing portfolio. The primary goals of our financial man-
agement are to secure the liquidity and creditworthiness of the Group, 
together with ensuring access at all times to the capital market, and to 
generate a sustainable improvement in shareholder value.

HENKEL ANNUAL REPORT 2025 
Net assets and financial position  
In 2025, the total assets of Henkel AG & Co. KGaA decreased compared to 
year-end 2024 by 1,095 million euros to 16,439 million euros. 
Non-current assets decreased by 406 million euros to 13,263 million euros, 
mainly due to a reduction in financial assets. This resulted in particular from 
the capital reduction of a foreign affiliated company.  
Substantial capital expenditures on property, plant and equipment in fiscal 
2025 related to the expansion of the fully automated warehouse center for 
consumer goods at the Düsseldorf site and numerous replacement and 
expansion investments. 
Current assets decreased year on year in 2025 from 3,823 million euros to 
3,142 million euros. The reduction is mainly due to lower receivables and 
other assets. The background to this is the sale of operating receivables 
within the Group to Henkel Global Supply Chain B.V. (-496 million euros). 
The purchase price was primarily settled through the Group-internal 
assumption of operating liabilities as of December 31, 2025 (-447 million 
euros). The difference resulted in an increase in financial receivables from 
Henkel Global Supply Chain B.V. In addition, a lower inventory of short-term 
money market instruments contributed to the reduction in current assets. 
The assets arising from the overfunding of pension obligations were 8 million 
euros lower year on year at 3 million euros and essentially reflect the netting 
of partial retirement obligations and associated plan assets.  
Equity declined from 7,367 million euros to 6,597 million euros due to the 
dividend payout and also the share buyback program that began during 
the fiscal year. At the end of 2025, Henkel repurchased ordinary shares for 
a total amount of 168 million euros and preferred shares for a total amount 
of 688 million euros. The equity ratio decreased by 1.9 percentage points to 
40.1 percent.  
For details of issued capital and treasury stock, please refer to the disclosures 
in the notes to the statutory financial statements of Henkel AG & Co. KGaA. 
Provisions decreased by 42 million euros to 974 million euros. The reduction 
was mainly due to lower provisions for taxes and for personnel expenses. 
Higher provisions for pensions were partially offset by the negative perfor-
mance of financial assets held in the cover pool.  
Year on year, liabilities and deferred income decreased overall in fiscal 2025 
by 279 million euros to 8,815 million euros. This was primarily due to the 
internal assumption of operating liabilities by Henkel Global Supply Chain B.V. 
The reduction was countervailed by an increase in financial liabilities to 
affiliated companies. These items are also affected by the cash pool manage-
ment function performed by Henkel AG & Co. KGaA within the Henkel 
Group. The use of cash pools allows largely centralized management of the 
Group’s liquidity, thus facilitating a high degree of financial flexibility.  
As of the reporting date, Henkel AG & Co. KGaA had four bonds on its books 
with a total volume of 1,779 million euros. These include one British pound-
denominated bond with a nominal volume of 350 million British pounds 
and three sustainability-linked bonds representing a nominal volume of 
1,150 million euros and 250 million US dollars respectively. In 2025, two 
waste reduction bonds with a nominal volume of 70 million US dollars and 
25 million euros respectively were redeemed. 
For an overview of the financing and capital management of Henkel AG & 
Co. KGaA, please refer to the information about the Henkel Group on pages 
105 and 106.
