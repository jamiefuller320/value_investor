GEA	is	one	of	the	world’s	largest	suppliers	of	systems	and	components	to	the	
food,	beverage	and	pharmaceutical	industries.	The	international	technology	
group,	founded	in	1881,	focuses	on	machinery	and	plants,	as	well	as	advanced	
process	technology,	components	and	comprehensive	services.
With	 more	 than	 18,000	 employees,	 GEA	 generated	 revenues	 of	 about		
EUR	5.5	billion	 in	more	than	 150	countries	 in	the	 2025	 financial	 year.	GEA	
plants,	 processes,	 components	 and	 services	 enhance	 the	 efficiency	 and	
sustainability	 of	 customer’s	 production.	 They	 contribute	 significantly	 to		
the	reduction	of	CO2	emissions,	plastic	usage	and	food	waste.	In	doing	so,	
GEA	makes	a	key	contribution	toward	a	sustainable	future,	in	line	with	the	
company’s	purpose:	„Engineering	for	a	better	world“.
GEA	is	listed	on	the	German	DAX	as	well	as	the	European	STOXX®	Europe

CEO 	 GEA 	 GROUP 	 AKTIENGESELLSCHAFT 	
On	 September	 22,	 2025,	we	made	 history.	GEA	 was	 promoted	to	 the	 DAX	via	 the	 particularly	 demanding		
“Fast	Entry”	route.	We	now	rank	among	the	40	largest	listed	companies	in	Germany.	This	is	a	milestone	in	the	
nearly	145-year	history	of	our	company.
What	makes	me	particularly	 proud	is	that	we	achieved	this	on	our	own	–	not	driven	by	economic	tailwinds		
but	propelled	by	our	resolve	and	dedication.	Back	in	2019,	this	seemed	unthinkable.	Following	seven	profit	
warnings	in	a	row,	GEA	was	in	a	deep	crisis.	The	capital	market	had	lost	confidence,	and	our	share	price	went	
into	 a	 tailspin.	 But	 we	 took	 decisive	 action.	 We	 repositioned	 ourselves,	 set	 clear	 priorities,	 strengthened	
accountability	and	then	delivered.	Quarter	after	quarter,	year	after	year.
This	turnaround	was	made	possible	by	our	more	than	18,000	employees,	together	with	our	leadership	team.	
They	all	 stuck	 together	 in	 difficult	 times,	 developed	strategies,	implemented	measures	and	 delivered	out-
standing	results	when	it	mattered	most.	Without	all	the	great	people	at	GEA,	we	would	not	be	where	we	are	
today.	For	that,	I	thank	them	from	the	bottom	of	my	heart.
I	also	want	to	express	my	gratitude	to	our	customers	and	partners	for	their	loyalty	–	as	well	as	to	you,	dear	
shareholders.	You	have	stuck	with	us	on	this	journey,	even	during	the	challenging	years	of	restructuring.	Your	
trust	has	paid	off,	as	the	performance	of	your	GEA	shares	impressively	demonstrates.

A successful year in figures
2025	was	a	strong	year	for	us	despite	the	high	level	of	uncertainty	around	the	globe.	GEA	achieved	profitable	
growth	once	again.	Key	metrics:
•	 Order	intake	went	up	by	6.7 percent	to	EUR 5.9 billion.	Organically	(i.e.,	excluding	portfolio	and	currency	
translation	effects),	growth	even	reached	9.1 percent.
•	 Revenue	came	to	EUR 5.5 billion.	Organically,	it	grew	by	3.7 percent.
•	 EBITDA	before	restructuring	 expenses	 rose	 to	 EUR  907 .4  million	 in	 the	 reporting	 year,	representing	an	
8.4 percent	increase.
•	 The	 EBITDA	 margin	 before	 restructuring	 expenses	 improved	 to	 16.5  percent	 –	 a	 level	 that	 seemed	
unachievable	just	a	few	years	ago.
•	 Our	return	on	capital	employed	(ROCE)	rose	to	36.2 percent.
Thanks	to	our	performance	in	the	first	six	months,	we	were	able	to	raise	our	outlook	for	financial	year	2025	in	
the	summer.	And	we	have	achieved	all	of	our	targets	–	partly	even	exceeding	them.	We	want	you	to	share	in	
this	success.	At	the	Annual	General	Meeting,	we	will	propose	a	dividend	of	EUR	1.30	per	share	–	15	cents	
higher	than	in	the	previous	year	and	the	fifth	increase	in	a	row.	
Technology with impact
Our	business	model	is	built	on	a	stable	foundation.	We	meet	basic	needs.	We	supply	technology	for	things	
people	will	always	require	–	food,	beverages	and	pharmaceuticals.
A	look	at	Algeria,	one	of	the	world’s	biggest	importers	of	milk	powder,	shows	just	how	relevant	our	work	is.	In	
2025,	we	 received	one	 of	 the	 most	 significant	 orders	 in	 our	 company’s	history	 there.	 For	the	 Qatari	 food		
producer	 Baladna,	 we	 are	 building	 the	 world’s	 largest	 fully	 integrated	 milk	 powder	 production	 plant.	 It		
covers	the	entire	process	chain	from	modern	milking	systems	to	milk	processing	and	filling.	This	project	will	
strengthen	the	long-term	food	security	of	an	entire	region	and	contribute	to	its	economic	development.
We	 are	 also	 shaping	 the	 nutrition	 of	 tomorrow.	In	 July	 2025,	 we	 opened	 our	 new	 technology	 center	 for		
New	Food	in	Janesville,	U.S.,	an	investment	of	EUR	18	million	in	the	future.	This	center	helps	customers	take	
novel	foods	from	the	 idea	 stage	to	industrial	production:	cultivated	meat	 and	fish,	proteins	from	precision	
fermentation	processes	and	plant-based	alternatives.
We	 feel	 equally	 at	 home	 in	 traditional	 dairy	 farming	 and	 biotech	 innovation	 –	 and	 are	 tech	 leaders	 in		
both	fields.
Taking responsibility
Economic	success	gives	us	room	to	maneuver.	We	are	using	this	flexibility	to	take	on	greater	responsibility.	In	
2025,	we	launched	the	GEA	Foundation	–	an	initiative	that	means	a	great	deal	to	me	personally.	It	pulls	all	of	
our	community	engagement	activities	together	and	gives	us	greater	reach	–	both	at	a	global	level	by	working	
together	 with	 strong	 partners	 like	 UNICEF,	 SOS	 Children’s	Villages	 and	 Viva	 con	 Agua,	 as	 well	 as	 locally	
through	initiatives	driven	by	our	teams	on	the	ground.
We	are	focusing	on	four	key	areas:	promoting	education	and	science,	fighting	child	poverty,	providing	access	
to	basic	infrastructure	as	well	as	supporting	disaster	relief.	This	approach	allows	us	to	be	even	more	effective	
at	implementing	our	pledge	to	donate	one	percent	of	the	Group’s	annual	net	profit.
We	also	have	high	ambitions	when	it	comes	to	protecting	the	climate.	Our	goal:	net-zero	emissions	across	the	
entire	value	chain	by	2040.	In	2025,	our	direct	emissions	(Scope	1	and	2)	were	already	62	percent	below	the	
2019	level	–	meaning	we	reached	our	interim	target	for	2026	a	year	early.	For	indirect	emissions	in	Scope	3,	
we	are	on	track	toward	our	2030	target	with	a	reduction	of	38	percent.	Independent	assessments	have	also	
confirmed	that	we	are	leading	the	way	on	sustainability.	In	the	TIME	ranking	of	the	world’s	most	sustainable	
companies,	 we	 moved	 up	 to	 twelfth	 place	 globally	 in	 2025	 and	 second	 place	 in	 Germany.	 We	 achieved		
Platinum	status	from	EcoVadis	for	the	second	time	in	a	row	–	an	accolade	awarded	to	only	the	top	one	percent	
of	all	companies	assessed.	And	we	were	once	again	included	on	the	CDP	Climate	A-List.
Social	engagement	and	climate	protection	are	not	side	issues	for	us.	They	are	a	vital	part	 of	what	defines	
GEA.	We	measure	success	not	just	in	numbers,	but	by	the	impact	we	make	on	the	world.	That	is	precisely	
what	we	mean	by	“Engineering	for	a	better	world.”

Restructuring from a position of strength
Precisely	because	we	are	this	successful,	we	cannot	afford	to	stand	still.	Those	who	rest	on	their	laurels	lose	
momentum.	Those	who	don’t	improve	fall	behind.	That	is	why	we	are	building	on	our	strong	starting	position	
to	make	GEA	even	faster,	leaner	and	more	agile.
The	Supervisory	Board	paved	the	way	for	this	in	October.	Since	the	start	of	2026,	the	Executive	Board	has	
been	expanded,	our	divisions	have	been	partly	restructured	and	are	now	placed	under	the	direct	responsibil-
ity	of	Board	members.	The	14-member	Global	Executive	Committee	will	be	dissolved,	and	the	matrix	structure	
in	the	regions	and	countries	will	be	replaced.	This	means	shorter	decision-making	processes,	clearer	respon-
sibilities	and	greater	proximity	to	customers	and	markets.	The	organizations	in	the	strategically	vital	countries	
of	China	and	India	now	report	directly	to	me.	All	of	this	helps	us	be	even	more	effective	at	achieving	the	goals	
of	our	Mission	30	strategy.	It	is	how	we	future-proof	GEA.
I	am	delighted	to	be	working	with	 my	new	Executive	Board	team.	We	have	all	known	each	other	 for	many	
years.	All	new	Board	members	have	played	a	key	role	in	shaping	GEA	and	contributed	to	our	success	in	recent	
years.	The	fact	that	the	Supervisory	Board	has	extended	my	contract	early	by	an	additional	two	years	until	
the	end	of	2028	is	a	great	vote	of	confidence	–	and	a	clear	mandate	to	continue	our	course	at	high	speed.
What lies ahead
We	are	confident	about	what	lies	ahead	in	2026.	The	conditions	are	right:	Our	balance	sheet	is	strong,	our	
technologies	are	in	demand	and	our	employees	are	motivated.	The	direction	is	clear:	We	want	to	accelerate	
our	profitable	growth	and	build	on	our	technology	leadership	with	even	more	sustainable	solutions.
Thank	 you,	dear	 shareholders,	 for	your	trust.	 Many	 of	 you	have	been	 on	 this	 journey	 with	 GEA	 for	years.		
In	2025,	we	made	the	step	up	to	a	new	league.	Believe	me	when	I	say:	We	are	here	to	stay	–	and	to	keep	
growing.	Joining	the	DAX	marks	the	beginning	of	a	new	chapter	for	us.	We	will	shape	it	with	the	combination	
of	ambition	and	commitment	that	define	GEA.
We	are	delighted	to	have	you	by	our	side.

GEA	Share
The	GEA	share	has	been	listed	on	the	stock	exchange	for	over	35	years	
and	has	been	included	in	Germany’s	DAX	index	since	September	22, 2025.	
The	strong	share	price	performance	in	the	preceding	months	enabled	
the	 entry	 into	 the	 leading	 index	 via	 the	 demanding	 “Fast	 Entry”	
procedure.	The	rise	to	the	DAX	reflects	GEA ’s	continuously	increasing	
valuation	 as	 a	 result	 of	 successful	 operational	 performance	 with	 a	
consistent	focus	on	innovation	and	sustainability.

In	the	same	way	as	it	does	for	its	own	business	operations,	GEA	uses	an

supplier	 assessment	 process	 with	 a	 focus	 on	 risk	 identification	 and	
preventive	action.	 The	 risks	 relating	 to	 all	 suppliers	 are	 evaluated	as	
part	of	the	annual	risk	analysis.	
In	 order	 to	 calculate	 the	 gross	 risk,	 an	 IT-based	solution	 is	 used	 to	
evaluate	the	areas	of	environment,	human	rights	and	occupational	safety,	
ethics,	sustainable	procurement	and	revenue	generated	with	GEA.	This	
evaluation	considers	the	risk	indicator	of	the	country	in	which	a	sup-
plier	 operates	 and	 the	 indicator	 of	 the	 industry	 or	 product	 group	 in	
which	 it	 is	 active.	 The	 software	 displays	 the	 relevant	 risks,	 such	 as	
adverse	media	coverage,	for	all	direct	suppliers	and	–	if	identifiable	–	
also	for	all	indirect	suppliers.	There	is	no	direct	participation	by	workers	
in	the	value	chain	or	their	legitimate	representatives	beyond	the	scope	
defined	in	the	ILO	standards.
