## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags Logitech across five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—despite failing classic deep-value screens (Graham Defensive, Schloss Low P/B, High Dividend Yield). That profile fits a **quality compounder at a reasonable price**: high ROE, rising margins, strong FCF conversion, and declining share count via buybacks.

Business quality indicators from the screen align with reported fundamentals (Yahoo fallback): FY2026 revenue of USD 4.84bn (+6.3% YoY), net income USD 711m (+12.6%), diluted EPS USD 4.80 (+16%), and free cash flow USD 976m (+24%). Gross margin expanded to ~43.2% (from ~37.9% in FY2023), and operating margin reached ~16.2%. The balance sheet is fortress-like: USD 1.74bn cash against USD 88m total debt (mostly capital leases), net cash ~USD 1.65bn, and working capital USD 1.60bn.

Capital allocation reinforces the value case: FY2026 share repurchases of USD 535m and dividends of USD 233m, with diluted shares falling from ~164m (FY2023) to ~148m (FY2026). Dividend growth model passes despite a modest 1.6% yield—consistent with a total-return story driven by buybacks and earnings growth rather than income.

For a value investor, the hook is **owner economics**: ~20% FCF margin, minimal leverage, and management returning essentially all excess cash. Growth is modest (screen: 3.1% trailing), but Neff PEGY at 0.14 suggests the market underprices earnings quality relative to growth and yield combined. Failed cheapness screens (P/E 22, P/B 6.9) confirm this is not a net-net or contrarian deep-value entry—it is a **quality-at-fair-price accumulate**.
