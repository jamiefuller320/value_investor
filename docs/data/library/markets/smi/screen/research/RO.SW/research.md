## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Roche Holding AG (RO.SW) screens as a buy on quality, dividend, GARP, and risk factors rather than classic deep value: seven of 22 models pass (composite 56%), anchored by a Neff PEGY of 0.09, 38% ROE, 2.7% yield, and a Piotroski F-Score of 7/9. The central debate is whether pipeline replenishment (oncology, obesity, ophthalmology) and diagnostics scale can sustain mid-teens returns after a patchy 2024 and recurring clinical setbacks—notably the March 2026 Giredestrant breast-cancer miss. Primary regulatory filings were not retrieved in this research pack; financial analysis below relies on Yahoo fallback data and should be verified against Roche’s annual report and 2026 half-year release before sizing a position.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor willing to pay up for quality, Roche offers a compounder profile that the screen captures well. Five factor families pass—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—driven by Economic Moat, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Earnings Quality, and Neff PEGY (PEGY 0.09 despite trailing earnings growth of −7.7%, implying the market prices limited near-term growth into an already de-rated base). Return on equity of 38.1% and operating cash conversion (Piotroski: positive net income, OCF > net income, leverage declining) speak to franchise durability across pharma and diagnostics.

What Roche is not is a net-net or low-P/B name: it fails Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Schloss, Deep Value, FCF Yield (3.6% vs 5% threshold), and Acquirer’s Multiple (EV/EBIT 17.0). The buy signal is therefore a quality-at-a-reasonable-growth-price call, not a distressed or asset-backed bargain. Yahoo-sourced financials (fallback—no filing bodies available) show group net income recovering to CHF 12.9bn in 2025 from CHF 8.3bn in 2024, with revenue stabilising at CHF 63.4bn and free cash flow of CHF 13.1bn comfortably covering dividends of c. CHF 7.7bn. The dual-engine model—therapeutics plus cobas diagnostics—supports recurring revenue and reinvestment capacity (R&D c. CHF 13.4bn in 2025). Conviction on the screen is still low at 36% (signal new for one week, trend improving), which aligns with treating this as an accumulate rather than high-conviction outright buy.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Pipeline and clinical risk.** Multiple March 2026 headlines report that the Giredestrant (PersevERA) breast-cancer study missed its primary endpoint, triggering a sharp share reaction (“Rückschlag im Milliardenmarkt,” 9 March 2026; “Giredestrant-Studie scheitert bei Persevera,” 9 March 2026). Oncology remains the earnings backbone; late-stage misses compress the growth option embedded in the PEGY screen.

**Leverage and liquidity.** High debt/equity fails the Financial Health model; total debt rose from CHF 26.5bn (2022) to CHF 36.4bn (2024) before moderating to CHF 33.2bn (2025). Piotroski flags a deteriorating current ratio trend (component failed). Without filing text, covenant headroom and going-concern language cannot be assessed.

**Valuation not margin of safety.** P/B 8.4, P/E 23.4, earnings yield 4.3%, and FCF yield 3.6% offer limited traditional margin of safety; the name fails 15 of 22 screen models including all Graham variants.

**Currency and geographic mix.** “Roche Aktie: Währungseffekte bremsen weiterhin” (23 July 2026) underscores USD/EUR translation risk on a globally denominated revenue base—relevant with EUR/USD at 1.15 in macro context but not used to override the screen signal.

**Governance and structure.** Roche’s dual-class/participation certificate structure (Genussscheine) is a known Swiss feature; no governance controversies surfaced in the news manifest, but insider alignment and capital-allocation priorities are not verifiable from filings in this pack.

**Pension and intangibles.** CHF 4.0bn+ of pension-related liabilities and heavy intangible asset base increase balance-sheet risk if impairments or discount-rate moves bite—details absent without annual report footnotes.

**Competitive pressure.** Biosimilar erosion on legacy franchises (e.g. Avastin, Herceptin, MabThera/Rituxan) is an industry-wide headwind; diagnostics faces competition from Abbott, Siemens Healthineers, and Danaher. Pipeline must offset mature-product decline.
