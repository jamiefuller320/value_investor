Tariffs Beginning in February 2025, the United States government announced additional tariffs on goods imported into the United States, and some nations have responded with retaliatory tariffs and other actions on U.S. products. Global trade policy continues to evolve and the ultimate impact of developments with respect to U.S. tariffs remains unclear . On February 20, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court struck down one set of tariffs (i.e., the tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act ("IEEPA")). As a result of this ruling, importers may be entitled to recover tariffs. Immediately following the Supreme Court's ruling, the U.S. government imposed a temporary 10% global tariff on most imports effective February 24, 2026 under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, which was valid for up to 150 days and expired on July 24, 2026. Immediately after the Section 122 tariffs expired, the U.S. government imposed new tariffs under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, which imposes tariffs ranging from 10% to 12.5% on imports from 86 countries. The Section 301 tariffs remained in place as of September 30, 2026. Furthermore, following the Supreme Court's decision, the U.S. Court of International Trade ("CIT") subsequently ordered the U.S. Customs and Border Protection ("CBP") to issue refunds to importers who paid IEEPA tariffs. To respond to the CIT's order, the CBP created the Consolidated Administration and Processing for Entries ("CAPE") framework to process IEEPA tariff refund requests. The CAPE framework only provides a refund mechanism for certain entries on which IEEPA tariffs were paid. In June 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice appealed the portion of the CIT order that required the government to refund IEEPA tariffs on entries that have liquidated and become final (i.e., entries liquidated for more than 90 days) for in the underlying CIT litigation. The total amount Alcon paid in IEEPA tariffs as of September 30, 2026 is approximately $64 million. During the three months ended September 30, 2026, Alcon received approximately $59 million in tariff refunds, including interest, related to IEEPA tariffs previously paid. During the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, tariff refunds of approximately $55 million were recognized as a reduction to Cost of net sales, with the related interest of approximately $3 million recognized in Other income in the Condensed Consolidated Income Statement. A tariff refund of approximately $1 million was recognized as a reduction to Property, plant & equipment in the September 30, 2026 Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheet. The timing, amount, and ultimate realization of any remaining IEEPA refunds is uncertain. The impact of tariffs incurred under IEEPA, Section 122 and Section 301 in the United States and in China during the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and after reflecting the tariff refunds discussed above amounted to $9 million, which was recognized in Cost of net sales in the Condensed Consolidated Income Statement. 12 The future effects of the tariffs, along with any further changes in trade policies including additional tariffs, are uncertain and could have an adverse effect on our business, financial condition, cash flows and results of operations. The standard is expected to improve comparability and transparency of financial statements by requiring five categories (operating, investing, financing, income taxes and discontinued operations) and defined subtotals and totals ("Operating income", "Income before financing and taxes" and "Net income") in the Consolidated Income Statement and adding new principles for aggregation and disaggregation of information in the primary financial statements and notes. A performance measure (“MPM”) is defined as a subtotal of income and expenses that a company uses in public communications outside of financial statements. IFRS 18 requires disclosure of information for all the Company’s MPMs within a single note to the financial statements that includes a description of each MPM, how the measure is calculated and a reconciliation to the most comparable subtotal or total in the income statement. Alcon currently uses a number of performance measures in public communications that may qualify as MPMs under IFRS 18 as described in our quarterly earnings press releases. Financial instruments Fair value by hierarchy As required by IFRS, financial assets and liabilities recorded at fair value in the Condensed Consolidated Interim Financial Statements are categorized based upon the level of judgment associated with the inputs used to measure their fair value. There are three hierarchical levels, based on an increasing amount of judgment associated with the inputs to derive fair value for these financial assets and liabilities, which are as follows: Financial assets and liabilities carried at Level 1 fair value hierarchy are listed in active markets. Financial assets and liabilities carried at Level 2 fair value hierarchy are valued using corroborated market data. Level 1 financial assets include money market funds, equity securities in public companies and deferred compensation assets. There were no financial liabilities carried at Level 1 fair value, and Level 2 financial assets and liabilities include derivative financial instruments. Investments in money market funds and equity securities in public companies are classified within Level 1 of the fair value hierarchy because they are valued using quoted market prices. Investments in money market funds are classified as Cash & cash equivalents within the Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheet. Deferred compensation investments for certain employee benefit plans are held in a rabbi trust and dedicated to pay the benefits under the associated plans but are not considered plan assets as the assets remain available to creditors of Alcon in certain events, including bankruptcy. Rabbi trust assets primarily consist of investments in mutual funds. These assets are classified within Level 1 of the fair value hierarchy because they are valued using quoted market prices. Hedge accounting Alcon's risk management strategy is to hedge foreign currency exposures arising from the net investments held in foreign operations. In 2026, Alcon has designated the principal amount and accrued interest of the Euro denominated Series 2033 Notes as a hedging instrument for its net investment in certain foreign operations, being the net assets of certain subsidiaries with a Euro functional currency. Hedge effectiveness is determined at the inception of the hedge relationship and is reviewed each reporting period or when significant changes occur to the hedged items or hedging instruments. Alcon performs a qualitative assessment of the hedge effectiveness using a critical terms match method. As the critical terms of the hedged items and the hedging instrument match, Alcon has concluded that an economic relationship exists between the hedged items and hedging instrument. Alcon does not expect the effect of credit risk to dominate the value changes resulting from the economic relationship. Alcon has applied a hedge ratio of 100%, which reflects both the actual quantities hedged and the hedge ratio used for hedge accounting purposes. As of September 30, 2026, the hedging instrument has a carrying value of $562 million recorded to Financial debts and accrued interest of $2 million recorded to Provisions & other current liabilities in the Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheet, and a nominal amount of EUR502 million including accrued interest. The hedging instrument has a principal maturity of September 2033 with interest payable annually.
