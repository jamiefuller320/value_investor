# Western Digital Corporation (WDC) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T13:04:20.535328+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Western Digital is a pure-play hard disk drive (HDD) supplier following the February 2025 spin-off of its Flash business (SanDisk). The quantitative screen rates WDC a buy (6/22 models; composite 54%), driven chiefly by GARP metrics on a sharp earnings rebound rather than classic deep-value criteria. Operationally, continuing HDD revenue and margins have improved materially through FY2026, with Cloud now ~89% of revenue and hyperscale AI/data-centre buildouts the dominant demand driver. The central debate is whether current profitability—partly inflated by mark-to-market gains on the retained SanDisk stake and one-off debt-exchange costs—can persist through the storage cycle, against customer concentration, convertible-debt dilution risk, and sector cyclicality. Valuation at ~21× P/E looks reasonable for a recovery name but is not statistically cheap on FCF yield, book, or Graham screens.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy signal rests on four passed factor families—quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with Lynch/Neff PEG models triggered by ~984% statutory earnings growth off a depressed base. That aligns with a business that has structurally simplified: post-separation WDC is an HDD-centric vendor with vertically integrated head/media capability, ~4,700 active patents, and a product roadmap (ePMR, OptiNAND, UltraSMR, HAMR) aimed at cloud areal-density leadership versus Seagate and Toshiba.

Filings support improving business quality on a continuing-operations basis. Nine-month FY2026 revenue rose to $9.17bn from $6.92bn (prior-year comparable period, 10-Q filed 1 May 2026), Cloud revenue to $8.16bn from $6.01bn, and operating income to $2.89bn from $1.65bn. Q3 FY2026 gross margin reached 50% ($1.68bn on $3.34bn revenue). Management has paired operational recovery with capital returns: a $6bn authorised repurchase ($1.92bn executed in nine months to April 2026), a new quarterly cash dividend ($0.125, raised to $0.15 in April 2026), and aggressive debt reduction via SanDisk share-for-debt exchanges.

For a value investor, the case is a **cyclical quality recovery** rather than a net-net or asset play. The screen correctly identifies inflecting fundamentals and shareholder-friendly capital allocation, but 16/22 models fail—including Graham Defensive, Deep Value, FCF Yield, and Piotroski F-Score (5/9)—signalling that headline multiples understate cyclical and earnings-quality risk. ROE of 1.3% on the screen reflects equity inflation from SanDisk fair-value gains, not underlying return on invested capital. The buy rating is defensible on GARP grounds; it is not a margin-of-safety deep-value entry.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclicality and demand.** Filings describe HDD demand as tied to hyperscale data-centre buildouts and inherently cyclical, with past periods of excess capacity, ASP pressure, and underutilisation charges. Management warns that generative AI infrastructure investment by cloud customers could slow unpredictably.

**Customer concentration.** FY2026 10-K risk language: Cloud = 89% of net revenue; top 10 customers = 73%; three customers each ≥10%. Q3 FY2026 10-Q: three customers at 17%, 15%, and 11% of quarterly revenue. Loss or pricing leverage from any major hyperscaler would materially affect results.

**Competitive.** Duopoly-plus dynamics with Seagate and Toshiba; NAND flash and alternative architectures remain long-term substitution threats in legacy client markets.

**Leverage and dilution.** Convertible notes ($1.60bn principal) are conversion-triggered and classified current; fair value per 10-Q ($12.5bn) implies substantial equity dilution if converted at elevated share prices. Capped-call overlays limit but do not eliminate dilution above ~$50.41.

**SanDisk stake and separation.** Retained SanDisk holding ($1.19bn) introduces earnings volatility via fair-value marks and execution risk on remaining monetisation. February 2026 debt exchanges incurred $545m in costs.

**Litigation.** SPEX patent jury verdict ($316m) amended to nominal $1 damages (appeal pending; no accrual). MRT patent matter settled for $130m in April 2025, reversing prior $384m accrual. $603m gross unrecognized tax benefits; ~$133m potentially payable within 12 months.

**Liquidity.** Current ratio 1.08 on screen (below Graham 2.0 threshold); Piotroski flags rising leverage and share dilution. No going-concern language in available extracts; covenant compliance confirmed at 3 April 2026.

**Governance / restructuring.** $103m business realignment charges (nine months FY2026); further ~$5m expected. Preferred stock converted to common in February 2026, eliminating $229m liquidation preference overhang.

**Pension.** Unfunded pension obligation $75m (Japan, Thailand, Philippines plans)— immaterial relative to scale but present.
