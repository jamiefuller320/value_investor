# Mondelez International, Inc. (MDLZ) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T11:28:07.386412+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Mondelez is a global branded-snacks franchise (Oreo, Cadbury, Milka, Toblerone) that the quantitative screen rates as a buy, driven mainly by dividend, GARP and risk models rather than deep-value metrics. The investment case rests on category-leading brands, a 3.2% dividend yield with growth, and a sharp earnings rebound as cocoa-driven margin compression in FY2025 eases; the screen’s PEG of 0.26 reflects this recovery off a depressed base, not classic cheapness. At 38× P/E and 3.0× P/B, the stock fails most Graham-style and FCF-yield screens, and leverage plus a 0.59 current ratio limit balance-sheet appeal. The central debate is whether margin normalisation and organic volume growth can sustain the recovery, or whether elevated cocoa, European pricing elasticity and ~4% Russia revenue exposure cap upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The buy signal is a **growth-at-a-reasonable-price plus income** call, not a net-net or deep-value entry. Five of 22 models pass across three families: **dividend** (High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth), **GARP** (Lynch PEG 0.26; Neff PEGY 0.08), and **risk** (Earnings Quality). The screen’s 144.9% earnings-growth input is a mechanical rebound from FY2025’s cocoa-hit trough ($2.5bn net earnings per the FY2025 10-K business summary) versus FY2024 ($4.6bn); it should not be read as a sustainable run-rate.

Business quality supports a quality-income overlay: global scale (~$38.5bn revenue), ~91,000 employees, and a portfolio skewed to chocolate, biscuits and baked snacks with strong shelf presence. Iconic brands, pricing power and geographic diversification underpin recurring cash generation — FY2025 operating cash flow was $4.5bn and free cash flow $3.2bn (Yahoo fallback; filing bodies lack annual cash-flow tables). Capital returns are consistent: FY2025 dividends of $2.5bn and share repurchases of $2.4bn (Yahoo).

What the screen **does not** capture is structural leverage (debt/equity 82.6%), negative working capital, and commodity cyclicality. For a value investor, MDLZ fits as an **accumulate-on-weakness income/GARP compounder** where the quantitative hook is dividend sustainability plus earnings normalisation, not absolute cheapness.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Commodity and margin:** Filings repeatedly flag cocoa volatility and hedging effectiveness. Q2 2026 margin recovery may partly reflect timing of cost pass-through and hedging rather than a permanent reset; European volume elasticity from prior price increases remains a headwind.

**Leverage and liquidity:** Debt/equity 82.6% and current ratio 0.59 fail Financial Health and Buffett Quality screens. Intangible-heavy balance sheet (goodwill impairment risk explicitly cited in 10-K risk factors). Pension obligations: accrued pension costs $370m non-current plus $1.0bn prepaid pension assets at Jun 2026 (Q2 10-Q).

**Geopolitical:** Russia ~3.8% of Q2 2026 revenue; Ukraine 0.4%. FY2025 10-K warns of asset expropriation, deconsolidation or impairment in Russia, sanctions compliance, and continued suspension of capex and advertising there. War-related supply-chain and commodity disruption persists.

**Regulatory and trade:** Q2 2026 MD&A cites US tariff uncertainty and potential adverse revenue/COGS impacts despite USMCA exemptions for Mexico/Canada imports. Global minimum tax (OECD Pillar Two) enacted but not material per interim filing.

**Operational:** Nationwide recall of Cadbury Dairy Milk Oreo candy bar for undeclared pistachio allergen (news, 1 Aug 2026). ERP implementation ($1.2bn through 2028) adds execution risk. Argentina hyperinflation accounting (extreme pricing >26%) requires adjusted organic revenue presentation.

**Governance/ESG:** May 2025 8-K: new Severance Plan for Key Executives; shareholder proposals on supplier due diligence, plastic packaging, climate lobbying and human rights all defeated at 2025 AGM. No management change flagged in news pack.

**Screen red flags:** Piotroski F-Score 5/9; FCF yield 2.8%; EV/EBIT 27.6×; ROE 13.3% below Economic Moat threshold (18%). These are consistent with a quality compounder priced for recovery, not a distressed value entry.
