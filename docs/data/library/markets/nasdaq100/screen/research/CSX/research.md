# CSX Corporation (CSX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:54:53.796139+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
CSX is a high-quality eastern US Class I railroad benefiting from a clear operational and earnings inflection in the first half of 2026, following a weaker 2025. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy on cheapness (PEGY), dividend growth, GARP, and earnings quality, with ROE of 24.4% and headline growth of 22.7%—likely reflecting the sharp H1 2026 earnings rebound rather than the full-year 2025 base. The valuation hook is growth-adjusted: trailing P/E of 30.6 looks full after a run to all-time highs, but Neff PEGY of 0.24 and strong margin expansion (Q2 2026 operating margin 38.3%, +240 bps YoY) suggest the market is repricing volume recovery, intermodal share gains, and pricing. The central debate is whether recent truck-to-rail conversion and intermodal momentum are structural or cyclical, and whether current multiples already embed that improvement.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
CSX fits a quality-at-a-reasonable-price framing more than deep value. The screen passes all four metric families—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—with Magic Formula and Earnings Quality endorsements pointing to durable cash conversion and accounting consistency. The franchise is defensible: CSXT operates roughly 20,000 route miles serving 26 states east of the Mississippi, with merchandise (62% of 2025 revenue), intermodal (15%), coal (13%), and trucking via Quality Carriers (6%) providing diversified exposure.

The buy case rests on three filing-backed pillars. First, earnings quality: H1 2026 net earnings of $1.81bn (+23% YoY) and diluted EPS of $0.97 versus $0.78 in H1 2025 (Q2 2026 10-Q), driven by 6% revenue growth, flat total expenses, and operating margin expansion to 37.2% for the half. Intermodal revenue rose 16% in H1 on 7% volume growth, with domestic shipments benefiting from truck-market tightness. Second, capital discipline and shareholder returns: the 2025 10-K shows $4.61bn operating cash flow against $2.90bn property additions; H1 2026 free cash flow before dividends was $1.62bn versus $444m in H1 2025. The Board authorised a new $5bn repurchase programme in May 2026 (total authority $5.7bn at 30 June 2026), and the quarterly dividend was raised 8% to $0.14 in February 2026. Third, balance-sheet capacity: $1.0bn cash at 30 June 2026, an undrawn $1.2bn revolver, and covenant compliance per the Q2 2026 10-Q—supporting continued buybacks without stressing liquidity, though net debt remains material (~$18.9bn total debt per interim balance sheet).

For a value investor, the screen’s PEGY pass (0.24) bridges the gap between a seemingly expensive trailing multiple and an accelerating earnings trajectory. The risk family pass is notable given railroad cyclicality, suggesting the models view balance-sheet and earnings stability favourably despite 2025’s earnings step-down.

Net earnings were $3.67bn (2023), $3.47bn (2024), and $2.89bn (2025); diluted EPS declined from $1.82 to $1.79 to $1.54. The 2025 deterioration included a $164m goodwill impairment (Quality Carriers; $108m in 2024) and higher labour and purchased-services costs, partially offset by lower fuel expense ($1.10bn vs $1.17bn).

Share count fell from ~1.94bn (2024 average diluted) to ~1.87bn (2025). ## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and operational:** Freight demand remains tied to industrial production, coal export prices, and truck competition. Coal was 13% of 2025 revenue; export metallurgical and thermal volumes drove Q2 2026 gains but remain commodity-sensitive. Fuel surcharge recovery boosted revenue in 2026 but reverses if diesel prices fall.

**Labour:** Approximately 16,900 of ~23,000 employees are rail-union represented (2025 10-K). The forward-looking statements cite labour stoppages as a material risk; no active dispute is flagged in interim filings, but East Coast rail bargaining remains an industry-wide watchpoint.

**Legal and environmental:** The Q2 2026 10-Q discloses ongoing environmental proceedings at ~210 potentially responsible party sites and complex Lower Passaic River remediation exposure (CSXT indemnifies Pharmacia). Fuel-surcharge antitrust litigation saw summary judgment granted for railroads in June 2025, with shipper appeals pending. Management estimates reasonably possible aggregate loss above established reserves at $3m–$87m (30 June 2026). Filing language warns that while no single contingency is expected to be material, “should a number of these items occur in the same period, however, their combined effect could be material.”

**Accounting and M&A:** Repeated goodwill impairments at Quality Carriers ($164m in 2025, $108m in 2024) raise questions about trucking acquisition economics. Q2 2026 MD&A cites advisory expenses related to “potential industry consolidation”—a strategic wild card that could create value or integration risk.

**Balance sheet:** ~$18.9bn debt with $1.7bn current maturities at 30 June 2026; interest expense was $424m in H1 2026. The company was in covenant compliance, but refinancing at higher rates remains a sensitivity (interest-rate swaps hedge $1.3bn notional).

**Valuation:** News coverage flags the stock at all-time highs (~$53.32, Investing.com, 24 July 2026) with some third-party “overvalued” calls (GuruFocus, simplywall.st, 23 July 2026). The screen’s low dividend yield (1.1%) offers limited downside cushion if earnings normalise.
