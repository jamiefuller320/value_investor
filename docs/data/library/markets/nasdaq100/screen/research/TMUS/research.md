# T-Mobile US, Inc. (TMUS) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:35:44.433304+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
T-Mobile US, Inc. (TMUS) is the second-largest US wireless carrier, positioned on network leadership, postpaid scale (142.4 million postpaid and prepaid customers at year-end 2025, per the FY2025 10-K body extract), and a growing broadband/adjacent portfolio. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy (9/22 models, 73% composite), supported by a 5.7% FCF yield, 17.8x P/E, 18.0% ROE, and passes across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families. The valuation hook is a high-quality oligopoly franchise trading at modest multiples after a sharp post–Q2 2026 earnings selloff, despite management raising full-year free cash flow guidance. The central debate is whether saturating US wireless markets, churn pressure, and satellite/cable competition cap growth, or whether superior network economics and rising cash returns justify accumulation at current levels.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, TMUS offers an unusual combination: oligopoly positioning with screen-validated cheapness and quality rather than a distressed turnaround. The screen passes nine models spanning FCF Yield, Neff PEGY (PEGY 0.07), Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth (2.4% yield), Magic Formula, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality — five factor families with full data quality (20/20 metrics). Business quality aligns with those signals: postpaid service revenue remains the economic engine (81% of 2025 service revenues from postpaid, FY2025 10-K), 5G network scale is a durable moat, and cash generation is improving — Yahoo annual data (fallback; see Financial Review) shows FCF rising from $9.98bn (FY2024) to $15.43bn (FY2025).

The strong-buy case is reinforced by interim filing trends: Q2 2026 total revenues of $22.8bn (+7.9% YoY) and service revenues of $19.0bn (+8.9% YoY) per the 23 July 2026 10-Q, with Q2 net income of $3.24bn roughly flat YoY despite higher interest and credit-loss charges. Management is actively returning capital ($7.1bn of share repurchases in H1 2026 per the same filing) while integrating UScellular (closed 1 August 2025) and expanding into fibre and advertising. The screen’s cheapness signal likely reflects recent market scepticism — not fundamental deterioration — creating a GARP/value entry for patient investors who accept telecom leverage and regulatory risk.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and covenants.** The FY2025 10-K risk factors state TMUS has “a substantial amount of debt” and that inability to service obligations or comply with restrictive covenants could force asset sales or additional borrowing. The Q2 2026 10-Q notes a financial maintenance covenant of 4.5x Leverage Ratio under the January 2026 credit agreement; no defaults are disclosed (Item 3: Defaults Upon Senior Securities — none). No going-concern language appears in available filing extracts.

**Intangible-heavy balance sheet.** Spectrum licences (~$98bn) and negative tangible book (Yahoo: −$56.4bn at FY2025) mean equity screens can overstate “asset” backing; impairment risk exists (FY2025 $278m capital asset impairment per Yahoo).

**Competition and saturation.** FY2025 10-K: industry “reaches saturation,” intensifying competition across prepaid, postpaid, enterprise, and broadband; cable MVNOs and satellite providers named as threats. Barron’s coverage (23 July 2026) highlights churn in saturated mobile markets as a post-earnings concern.

**Regulatory and legal.** Extensive FCC oversight, Sprint merger government commitments, and ongoing cyber-related contingencies. The 10-K references material costs from August 2021 and January 2023 cyberattacks (mass arbitration, class actions, FCC investigation). Commitments and contingencies are cross-referenced in Note 18; full quantification is not in truncated extracts.

**Integration and execution.** UScellular acquisition closed 1 August 2025 ($4.4bn headline consideration per Q2 2026 10-Q Note 2); restructuring/integration risks over two years are flagged in the 10-K. CEO transition: Mike Sievert retired as CEO on 1 November 2025; Srinivasan Gopalan became President and CEO — a leadership handover during integration.

**Controlling shareholder.** Deutsche Telekom AG interests may diverge from minority holders (FY2025 10-K risk factor); DT ownership can affect capital allocation and FCC foreign-ownership limits.

**Credit quality.** EIP receivable bad debt expense rose to $824m in H1 2026 vs $588m H1 2025 (Q2 2026 10-Q), signalling consumer stress in subprime segments flagged in macro risk disclosures.

**Pension.** Actuarial items appear in other comprehensive income but are immaterial in quarterly filings; no large pension deficit is highlighted in available extracts.
