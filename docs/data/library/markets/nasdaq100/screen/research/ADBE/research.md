# Adobe Inc. (ADBE) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:43:24.967716+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Adobe is a subscription-led creative and marketing software franchise trading at a depressed multiple after a sharp share-price reset, which the quantitative screen flags as a strong buy on cheapness, quality and risk metrics (P/E 12.1, FCF yield 10.3%, ROE 63%). Filings show durable fundamentals—double-digit revenue and ARR growth, a $22bn+ contracted backlog, and robust cash generation—yet the market debate centres on whether generative AI is a monetisation accelerator or a structural threat to pricing power. Valuation looks compelling for a value investor willing to underwrite AI execution risk; near-term sentiment remains fragile after analyst downgrades and concerns over ARR growth pacing. This memo largely confirms the screen’s quality-at-a-reasonable-price case, with medium conviction pending clearer AI monetisation evidence.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal rests on three families—cheapness, quality and risk—and Adobe scores well on all three despite being a Nasdaq-100 software name. At P/E 12.1 and FCF yield ~10.3%, the stock screens cheap relative to its historical band and to software peers with inferior economics. Ten models pass, including Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Piotroski F-Score (9), Composite Value and Earnings Quality—consistent with a high-return, cash-generative compounder rather than a distressed cyclical.

Business quality supports the screen. Subscription revenue was $22.9bn of $23.8bn total revenue in fiscal 2025 (10-K, filed 15 January 2026), with remaining performance obligations of ~$22.5bn and deferred revenue of $7.0bn providing multi-year visibility. ROE of 63% and operating margins above 35% (operating income per Yahoo fallback—see Financial Review) reflect pricing power, low capex intensity (~$179m in FY2025 per 10-K notes) and a capital-light model. Management has returned substantial cash via buybacks ($11.3bn in FY2025; $4.6bn in H1 FY2026 per Q2 10-Q), shrinking the diluted share count from 450m (FY2024) to 427m (FY2025) and further to ~407m (H1 FY2026)—directly supporting per-share value metrics that the screen captures.

For a value investor, the setup is a quality franchise marked down on narrative risk (AI disruption, slower headline ARR growth) rather than balance-sheet distress. The screen’s miss on the growth family (3/4 families passed) is appropriate: ARR growth of 12.5% (Q2 FY2026) is solid but below the premium-multiple era. The opportunity is to buy proven cash conversion and moat at a single-digit earnings yield while the market reprices AI optionality.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**AI and competitive disruption.** FY2024 10-K risk-factor language describes a "highly competitive global environment" with "hundreds of competitors worldwide," rapid technological change and evolving distribution models. Generative AI is framed as both opportunity and threat; the market's Morgan Stanley downgrade (21 July 2025, news manifest) cited "cleaner growth and AI monetisation elsewhere," reflecting concern that Adobe's AI monetisation path is slower or less visible than peers.

**Regulatory and litigation.** The DOJ/FTC action under the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA), alleging inadequate subscription disclosure and cancellation mechanisms, was active through FY2025 (10-K: "discovery phase is ongoing"; defence could involve "significant monetary costs or penalties"). The Q2 FY2026 10-Q states Adobe reached a settlement agreement on 12 March 2026 and filed a stipulation of dismissal—terms not disclosed in the extract; loss contingency accruals of $92m in H1 FY2026 (segment note) suggest material but bounded exposure. Securities class action relating to Figma competition statements was dismissed (27 March 2025) but is on appeal; multiple shareholder derivative actions remain stayed. Accrued legal provisions were "immaterial" at Nov 2025 but "probable and estimable" amounts were accrued at May 2026.

**Capital structure and buybacks.** Aggressive repurchases ($11.3bn FY2025; $25bn authority through 2028, expanded by another $25bn through 2030) shrink float and support EPS but reduce cash and, combined with Semrush ($1.87bn, closed April 2026) and debt issuance ($2.0bn Jan 2025 notes), increase gross leverage. Net debt ~$0.8bn per Yahoo at FY2025 year-end is manageable, but negative tangible equity ($1.7bn) limits traditional book-value screens.

**Segment and reporting changes.** From Q1 FY2026 Adobe combined Digital Media, Digital Experience and Publishing into a single reportable segment (Q2 10-Q), reducing transparency versus historical segment disclosure.

**Publishing impairment.** $70m goodwill impairment in Publishing & Advertising (Q2 FY2026) indicates ongoing weakness in a non-core unit.

**Macro.** MD&A cites inflation, rates, FX and geopolitical risks; foreign-exchange hedging notional $6.2bn (May 2026). Macro context (10Y UST 4.68%, USD index 101.5) is neutral colour only—not a screen veto.

**Governance / pension.** No going-concern language in any filing body reviewed. Pension exposure is limited to a 401(k) match ($96m FY2025) and an unfunded deferred compensation plan ($354m liability)—not a defined-benefit pension red flag. Senior notes carry no financial covenants; revolver negative covenants on liens and sale-leasebacks are standard.
