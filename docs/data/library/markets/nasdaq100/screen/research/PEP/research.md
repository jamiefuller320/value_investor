# PepsiCo, Inc. (PEP) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:29:36.933692+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
PepsiCo offers a classic defensive value setup: a global snacks-and-beverages franchise trading on ~17.7x P/E and ~4.4% dividend yield after a sharp share-price de-rating, with the quantitative screen passing cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families. The investment case rests on durable cash generation, a 52-year dividend-increase record (4% raise to $5.92 annualised announced February 2026), and portfolio breadth across geographies and categories. The central debate is whether North American volume weakness—organic volume down 2% in FY2025 and still soft in beverages—is cyclical (inflation-weary consumers trading down) or structural (share loss to private label and energy drinks). FY2025 GAAP earnings were distorted by a $1.86bn Rockstar impairment and poppi/Siete integration costs, masking flat core EPS of $8.14; interim 2026 results show revenue re-acceleration but operating cash flow in H1 remains well below prior-year full-year run-rate. We view the screen signal as directionally correct on valuation, with medium execution risk in the turnaround.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags PEP as a strong buy across ten models—including Lynch PEG (0.13), Neff PEGY (0.03), Quality Value, Buffett Quality, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality—with full data quality (20/20 metrics). That profile fits a value investor seeking a high-quality compounder at a cyclical trough: ROE of 51.5% (screen), P/B 8.4x (elevated but typical for asset-light brand equity), and FCF of ~$7.8bn (Yahoo) to ~$8.2bn (FY2025 10-K).

Business quality remains intact despite near-term noise. PepsiCo's portfolio spans Lay's, Doritos, Gatorade, Pepsi, Quaker, and recent additions Siete, Sabra, and poppi, with 44% of net revenue generated outside the US (FY2025 10-K). The pep+ transformation and 2019 Productivity Plan (extended to 2030; ~$6.15bn expected charges) target cost savings to fund brand reinvestment. Management is integrating North American foods and beverages under a unified operating model to capture supply-chain and commercial synergies.

For a value investor, the screen's cheapness signal aligns with fundamentals on a core basis: core operating profit grew 1.5% in FY2025 and core diluted EPS was essentially flat at $8.14 versus $8.16, while the share price has underperformed peers (MarketWatch, July 2026). FCF rose 9% to $8.2bn in FY2025 despite GAAP net income falling 14%. The 4.4% yield and sub-0.15 PEG ratios (screen; note the 137% headline growth figure likely reflects EPS rebound from impairment-depressed comparatives and should not be taken at face value) create an asymmetric entry if North America stabilises. The screen's risk-family pass acknowledges leverage and macro headwinds but does not auto-veto; our filing review suggests those risks are real but manageable.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Operational / competitive**
- Persistent North American volume decline: PFNA organic volume −2%, PBNA −3.5% in FY2025; PBNA operating profit fell 53% on Rockstar impairment and poppi integration (FY2025 10-K).
- Consumer budget tightening: CEO flagged US inflationary pressure on spending (CNBC, 9 July 2026), consistent with filing language on reduced volume when pricing outpaces willingness to pay.
- Retail disruption and buyer consolidation may reduce shelf space (both 10-Ks).

**Financial / accounting**
- Rockstar impairment ($1.78bn) and SodaStream goodwill where fair value "narrowly exceeded" carrying value—further impairment possible if forecasts miss (FY2025 10-K).
- GAAP EPS −14% in FY2025 masks flat core EPS; screen growth metrics may overstate normalised earnings power.
- Leverage elevated: total debt ~$50bn (Yahoo FY2025); H1 2026 current debt rose to $10.6bn. Credit downgrade could impair commercial-paper access (FY2025 10-K risk language).
- Pension: other pension expense increased $111m in FY2025; US salaried accruals frozen effective 31 December 2025 (FY2025 10-K).

**Geopolitical / regulatory**
- Russia: 5% of FY2025 net revenue, 20% of consolidated cash (FY2025 10-K)—sanctions and repatriation risk.
- Tariffs, sugar/sodium taxes, OECD 15% global minimum tax—all flagged as potential margin headwinds (FY2025 10-K).
- 2019 Productivity Plan: ~$900m charges expected in 2026; total plan ~$6.15bn through 2030.

**Legal / governance**
- Note 13 Legal Contingencies (Q2 2026 10-Q): company is party to various litigation and regulatory proceedings; management does not expect material adverse effect on financial condition, results, or cash flows.
- No material governance red flags in filings reviewed.
