# O'Reilly Automotive, Inc. (ORLY) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T11:58:38.707835+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
O'Reilly is a high-quality North American auto-parts retailer with a durable dual-market model (roughly 50/50 DIY and professional), 33 consecutive years of positive comparable-store sales, and a compounding store rollout (6,585 stores at year-end 2025; 225–235 net new stores planned for 2026). The quantitative screen flags **buy** on quality, earnings quality, and risk-adjusted cheapness (composite 70%; Piotroski 7/9; ROIC rank 92%), but the name fails most classic deep-value screens (P/E 28.4, FCF yield 2.2%, EV/EBIT 23.8). The investment case rests on sustained mid-single- to high-single-digit comp growth, margin resilience, and aggressive capital return—not on a statistically cheap entry. The central debate is whether premium quality justifies a full multiple against rising leverage, negative book equity from buybacks, and long-term EV/competitive risks.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented process, ORLY qualifies as a **quality compounder at a fair price**, not a net-net or deep-value pick. The screen's buy signal is driven by three families—cheapness, quality, and risk—rather than absolute valuation:

- **Magic Formula** and **Earnings Quality** passes reflect strong operating returns and cash-backed earnings.
- **Piotroski F-Score 7/9** confirms profitability, positive OCF, declining leverage (per screen components), no dilution, and improving gross margin—consistent with filing trends.
- **ROIC rank 92%** and **earnings yield rank 70%** place ORLY in the upper tier of the Nasdaq 100 screen universe on economic returns, offsetting a headline P/E of 28.4.

Business quality anchors the thesis. The FY2025 10-K describes a moat built on technically proficient store staff, a tiered distribution network (32 DCs, 399 Hub stores), and a proven dual-market strategy across dense and rural geographies. Management has delivered record revenues and earnings for 33 straight years. Interim 2026 data show acceleration: Q1 comp sales +8.1% (10-Q, March 2026), and Q2 2026 press coverage reports comp +6% with revenue of $4.89bn (+8.1% YoY)—though the Q2 10-Q is not yet in the filing index.

Capital allocation reinforces the case: sustained share repurchases (roughly $2.1bn in 2025 per Yahoo cash-flow data, used as secondary source) and completion/ramping of buyback programmes cited in recent news. ORLY behaves like a self-funded roll-up of a fragmented aftermarket, with organic growth supplemented by selective M&A (e.g., Vast Auto Canada, January 2024, per FY2024 10-K).

The screen's failures (Graham Defensive, Deep Value, FCF Yield, Acquirer's Multiple) correctly flag that **absolute cheapness is absent**. The buy call is a relative-quality signal within sector, not a margin-of-safety deep value entry.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and capital structure.** Total debt ~$8.5bn (Yahoo FY2025) plus $850m new notes; shareholders' deficit deepening. The FY2025 10-K risk factors explicitly cite "increased debt levels" and "credit ratings on public debt" as material risks. No going-concern language appears in available extracts.

**Valuation.** Screen failures on Deep Value, FCF Yield (2.2%), Lynch PEG (2.75), and Acquirer's Multiple (EV/EBIT 23.8) indicate limited margin of safety at current prices. Negative P/B (−39.7) is an accounting artefact, not a deep-value signal.

**Cyclical and structural.** Filings flag sensitivity to miles driven, vehicle age, inflation, tariffs, and weather seasonality. Longer-term EV adoption may reduce repair frequency—a risk O'Reilly acknowledges in risk factors. OEM telematics/repair restrictions could favour dealers over independents.

**Competitive.** Named competitors include AutoZone, Advance Auto Parts, NAPA/CARQUEST, dealers, Walmart, and Amazon. Online price transparency creates pricing pressure; O'Reilly's store/distribution density is the defence.

**M&A uncertainty.** July 2026 news reports a reported cash bid for Genuine Parts' automotive unit (~$10bn), which coincided with a ~7% share price drop ("O'Reilly Automotive Stock Tumbles 7% Despite Analyst Praise For Potential Genuine Parts Acquisition," TIKR.com, 7 Jul 2026). Integration, financing, and antitrust risk are unquantified; no confirmation in SEC filing bodies reviewed.

**Governance/litigation.** Q1 2026 10-Q Note 15: litigation is "incidental to ordinary conduct"; company does not expect aggregate material adverse effect. Union representation is limited (~464 Bay Area store employees; small DC and international unions per 10-K). No pension red flags in available extracts.

**Insider activity.** GuruFocus headlines (Jul 2026) flag insider selling alongside GF Value "undervalued" claims—directionally worth monitoring; manifest lacks detail.
