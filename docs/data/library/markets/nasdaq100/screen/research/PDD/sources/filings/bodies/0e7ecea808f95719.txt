Use of Financial Measures In evaluating the business, the Company considers and uses measures, such as operating profit, net income attributable to ordinary shareholders, diluted earnings per ordinary share, and diluted earnings per ADS, as supplemental measures to review and assess operating performance. The presentation of these financial measures is not intended to be considered in isolation or as a substitute for the financial information prepared and presented in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America (“U.S. GAAP”). The Company’s financial measures exclude the impact of compensation expenses. The Company presents these financial measures because they are used by management to evaluate operating performance and formulate business plans. The Company believes that the financial measures help identify underlying trends in its business by excluding the impact of compensation expenses, which are charges. The Company also believes that the financial measures may provide further information about the Company’s results of operations, and enhance the overall understanding of the Company’s past performance and future prospects. The Company’s financial measures are not defined under U.S. GAAP and are not presented in accordance with U.S. GAAP. The financial measures have limitations as analytical tools. These financial measures do not reflect all items of income and expenses that affect the Company’s operations and do not represent the residual cash flow available for discretionary expenditures. Further, these measures may differ from the information used by other companies, including peer companies, and therefore their comparability may be limited. The Company compensates for these limitations by reconciling the financial measures to the nearest U.S. GAAP performance measure, all of which should be considered when evaluating performance. The Company encourages you to review the Company’s financial information in its entirety and not rely on a single financial measure.
