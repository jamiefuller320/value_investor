# Keurig Dr Pepper Inc. (KDP) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T12:46:25.695152+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Keurig Dr Pepper Inc. (KDP) is a North American beverage and single-serve coffee franchise rated **buy** on the quantitative screen (7/22 models; composite 69%), chiefly on **FCF yield (10.8%)**, **dividend yield (2.9%)**, **Piotroski F-Score (7/9)**, and composite value metrics — not on classic quality or deep-value screens (ROE 5.1%, P/E 31.8x, high leverage). The investment case rests on cash-generative staples brands (Dr Pepper, Keurig, Canada Dry) and a planned split into separate beverage and global coffee companies following the **JDE Peet’s** acquisition (closed 1 April 2026). The central debate is whether post-deal cash flows justify the screen’s cheapness signal, or whether **~$25bn of notes**, **$4.5bn convertible preferred stock**, minority interests, and integration/separation execution risk overwhelm it. Filings confirm covenant compliance, but the balance sheet has materially changed; headline earnings are not directly comparable pre- and post-acquisition.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, KDP offers a **cash-yield and dividend** entry into a defensive category with strong route-to-market (DSD network, Keurig pod ecosystem) rather than a low-multiple compounder. The screen passes five factor families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with standout FCF yield and Neff PEGY (0.16), while failing Graham, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, and Financial Health on **low ROE**, **rich P/E**, and **leverage** (debt/equity ~100; current ratio 0.64 on screen inputs, pre-deal).

Pre-transformation fundamentals were respectable: per **Yahoo annual data (fallback — 10-K body extracts lack full income-statement tables)**, net sales rose from **$15.4bn (2024)** to **$16.6bn (2025)**, with net income recovering to **$2.1bn** after a **$718m impairment** weighed on 2024. Operating cash flow was **$2.0bn** and free cash flow **$1.5bn** in 2025 — consistent with the screen’s FCF-yield signal. Interim filings before the deal showed organic momentum in U.S. Refreshment Beverages (Q1 2026 net sales **+11.9%** to **$2.6bn** per the Q1 2026 10-Q MD&A) and share gains in CSD, energy, and hydration.

The buy case is therefore **not** deep value on book (P/B 1.7x; tangible book deeply negative) but ** income plus optionality** from portfolio reshaping: JDE Peet’s adds global coffee scale; management targets **~$400m cost savings** (news); and a **separation into two listed companies** could unlock sum-of-the-parts value. Piotroski 7/9 supports operational stability in the legacy base. Conviction is tempered by transformation complexity and dilution from preferred equity and non-controlling interests — areas traditional value screens under-weight.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and capital structure:** Post-JDE Peet’s, KDP carries **~$24.8bn of notes**, **$4.5bn convertible preferred stock** (4.75% dividend; conversion at **$37.25**), a **$3.2bn delayed-draw term loan**, and **$4.2bn non-controlling interests** from the Pod Manufacturing JV ($4bn cash from KKR/Apollo/Goldman). Interest expense **doubled** H1 YoY (**$617m** vs **$328m**). Filings warn of potential **credit-rating downgrade** and restricted flexibility until separation.

**Integration and separation:** The **JDE Peet’s Acquisition** ($17.4bn cash consideration) and planned **separation** into beverage and coffee companies are multi-year, management-intensive, and may incur material transaction, synergy-shortfall, and dis-synergy costs. JDE Peet’s segment posted a **Q2 operating loss**; inventory step-up amortisation (**$314m** in Q2) distorts margins.

**Intangibles and accounting:** **Goodwill rose to $29.8bn**; indefinite-lived brand intangibles **~$29.6bn**. Both 10-Ks disclose **impairments in 2024 and 2025** and caution that future impairments are unpredictable. Screen ROE (**5.1%**) reflects heavy intangibles and preferred dividends — not operating weakness alone, but limits quality-screen passes.

**Customer concentration:** **Walmart ~16%** of net sales (10-K) — material buyer power risk.

**Competitive and regulatory:** 10-K risk factors cite intense competition from Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, and Nestlé; health/packaging regulation (sugar taxes, PFAS, extended producer responsibility); and GLP-1-related demand shifts. No going-concern flag; litigation/contingencies referenced generically (Item 3 in 10-Q points to legal proceedings section — detail not in truncated extracts).

**Pension and labour:** References to **Central States pension fund** and rising employee benefit costs; ~**30% of employees unionised** — renewal and strike risk.

**Liquidity:** Working capital turned sharply negative post-deal (current assets **$9.5bn** vs current liabilities **$19.7bn** at 30 Jun 2026). Filings note **supplier payment-term compression** as a recurring liquidity pressure.
