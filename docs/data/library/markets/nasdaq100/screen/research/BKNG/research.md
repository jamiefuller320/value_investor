# Booking Holdings Inc. (BKNG) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T11:36:31.737220+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Booking Holdings is the global leader in online travel, anchored by Booking.com, with record room nights, double-digit revenue growth, and exceptional free cash flow generation. The quantitative screen flags the name as a strong buy on cheapness, GARP, dividend, and risk metrics (FCF yield ~5.1%, P/E ~22.8, Lynch PEG ~0.09), consistent with a high-quality compounder trading below intrinsic momentum. The central debate is whether Middle East geopolitical disruption, rising interest expense, and Gen AI/platform competition will compress growth and margins, or whether scale, the Connected Trip strategy, and capital returns offset those headwinds. Negative reported equity (aggressive buybacks) and a goodwill impairment in 2025 complicate balance-sheet optics but do not, on filing evidence, threaten solvency. We lean constructive but not blindly so.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, BKNG combines **durability, cash conversion, and relative cheapness on growth-adjusted metrics**—the screen’s core signal.

**Business quality.** Filings describe a five-brand portfolio (Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK, OpenTable) with ~4.4 million listed properties and record annual room nights in both FY2024 and FY2025. Merchant payments, loyalty (Genius), and the Connected Trip (flights, attractions, payments) deepen monetisation. FY2025 revenue reached **$26.9 billion** (10-K, filed Feb 2026), up from **$23.7 billion** in FY2024.

**Quantitative alignment.** The screen passes four families—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—with models including FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality. FCF of **~$7.1 billion** in the snapshot and a **5.1% FCF yield** sit well above typical mega-cap consumer cyclicals. PEG ~0.09 (screen) implies the market prices growth conservatively relative to trailing earnings momentum. The negative P/B (-15.9) is a **mechanical artefact of treasury-stock accounting**, not asset impairment: retained earnings are **$40.7 billion** at Dec 2025 while treasury stock is **$54.3 billion** (Q1 2026 10-Q).

**Capital allocation.** Management deploys cash into buybacks ($4.0 billion in Q1 2026 alone; $4.3 billion in 9M 2025 per Q3 10-Q), dividends ($333 million in Q1 2026), and debt management, while maintaining **~$16–17 billion** in cash. The 25-for-1 stock split (effective 2 April 2026, per Q1 2026 10-Q) broadens retail access without altering economics.

**Why strong buy, not just quality.** Unlike many Nasdaq 100 growth names, BKNG screens as **GARP plus yield plus FCF** simultaneously—a rare combination for a wide-moat platform business with 13%+ revenue CAGR (FY2024→FY2025, Yahoo fallback below) and room-night growth of 6–8% through early 2026.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Geopolitical / cyclical.** Filings explicitly cite wars in Ukraine and the Middle East, inflation, and macro uncertainty as demand risks. Q1 2026 10-Q quantifies Middle East impact: cancellations and slower bookings from March 2026, with room-night growth falling to **+1% in March** vs ~**+8%** adjusted. Travel is discretionary; ADR pressure directly hits revenue.

**Competition / disintermediation.** FY2025 10-K warns that Google, Gen AI agents, and large tech platforms can bypass OTAs, reduce direct traffic, and compress margins. Expedia/Airbnb remain direct competitors; news flow (Jul 2026) highlights Expedia relative strength.

**Regulatory.** Designated EU **DMA gatekeeper** and Booking.com a **DSA VLOP**—parity commitments, registration rules for alternative accommodations, DMA/DSA compliance costs, and potential fines (up to 4% of global revenue under GDPR cited in risk factors). Dutch consumer litigation on historical parity provisions ongoing (Note 16 references). US lawmakers urging action against EU tech rules (21 Jul 2026 news)—indirect regulatory overhang.

**Financial structure.** Negative book equity, rising gross debt (~$19bn), and **$5.4bn** cumulative interest to maturity on senior notes ($650m due within 12 months, Q1 2026 10-Q) increase refinancing and rate sensitivity. Interest expense was **$1.37bn** in 9M 2025 vs **$788m** in 9M 2024.

**One-offs / earnings quality.** Q3 2025 **$457m impairment**; recurring investment/security gains and losses in other income (e.g. **-$963m** Q2 2025; **+$302m** gain Q1 2026 per Yahoo quarterly)—reported net income is volatile. Screen “Earnings Quality” pass should be weighed against this noise.

**Operational.** Transformation Programme cost savings not guaranteed (10-K risk factors). Cybersecurity, data privacy (GDPR/CCPA), and partner credit/refund obligations remain material contingencies (Note 13 referenced; bodies not fully extracted).

**Governance / pension.** No material pension red flags in available extracts. Heavy insider-aligned capital return (buybacks > dividends) benefits continuing shareholders but concentrates risk if travel demand falters.

**Screen metric caution.** Reported **93% yield** in `screening_snapshot.json` is inconsistent with cash dividend levels (~$333m/quarter post-split) and likely a data artefact post stock split; do not treat as economic yield.

No going-concern qualification found. Covenant compliance confirmed on revolving facility.
