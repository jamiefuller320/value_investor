# Booking Holdings Inc. (BKNG) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:39:43.490584+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Booking Holdings Inc. (BKNG) is the global leader in online travel distribution through Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK, and OpenTable. The quantitative screen flags a **strong buy** (7/22 models; composite 79%), driven mainly by free cash flow yield (~5.1%), low PEG (~0.09), earnings quality, and shareholder-return metrics. The investment case rests on a high-quality, asset-light franchise generating substantial cash, returning capital aggressively, and trading at a mid-teens earnings multiple after a 2026 share-price drawdown. The central debate is whether near-term geopolitical disruption (Middle East), rising interest expense, and platform/AI competition from Google and others are temporary headwinds—or structural threats to growth and margins. Deep research broadly supports the screen, but with caveats on GAAP equity, 2025 earnings volatility, and incomplete annual filing extracts.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, BKNG combines **business quality** with **valuation support**:

**Quantitative alignment.** The screen passes all four factor families (cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk) with high data quality (18/20 metrics). Models clearing include FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality. At P/E ~22.8x and FCF ~$7.1bn (screen snapshot), the market prices a cyclical travel name rather than a dominant platform with record room nights and mid-teens revenue growth.

**Franchise quality.** SEC filings describe Booking.com as the world’s leading online accommodation brand by room nights, with ~4.4m properties across 220+ countries (FY2025 10-K). FY2025 achieved **record annual room nights**; management is investing in Connected Trip, payments, Gen AI, and the Genius loyalty programme. Merchant mix is rising (72% of gross bookings on a merchant basis in Q1 2026 vs 67% in Q1 2025, per Q1 2026 10-Q)—a structurally higher-revenue, higher-working-capital model.

**Capital return.** The company is a prodigious repurchaser: treasury stock was $(58.3)bn at 31 March 2026 (Q1 2026 10-Q), with authorised repurchases of $774m (9M to September 2025, Q3 2025 10-Q). Quarterly dividends of $9.00 per share were declared in 9M 2025. The screen’s elevated “yield” metric (~93%) likely reflects **total shareholder yield** (buybacks plus dividends), not a cash dividend yield—an important distinction for income investors.

**Value hook.** Negative GAAP book value (P/B -15.9 in the screen) reflects treasury-stock accounting after decades of buybacks, not impaired operations. Retained earnings were $41.4bn at 31 March 2026 despite a $(8.7)bn stockholders’ deficit. The stock appears inexpensive on cash-earnings and FCF metrics relative to growth and competitive position.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and legal.** Designated an EU **DMA gatekeeper** and Booking.com a **DSA Very Large Online Platform**—extra compliance costs, parity-rule changes, and potential fines (GDPR up to 4% of global revenue cited in risk factors). Ongoing competition-authority investigations on parity, ranking, and pricing. Italian tax settlement referenced in Q3 2025 10-Q (withholding on rental partners). Travel transaction tax litigation in multiple jurisdictions remains unresolved.

**Competitive.** Filings emphasise intensifying competition from **Google** (Maps, Gemini, integrated travel), large tech platforms, Airbnb/Vrbo, and **Gen AI agents** that could disintermediate OTAs. Performance marketing dependence on search platforms creates ranking and cost-per-click risk.

**Cyclical and geopolitical.** Travel demand is discretionary; Middle East conflict materially affected Q1 2026 bookings and marketing ROI (filings). Macro uncertainty, overtourism restrictions, and ADR pressure can compress revenue per room night.

**Financial structure.** Aggressive buybacks have driven **negative GAAP equity** and rising gross debt (~$19bn). Interest expense rose materially in 2025 interim periods (Q3 9M interest expense $1,368m vs $788m prior year, per Q3 2025 10-Q structure). FY2025 goodwill/intangible **impairment** recorded.

**Operational.** Transformation Programme execution risk (workforce reduction, system modernisation). Cybersecurity and data-privacy incidents (including inadvertent customer data disclosure to marketing partners noted in FY2024 10-K risk factors). Innovation Box Tax benefit in the Netherlands could be reduced, raising effective tax rate.

**Governance/pension.** No pension red flags surfaced in extracts; commitments and contingencies cross-referenced to Note 13 without detail in truncated bodies.
