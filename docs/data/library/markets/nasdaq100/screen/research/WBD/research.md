# Warner Bros. Discovery, Inc. (WBD) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:56:13.565375+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Warner Bros. Discovery screens as a buy on cheapness, quality, and risk factors—chiefly FCF yield (~28.6%), Piotroski F-Score (7/9), and a return to positive reported net income in FY2025—against a leveraged balance sheet and structurally declining linear TV. The valuation hook is a cash-generative IP library (HBO, Warner Bros., sports rights) trading at P/B ~2.0 with net debt falling from ~$39.5bn (Dec-2024, 10-K) toward ~$32.7bn (Mar-2026, 10-Q), but with negative tangible book and heavy amortisation from the WarnerMedia merger. The central debate is no longer standalone streaming turnaround alone: Paramount Skydance’s $31.00/share cash merger (8-K, 27 Feb 2026) offers a defined takeout path, yet antitrust litigation has pushed closing to as late as June 2027, whilst WBD paid a $2.8bn Netflix break fee in Q1 2026 that materially distorts near-term earnings.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags WBD as a buy (4/22 models; composite 85%; families passed: cheapness, quality, risk). That aligns with filing evidence of operational cash generation and balance-sheet repair: Yahoo (fallback—annual filing bodies truncated before Item 8) shows FY2025 revenue of $37.3bn, net income of $727m (vs a $11.3bn loss in FY2024 driven by a $9.1bn goodwill impairment disclosed in the FY2024 10-K), and free cash flow of $3.1bn. Interim filings confirm deleveraging—total debt fell from $39.5bn (Dec-2024, per FY2024 10-K risk factors) to $32.7bn (Mar-2026, Q1 2026 10-Q)—and streaming scale rising to 131.6m subscribers (FY2025 10-K narrative) from 116.9m (FY2024 10-K). For a value investor, the case is a mispriced cash-flow asset: high D&A and merger-related intangibles depress accounting earnings and ROE (-5.0% per screen) whilst underlying FCF remains substantial. Piotroski F-Score 7/9 and positive FY2025 net income support earnings-quality and financial-health screens. The offset is that much of the equity story is now event-driven: merger consideration caps upside near $31 plus ticking fees, whilst standalone value depends on streaming growth offsetting linear advertising and distribution declines—trends management explicitly flags as material headwinds in both annual 10-Ks.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Merger and transaction risk.** The Paramount Skydance merger ($31.00/share cash) faces antitrust challenges from 12 state attorneys general and the Writers Guild; Paramount agreed to pause closing until legal resolution or 1 Jun 2027 (news, 24 Jul 2026). Failure triggers complex break-fee mechanics: WBD could owe $3.0bn plus Netflix fee reimbursement; PSKY could owe $7.0bn regulatory termination fee (8-K, 27 Feb 2026). Merger pendency restricts operations and creates employee and partner uncertainty (FY2025 10-K risk factors).

**One-off charges.** The $2.8bn Netflix termination fee (Q1 2026 10-Q) materially impairs near-term profitability and cash; it was paid by PSKY on WBD’s behalf but remains a reimbursement obligation in certain termination scenarios.

**Structural industry decline.** Both 10-Ks warn that linear subscriber losses, U.S. linear advertising softness, and audience fragmentation have had, and are expected to continue to have, material negative impact. Advertising revenue fell across interim periods (e.g. nine-month 2025: $5,603m vs $6,260m).

**Leverage and covenants.** FY2024 10-K: “significant debt,” restrictive covenants, negative ratings outlook, and risk of inability to meet leverage-ratio tests on the revolving credit facility and term loans. Bridge Loan Facility terms may restrict operations (FY2025 10-K).

**Impairment and intangibles.** $9.1bn goodwill impairment in 2024 (Networks); filings warn of further goodwill/intangible impairment if linear trends worsen or merger uncertainty persists.

**Governance.** CEO David Zaslav sold ~$59.5m of WBD stock (Variety/Deadline, 13 Jul 2026)—a negative signal alongside pending merger.

**Pension.** Multiemployer defined benefit pension plan exposure cited in FY2025 10-K risk-factor summary; no quantified liability extracted from available bodies.

**Competitive/regulatory.** EU approved the merger (22 Jul 2026 news), but U.S. state litigation remains the binding constraint. FCC/program-carriage regulation, content piracy, and sports-rights renewal risk (including past NBA dispute, FY2024 10-K) add operational uncertainty.
