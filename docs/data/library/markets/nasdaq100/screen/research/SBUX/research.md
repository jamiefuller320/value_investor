## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Starbucks Corporation (SBUX) enters the screen as a buy on growth-adjusted and dividend metrics (Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Earnings Quality), not on classic deep-value criteria. The investment case rests on a credible “Back to Starbucks” turnaround under CEO Brian Niccol, early operational momentum in fiscal 2026, and a capital-light pivot in China via the Boyu Capital joint venture. Headline valuation remains demanding (screen P/E ~61; earnings yield ~1.6%), and GAAP earnings are distorted by restructuring and divestiture gains. The central debate is whether comp-store recovery and margin repair can close the gap between a premium brand and a screen that flags leverage, weak liquidity, and negative book equity.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen rates SBUX as a buy (4/22 models; composite 56%; conviction 19%, new signal, improving trend). The signal is driven by growth-at-a-reasonable-price and income characteristics rather than asset-based or earnings-yield value: PEG 0.71, PEGY 0.19, reported earnings growth 85.7%, dividend yield 2.4%, and passes on Earnings Quality and Dividend Growth. Classic value and quality gates fail—Graham, Deep Value, FCF Yield, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, and Piotroski F-Score (5/9)—reflecting high leverage, current ratio 0.72, negative P/B, and thin headline margins.

For a value-oriented allocator, the case is a quality franchise at an inflection point rather than a statistically cheap stock. Filings describe a global brand with ~41,000 stores, North America at ~74% of revenue, and a reset plan focused on store experience, partner investment, portfolio pruning, and licensing. Interim results show revenue stabilising and operating income improving before one-offs; the China divestiture reduces capital intensity while retaining brand economics. The screen’s buy rating aligns with a recovery narrative, but most balance-sheet and multiple tests argue for sizing discipline rather than full conviction.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Balance sheet and covenants:** Negative shareholders’ equity, lease-heavy capital structure (~$9.2bn operating lease liability at Jun 2026), and sub-1.0 current ratio. The $3.0bn revolving credit facility requires a minimum fixed charge coverage ratio; the Q3 FY2026 10-Q states compliance as of 28 Jun 2026, with no borrowings outstanding. Credit deterioration could constrain flexibility.

**Restructuring execution:** Ongoing store closures, support-organisation cuts, Reserve/Roastery impairments, and Nashville relocation create charge risk and operational disruption. FY2025 plan liabilities of **$162m** remained at Jun 2026.

**Labour and legal:** ~6% of US stores are unionised (FY2025 10-K). Note 15 (Q3 FY2026 10-Q) flags labour-union and employment class-action litigation; management assesses material contingent loss as remote, but brand and cost risks persist.

**China strategic shift:** Deconsolidation reduces direct exposure but concentrates growth on JV performance and licensing economics; Q3 China revenue fell to **$69m** (vs $807m prior-year quarter) post-divestiture.

**Commodity and macro:** Filings emphasise arabica coffee price volatility, tariff exposure, and discretionary-spending sensitivity. North America (~74% of revenue) leaves the group exposed to US consumer slowdown.

**Governance turnover:** New CFO (Cathy R. Smith, Mar 2025) and CLO (Pilar Ramos, Nov 2025) under a recently installed CEO—execution risk during transition.

**No going-concern flag** in available filing extracts; defaults on senior securities: none reported.
