# Roper Technologies, Inc. (ROP) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T11:39:06.581352+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Roper Technologies is a high-quality vertical-software and technology-enabled products compounder that the quantitative screen rates **Strong Buy** (10/22 models, 87% composite, P/E 14.8, P/B 1.9). The investment case rests on durable niche-market franchises, mid-single-digit organic growth, high segment margins, and a valuation that appears inexpensive relative to software peers after a prolonged share-price drawdown. The central debate is whether reported earnings and cash flow—boosted by M&A, rising leverage, and volatile fair-value marks on the retained Indicor stake—mask balance-sheet risk from goodwill-heavy intangibles and debt-funded buybacks. Deep research broadly supports the screen’s quality-and-value thesis, but with material caveats on leverage and earnings quality.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy signal aligns with a business that screens like a value stock but operates like a software compounder. Roper passes five model families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—including Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Magic Formula, and Earnings Quality, with full data coverage (20/20 metrics) and building conviction (74%, three weeks stable).

Operationally, filings describe a decentralised portfolio of 29+ niche-market businesses across Application Software (~57% of 2025 revenue), Network Software (~20%), and Technology Enabled Products (~23%). The model prioritises high-margin recurring revenue, bolt-on M&A (~$8.96bn deployed over three years per the FY2025 10-K), and cash compounding; management cites remaining performance obligations of **$5,204.2m** at 31 Dec 2025 (vs $4,754.9m prior year) and 12-month backlog of **$3,424.6m** (vs $3,105.4m).

For a value investor, the hook is the mismatch between business quality (organic growth consistently **~5–7%** in 2025–2026 interim filings, segment operating margins **~27–44%**) and headline multiples (P/E 14.8, yield 1.0%, ROE 13.1%, D/E 61%). The stock appears to offer a GARP-style entry into a serial acquirer with demonstrated dividend growth (quarterly dividend raised to **$0.91** in Nov 2025, +10%) without requiring a heroic cyclical recovery.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Balance sheet / acquisition model.** Goodwill and intangibles dominate the asset base; tangible book is deeply negative (Yahoo: **−$11.2bn** at 2025 year-end). Impairment or higher discount rates would hit operating income—explicitly flagged in 10-K risk factors.

**Leverage and capital allocation.** Debt has risen to fund acquisitions (CentralReach, Subsplash, Convoy, Orchard Software in 2025) and, from Q1 2026, **$1,500m of share repurchases** (4.27m shares). The credit facility contains **financial covenants**; breach could accelerate repayment. Management acknowledges limitations on dividends, M&A, and capex from indebtedness.

**Earnings quality — Indicor mark-to-market.** The retained minority stake in Indicor (post-2022 industrial divestiture) is fair-valued, creating large non-operating swings: Q1 2025 loss **$44.4m**, Q1 2026 gain **$167.3m**. News headline *“Roper Technologies (ROP) Stock Faces One Off $1.1b Gain Driving EPS Surge”* (25 Jul 2026, simplywall.st) underscores that reported EPS can diverge sharply from recurring software economics. Yahoo’s May 2025 piece *“Profits Appear To Have Quality Issues”* reflects the same concern.

**Interest and refinancing.** Interest expense rose with debt balances and rates (Q1 2026: **$99.3m** vs $62.9m prior year). A ratings downgrade could increase borrowing costs per 10-K disclosure.

**Operational / regulatory.** Filings highlight cybersecurity (Vertafore data incident historically, dismissed), GDPR/state privacy patchwork, FDA regulation for medical devices, AI governance (EU AI Act), tariffs/trade policy, and government-contract termination risk. Foundry (media/entertainment) cited as a recurring weak spot in 2025 interim MD&A.

**Legal.** Bodies reference litigation/contingencies via notes (e.g. **$24.0m** settled litigation payment in 2025) without quantifying all pending matters—full contingency detail not in truncated extracts.

**Pension.** Current pension/post-retirement liabilities appear on the balance sheet (~$293m current at 2025 year-end per Yahoo); no acute pension funding crisis disclosed in filing bodies reviewed.
