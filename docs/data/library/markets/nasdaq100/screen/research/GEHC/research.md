# GE HealthCare Technologies Inc. (GEHC) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T17:26:30.208423+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
GE HealthCare Technologies Inc. (GEHC) is a global leader in imaging, ultrasound, patient monitoring, and pharmaceutical diagnostics, spun off from GE in January 2023. The quantitative screen rates the name a **strong buy** (9/22 models; composite 76%; families passed: cheapness, quality, dividend, risk), anchored on mid-teens P/E, ~20% ROE, sub-100% debt/equity, and ~$1.5bn free cash flow. The investment case is a durable installed-base franchise trading at a discount to medtech peers after a weak share-price run, with recurring service/consumables revenue and AI/software optionality. The central debate is whether tariff and China demand headwinds, rising leverage from M&A, and a CFO transition are temporary noise—or a structural margin reset that invalidates the screen’s quality assumptions.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, GEHC combines **scale, cash generation, and franchise breadth** across four segments (Imaging, Advanced Visualization Solutions, Patient Care Solutions, Pharmaceutical Diagnostics) with valuation that screens inexpensive on earnings and cash flow. The screen passes nine models including Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, and Earnings Quality—consistent with a business generating high-teens ROE on a global medtech platform rather than a cyclical deep-value trap.

Business quality supports the signal: ~54,000 employees, sales in 160+ countries, large installed base, and meaningful recurring revenue from services and consumables. Management is pivoting toward precision care, AI, and software (e.g. MIM Anyware, Mayo Clinic theranostics collaboration, Catholic Health alliance), which should deepen customer stickiness even if near-term margins are pressured.

The screen’s **Piotroski F-Score of 4/9** (failed) and weak trends in ROA, leverage, gross margin, and asset turnover warrant caution—these are not fully offset by the cheapness/quality pass. The reported **23.0% dividend yield** in the screening snapshot appears inconsistent with the company’s stated quarterly dividend of $0.035 (annualised ~$0.14); treat screen yield as unreliable until verified against filings.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Tariffs and trade policy.** FY2025 10-K language flags material 2025 tariff impact (notably US–China) and ongoing cost volatility; this aligns with Q1 2026 gross-margin pressure despite revenue growth.

**China demand.** FY2024 10-K cites delayed orders from China stimulus rollout and sector campaigns; China remains ~7,000 employees and a top revenue region—prolonged weakness would hit Imaging and PDx.

**Competition.** Primary global competitors include Siemens Healthineers, Philips Healthcare, Canon, Mindray, and United Imaging; ISO/right-to-repair trends could erode high-margin service revenue.

**Leverage and M&A.** Post-Intelerad, goodwill/intangibles exceed **$17bn** on a **$37.1bn** asset base (Q1 2026 filing). Debt covenants are referenced repeatedly in risk disclosures (“**comply with covenants under our debt instruments**”); no default language, but leverage rose with the acquisition.

**Pension and postretirement.** “**Significant postretirement benefit liabilities**” with uncertain cash costs; Q1 2026 postretirement contributions **$97m** in one quarter.

**Governance / management.** CFO James K. Saccaro to step down **14 August** (news, July 2026)—succession risk during tariff and integration cycle.

**Screen-specific red flags.** Piotroski 4/9; OCF < net income component failed; screen yield metric likely erroneous. Tangible book deeply negative.

**Regulatory/legal.** Extensive FDA/EMA/NMPA, anti-kickback, FCPA, and product-liability exposure in 10-K risk factors; Item 3 defaults upon senior securities listed with no issues flagged in Q1 2026 10-Q.
