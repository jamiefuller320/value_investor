# Open Text Corporation (OTEX.TO) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T17:29:27.819804+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
OpenText is a Canadian-listed enterprise information-management software group trading at a depressed multiple (P/E ~10.9, P/B ~1.4, yield ~4.9%) after digesting the $6.2bn Micro Focus acquisition and subsequent portfolio reshaping. The investment case rests on durable recurring revenue, rising cloud mix, substantial free cash flow, and aggressive capital return (dividends plus a expanded US$500m repurchase programme), set against ~US$6.2bn of gross debt and an intangible-heavy balance sheet. The central debate is whether post-divestiture earnings power and deleveraging will close the valuation gap, or whether modest organic growth, integration residue, and tax/leverage overhangs keep the stock structurally cheap. Deep research broadly supports the screen’s value framing but flags execution and balance-sheet risk that the quantitative models under-weight.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen rates OTEX a **Strong Buy** (13/22 models passed; composite score 86%; sector-relative 81%), with passes across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families. Key screen metrics — P/E 10.9, P/B 1.4, dividend yield 4.9%, ROE 12.8%, FCF ~US$837m — align with a classic value-plus-income profile rare in large-cap software.

Business quality, as described in SEC filings, is anchored in mission-critical information management across six “business clouds” (Content, Cybersecurity, DevOps, Business Network, Observability/Service Management, Analytics), embedded in Global 10,000 customer workflows with high switching costs via customer support and cloud subscriptions. The January 2023 Micro Focus deal expanded scale; the May 2024 AMC divestiture to Rocket Software for US$2.275bn (before adjustments) funded ~US$2.0bn of debt reduction — a deliberate pivot toward core information management and capital return.

For a value investor, the hook is clear: the market prices OTEX as a levered, ex-growth legacy vendor, yet interim filings show stabilising revenue, accelerating cloud growth, expanding adjusted EBITDA margins, and nine-month net income up ~20% year-on-year. Management’s Fiscal 2026 outlook (filed November 2025) targets total revenue growth of 1–2%, cloud revenue growth of 3–4%, adjusted EBITDA margin expansion of 50–100bps, and free cash flow growth of 17–20% — consistent with a cash-compounding, not hyper-growth, thesis. The screen’s earnings-yield, FCF-yield, and dividend models capture this; failed models (Graham Defensive, Buffett Quality, Piotroski F-Score 4/9, Financial Health) correctly flag leverage, negative tangible book, and balance-sheet trend weakness that warrant a measured rather than maximal position.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and covenants.** Total debt ~US$6.2bn against ~US$1.25bn cash (March 2026 10-Q). The Q3 FY2025 MD&A states that if operating cash flows were insufficient, the company “may require additional sources of financing and would be subject to the financial covenants established under our credit facilities.” No going-concern language appears in available extracts, but covenant headroom is an unresolved verify-before-trade item not captured by cheapness screens.

**Tax dispute.** Both annual 10-K narratives warn that CRA reassessments for Fiscal 2012 through Fiscal 2020 are under appeal, with an adverse outcome potentially having a “material adverse effect” on financial position and results.

**Pension.** FY2025 10-K risk summary flags potential inability to generate sufficient cash flow to satisfy unfunded pension obligations; pension liability stood at US$140m (net) at March 2026.

**Acquisition/integration and intangibles.** Goodwill impairment risk persists given US$7.3bn goodwill against US$3.96bn equity. Micro Focus integration and restructuring (up to US$260m programme) remain cost and execution overhangs.

**Governance and management.** CEO transition: Ayman Antoun appointed CEO (29 January 2026, per news/filings index), following an August 2025 leadership transition announcement. Succession adds strategic uncertainty during portfolio rationalisation (Vertica divestiture ~US$150m to Rocket Software, completed May 2026).

**Competitive and technology.** Filings identify IBM as primary competitor alongside ServiceNow, Microsoft (partner and competitor), and others. AI integration presents both opportunity and regulatory/product-liability risk; OpenText joined the OECD Hiroshima AI Process Reporting Framework (May 2026).

**Screen blind spots.** Failed Piotroski (4/9: leverage not declining, current ratio not improving, gross margin and asset turnover not improving) and Financial Health models align with filing evidence of persistent leverage and acquisition-related balance-sheet heaviness.
