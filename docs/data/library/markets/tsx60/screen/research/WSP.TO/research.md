# WSP Global Inc. (WSP.TO) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T17:48:03.309245+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
WSP Global Inc. is a global professional services and engineering consultancy benefiting from secular infrastructure, energy-transition, and data-centre demand, with a record backlog and a multi-year track record of revenue and earnings compounding. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy (8/22 models, 81% composite), supported by reasonable absolute valuation (P/E ~22, P/B ~2.2), strong free cash flow, a Piotroski F-Score of 8/9, and passes across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families. The central debate is whether recent share-price weakness—driven partly by AI-disruption fears and integration risk from large M&A (TRC closed; non-binding Arcadis approach disclosed)—creates a genuine value entry or reflects structural margin and leverage concerns that screens understate. Primary SEDAR+ filing bodies were not available in the research pack; financial analysis below relies on Yahoo fallback data and should be treated as indicative pending full annual-report review.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
WSP fits a value-oriented GARP profile: the business is asset-light, generates substantial operating cash flow, and trades at a mid-teens-to-low-20s earnings multiple despite EPS having roughly doubled from 2022 to 2025. The screen’s strong-buy signal is anchored in five factor families—cheapness (P/E below 25, P/B below 3), quality (Piotroski 8/9, earnings quality pass), dividend growth pass, Neff PEGY and FCF yield passes, and acceptable leverage (D/E ~87%). Business quality aligns with these metrics: revenue grew from C$11.9bn (2022) to C$18.3bn (2025), a ~15% CAGR, while diluted EPS rose from C$3.58 to C$7.36 over the same period. Normalised earnings power is higher than reported net income once M&A-related restructuring charges (C$183m in 2025) are stripped out—Yahoo shows normalised net income of ~C$1.06bn versus reported C$964m. Free cash flow strengthened materially to C$2.09bn in 2025 from C$648m in 2022, and net debt fell to C$2.16bn at year-end 2025 from C$3.98bn in 2024, improving the balance-sheet profile after the TRC acquisition. For a value investor, the hook is a high-quality compounder temporarily de-rated on sentiment (AI fears, 52-week lows in July 2026 headlines) while fundamentals—margin expansion, backlog growth, and FCF conversion—remain intact. One screen metric appears erroneous: the reported “Yield 93.0%” is inconsistent with cash dividends paid of C$196m on ~131m shares (~C$1.50/share); treat dividend yield as unverified until confirmed from filings.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**M&A and integration:** WSP completed the US$3.3bn TRC acquisition (December 2025 headlines: “WSP to Buy Warburg-Backed Engineering Firm for $3.3 Billion”; equity raise of C$732m). It has also confirmed a non-binding indicative offer for Arcadis at EUR 51.50/share (24 July 2026). Large, serial acquisitions elevate integration execution risk, goodwill impairment risk (goodwill C$9.7bn on C$20.8bn total assets), and further dilution or leverage if additional deals close. Restructuring/M&A charges have been recurring (C$105m–C$183m annually, 2022–2025).

**Balance-sheet structure:** Negative tangible book value (C$1.27bn) means equity is largely goodwill-dependent; an impairment cycle would hit reported book value and potentially covenant headroom (covenant language unavailable without filing bodies).

**Leverage and interest:** Total debt of C$4.86bn and interest expense of C$304m create sensitivity to rate resets and any earnings downturn. Net debt/EBITDA is roughly ~0.9x on 2025 EBITDA—a manageable level post-deleveraging, but this could deteriorate if another large acquisition is funded with debt.

**Share dilution:** Equity issuance for TRC and broader M&A strategy has expanded the share count; the screen’s Piotroski failure on “no share dilution” is warranted.

**Cyclical and sentiment:** Engineering consultancies are tied to public and private infrastructure capex cycles. Recent headlines (“AI fears sink Stantec and WSP shares”, 18 May 2026; “WSP Global (TSX:WSP) Touches Annual Low Amid Market Volatility”, 24 July 2026) highlight market concern that generative AI could compress billable hours—partially countered by management commentary (“AI a 'great thing' for engineering amid labour shortage”, 7 May 2026).

**Governance / pension:** La Caisse de dépôt participated in TRC deal financing (December 2025), adding institutional oversight but also signalling reliance on external capital for large deals. Pension obligations (~C$214m non-current) are present but not flagged as distressed in available data.

**Data quality:** The screen’s 93% yield figure is almost certainly erroneous and should not inform the dividend thesis. No going-concern, covenant breach, or contingency language could be reviewed from filing bodies (none downloaded).
