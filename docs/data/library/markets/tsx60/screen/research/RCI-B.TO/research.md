# Rogers Communications Inc. (RCI-B.TO) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:12:38.264736+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Rogers Communications is Canada's largest integrated wireless, cable, and media operator, now trading on screen metrics that look unusually cheap (P/E 4.1, FCF yield 30.4%, dividend yield 4.3%) after a post-Shaw scale-up and a sharp 2025 earnings step-change. The quantitative screen flags a buy across cheapness, quality, dividend, and risk families (9/22 models, 60% composite), but headline multiples appear materially inflated by non-recurring and mark-to-market income rather than recurring wireless cash flow. The central debate is whether Shaw integration synergies, capex normalisation, and a planned MLSE monetisation can sustain free cash flow and the CA$0.50 quarterly dividend — or whether reported 2025 profitability overstates the through-the-cycle earnings power that value screens are capturing.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Rogers offers a rare combination of oligopoly telecom assets, a well-covered dividend (CA$2.00 annualised; 4.3% yield), and screen-passing cheapness on earnings yield (24.7%), FCF yield, low P/E plus high yield, quality value, Buffett quality, and dividend growth models. Business quality rests on a three-player Canadian wireless market, the Shaw cable footprint, and a sports/media portfolio (MLSE, Sportsnet, specialty channels) that management is repositioning for monetisation.

The screen's quality and risk passes are partly supported by improving leverage (net debt fell to C$39.7B at year-end 2025 from C$44.0B in 2024, per Yahoo fallback data) and a Piotroski F-Score of 5/9 with positive net income, operating cash flow, declining leverage, and no dilution. However, the same screen fails Graham defensive/enterprising, financial health, magic formula, and Piotroski composite — signalling that the cheapness is not clean deep-value but rather a leveraged, intangibles-heavy telco where reported ROE (30.9%) and P/E are distorted by 2025's exceptional net income. The buy case therefore rests on normalised cash generation from a scaled network business plus optional asset-sale proceeds, not on the literal trailing P/E.

The 2025 net income surge to C$6.9B (EPS C$12.74) is driven largely by other income/expense of C$4.84B, including other non-operating income of C$4.98B and restructuring/M&A-related special charges of C$219M; Yahoo reports normalised net income of C$7.05B versus reported C$6.89B. Balance-sheet intangibles and goodwill total C$48.9B at year-end 2025, producing negative tangible book value of C$31.2B — a structural feature, not a near-term liquidity signal, but one that limits asset-based valuation anchors.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Earnings quality and screen distortion.** Trailing P/E and ROE appear flattered by 2025 non-operating gains; the screen fails earnings-quality-adjacent models (financial health, Piotroski composite 5/9; operating cash flow did not exceed net income). Treat headline cheapness as conditional on normalised earnings.

**Leverage and refinancing.** Total debt C$44.2B; net debt C$39.7B; negative working capital C$6.2B. March 2026 subordinated note offerings (US$750M public plus C$1.25B private placement, per news 24 March 2026) and multiple supplemental indentures in the filings index indicate active debt-stack management. Covenant detail is unavailable without MD&A or credit-agreement extracts.

**Regulatory and competitive.** Q2 2026 earnings commentary (22 July 2026) cites low wireless market growth and regulatory hurdles. Canadian telecom remains a three-player oligopoly, but CRTC pricing and MVNO policy remain overhangs not captured in value screens.

**Legal / reputational.** A national class action over the April 2021 nationwide service outage was authorised by the Quebec Superior Court (filing body, 20 October 2025). Plaintiffs seek compensatory, moral, and punitive damages beyond credits already issued. Outcome and quantum are unresolved; exclusion deadline was 23 November 2025.

**Integration and governance.** Shaw integration charges persisted (C$439M restructuring/M&A in 2025 income statement per Yahoo). Dual-class structure (RCI.A/RCI.B) and controlling Rogers family interest are standard but limit minority influence.

**Intangibles and MLSE transaction.** Full acquisition of the remaining 25% MLSE stake (news, 7 July 2026) requires premium payment and adds consolidation complexity; monetisation timing and valuation are strategic options, not assured catalysts. Failure to execute could leave additional leverage on the balance sheet without offsetting proceeds.

No going-concern language was available in retrieved filing bodies.
