A BOE is derived by converting six thousand cubic feet ("Mcf") of natural gas to one barrel ("bbl") of crude oil ("6 bbl"). This conversion may be misleading, particularly if used in isolation, since the 6 bbl ratio is based on an energy equivalency conversion method primarily applicable at the burner tip and does not represent a value equivalency at the wellhead. In comparing the value ratio using current crude oil prices relative to natural gas prices, the 6 bbl conversion ratio may be misleading as an indication of value. This decrease was partially offset by escalating geopolitical tensions toward the end of the third quarter, as global crude oil supply disruptions persisted following renewed conflict in the Middle East. Crude oil pricing is expected to remain volatile in the amid ongoing geopolitical uncertainty. Natural gas benchmark pricing remained relatively consistent in the third quarter of 2026, as higher US production and planned LNG maintenance activities in the US Gulf Coast were offset by increased summer electricity demand. In Canada, increased demand from higher processing capacity at LNG Canada supported AECO benchmark pricing, partially offsetting elevated Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin ("WCSB") production. Demand from LNG Canada is expected to continue supporting AECO pricing as the facility operates toward full processing capacity.
