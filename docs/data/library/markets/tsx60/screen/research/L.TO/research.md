# Loblaw Companies Limited (L.TO) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:04:53.984433+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Loblaw Companies Limited (L.TO) is Canada’s largest food and pharmacy retailer, combining scale in grocery (Loblaws, NoFrills, Real Canadian Superstore), Shoppers Drug Mart, PC Optimum loyalty, and a growing healthcare/clinic footprint. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy (7/22 models; composite 62%), driven by FCF yield, PEGY, dividend growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Piotroski F-Score (8/9), and earnings quality — a GARP/quality compounder profile rather than a deep-value cheque. Filings show consistent revenue and adjusted EPS growth through FY2024 and into 2025, with management guiding to low double-digit adjusted EPS growth for FY2025 (excluding a 53rd week). The central debate is whether premium multiples (screen P/E ~29x) are justified by durable market share, pharmacy mix, and capital return, or whether regulatory scrutiny, grocery concentration, and political sensitivity cap re-rating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Loblaw fits the “quality at a reasonable growth price” bucket more than classic deep value. The screen passes five factor families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk) despite failing Graham, Schloss, deep value, and high-yield screens — consistent with a franchise business trading above trough multiples.

Business quality is supported by filings: FY2024 revenue of C$61.0bn (+2.5%), adjusted diluted EPS of C$8.55 (+10.3% pre-split), retail adjusted EBITDA margin of 11.1%, and retail free cash flow of C$1.5bn. Momentum continued in 2025: Q1 revenue +4.1% with adjusted EPS +9.3%; Q3 revenue +4.6% with adjusted EPS +11.3% (post-split basis). Same-store sales remain positive in food and pharmacy; e-commerce grew ~17–18%. Hard-discount expansion (Maxi/NoFrills) and 100 planned pharmacy clinics align with a value-seeking consumer and connected-healthcare strategy.

Capital allocation reinforces the case: 14th consecutive dividend increase (+10% in Q1 2025), aggressive NCIB activity (C$1.75bn repurchases in FY2024; C$1.28bn YTD through Q3 2025), and heavy but funded capex (automated DCs, ~80 new stores in 2025). Shoppers intangible amortisation rolling off materially boosts reported earnings quality from 2025 onward. PC Financial divestiture to EQB simplifies the group and monetises non-core banking, while retaining the PC Optimum ecosystem.

The screen’s FCF yield (~5.5%) and ROE (~24.5%) align with filing-backed cash generation and profitability; PEGY (0.25) and 21% growth input suggest the market may still under-price earnings momentum relative to quality peers. This is a buy on compounder metrics, not on absolute cheapness.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and legal:** Filings disclose a nationwide class-action settlement relating to an industry-wide bread price-fixing arrangement (C$164m charge in FY2024; settlement agreement entered January 2025). Grocery pricing and market concentration remain politically sensitive in Canada. Forward-looking statements in releases reference risks in the 2024 Annual Report MD&A and AIF — full going-concern and contingency language was not available in body extracts (annual report body missing).

**Competitive and cyclical:** Consumer trade-down supports hard-discount but intensifies price competition. Food inflation running below Loblaw’s internal measures in Q3 2025 may compress pricing power if sustained. Canada Post strike impacts (Q4 2024) illustrate operational vulnerability.

**Financial structure:** Balance sheet is lease-heavy (C$8.5bn+ non-current lease liabilities at FY2024 year-end per filing; C$10.4bn capital leases per Yahoo 2025). Financial Services segment earnings are volatile (Q3 2025 EBT down 76% YoY on lapping of PC Bank tax recovery and higher acquisition costs). PC Financial sale to EQB (agreement December 2025; Competition Bureau clearance reported May 2025) reduces complexity but removes a loyalty-integrated banking asset.

**Governance and control:** News coverage (1 December 2025) notes private companies control ~49% of shares; Weston family influence via George Weston Ltd. is structurally significant for minority holders.

**Operational:** Product recalls (January 2025), Theodore & Pringle optical wind-down (C$30m charge, Q3 2025), and ongoing capex execution risk on automated distribution centres (East Gwillimbury live; Caledon under construction).

**Screen data caveat:** The quoted dividend yield of 96.0% in `screening_snapshot.json` is inconsistent with filing-declared dividends (~C$0.56/quarter pre-split; C$0.141 post-split) and likely reflects a post-split data artefact; treat screen yield metrics as unreliable for this name.
