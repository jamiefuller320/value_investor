# Metro Inc. (MRU.TO) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:14:16.326237+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Metro Inc. is a defensive Canadian food-and-pharmacy operator (~$22B revenue, Québec/Ontario duopoly-adjacent footprint) whose quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, and risk families — P/E 19.7, ROE 13.9%, Piotroski 8/9, and 32 consecutive annual dividend increases. Adjusted earnings have re-accelerated after a FY2024 supply-chain investment trough, with FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS of $4.77 (+10.9%) and H1 FY2026 adjusted EPS of $2.27 (+7.1%), though recent operational shocks (Toronto frozen DC failure, ongoing Laval produce strike) and a pending CEO transition temper the near-term picture. The central debate is whether post-investment efficiency gains and pharmacy/discount expansion justify a ~20x P/E, or whether labour disruption, competitive grocery dynamics, and elevated net debt (~$2.9B) limit upside. Full SEDAR+ annual report/MD&A extracts are absent from the filing pack; balance-sheet detail below falls back to Yahoo where noted.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, Metro offers a compounder profile rather than a deep-discount name: the screen passes four families but fails Graham, FCF Yield, Deep Value, and Buffett Quality, signalling reasonable quality at a fair—not distressed—price. Business quality rests on a franchised/integrated network (~1,000 food stores, ~640 pharmacies), a completed ~$1B supply-chain modernisation (Terrebonne, Toronto fresh/frozen), and a dual-engine growth model — discount banner expansion (Super C, Food Basics) plus Jean Coutu pharmacy comp (+5–6% prescription growth). Filing evidence supports earnings durability: FY2025 net earnings of $1,019.5M (+9.4%) and adjusted net earnings of $1,049.8M (+7.9%) on sales of $22,006.7M (+3.7%), with gross margin expanding to 19.9% on distribution productivity and shrink improvement. Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly — NCIB repurchases of 8.7M shares for $848.3M in FY2025, quarterly dividend raised 10.1% to $0.4075 in Q1 FY2026 — aligning with the Dividend Growth and Earnings Quality screen passes. Magic Formula and Piotroski (8/9: positive NI, OCF, improving ROA, OCF > NI, no dilution, improving margins/turnover; only leverage not improving) confirm operational health. The buy case is a high-quality defensive holding trading at a modest earnings yield (~5%) after a period of share-price weakness, not a net-net or contrarian deep value play.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Operational / supply chain:** Two distribution-centre disruptions within seven months — Toronto frozen DC refrigeration failure (Sep 2025, $24.5M inventory write-down; resumed Nov 2025) and an ongoing union strike at the Laval produce DC, transportation, and head office (from 30 Mar 2026). Filing language warns the Laval dispute "will have an impact on our third quarter results." Concentration of perishable logistics in a few automated facilities raises single-point-of-failure risk despite contingency plans.

**Labour and regulatory:** Canadian grocery remains intensely competitive (Loblaws, Sobeys, discounters). Metro signed the Grocery Code of Conduct (8 Dec 2025; body not extracted); industry commentary suggests limited near-term impact, but supplier-retailer power dynamics bear watching. Forward-looking statements in every release reference risks under "Risk Management" in the annual report — full risk taxonomy not available in this pack.

**Management transition:** Long-serving CEO Eric La Flèche retires at end of FY2026; COO Marc Giroux named successor (2 Jun 2026). CFO transition completed: Nicolas Amyot appointed effective 18 Apr 2025, succeeding François Thibault. Succession execution risk is moderate.

**Financial structure:** Net debt ~$2.9B with minimal cash ($67M per Yahoo); total debt rose FY2024–FY2025. Interest expense increasing in FY2026 ($37.3M/quarter vs ~$30–34M prior year). Heavy intangibles from Jean Coutu acquisition ($3.3B goodwill); ongoing amortisation adjustments (~$28M/year net of tax). NCIB repurchases at ~$97/share consume FCF alongside dividends ($317M FY2025).

**Competitive / cyclical:** Food inflation running 4.1–4.3% CPI; Metro's basket inflation at or below CPI in recent quarters, suggesting limited pricing power. Failed screen models (FCF Yield, Deep Value, Economic Moat) indicate the market may not be mispricing a wide moat.

**Governance:** AGM materials (19 Dec 2025) note two shareholder proposals on the ballot — details not in extracted body. No going-concern or covenant breach language found in available filing bodies.
