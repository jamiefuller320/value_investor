Business Overview Agnico Eagle is a senior Canadian gold mining company that has produced precious metals since its formation in 1972. The Company’s mining operations are located in Canada, Australia, Finland and Mexico and the Company has exploration activities in Canada, Europe, Latin America, Australia and the United States. The Company and its shareholders have full exposure to gold prices due to its policy of no forward gold sales. Agnico Eagle has declared a cash dividend every year since 1983. Agnico Eagle’s operating mines, exploration and development projects are located in what the Company believes to be politically stable countries that are generally supportive of the mining industry. The political stability of the regions in which Agnico Eagle operates helps to provide confidence in its current and future prospects and profitability. This is important for Agnico Eagle as it believes that many of its current mines and projects have mining potential. Agnico Eagle earns substantially all of its revenue and cash flow from the production and sale of gold in both doré bar and concentrate form. Recent Developments Barnat Pit Rock Mass Movement at Canadian Malartic On July 1, 2026, a rock mass movement occurred along the north wall of the Barnat open pit at the Canadian Malartic complex in Quebec, Canada. There were no injuries, equipment damage or environmental impact as a result of the event. As a precautionary measure, the Company temporarily suspended mining operations in the Barnat open pit. Remediation work was completed in the third quarter of 2026, with mining activities resuming on September 27, 2026. During the suspension of mining operations at the Barnat open pit, the Canadian Malartic processing plant used ore from existing stockpiles in place of planned Barnat ore feed. The Company believes that this approach helped mitigate the impact of the closure on production at Canadian Malartic. As reported on July 2, 2026, the expected impact of this event is a reduction of gold production at Canadian Malartic by 60,000 to 80,000 ounces in the second half of 2026 and by up to 150,000 ounces in each of 2027 and 2028. Guidance update For the full year 2026, expected payable gold production remains at the low end of the range of 3.3 to 3.5 million ounces, reflecting the adjusted mine plan for the Barnat open pit at Canadian Malartic. Full year total cash costs per ounce and AISC per ounce guidance for 2026 are expected to be near the upper end of the guidance range of $1,020 to $1,120 and $1,400 to $1,550, respectively, reflecting the revised production plan and input cost pressures. Total capital expenditures for 2026 (excluding capitalized exploration) remain between $2.6 billion and $2.8 billion, and expected capitalized exploration remains between $290.0 million and $330.0 million. The Company now expects depreciation and amortization expense to be towards the upper end of the $1.55 billion to $1.75 billion guidance range, primarily due to a higher amortization rate used at Canadian Malartic following adjustments to the Barnat open pit mine plan. The revised mine plan uses a higher amortization rate resulting from a decrease in the total tonnes milled over the life of mine. Cost Considerations Amid Continued Market Uncertainty The Company is experiencing input cost pressures, primarily related to diesel and consumables. Despite these pressures, total cash costs and sustaining costs per ounce are expected to remain within the guidance ranges, albeit towards the upper end. Diesel The Company’s full year 2026 cost guidance is based on an assumed diesel benchmark price of $0.78 per litre (excluding transportation and taxes). Diesel now represents approximately 11% of the Company’s operating costs. For the fourth quarter of 2026, the Company expects the 2026 Nunavut sealift purchases to impact production costs by approximately $21 million. The Company also estimates that for every 10% change in diesel prices, total cash costs will be affected by approximately $3 per ounce from direct diesel exposure and approximately $1 per ounce from indirect diesel exposure related to transportation. The Company will continue to monitor market conditions and opportunistically add to its diesel hedges. Consumables Consumables and supplies, which represent approximately 30% of the Company’s total operating costs, are beginning to face inflationary pressures of approximately 6%. Tariffs The international trade disputes initiated by U.S. tariffs on certain Canadian imports, and the subsequent retaliatory tariffs imposed by Canada on certain U.S. imports in September 2026 following the collapse of trade negotiations in August 2026, continue to create uncertainty in the operating environment. The Company expects its revenue to remain unaffected, as its gold production is refined in Canada, Australia and Europe. Following a review of its supply chain exposure, the Company estimates that the Canadian retaliatory tariffs will increase operating costs by approximately $5 per ounce. This estimated impact has been incorporated into 4 ​ AGNICO EAGLE MINES LIMITED MANAGEMENT’S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS For the Three and Nine Months Ended September 30, 2026 the cost guidance provided in this MD&A. The Company continues to monitor developments in the trade environment and evaluate alternative sourcing strategies where appropriate to mitigate potential impacts on its operations and cost structure.
