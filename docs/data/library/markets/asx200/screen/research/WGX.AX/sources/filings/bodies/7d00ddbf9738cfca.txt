Introduction 
 
Commitment to Good Governance 
The Directors of Westgold Resources Limited (ASX: WGX) (Westgold or the Company) believe that 
effective corporate governance supports sustainable company performance, strengthens 
accountability and responsible business conduct, and creates long-term value for shareholders 
and other stakeholders.  
The board of Directors (Board) has responsibility for the corporate governance of Westgold and its 
subsidiaries and has implemented a governance framework comprising policies, procedures and 
systems of control designed to provide practical means for ensuring good governance outcomes, 
which meet the expectations of all stakeholders. 
Westgold recognises that governance practices are not a static set of principles, and Westgold will 
continue to review its corporate governance practices and policies at least annually to ensure they 
remain current, effective and aligned to accepted principles and good practice. Changes and 
improvements are made in a substance over form manner, which appropriately reflect the 
changing circumstances of Westgold as it grows and evolves. 
Further details of Westgold’s stakeholders and Westgold’s approach to stakeholder engagement 
are contained in Westgold’s 2026 Annual Report which can be found on our website at: 
https://www.westgold.com.au/annual-reports.

Board and management 
 
Roles and Responsibilities 
The roles and responsibilities of the Board, Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer and the 
Executive Leadership Team (being the senior management who report to the Managing Director 
and Chief Executive Officer) are detailed in the Board Charter.  
The Board is collectively responsible for the overall governance of Westgold. The Board derives its 
authority from Westgold’s Constitution which provides that the business and affairs of Westgold 
shall be managed, and all corporate powers shall be exercised by or under the direction of the 
Board. The role of the Board is to provide overarching strategic guidance and oversee operations, 
financial performance, risk management and governance, with a focus on the creation of 
sustainable long-term value for shareholders. In carrying out its responsibilities, the Board also has 
regard to the interests of Westgold employees, contractors, customers, suppliers and t the 
communities in which Westgold operates.  
The Board ensures that it and Westgold comply with the Constitution and all applicable legal and 
regulatory obligations including, but not limited to, the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) (Corporations 
Act) and the ASX Listing Rules. 
The Board Charter provides that the responsibilities of the Board pertain to matters of strategy, 
appointment, management, performance monitoring, governance and risk management. In 
particular, the Board is responsible for:

Chair of the Board 
The Chair has a primary responsibility to lead the Board and ensure the efficient organisation 
and conduct of the Board’s function. A key part of the Chair’s role is to facilitate effective 
contribution by all Board members and promote constructive and respectful relationships 
between the Board and Westgold's Executive Leadership Team. Further responsibilities of the 
Chair are set out in the Board Charter. 
Hon. Cheryl Edwardes AO was appointed to the role of Non-Executive Chair by the Board in 
March 2022 and is considered to be independent. 
 
Company Secretary 
The Company Secretary is appointed and removed by the Board and is directly accountable to 
the Board, through the Chair, on all matters to do with the proper function of the Board and any 
Committee. The Company Secretary is responsible for facilitating Board and Committee 
meetings and the preparation of minutes, provision of advice on corporate governance matters, 
assisting with the induction and professional development of new Directors, management of 
communications with the ASX about listing rule matters.  Further responsibilities of the 
Company Secretary are set out in the Board Charter. 
Susan Park has served as the Company Secretary since her appointment in April 2022.

Board Skills and Experience 
Westgold is committed to maintaining a Board with an appropriate mix of skills, expertise, 
experience and diversity to effectively discharge its responsibilities and provide strong governance 
oversight. The Directors are all professionals with significant experience across a range of areas 
considered relevant to Westgold’s business, strategy and long-term objectives.  Westgold’s 2026 
Annual Report includes information about the Directors, including their qualifications, skills and 
experience. 
The Board, supported by the Remuneration and Nomination Committee, annually reviews the 
composition of the Board and the Board skills to ensure it remains aligned with the current 
strategic goals and governance requirements of Westgold. The Board ensures that it monitors its 
skills to ensure the mix of skills and diversity that the Board has or is looking to achieve in its 
membership to support the effective oversight of Westgold's operations and strategic objectives, 
and takes these factors into account when reviewing, proposing or appointing Directors. 
The Board is confident that the Directors, collectively, have a diverse and relevant range of skills, 
backgrounds, knowledge and experience across the range of areas identified in the Board Skills 
Matrix to ensure effective governance of Westgold in line with its current strategy and goals.

three years (whichever is longer).

Remuneration 
Westgold’s remuneration policies and practices for the Chair, Non-Executive Directors, the 
Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer and any other Executive Directors and Executive 
Leadership Team, including its equity-based remuneration scheme, are detailed in the Board 
Charter, Remuneration and Nomination Committee Charter and the Remuneration Report 
contained in the 2026 Annual Report.   
The Board believes our people are our most important resource, and the objective is to ensure that 
remuneration structures are competitive, equitable, transparent and non-discriminatory, while 
aligning executives' interests with those of shareholders. The Board ensures Westgold’s 
remuneration framework and policies are aligned with the Company's purpose, values, strategic 
objectives and risk appetite. 
Senior executive remuneration comprises fixed and variable components, with a significant 
proportion performance-based through Short-Term Incentives (STIs) and Long-Term Incentives 
(LTIs), with greater weighting on LTIs. A substantial proportion of at-risk remuneration is delivered 
through performance rights, which are subject to appropriate performance hurdles, vesting 
periods, clawback provisions and Board discretion.  
An independent remuneration consultant was engaged to provide benchmarking data and insights 
on remuneration structures, levels and trends across the Australian mining sector. Following this 
review, the Board considered the remuneration of the Chair, Non-Executive Directors, the 
Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer and the Executive Leadership Team for FY27 having 
regard to role scope, market positioning, internal relativities and the Company’s remuneration 
philosophy of targeting remuneration at up to the 75th percentile of comparable market peers. 
Further information on the remuneration arrangements of the Company is available in the 
Company’s 2026 Annual Report.

Risk Management 
Westgold has adopted a risk management framework to ensure that risk management is effective 
and that Westgold is operating with due regard to the risk appetite set by the Board.  
The Audit, Risk and Compliance Committee Charter outlines the process of risk management and 
internal compliance and control. The Audit, Risk and Compliance Committee has overall 
responsibility for oversight of risk management and, at least annually, considers the overall risk 
management framework and risk profile and reviews its effectiveness in meeting sound corporate 
governance principles and keeps the Board informed of all material business risks. The risk 
registers are also reviewed by the Audit, Risk and Compliance Committee periodically during the 
year.  
Westgold integrates risk management into strategic planning, business planning and material 
investment decisions across the business. Depending on the nature of the decision, relevant 
financial, operational, health and safety, environmental, social, climate-related and regulatory 
risks and opportunities are considered. These considerations help inform our strategic priorities, 
capital allocation, project design and implementation, and how we manage trade-offs between 
near-term performance and longer-term value creation. 
Westgold operates in a complex environment with risks across safety, health, environment, 
heritage, climate, legal, financial, operational, social licence and governance matters.  Westgold’s 
enterprise risk management framework provides a structured approach to identifying, assessing, 
managing and monitoring risks across the business. It is aligned with ISO 31000 and forms part of 
our Risk Management Policy, Risk Management Plan and Integrated Management System. Material 
risks are overseen by the executive leadership team and reviewed by the Board through the Audit, 
Risk and Compliance Committee. The Company’s Risk Management Policy is available on the 
Company’s website.  
Risks are managed through inspections, incident investigations, audits, task-based assessments, 
consultation and change management processes. Risk registers, procedures and frontline tools 
support hazard identification, control implementation and review. Controls are applied using the 
hierarchy of controls, with preference given to elimination, substitution, isolation and engineering 
controls over administrative controls and personal protective equipment. 
Westgold’s material risks are regularly presented to the Board by the Managing Director and Chief 
Executive Officer, and the Board discusses any material matters or changes to the risks during 
Board meetings. The Audit, Risk and Compliance Committee and the Board completed a review of 
Westgold’s risk management framework during FY26 and the Board satisfied itself that the 
framework continues to be sound and that Westgold operates with due regard to the risk appetite 
set by the Board. The Board is encouraged to update and challenge the matters disclosed with 
respect to Westgold’s risks at and between each Board meeting and to raise any material risks that 
they believe are not adequately dealt with.

Internal Audit 
The Board oversees the effectiveness of governance, risk management and internal control 
processes. Management is charged with resourcing, operating and monitoring the system of 
internal control, incorporating risk responses in the form of controls into its management systems, 
and reporting results of the effectiveness of those systems to the Board. To assist in the 
management of internal controls and to increase the level of effectiveness of the control 
environment, the Board has appointed an independent third party as Westgold’s independent 
internal audit partner.  The third party assists in providing independent assurance over key financial 
and operational controls, including the review and confirmation that policies and procedures 
previously approved by the Audit, Risk and Compliance Committee and Board are being adhered to 
by the broader business and supports the Audit, Risk and Compliance Committee with structured 
reporting and insights across the Company’s relevant enterprise risk items enhancing the 
Committee’s overall governance and risk management practices.   
Westgold commenced a three-year internal audit plan during FY26 to assess the design and 
operating effectiveness of controls across the Company’s enterprise risks in the Company’s risk 
register, which involves twenty-four (24) planned internal audits (mapped to relevant enterprise 
risk(s)), with two internal audits undertaken per quarter over the three-year period, ensuring 
consistent and balanced coverage. Individual audits may address multiple enterprise risks where 
appropriate to enable efficient and sufficient coverage. In addition, periodic follow-up reviews are 
performed to monitor the implementation of agreed internal audit actions, supporting 
accountability and timely remediation. The results of these internal audits are presented to the 
Company’s Audit, Risk and Compliance Committee with any material matters escalated to the 
Board, if applicable.

Although no system of internal control can provide absolute assurance that the business risks will 
be fully mitigated, the internal control systems adopted by Westgold have been designed to meet

Verification of Periodic Corporate Reports 
Westgold prepares periodic corporate reports for the benefit of investors, which include annual 
reports, half year reports, quarterly reports and sustainability reports. Westgold’s Executive 
Leadership Team ensures that the integrity of Westgold’s periodic reports, which are not audited or 
reviewed by an external auditor, are materially accurate, balanced and provide investors with 
appropriate information to make informed investment decisions. 
Where a periodic corporate report is not required to be audited or reviewed by an external auditor, 
Westgold conducts an internal verification process to confirm the integrity of the report, to ensure 
that the content of the report is materially accurate. Appropriate supporting information for such 
corporate reports is sought and retained on preparation of the report. 
Copies of all material market announcements are also required to be circulated to the Board in 
draft for approval and promptly on release, to ensure the Board has timely oversight of the nature 
and quality of information being disclosed to the market. 
 
Management Assurance 
Before the Board approves Westgold’s financial statements for each financial period, including the 
full-year and half-year financial statements, the Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer and 
the Chief Financial Officer provide the Board with a declaration in accordance with section 295A of 
the Corporations Act. The declaration states that, in their opinion, the financial records of 
Westgold have been properly maintained, the financial statements comply with the appropriate 
accounting standards and give a true and fair view of the financial position and performance of 
Westgold and that the opinion has been formed on the basis of a sound system of risk 
management and internal control which is operating effectively. Westgold has obtained sign-offs 
on these terms for FY26.
