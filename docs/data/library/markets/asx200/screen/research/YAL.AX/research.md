# Yancoal Australia Ltd (YAL.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T19:29:51.290302+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Yancoal Australia is a pure-play ASX thermal and metallurgical coal producer trading at a material discount to tangible book (screen P/B 0.79; filing NTA A$6.57 at 30 Jun 2025) with net-cash balance-sheet strength that supports the quantitative buy signal across cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk families. The value case rests on trough-cycle asset backing and operational resilience—attributable saleable production rose 11% to 18.9Mt in 1H 2025 despite a 15% fall in realised coal price to A$149/t—while earnings remain in a sharp downcycle (NPAT A$163m in 1H 2025 vs A$420m prior year). The central debate is whether management’s capital-allocation pivot—hoarding cash for M&A, a tiny 6.2c interim dividend, and the subsequent US$2.4bn Kestrel acquisition—represents disciplined cyclical reinvestment or a structural reset that undermines the dividend-led value thesis. FY2025 full-year annual filing is not yet indexed; primary verification stops at the Dec 2024 annual report and Jun 2025 half-year report.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen rates YAL as a buy with eight of 22 models passing (Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Financial Health) across all four factor families, with 62% composite score, 47% conviction, and a stable four-week signal. Headline metrics—P/E 16.3, P/B 0.79, yield 3.4%, ROE 4.8%, D/E ~1%, FCF ~A$543m—point to an asset-backed cyclical entry rather than a quality compounder.

For a value investor, the hook is tangible-book cheapness at a commodity trough. The FY2024 annual report (year ended 31 Dec 2024) shows NTA of A$6.95 per share and a cash balance of A$2.46bn against interest-bearing liabilities of just A$112m. The 1H 2025 interim report confirms net cash of A$1,667m (cash A$1,795m less liabilities A$128m) and gearing effectively nil. Production execution has improved through the downcycle: attributable saleable coal reached 36.9Mt in FY2024 (within 35–39Mt guidance) and 18.9Mt in 1H 2025, annualising above guidance midpoint, with cash operating costs at A$93/t—midpoint of the A$89–97/t range. The risk-family pass aligns with this balance-sheet profile; the cheapness and FCF-yield passes suggest the market may be pricing permanent impairment rather than normal cyclicality. Weak ROE (4.8%) and sub-50% screen conviction temper enthusiasm on business quality, and the dividend track record has become uneven as management prioritises corporate initiatives.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and commodity exposure.** Filings describe coal indices as cyclical, with 1H 2025 prices down 15% on lower API5 and met-coal benchmarks amid elevated global supply and subdued demand. Management anticipates supply-side responses and potential recovery over 12–18 months, but this is forward commentary, not assured.

**Dividend and capital-allocation risk.** The constitution requires minimum payouts of 50% of NPAT or 50% of FCF annually, reducible to 25% if directors deem it prudent. The A$82m interim dividend (~1% yield) triggered a sharp selloff (Market Index, 20 Aug 2025), repeating the Aug 2024 no-interim dividend shock. FY2024's A$687m final dividend partially restored confidence, but the pattern signals M&A priority over income. The US$2.4bn Kestrel deal (Apr 2026 news; not disclosed in indexed filing bodies) would consume substantially all net cash if balance-sheet funded, materially altering the risk profile underpinning the screen pass.

**Safety and regulatory.** A fatality occurred during Austar mine closure operations in Sep 2024; NSW Resources Regulator investigation ongoing (FY2024 annual report). TRIFR 6.3 at 30 Jun 2025 (below industry 7.9) but above Yancoal's internal target; prior TRIFR deterioration noted in remuneration report.

**Contingent liabilities.** Coal & Allied contingent royalty to Rio Tinto (to Aug 2030) creates ongoing cash outflows and remeasurement volatility. Rehabilitation provisions are referenced (Donaldson divestiture transfers liabilities to Bloomfield; Aug 2025) but provision magnitude and discount-rate assumptions are not extractable from the truncated filing bodies reviewed.

**Governance and related-party.** Yankuang Energy Group is the majority shareholder with board nomination rights per the constitution and directors' report. Continuing connected transactions are referenced but detail is not in the extracted body text. Public float ~29.5% (Dec 2024). No going-concern or covenant breach language found; interest-bearing liabilities are minimal lease obligations (A$128m).

**Structural/ESG.** Thermal coal faces long-term demand uncertainty; Kestrel shifts mix toward metallurgical coal but does not eliminate transition risk. Jul 2026 news references rising ESG scrutiny (simplywall.st).

**Filing gaps.** FY2025 annual report absent from index. Kestrel transaction terms, funding structure, and integration risks cannot be verified from primary filings in this library.
