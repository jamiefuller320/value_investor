# West African Resources Limited (WAF.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T19:28:06.110047+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
West African Resources is an unhedged Burkina Faso gold producer operating Sanbrado and Kiaka, rated **Strong Buy** by the quantitative screen (12/22 models, 86% composite) on deep value metrics—P/E 6.5, P/B 1.8, ROE 36.9%, debt/equity 26%—with all four factor families passing (cheapness, quality, GARP, risk). Yahoo-sourced annual data (no usable annual or interim filing bodies are indexed) show a FY2025 step-change: revenue A$1.53bn and net profit A$474m, with free cash flow turning positive at A$358m after a capex-heavy Kiaka build. The valuation hook is a single-digit earnings multiple on a cash-generative producer ramping toward 300–490koz annual output, while the market discounts Burkina Faso sovereign risk and gold-cycle mean reversion. The central debate is whether current earnings are sustainable at prevailing bullion prices, or peak-cycle output that screens overstate once government stake demands, royalties, and normalised capex compress returns.

Screen conviction is moderate but persistent (62%, four weeks at signal). This remains an accumulate-on-research thesis, not a blind quantitative entry, given thin primary financial filing coverage.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Data and governance:** Absence of usable annual/interim ASX filing extracts is the largest research red flag. Going-concern statements, contingent liabilities, debt covenants, related-party transactions, and reserve audits cannot be reviewed from primary sources in this library.

**Sovereign and fiscal risk (Burkina Faso):** News coverage highlights material jurisdiction risk not captured by screens. In April 2026, headlines reported the Burkina Faso government seeking a 40% stake in Kiaka, reducing WAF’s interest to 60% and triggering a reported A$175m special dividend (“West African Resources (ASX:WAF) to Pay A$175 Million Special Dividend After Burkina Faso Cuts Kiaka Stake to 60%”, stocksdownunder.com, 22 April 2026). The July 2026 filing body confirms government ownership at Sanbrado (15% of SOMISA SA) and a production-linked royalty regime that rises with gold price—creating direct fiscal leakage at higher bullion levels. The February 2025 guidance notes royalties added ~US$80/oz to Sanbrado costs as gold rose nearly US$1,000/oz since prior guidance. Resource nationalism is active, not theoretical.

**Commodity cyclicality:** WAF is explicitly unhedged (progress report and guidance filings). FY2025 EBITDA of A$940m embeds favourable gold-price conditions. A material gold decline would compress margins quickly; the low P/E may reflect cyclical discount rather than mispricing.

**Balance-sheet and cash-flow signals:**
- Inventory doubled YoY to A$264.9m—possible stockpile build or working-capital drag; uninterpretable without filing disclosure
- FY2024 negative FCF (A$236m) during peak capex; FY2025 recovery depends on sustaining production rates
- Rising interest costs and A$453m gross debt create sensitivity despite net cash
- Dividends payable A$72.9m alongside A$431m FY2025 capex—capital allocation tension if gold weakens

**Operational and exploration:** Forward-looking language in indexed filings warns that production forecasts and reserve growth are subject to material risks (infrastructure, metallurgy, permitting, sabotage/terrorism, skilled labour). High-grade drill results (e.g. 27m at 6.7 g/t Au) are encouraging but do not translate to reserve additions until the Q1 2027 update.

**Governance:** “Have Insiders Sold West African Resources Shares Recently?” (simplywall.st, 7 May 2026) flags insider activity as a watchpoint; details cannot be verified from indexed filings.

**Pension:** Non-current employee/post-retirement benefits of A$3.7m are immaterial; no pension deficit flagged in available sources. The misindexed 2011 half-year body stated no contingent liabilities and a going-concern declaration for that period—irrelevant to the current entity.
