# West African Resources Limited (WAF.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:02:39.773789+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
West African Resources Limited (WAF.AX) is an unhedged Burkina Faso gold producer whose quantitative screen reads **Strong Buy** (12/22 models, 86% composite), anchored on deep value (P/E 6.5, P/B 1.8), high reported ROE (36.9%), and positive free cash flow. Yahoo-sourced financials (no annual or interim filing bodies are indexed) show a step-change in FY2025: revenue roughly doubled to A$1.53bn and net profit to A$474m, with FY2025 free cash flow turning positive at A$358m after a capex-heavy build phase. The valuation hook is a low-single-digit earnings multiple on a producer that appears to be converting Kiaka/Sanbrado ramp into cash, while the market prices in Burkina Faso sovereign risk and gold-cycle mean reversion. The central debate is whether current earnings are sustainable at prevailing gold prices, or peak-cycle output that screens overstate once growth capex normalises and government stake demands compress economics.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Data and governance:** Absence of annual/interim ASX filing extracts is the largest research red flag. Going-concern statements, contingent liabilities, debt covenants, related-party transactions, and reserve/resource audits cannot be reviewed from primary sources in this library.

**Sovereign and fiscal risk (Burkina Faso):** News coverage highlights material jurisdiction risk not captured by screens. In April 2026, headlines reported the Burkina Faso government seeking a 40% stake in the Kiaka mine, reducing WAF's interest to 60% and triggering a reported A$175m special dividend ("West African Resources (ASX:WAF) to Pay A$175 Million Special Dividend After Burkina Faso Cuts Kiaka Stake to 60%", stocksdownunder.com, 22 April 2026). The July 2026 filing body confirms government ownership at Sanbrado (15% of SOMISA SA) and a production-linked royalty regime that rises with gold price—creating direct fiscal leakage at higher bullion levels. Resource nationalism is an active, not theoretical, risk.

**Commodity cyclicality:** WAF is explicitly unhedged (July 2026 progress report). FY2025 EBITDA of A$940m on revenue A$1.53bn embeds favourable gold-price conditions. A material gold decline would compress margins quickly; the low P/E may reflect cyclical discount rather than mispricing.

**Balance-sheet and cash-flow signals:**
- Inventory doubled YoY to A$264.9m—possible stockpile build or working-capital drag; uninterpretable without filing disclosure
- FY2024 negative FCF (A$236m) during peak capex; FY2025 recovery depends on sustaining production rates
- Rising interest costs and A$453m gross debt create sensitivity despite net cash
- Dividends payable A$72.9m alongside A$431m FY2025 capex—capital allocation tension if gold weakens

**Operational and exploration:** Forward-looking language in the progress report warns that production forecasts and reserve growth are subject to material risks and uncertainties. High-grade drill results (e.g. 27m at 6.7 g/t Au) are encouraging but do not yet translate to reserve additions until the Q1 2027 update.

**Governance:** "Have Insiders Sold West African Resources Shares Recently?" (simplywall.st, 7 May 2026) flags insider activity as a watchpoint; details cannot be verified from indexed filings.

**Pension:** Non-current employee/post-retirement benefits of A$3.7m are immaterial; no pension deficit flagged in available sources.
