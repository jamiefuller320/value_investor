# Premier Investments Limited (PMV.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T19:19:38.997421+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Premier Investments Limited is an ASX-listed retail holding company that, following the January 2025 sale of its five Apparel Brands to Myer Holdings Limited, now comprises Peter Alexander, Smiggle, a 25.4% stake in Breville Group Limited, strategic property, and substantial cash. The quantitative screen rates PMV as a **buy** (10/22 models, composite 60%, P/E 16.0, P/B 2.2, trailing yield 6.6%, ROE 16.0%), reflecting cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk factor passes on largely pre-restructure data. The valuation hook is a sum-of-parts narrative: FY25-end Breville fair value of $1.17 billion plus $333 million cash against a materially lower market capitalisation, with Peter Alexander delivering record sales. The central debate is whether headline FY25 statutory NPAT of $338.2 million (up 31.1%) signals recovery, or whether continuing-operations NPAT of $144.0 million (down 22.5%), Smiggle weakness, and FY26 half-year margin compression indicate that trailing screen metrics overstate the earnings and dividend capacity of the restructured entity.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The buy case for a value investor rests on a persistent quantitative signal (four weeks, stable trend) passing ten models across five factor families — Graham Enterprising, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Financial Health, High Dividend Yield, and Dividend Growth — with high data quality (19/20 metrics). At 16× P/E and 2.2× P/B with reported debt/equity of 24%, the screen identifies a cash-generative, brand-led retailer trading below typical quality multiples rather than a distressed cyclical.

Business quality, verified from primary filings, supports elements of that profile. FY25 continuing retail gross margin reached 65.7% with underlying EBIT margin of 24.1% (pre-AASB 16), which management describes as "best in class." Peter Alexander delivered record sales of $548.0 million (up 7.7% on FY24). The Breville associate contributed $34.4 million of equity-accounted profit and $12.8 million of cash dividends in FY25, with a quoted fair value of $1,169.1 million against a carrying value of $372.0 million. Cash on hand at FY25-end was $333.3 million against property-related debt of only $69.0 million. Over five years, Premier returned $825.4 million in cash dividends plus a $1.03 billion in-specie distribution of Myer shares (September 2025 results announcement).

The disconnect the screen may exploit is the gap between asset backing and share price: news flow cites one-year shareholder losses of roughly 30–45% even as FY25 statutory earnings recovered on disposal gains, and commentary repeatedly asks whether fundamentals are stronger than the share price implies (e.g. simplywall.st, 21 January 2026). Independent director Terrence McCartney's insider purchase of approximately A$257,000 of shares (simplywall.st / Yahoo Finance, 14 April 2026) aligns with that undervaluation narrative.

Important caveat: the screen's 6.6% yield and consolidated ROE largely reflect the pre-restructure five-brand group. FY25 ordinary cash dividends fell to 50 cents per share fully franked (from 133 cents in FY24), and continuing-operations earnings declined. A patient value investor must weigh asset backing and Peter Alexander momentum against Smiggle contraction, transitional execution risk, and potentially stale trailing multiples.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Structural transformation risk.** The January 2025 Myer transaction fundamentally changed Premier's earnings base. Trailing screen metrics may not reflect the smaller, two-brand continuing entity. Continuing-operations NPAT fell 22.5% in FY25 despite Peter Alexander growth.

**Headline vs underlying earnings.** Statutory NPAT of $338.2 million is flattered by disposal gains and transitional services income. Retail underlying EBIT declined 18% with margin compression from 29.7% to 24.1%. Executive STI was not paid to the Interim CEO in FY25 due to EBIT shortfall (Directors' Report).

**Smiggle weakness.** Global sales fell 10.7% to $264.2 million in FY25. The Board is conducting an international search for new Smiggle leadership with no appointment confirmed (FY25 results announcement). Smiggle sales were down 4.0% in the first six weeks of FY26, including a shipping delay impact.

**Dividend reset.** FY25 ordinary dividends of 50 cents compare with 133 cents in FY24, despite the separate $6.46 in-specie distribution. The screen's 6.6% trailing yield overstates forward income capacity on a continuing basis. A July 2026 headline declares a $0.45 interim dividend (*Kalkine*, 21 July 2026), but this is not verified in filing bodies.

**Governance and control.** Solomon Lew, via associated entities (Century Plaza Investments and others), holds a relevant interest in approximately 64.2 million shares (~40% of issued capital; Directors' Report). Concentrated ownership supports long-term alignment but raises related-party and capital-allocation scrutiny. Terrence McCartney serves on both Premier and Myer boards.

**Transitional services dependency.** $20.9 million of FY25 other income arose from transitional services to Myer post-disposal. This is finite and distorts continuing revenue.

**Lease-adjusted leverage.** Yahoo (FY24 fallback) shows capital lease obligations of ~A$409 million. Fixed occupancy costs amplify operating leverage in a downturn and are not fully captured by the screen's 24% debt/equity ratio.

**Goodwill and intangibles.** ~A$823 million of intangibles on the pre-restructure balance sheet (Yahoo, FY24) create impairment risk; FY23 included a A$5 million impairment (Directors' Report reference).

**Asset concentration in Breville.** At FY25-end, quoted Breville fair value ($1,169.1 million) exceeds carrying value by ~$797 million. Earnings and NAV are materially exposed to a single listed associate (25.36% stake).

**Share price and sentiment.** News cites shares down roughly 30% (*Kalkine*, 15 July 2026) to 45% over one year (Yahoo, 8 January 2026). December 2025 AGM update triggered 12–15% falls ("Premier Investments (ASX:PMV) Falls 15% After Major Corporate Overhaul," stocksdownunder.com, 5 December 2025).

**Filing gaps.** No interim body extract; no auditor's report, full balance sheet, cash flow statement, or covenant/contingency footnotes in available bodies. FY26 half-year cannot be audited from primary sources in this library.

**Peter Alexander UK execution.** UK launch incurred $10.9 million of significant entry costs in FY25. International expansion adds operational risk in an unproven market.

**Key person / succession.** John Bryce serves as Interim CEO (Retail). No permanent CEO appointment is disclosed in available filings.
