Chairman’s Report
On behalf of the Premier 
Investments Limited 
("Premier") Board of 
Directors, I am pleased to 
present the 2026 Annual 
Report for the financial 
year ended 25 July 2026 
("FY26").
FY26 was a year of purposeful reassessment and strategic execution for Premier. 
Following the significant transformation completed in FY25, our focus throughout 
the year was on advancing the growth strategies for Peter Alexander and Smiggle, 
maintaining disciplined control of inventory and costs, and preserving the financial 
strength and flexibility of the Group.
The discretionary retail environment remained challenging as consumers continued 
to face cost of living pressures. Against this backdrop, Premier remained focused on 
what it can control: delivering value and compelling experiences for customers, 
executing the strategic priorities of both brands and driving operational efficiency 
across the business.
FY26 Performance
Premier reported statutory Net Profit After Tax of $129.2 million for FY26. The result 
was underpinned by underlying Premier Retail Earnings Before Interest and Taxation 
(EBIT) of $175.9 million1, together with the equity accounted contribution from 
Premier's investment in Breville Group Limited.
Premier Retail delivered global sales of $793.7 million and maintained strong 
margins, with an EBIT margin of 22.2%. The disciplined focus on operational 
efficiency largely offset inflationary pressures, with Premier Retail cost of doing 
business increasing by 1.2% on FY25.
Inventory productivity improved during FY26. Premier Retail closed the year 
with inventory of $96.7 million, down 8.9% compared with July 2025. This clean 
inventory position provides both brands with a sound platform as they enter the 
critical first half trading period of FY27.

Peter Alexander - another year of record sales
Peter Alexander delivered another record sales result in 
FY26, with sales increasing by 3.2% to $565.3 million1. 
This continued growth reflects the strength of the brand, 
its distinctive design-led product, its position as a gifting 
destination for the whole family and the loyalty of 
its customers.
During the year, four new stores were opened and five 
existing stores were relocated or expanded. Premier's 
continued investment in larger formats, upgraded fit-outs 
and customer experience enables the brand to showcase 
its broader product offering and deepen its engagement 
with customers.
The launch of the Peter's Dreamers loyalty program in 
October 2025 was an important strategic milestone. 
The program attracted more than 1.4 million members in 
its first 10 months. As the program develops, it will provide 
further opportunities to strengthen customer engagement 
and enhance the customer experience through improved 
data and insights.
Smiggle - strategy executed ahead of peak trade
As foreshadowed in March 2026, the second half of FY26 
was a planned transition period for Smiggle as the business 
focused on executing its strategic growth plan. Significant 
progress was made during the second half and the key 
relaunch initiatives were delivered in line with plan. Smiggle 
closed FY26 with a clean inventory position, enabling new 
product innovation to be introduced throughout the first half 
of FY27 ahead of the critical Black Friday, Christmas, Boxing 
Day and Back-to-School trading periods.
The clear strategic objective for Smiggle is to reclaim its core 
6 to 12-year-old customer market through innovative product, 
elevated marketing and visual merchandising, while using the 
brand's established multi-channel platform to drive 
sustainable sales and profit growth.
The growth strategy is centred on product repositioning, 
brand elevation, channel optimisation and simplification. 
New Lifestyle, Bath & Body and Personalisation ranges have 
been developed for launch during the first half of FY27. 
Refreshed marketing and visual merchandising will support 
the brand's renewed customer proposition, while an ongoing 
focus on sourcing agility and speed to market will help 
Smiggle respond more quickly to customer demand.
The Smiggle Club loyalty program launched on 30 July 2026. 
While this occurred after the end of FY26, its launch is 
another important step in rebuilding customer engagement 
ahead of FY27 peak trade.

A strong and flexible financial position
Premier's strong balance sheet remains a key source of 
competitive advantage. At 25 July 2026, Premier held 
cash of $391.4 million.
Premier's 25.2% investment in Breville Group Limited had 
a market value of approximately $1.08 billion at year end 
and a balance sheet accounting value of $384.2 million. 
Premier also owns its Australian distribution centre in 
Truganina and its Melbourne Premier Retail head office, 
which are carried at a historical cost of $66.6 million.
This financial strength provides the flexibility to continue 
investing in our business, consider new opportunities as they 
arise and return capital to shareholders while maintaining an 
appropriate capacity to manage risk. As always, opportunities 
will be assessed with long-term shareholder wealth creation 
foremost in the Board's consideration, rather than growth 
for growth's sake.
Dividends and capital management
The Board recognises that shareholders, as the owners of 
the Company, place capital at risk with the expectation of 
appropriate returns. The Board has balanced the Group’s 
strong financial position and growth opportunities against 
the continuing uncertainty in the global retail environment 
in assessing this year’s dividends.
For FY26, the Board approved an interim fully franked 
ordinary dividend of 45 cents per share, and a final fully 
franked ordinary dividend of 36 cents per share. This brings 
total fully franked ordinary dividends for FY26 to 81 cents 
per share, up 62% on FY25 ordinary dividends.
The on-market share buy-back announced in December 2025, 
continues as part of the Board’s ongoing capital management 
strategy. The Board maintains its view that Premier’s 
current capital position provides scope to return capital 
to shareholders while preserving a strong balance sheet 
to support future growth initiatives.

Health and safety
Our team members have a right to be safe at work in an increasingly complex retail 
environment, which is particularly evident in light of the rise of occupational violence 
and aggression directed at frontline staff, a trend we take extremely seriously.
Over the past year, we have strengthened our approach through enhanced training, clearer incident 
reporting and escalation pathways, and closer engagement with store leadership to identify and address 
emerging risks. We remain committed to reducing these incidents and to ensuring every team member 
feels supported and protected while at work. 
To address these risks, we completed a thorough psychosocial risk assessment and are implementing 
a range of improvements, including a new Occupational Violence guideline. We aim to use additional 
funding to expand Mental Health First Aid training for our regional managers and to develop 
de-escalation training for our frontline teams. We are also moving to online incident reporting to 
streamline the process for our teams.
We continue to monitor Lost Time Injuries (LTIs) and the Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR), which 
measures the number of LTIs per million hours worked. In FY26, LTIs and LTIFR both increased on the 
prior year, reinforcing the importance of the initiatives outlined above. Encouragingly, our average time 
to recovery from injury improved compared to the previous financial year. We have also introduced the 
Medical Treatment Injury Frequency Rate (MTIFR) as a new metric, which will give us a more accurate 
picture of incidents and injuries going forward.
Partners
Supply chain due diligence
In FY26, our two iconic brands sourced from trusted international suppliers located in China 
and India. We take pride in our direct sourcing strategy, working closely with our supply 
chain partners to build strong relationships and maintain ethical compliance.
Our modern slavery statement comprehensively sets out our work to embed our ethical sourcing 
program throughout our supply chain - both in our own operations and in our work with our valued 
suppliers. Our program framework includes:
•	 Risk assessment: we partner with LRQA and utilise its EiQ system to draw on proprietary audit 
data and publicly available risk indices to assign risk levels across the regions in which we source. 
•	 Audit, due diligence and worker rights: audits of primary factories have a heightened focus on 
human rights and transparency. Our production verification audit program assists in the identification 
of unauthorised subcontracting. 
•	 Remediation & corrective action: our ethical sourcing teams manage corrective action with 
suppliers, with support from LRQA.
•	 Worker voice: grievance mechanisms for our own team members ensure our people have access to 
support and resolution. 
•	 Industry engagement & training: we collaborate with industry peers to gain insights and improve 
worker conditions. 
•	 Policies & contractual governance: our package of contractual terms and conditions, incorporating 
our ethical sourcing policies, is an additional lever in our governance framework.

Community partnerships
Key partnerships in FY26 included:
Peter Alexander raised more than $140,000 for the RSPCA in FY26, bringing total 
funds raised since our partnership began to more than $1.6 million. 
Peter Alexander donated 4,475 products in FY26 which provided new sleepwear

Planet
We recognise our responsibility to ensure we have a positive impact on the environment and 
play our part in making better decisions in our sourcing and operations. Premier published its 
first Sustainability Report in this year’s Annual Report, which can be found on page 91 of the 
Financial Report. 
During FY26 we improved the accuracy of our packaging and waste data for the purposes of 
Australian Packaging Covenant (APCO) reporting. We published our APCO annual report and 
action plan, committing to reduce the environmental impact of our packaging.
We also note that paper bags now make up 97.8% of total consumer shopping bags, with 
Peter Alexander not using plastic consumer shopping bags in any market. The small percentage 
of plastic shopping bags comprises two small bags only available in Singapore, which we are 
moving through legacy-stock, with no new orders of those bags planned.
Product
Our product ranges keep on evolving and improving to meet changing customer trends 
and values.
Key initiatives include:
•	 Better Cotton - our continued membership of the Better Cotton Initiative and our sourcing of BCI 
Cotton through the mass-balance chain of custody model.
•	 Global Organic Textile Standard - we remain committed to incorporating certified organic cotton 
across our baby and junior collections wherever possible. In FY26, over 109,000 products were 
sourced using organic cotton, reflecting our ongoing focus on more sustainable materials.
•	 Recycled Polyester - where suitable, recycled polyester is being introduced into new ranges as part 
of our ongoing efforts to reduce environmental impact through material choices.
•	 Forest Stewardship Council (FSC) - all swing tickets used in Peter Alexander are FSC certified, 
reflecting our commitment to responsibly sourced paper products.
•	 Cotton Pledge - forced labour has no place in our supply chain. We do not source cotton from 
regions where state sanctioned or other forced practices are known to exist. 
Product initiatives also have an impact on our modern slavery work program, by assisting us with 
visibility and transparency of our supply chain. Please also see our modern slavery statement, 
published on our website, for more information about our initiatives.

The Group determines and presents operating segments based on the information that is internally provided 
and used by the chief operating decision maker in assessing the performance of the Group and in determining 
the allocation of resources.  
An operating segment is a component of the Group that engages in business activities from which it may earn 
revenues and incur expenses, including revenues and expenses that relate to transactions with any of the 
Group’s other components.  The operating segments are identified by management based on the nature of the 
business conducted, and for which discrete financial information is available and reported to the chief operating 
decision maker on at least a monthly basis.   
Segment results that are reported to the chief operating decision maker include items directly attributable to a 
segment as well as those that can be allocated on a reasonable basis.  Unallocated items comprise mainly of

INCOME TAX ACCOUNTING POLICY 
Income tax expense comprises current tax (amounts payable or receivable within 12 months) and deferred tax 
(amounts payable or receivable after 12 months). Tax expense is recognised in profit or loss, unless it relates to 
items that have been recognised in equity as part of other comprehensive income or directly in equity. In this 
instance, the related tax expense is also recognised in other comprehensive income or directly in equity.  
Current income tax 
Current income tax assets and liabilities for the current and prior periods are measured at the amount expected 
to be recovered from or paid to the tax authorities based on the current and prior period taxable income. The tax 
rates and tax laws used to calculate tax amounts are those that are enacted or substantially enacted by the 
reporting date. 
Deferred income tax 
Deferred income tax is recognised on temporary differences at the reporting date between the tax base of the 
assets and liabilities and their carrying amounts for financial reporting purposes based on the expected manner 
of recovery of the carrying value of an asset or liability.  
Deferred tax assets and liabilities are measured at the tax rates that are expected to apply to the year when the 
asset is realised or the liability is settled, based on tax rates (and tax laws) that have been enacted or 
substantially enacted at the reporting date.

RIGHT-OF-USE ASSETS ACCOUNTING POLICY 
The Group recognises right-of-use assets at the commencement date of the lease, being the date that the 
underlying asset is available for use. Right-of-use assets are measured at cost, less any accumulated 
depreciation and impairment losses, and adjusted for any remeasurement of lease liabilities. The cost of right-
of-use assets includes the amount of lease liabilities recognised, initial direct costs incurred, and lease 
payments made at or before the commencement date of the lease less any lease incentives received and an 
estimate of costs to be incurred by the lessee in dismantling and removing the underlying asset, restoring the 
site on which it is located or restoring the underlying asset to the condition required by the terms and conditions 
of the lease, unless those costs are incurred to produce inventories. Unless the Group is reasonably certain to 
obtain ownership of the leased asset at the end of the lease term, the recognised right-of-use assets are 
depreciated on a straight-line basis over the shorter of its estimated useful life and the lease term. Right-of-use 
assets are subject to impairment.
