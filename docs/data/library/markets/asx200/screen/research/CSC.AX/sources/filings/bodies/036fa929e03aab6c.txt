BUSINESS OVERVIEW
Capstone Copper Corp. (“Capstone Copper”, the “Company” or "we") is an Americas-focused copper mining 
company headquartered in Vancouver, Canada. We own and operate the Pinto Valley copper mine located in 
Arizona, USA, the Cozamin copper-silver mine located in Zacatecas, Mexico, the Mantos Blancos copper-silver 
mine located in the Antofagasta region, Chile and 70% of the Mantoverde copper-gold mine located in the Atacama 
region, Chile. In addition, we own the fully permitted Santo Domingo copper-iron-gold project, located 35 kilometers 
northeast of Mantoverde in the Atacama region, Chile, as well as a portfolio of exploration properties in the 
Americas. We also hold land packages totalling approximately 23,000 hectares in the Atacama region of Chile, near 
the Mantoverde mine and the Santo Domingo project. The Company is listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange 
("TSX"), and Australian Securities Exchange ("ASX") as an ASX Foreign Exempt Listing.

Sulphide Business
Q3 2026 sulphide production of 39,815 tonnes of copper in concentrate was 11% lower than 44,904 tonnes in Q3 
2025. The decrease was mainly driven by lower sulphide production at Pinto Valley due to unplanned 
maintenance in July and August, followed by a planned shutdown in September for the primary crusher and filter 
plant rebuilds, which significantly reduced sulphide plant throughput. At Mantos Blancos, production declined due 
to three days of planned maintenance and lower sulphide grades and recoveries, consistent with mine sequence 
expectations. At Cozamin, sulphide production decreased slightly due to lower grades consistent with the mine 
sequence. These impacts were partially offset by higher sulphide production at Mantoverde, supported by higher 
mill throughput and recoveries compared to Q3 2025, despite completing the critical project tie-ins for MV 
Optimized during a planned plant shutdown in September and adverse weather in July and August that disrupted 
mine access and operations, resulting in the processing of lower grade stockpiles. 
                                                                                                                                                                                                          
2026 YTD sulphide production of 124,272 tonnes of copper in concentrate was 10% lower than 137,940 tonnes in 
2025 YTD. The decrease was driven by the same factors described above, in addition to the impacts of the 35-
day strike at Mantoverde which resulted in lower mill throughput and lower sulphide grades in Q1 2026.

Q3 2026 sulphide C1 cash costs1 increased by 28% to $2.56/lb from $2.00/lb in Q3 2025, impacted by lower 
production volumes and higher unit operating costs at Mantos Blancos ($3.73/lb), Pinto Valley ($6.11/lb), and 
Cozamin ($1.64/lb). These impacts were partially offset by lower unit costs from Mantoverde ($0.90/lb), reflecting 
higher sulphide production, stronger by-product credits driven by higher gold prices, favourable treatment and 
refining charges and realized gains on diesel hedge contracts. 
2026 YTD sulphide C1 cash costs1 of $2.37/lb were 14% higher than $2.07/lb in 2025 YTD, driven by higher unit 
production costs at Mantos Blancos ($3.47/lb) and Pinto Valley ($4.35/lb) impacted by lower production volumes. 
This was partially offset by lower unit costs at Mantoverde ($1.00/lb) and Cozamin ($1.28/lb) compared to 2025 
YTD, reflecting higher by-product credits from stronger gold and silver prices, favourable treatment and refining 
charges and realized gain on diesel hedge contracts in Chile.
Cathode Business
Q3 2026 cathode production of 5,403 tonnes decreased by 48% from 10,376 tonnes in Q3 2025, while 2026 YTD 
cathode production of 20,667 tonnes decreased by 28% from 28,552 tonnes in 2025 YTD. The decline was 
largely driven by lower cathode production at Mantoverde, primarily reflecting a cash flow optimization strategy to 
reduce heap throughput of high calcium carbonate content ore, which requires higher sulphuric acid consumption 
per tonne and is therefore uneconomic to process at elevated sulphuric acid prices. This was partially offset by 
higher cathode production at Mantos Blancos, supported by improved dump grades in line with mine sequence 
expectations.  
Q3 2026 C1 cash costs1 for the cathode business increased to $5.68/lb from $3.87/lb in Q3 2025, while 2026 YTD 
cathode C1 cash costs1 of $5.33/lb increased from $4.09/lb in 2025 YTD. The increase in cathode C1 cash costs1 
was due to lower production volumes resulting from reduced heap and dump throughput, lower heap leach grades 
and recoveries, and higher sulphuric acid prices. The Company continues to actively manage this business 
segment through grade optimization and cost hedging strategies to maintain positive margin contribution.
Consolidated
Q3 2026 copper production of 45,218 tonnes was 18% lower than 55,280 tonnes in Q3 2025, while 2026 YTD 
consolidated production of 144,939 tonnes of copper was 13% lower than 166,492 tonnes in 2025 YTD, resulting 
from lower sulphide production at Mantos Blancos, Pinto Valley and Cozamin and lower cathode production at 
Mantoverde.
Q3 2026 C1 cash costs1 of $2.95/lb were 22% higher than $2.42/lb in Q3 2025, while 2026 YTD consolidated C1 
cash costs1 of $2.81/lb were 13% higher than $2.49/lb in 2025 YTD. Results were impacted by lower production 
volumes (Q3 2026: +$0.56/lb, 2026 YTD: +$0.38/lb) and higher input costs (Q3 2026: +$0.12/lb, 2026 YTD: +
$0.13/lb), attributable in part to higher diesel and sulphuric acid prices, both directly and through related inputs 
and services. The higher costs were partially offset by higher by-product credits (Q3 2026: -$0.04/lb, 2026 YTD: 
-$0.14/lb), which benefited from stronger gold and silver prices, along with favourable treatment and refining 
charges (Q3 2026: -$0.06/lb and 2026 YTD: -$0.03/lb) and the realized gains from diesel hedge contracts in Chile 
(Q3 2026: -$0.05/lb and 2026 YTD: -$0.02/lb).

Key Updates
Capstone Copper has expansion optionality across its portfolio with a combination of attractive brownfield and 
greenfield opportunities in top-tier mining jurisdictions in the Americas. Capstone Copper is advancing these 
growth opportunities, which are at various stages. A potential sanctioning decision for each project is subject to a

Cozamin Divestiture 
On September 21, 2026, the Company entered into a definitive agreement with Luca Mining Corp. (“Luca”) to sell 
its Cozamin mine in Zacatecas, Mexico, together with certain related entities and its obligations under the Silver 
PMPA, for total consideration of up to $385.0 million. The consideration comprises:
• $275.0 million in cash payable at closing, subject to customary closing adjustments;
• $15.0 million in Luca common shares to be issued at closing;
• $35.0 million in deferred consideration payable on the first anniversary of closing in cash and/or Luca common 
shares at Luca’s option; and 
• up to $60.0 million in contingent cash consideration based on average annual LME copper prices for the 2027, 
2028 and 2029 calendar years.
The transaction is expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to the satisfaction of customary closing 
conditions and receipt of regulatory approvals, including stock exchange approval and approval from the Mexican 
National Antitrust Commission. The transaction is not subject to shareholder approval or any financing conditions.

Senior Unsecured Notes
On March 25, 2025, the Company completed an offering of $600 million aggregate principal amount of senior 
unsecured notes due March 2033 (the “Senior Notes”). The Senior Notes bear interest at 6.75%, payable semi-
annually in March and September of each year.
The Senior Notes are guaranteed on an unsecured basis by each of the Company’s subsidiaries that provides a 
guarantee of the Revolving Credit Facility.
Revolving Credit Facility ("RCF")
On May 6, 2025, the Company amended its corporate RCF. The amended RCF was increased to an aggregate 
commitment of $1.0 billion, plus a $200 million accordion option available 180 days after closing, and matures in 
May 2029. The amended RCF bears interest on a sliding scale based on adjusted term SOFR plus a margin 
ranging from 1.75% to 2.75% depending on the total net leverage ratio. The amended RCF became effective on 
June 30, 2025 after all the required closing conditions were met. At September 30, 2026, $174 million was drawn 
on the RCF. 
Mantoverde Term Loan
In June 2025, Mantoverde obtained a term loan of a principal amount of $145.0 million, maturing in June 2032. 
During the nine months ended September 30, 2026, the Company made principal repayments of $137 million on 
the Mantoverde Term Loan, with a principal balance of $8.0 million remaining at the end of the period. 
Working Capital Facilities 
Two of the Company’s Chilean subsidiaries entered into a series of short-term working capital facilities to support 
general working capital management. The aggregate balance of these facilities reflects accrued interest as at the 
end of the reporting period and includes $36.4 million received through a receivable financing arrangement with a 
customer (see Note 21). During the nine months ended September 30, 2026, the Company drew $249.1 million 
from its working capital facilities, and repaid $42.0 million.
Mantoverde Cost Overrun Facility ("COF")

NON-GAAP AND OTHER PERFORMANCE MEASURES
The Company uses certain performance measures in its analysis. These Non-GAAP performance measures are 
included in this MD&A because these statistics are key performance measures that management uses to monitor 
performance, to assess how the Company is performing, and to plan and assess the overall effectiveness and 
efficiency of mining operations. These performance measures do not have a standard meaning within IFRS 
Accounting Standards and, therefore, amounts presented may not be comparable to similar data presented by 
other mining companies. These performance measures should not be considered in isolation as a substitute for 
measures of performance in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards.
Some of these performance measures are presented in Highlights and discussed further in other sections of the 
MD&A. These measures provide meaningful supplemental information regarding operating results because they 
exclude certain significant items that are not considered indicative of future financial trends either by nature or 
amount. As a result, these items are excluded from management assessment of operational performance and 
preparation of annual budgets. These significant items may include, but are not limited to, restructuring and asset 
impairment charges, individually significant gains and losses from sales of assets, share-based compensation, 
unrealized gains or losses, and certain items outside the control of management. These items may not be non-
recurring. However, excluding these items from GAAP or Non-GAAP results allows for a consistent understanding 
of the Company's consolidated financial performance when performing a multi-period assessment including 
assessing the likelihood of future results. Accordingly, these Non-GAAP financial measures may provide insight to 
investors and other external users of the Company's consolidated financial information.

Breakdown of C1 Cash Costs and All-in Sustaining Cost Per Pound of Payable Copper Produced
C1 cash costs per payable pound of copper produced is a measure reflective of operating costs per unit. C1 cash

management uses to monitor cost performance. Management uses this measure to assess how well the 
Company’s producing mines are performing and to assess the overall efficiency and effectiveness of the mining 
operations and assumes that realized by-product prices are consistent with those prevailing during the reporting 
period.

discussed above and is also a non-GAAP performance measure that management uses to monitor cost 
performance. Management uses this measure to analyze margins achieved on existing assets while sustaining 
and maintaining production at current levels. Consolidated All-in sustaining costs includes sustaining capital and 
corporate general and administrative costs.
