It comes with the territory as a trend follower because you’re not fighting the market all the time, you’re going with it . When global funds are flowing in a particular direction, they can do so for many months as the major macroeconomic themes that drive them can play out over substantially long timeframes. This pervasive flow of money — particularly when sloshing past a tiny-Tin-Pot-market like ours — tends to grow the trends you’ve attached yourself to each day. But never confuse being on the right side of a trend with intelligence or excellent judgment! What the trend gives, it's bound to take away when it eventually bends… and ends! Take your wins — but stay level headed — and always remain attentive for signals in the trends, price action, candles, and volume that the balance between demand and supply is shifting. ⚖️ What’s my point? 🤔 Yes, yes, I was getting there… Stop interrupting! 😉 Today was yet another day where fund flows went almost exactly as we’d expect based on the prevailing trends : Winner Sectors (Short duration / Pricing Power) Gold Sub-Index ( XGD ) (+1.3%) Resources (XJR) (+1.0%) Energy ( XEJ ) (+0.9%) Loser Sectors (Long duration / Consumer Discretionary / Bond Proxies) Information Technology ( XIJ ) (-1.9%) Consumer Discretionary ( XDJ ) (-0.8%) Health Care ( XHJ ) (-0.7%) Communication Services ( XTJ ) (-0.7%) Swinger Sector (Bond / Proxies) Financials ( XFJ ) (-0.3%) And, yes, there’s a MOTN ( M ore O ften T han N ot) aspect associated with those prevailing trends which suggests they’ll likely do the same tomorrow . ChartWatch Nasdaq Composite Index Analysis The regular financial press headlines will claim a win for the Comp on Wednesday, but we know that the black-bodied candle suggests otherwise: there was some modest excess supply in the system in the shadow of the 24020 all-time high. But, looking that the size of that black-body, and considering the diminutive stature of Wednesday's candle overall, plus the smidge of downward pointing shadow (i.e., a hint of demand-side participation) — it was definitely not a dominant supply-side showing . Rather, it remains perfectly consistent with the prevailing set of demand-side controlled technicals: Short term / Long term trends: ⬆️/⬆️ (agreement between short and long term traders / investors) ✅ Price Action: Rising peaks and rising troughs (i.e. consistent with supply removal and demand reinforcement, buy the dip + FOMO into rallies). ✅ Candles: Predominantly demand-side in nature (i.e. consistent with pervasive programmed buy orders, lack of strong opposing sell orders). ✅ Volume: Benign, nothing special going on there… which is okay… symptomatic of very little motivated / large supply. ✅ ✅+✅+✅+✅+✅ = Stay the course = No change to present risk settings. I accept that many who are reading my work for the first time this week may consider it to be utterly boring: “ This guy says the same bloody thing all the time… demand… blah… supply… blah… trend is up… stay the course… blah-blah-blahdy-blah-blah… ” 🥱💤 If that’s you, then you’d be correct! I’ve been called a lot of things by people in this industry over the last 3-decades, but I hope you’ll agree on this description: Consistent . Of course, I could predict the exact day/time and precise price the Nasdaq Composite will peak and then crash… but that would be a load of 🐂. Because I can’t. Because that’s impossible to do with any consistency . Because the future is unknown = Prediction is futile! There’s an old saying among computer programmers: Garbage in equals garbage out . It means poor inputs produce poor results. 🤖 Trading works the same way. If your rules, data, or decision process are sloppy, inconsistent, or emotional, the outcomes will be unreliable — no matter how good you think your strategy is. Consistency is the most important habit a trader can develop . By applying the same rules the same way, every time, you can clearly see what’s working and what isn’t . Losses become information , not confusion, allowing you to refine entries, exits, and risk management with purpose. Basically, this means: stop doing the stuff that consistently causes you losses . 🛑 Wins, equally, are information, confirming which parts of your edge should be repeated and reinforced . Basically, this means: do more of the stuff that consistently causes you to win ! 🏆 The opposite of consistency is randomness . ⚠️⚠️⚠️ A random trader cannot diagnose mistakes because there is no stable process to analyse . Random in equals random out ! This is why being consistent and wrong is still better than being random and right —because only consistency creates learning, improvement, and long-term success. I bet you know ten investors you’d describe as “random and right” — achieving occasional success, but whose lack of consistency ensures they never truly progress in the long run. I trust you’re not one of the ten, and instead are on your way to becoming a more consistent trader. 😉 View I remain at a 2/3RP portfolio risk limit 🪣 ( RP = R isk P osition — it reflects my personal allowable capital allocation limit for my investments in US stocks. So 1/2RP is 50%, 2/3RP is 67% and FRP is 100% ). A credible demand-side candle from here will trigger a move to FRP vs a close below 22692 would trigger a move to 1/2RP .
