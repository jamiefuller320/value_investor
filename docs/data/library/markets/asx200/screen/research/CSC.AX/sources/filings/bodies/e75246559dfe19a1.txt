Now, given the benchmark was up, you expect that more stocks were up than down… Right? Not necessarily. Take the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) for example – where advancers lagged decliners by 137 to 138. The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (+4.0%) delivered a session worth pausing on. Gold and silver rallied despite the typical headwinds — Brent crude gained, bond yields rose, and the US dollar strengthened. Each of those factors would normally weigh on precious metals. The fact that COMEX gold futures still gained 1.5% to US$4,135.70/oz and COMEX silver futures gained 1.5% to US$60.00/oz suggests genuine safe-haven demand is beginning to reassert itself independently of the oil-yield dynamic, perhaps reflecting investors seeking cover against the broader geopolitical deterioration rather than simply tracking the rate expectations narrative. In the sector, Kingsgate Consolidated (KCN) (+7.6%) , Ora Banda Mining (OBM) (+7.3%) , Regis Resources (RRL) (+6.6%) , Westgold Resources (WGX) (+6.1%) , and Ramelius Resources (RMS) (+2.7%) — the latter buoyed by strong high-grade drilling results reinforcing its path to 500,000 ounces per annum production by 2029-30 — were the best. ASX 200 Materials (XMJ) intraday chart Materials (XMJ) (+2.3%) shared the same improbable logic. LME copper gained 1.7% overnight and COMEX copper futures added a further 3.3% overnight to approach within 2% of the June record, before easing 0.7% to US$6.505/lb in Asian trade. Aluminium and tin were also higher. Base metals up against that same trio of negatives (oil, bond yields, US dollar all up!)? Super impressive. South32 (S32) (+4.6%) , Capstone Copper (CSC) (+3.7%) , Rio Tinto (RIO) (+2.5%) , BHP (BHP) (+2.5%) , and Sandfire Resources (SFR) (+2.2%) all advanced. SGX iron ore futures fell 1.2% to US$97.30/t, keeping a lid on Fortescue (FMG) (-1.4%) . Energy (XEJ) (+1.7%) was the third pillar of today's unusual triple-resources-sector rally. ICE Brent crude futures gained 1.3% to US$92.15/bbl as President Trump played down near-term Iran talks and threatened broader strikes, and coal stocks were also among the leaders. Yancoal Australia (YAL) (+5.0%) was the session's energy standout, with Karoon Energy (KAR) (+4.3%) , New Hope Corp. (NHC) (+2.4%) , and Woodside Energy (WDS) (+1.3%) all advancing. ASX 200 Financials (XFJ) intraday chart Financials (XFJ) (+0.2%) technically finished positive, but only because a burst of institutional activity in the closing price auction pushed the sector up 0.22% instantaneously in the final minutes of trade — a phenomenon that has now occurred several times over recent weeks. Absent that late intervention, the sector was flat to slightly negative. Commonwealth Bank (CBA) (+0.7%) and Westpac (WBC) (+0.3%) edged higher; ANZ (ANZ) and National Australia Bank (NAB) finished little changed. ASX 200 Healthcare (XHJ) intraday chart Health Care (XHJ) (-1.9%) was the session's worst performer as rising benchmark bond yields reduced the present value of the sector's long-duration earnings streams — the mechanism that has both inflated and deflated healthcare valuations repeatedly throughout this year. Cochlear (COH) (-4.9%) led the selling, with Pro Medicus (PME) (-3.7%) , Telix Pharmaceuticals (TLX) (-2.9%) , and CSL (CSL) (-2.8%) — the latter separately receiving a Morgan Stanley price target cut to $163 from $174 — all lower. Real Estate (XPJ) (-1.4%) was the next most affected bond-proxy sector — higher risk-free yields make property trust income streams less attractive by comparison. Lendlease (LLC) (-2.1%) , Goodman Group (GMG) (-2.0%) , Stockland (SGP) (-1.9%) , and Charter Hall (CHC) (-1.7%) all fell. Consumer Discretionary (XDJ) (-1.4%) was caught in both the rising-yield headwind — higher borrowing costs squeeze household disposable income — and a significant company-specific overhang from Wesfarmers (WES) (-2.1%) , which triggered its $1.4 billion expansion of the Chilean lithium joint venture, committing capital to a sector still mired in a commodity price correction. Harvey Norman (HVN) (-2.1%) and JB Hi-Fi (JBH) (-1.5%) were also lower. Information Technology (XIJ) (-1.1%) was dragged by the same bond yield pressure that hit healthcare — high-P/E SaaS names with long-duration earnings are among the most sensitive to rising discount rates. Xero (XRO) (-2.6%) and Technology One (TNE) (-2.3%) led the selling, while WiseTech Global (WTC) (-0.5%) eased modestly after acquiring an AI-powered supply chain technology company for $10 million to develop its VerifyWise governance platform. Lithium Carbonate Futures (Benchmark month, back-adjusted) GFEX Lithium stocks recovered modestly as GFEX lithium carbonate futures gained 1.2% to CNY 143,300/t, closing 4.4% above their session lows in what appeared to be bargain-hunting at key technical support levels following the prior week's sharp selldown. IGO (IGO) (+2.9%) , Elevra Lithium (ELV) (+2.3%) , Liontown Resources (LTR) (+2.1%) , Mineral Resources (MIN) (+1.8%) , and Pilbara Minerals (PLS) (+0.7%) all advanced. Uranium stocks also found buyers as the Sprott Uranium Miners ETF rebounded 4.0% overnight in New York, triggering a recovery across local uranium names. Paladin Energy (PDN) (+6.7%) led — separately boosted by its Langer Heinrich production upgrade for FY27 — with Bannerman Energy (BMN) (+4.9%) , Silex Systems (SLX) (+4.8%) , Deep Yellow (DYL) (+3.1%) , and Boss Energy (BOE) (+2.4%) all performing strongly.
