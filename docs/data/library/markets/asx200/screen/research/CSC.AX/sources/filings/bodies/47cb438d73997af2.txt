In the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers lagged decliners by 119 to 153. Take a look at the intraday chart above. 15 of those 37.5 points came in the closing price auction. Why / how does this occur? Big funds had fill-by-close sell orders that were too large to complete during regular trading due to low-liquidity. They have no choice but to offer the balance of their orders at prices low enough to attract other big fundies in to soak them up. But check out the sectors the big money went / was withdrawn from based on the intraday charts below... the results will suprise you! S&P/ASX 200 Information Technology Sector Index (XIJ) intraday chart Information Technology (XIJ) (+1.1%) shrugged off a weak Friday lead from the Nasdaq, where Nvidia extended losses after chief executive Jensen Huang resigned. Gains were concentrated in two major constituents that had badly lagged the Nasdaq’s AI-frenzied rise as perceived victims of the ‘SaaSpocalypse’ – fears that AI will gut demand for subscription software. Xero (XRO) (+4.7%) rallied, with last week’s positive update appearing to lure capital back into beaten-down ASX tech leaders, while WiseTech Global (WTC) (+2.8%) also gained. A-REIT (XPJ) (+0.8%) also rose, albeit with few clear drivers today. The sector has shown resilience since the RBA’s last board meeting, with investors betting local official interest rates have peaked – a tailwind for property trusts, whose valuations and borrowing costs are highly sensitive to rates. Centuria Capital Group (CNI) (+6.5%) and HMC Capital (HMC) (+4.1%) led the gains. David Jones landlord Vicinity Centres (VCX) (+2.9%) also rallied, while GPT Group (GPT) (+1.4%) and Stockland (SGP) (+1.1%) added to the advance. Consumer Staples (XSJ) (+0.7%) led the defensive rotation, with capital freed up from dumping miners finding its way into sectors whose earnings tend to hold up regardless of the economic cycle. Agribusiness group Elders (ELD) (+7.5%) rallied after reporting a 187% jump in full-year net profit. S&P/ASX 200 Consumer Staples Sector Index (XIJ) intraday chart Communication Services (XTJ) (+0.5%) was another beneficiary of the rotation, with investors favouring utility-like telcos. Utilities (XUJ) (+0.1%) edged higher on the same defensive flows. Financials (XFJ) (+0.0%) played its typical anchor role. The banks were strong early as they soaked up rotational flows out of materials, but ebbed substantially into the close to land mixed. ANZ Group Holdings (ANZ) (+0.6%) and Commonwealth Bank of Australia (CBA) (+0.2%) finished higher, while National Australia Bank (NAB) (-0.1%) and Westpac Banking Corporation (WBC) (-0.2%) slipped. Insurers edged higher, with Suncorp Group (SUN) (+0.5%) and QBE Insurance Group (QBE) (+0.3%) both in the green. S&P/ASX 200 Financials Sector Index (XFJ) intraday chart Materials (XMJ) (-2.2%) was the session’s chief casualty as investors dumped miners across the board. The leads for mining and resources stocks could not have been weaker — and reflected some poor economic data out of China today. They are, however, consistent with recent trends flagged here and in ChartWatch — and therefore should be no surprise to readers of this column! October’s Chinese activity data missed across the board. Fixed asset investment fell 8.1% year-to-date against a forecast 7.0% decline, industrial production grew 4.8% annually (forecast 5.3%), and retail sales rose just 0.4% (forecast 0.8%), while new home prices fell again. A slight dip in unemployment to 5.1% was the lone bright spot. Weaker investment and factory activity dampens the demand outlook for the commodities China imports in bulk – much of it from Australia.
