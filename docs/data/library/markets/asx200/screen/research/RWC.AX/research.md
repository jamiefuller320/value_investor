## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Reliance Worldwide Corporation Limited (ASX: RWC) screens as a buy on valuation, cash generation, and balance-sheet metrics, despite weak headline return on equity and sharply negative screened earnings growth (−33.7%). The hook is a 5.1% free-cash-flow yield, P/E of 19.8, P/B of 1.4, and Neff PEGY of 0.14, with four of four factor families passing (cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk). The central debate is whether revenue growth, deleveraging, share buybacks, and the announced closure of Melbourne brass operations can restore operating margins after a multi-year decline in operating profit, recurring impairments, and a volatile share price. Primary ASX financial filings are largely absent from the research library—only an FY2026 results webcast notice is indexed—so financial analysis below relies on Yahoo Finance (`financials_annual.json`) with that limitation stated explicitly.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, RWC offers a cash-generative industrial at a modest absolute and sector-relative multiple (composite score 64%, sector-relative 66%) rather than a high-quality compounder. The screen passes five of 22 models—FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, and Financial Health—suggesting the market is pricing in pessimism on growth while the business still converts revenue to cash. Business quality is mixed: revenue has risen from A$1.17bn (FY2022) to A$1.31bn (FY2025) per Yahoo data, and net debt has fallen from A$551m to A$329m over the same period, yet operating income has drifted lower (A$159m → A$134m) and ROE remains subdued at 7.6%. The buy case rests on a valuation floor supported by FCF (A$200m in FY2025 Yahoo cash flow; screen FCF ~A$143m), improving leverage, dividend continuity (1.8% yield; Dividend Growth model pass), and capital return via buybacks (A$39m in FY2025), with re-rating contingent on operating-margin stabilisation and successful restructuring rather than top-line acceleration alone.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Without annual or interim filing body extracts, going-concern, covenant, and contingency language cannot be cited directly; the following risks are inferred from Yahoo financials and news.

- **Operating-margin erosion:** Four-year decline in operating income despite revenue growth suggests cost inflation, mix shift, or integration drag not fully offset by pricing.
- **Goodwill and impairment risk:** Over half of assets are intangible; FY2024–FY2025 impairments (A$16.1m and A$5.5m) indicate ongoing asset write-downs. Further housing-cycle weakness could trigger larger charges.
- **Cyclical/end-market exposure:** Plumbing and fluid-control products are tied to residential and commercial construction; screened earnings growth of −33.7% and share-price underperformance in news flow (investors down ~29% over one year per Oct 2025 headline) reflect cyclical sentiment.
- **Restructuring execution:** The announced closure of brass casting, forging and machining operations in Moorabbin and Braeside, Melbourne (23 Jun 2026) introduces redundancy, transition, and supply-chain risks; savings timing is unverified without filings.
- **Tariff and macro headwinds:** April 2026 news references an FY26 outlook reset amid tariff and Middle East uncertainty; RWC earns a material share of revenue in North America and Europe, exposing it to FX and trade-policy shifts (AUD/USD ~0.70 per macro context is colour only).
- **Leverage and interest:** Although net debt is falling, interest expense remains ~A$25m annually; refinancing at higher rates would pressure net income.
- **Governance/quality signals:** Declining absolute dividends, FY2025 EPS miss, and repeated third-party commentary on "mixed financials" and "unappealing return trends" (Sep–Nov 2025) suggest the market discounts management's ability to restore ROIC—not a governance red flag per se, but a quality concern.
- **Data limitation:** Absence of ASX primary financial filings prevents verification of debt covenants, related-party transactions, or contingent liabilities.
