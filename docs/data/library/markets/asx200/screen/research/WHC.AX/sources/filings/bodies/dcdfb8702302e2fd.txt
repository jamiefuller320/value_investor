Despite the strong performance in the benchmark, in the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers lagged decliners by 128 to 156. Energy (XEJ) (+2.0%) — coal stocks led the sector after New Hope Corp. (NHC) (+5.8%) announced a takeover of unlisted Malabar Coal, giving the whole sub-sector a sharp positive lead. GlobalCoal Newcastle coal futures rose +1.2% to US$156.50/t in the Asian session, and SGX Australian Premium Coking Coal added +0.4% to US$267/t. Yancoal Australia (YAL) (+5.7%) and Whitehaven Coal (WHC) (+5.2%) followed closely behind NHC. S&P-ASX 200 Energy Sector Index intraday chart In other Energy sector moves, ICE benchmark Brent crude oil futures pulled back -2.0% to US$107.58/bbl in Asian trade, after surging 3.7% overnight — but the dip did little to deter capital flowing into oil and gas stocks. Beach Energy (BPT) (+4.0%) and Karoon Energy (KAR) (+3.9%) were the standout movers, with Woodside Energy (WDS) (+2.7%) and Santos (STO) (+2.3%) also adding ground. Financials (XFJ) (+0.6%) got an M&A boost, with Challenger (CGF) (+19.3%) and Fleetpartners (FPR) (+7.1%) surging as takeover activity sparked buying in the sector. Elsewhere, defensive flows supported the major banks, with AMP (AMP) (+3.8%) , National Australia Bank (NAB) (+1.5%) , and ANZ (ANZ) (+1.0%) all advancing — likely reflecting a preference for reliable, income-generating names on a cautious day. S&P-ASX 200 Financials Sector Index intraday chart At the other end of the sector performance table, Information Technology (XIJ) (-1.6%) followed the lead of US tech stocks lower after fresh concerns about global AI spending weighed on the sector overnight. The damage could have been worse: Wisetech Global (WTC) (+1.4%) bucked the trend and provided a partial offset, given its heavy index weight. Still, Megaport (MP1) (-3.5%) , Life360 (360) (-3.1%) , and Catapult Sports (CAT) (-3.1%) were among the hardest hit, followed by Xero (XRO) (-2.4%) and Technology One (TNE) (-1.9%) . S&P-ASX 200 Information Technology Sector Index intraday chart The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (-0.7%) was the next-worst performer, slipping despite COMEX benchmark gold futures rising +0.5% to US$4,204/oz in the Asian session. Emerald Resources (EMR) (-2.7%) and Pantoro Gold (PNR) (-2.7%) were the notable fallers. The large-cap miners held their ground within a flat Materials (XMJ) (+0.0%) sector, as SGX benchmark iron ore futures rose +0.9% to US$91.85/t in the Asian session. Defensive positioning among fund managers also likely helped direct flows toward the sector’s heavyweights. BHP (BHP) (+0.7%) , Fortescue (FMG) (+0.5%) , and Rio Tinto (RIO) (+0.4%) all finished in positive territory. Base metals stocks were a drag, however, despite modestly firmer prices on the London Metals Exchange on Friday — aluminium +0.5%, copper +0.4%, and nickel +1.9%. Further highlighting the disconnect between commodity prices and share prices, COMEX benchmark copper futures added +0.7% to US$6.62/lb in the Asian session. Metals X (MLX) (-3.8%) was the biggest loser, followed by South32 (S32) (-2.6%) , IGO (IGO) (-2.4%) , and Nickel Industries (NIC) (-1.4%) . S&P-ASX 200 Materials Sector Index intraday chart In other commodities moves, lithium stocks continued to struggle despite a sharp +4.7% rebound in GFEX lithium carbonate futures in China to 100,860 CNY/t — a gain that, while welcome, barely registers against a 45% two-month decline in the benchmark contract. Elevra Lithium (ELV) (-5.5%) was the heaviest faller, with Liontown (LTR) (-3.5%) , PLS Group (PLS) (-2.7%) , Core Lithium (CXO) (-2.4%) , and Wildcat Resources (WC8) (-2.2%) all lower.
