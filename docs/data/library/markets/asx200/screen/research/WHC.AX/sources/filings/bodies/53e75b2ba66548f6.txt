Chairman’s Address  
The Hon. Mark Vaile AO 
Ladies and Gentlemen, 
I begin by acknowledging the Traditional Owners of the land on which we meet, the Gadigal people of the 
Eora Nation, and pay my respects to their Elders past and present. I also acknowledge the Gomeroi people of 
New South Wales and the Barada Barna and Gaangulu people of Central Queensland, recognising their 
enduring connection to the lands and waterways where we operate. 
Whitehaven is committed to building constructive and respectful relationships with Traditional Owners and 
Indigenous communities, and to creating enduring benefits for all in the regions in which we operate. 
Transformation realised 
FY26 was an important year of consolidation for Whitehaven. 
Following the acquisition of the Queensland metallurgical coal assets in 2024, FY26 was a year in which the 
benefits of our strategic transformation became increasingly evident. 
With a larger, more diversified portfolio, Whitehaven demonstrated its ability to deliver strong operational and 
financial outcomes through a low point in the commodity price cycle.  
Production and sales were at the top end of guidance, unit costs were at the low end of guidance, and the 
business generated strong cash flows. 
Safety and environmental results continued to improve, while the business demonstrated its resilience in 
responding to significant wet weather in Queensland, higher diesel costs and fuel supply uncertainty. 
Our balance sheet was further strengthened with the successful refinancing of our acquisition debt, 
materially reducing borrowing costs.  
The outcomes delivered in FY26 reinforce the Board's confidence that the strategy we have pursued has 
created a stronger, more diversified and more resilient business. 
Safe and responsible operations 
We were pleased to see the Company further improve its Total Recordable Injury Frequency Rate to 3.3 for 
FY26, representing a record result for the enlarged Whitehaven business. 
The Company also maintained a strong focus on environmental performance and compliance throughout the 
year. 
While no environmental enforcement action events were recorded for FY26, a Penalty Infringement Notice 
relating to an FY25 event was received during the year. While formally attributed to FY25, the matter was 
appropriately considered in determining FY26 remuneration outcomes. Overall, the Board remains encouraged 
by the broader improvement in environmental systems, governance and performance across the business. 
The improving safety and environmental outcomes achieved in recent years reflect the continued 
strengthening of Whitehaven's operating systems and governance frameworks, the integration of the 
Queensland assets and, most importantly, the professionalism and diligence of our people.  
During FY26, Whitehaven published its first climate disclosures aligned with the Australian Sustainability 
Reporting Standards, building on several years of climate-related reporting informed by the Task Force on 
Climate-related Financial Disclosures framework.  
We continue to focus on improving operational efficiency, reducing emissions intensity where practical, and 
meeting our obligations under Australia's Safeguard Mechanism, while recognising the important role our

Delivering value for shareholders 
In FY26, while coal prices remained well below recent highs, Whitehaven generated revenue of $5.4 billion, 
underlying EBITDA of $1.3 billion and underlying NPAT of $227 million. Cash flow from operations was strong 
at approximately $1.1 billion. 
Of the $227 million of underlying NPAT, we are returning approximately 70% to shareholders through a 10 
cent fully franked dividend for the year and an equal amount of around $79 million allocated to our share buy-
back program. 
Through share price appreciation and franked dividends, Whitehaven delivered a Total Shareholder Return of 
41% for the year ended 30 June 2026, ranking 17th amongst ASX 100 companies.  
Over a five-year period to 30 June 2026, shareholders have enjoyed a TSR of 448% placing Whitehaven 
amongst the top five performing companies in the ASX 100 over that period. 
The transformation of Whitehaven over recent years has delivered significant benefits for shareholders. Since 
FY22, equity sales per share have increased substantially through the Queensland acquisition and our ongoing 
share buy-back program, increasing each shareholder's exposure to Whitehaven’s earnings and cash flows. 
Disciplined allocation of capital  
Our Capital Allocation Framework sets out how the Board prioritises capital across four key areas: 
maintaining and optimising our operations, maintaining a strong balance sheet, rewarding shareholders and 
investing in compelling growth opportunities. 
Our target debt gearing and leverage ranges, and payout ratio for shareholders, are clearly defined, and we 
continue to operate comfortably within these ranges through the cycle. 
During FY26, we paid the second US$500 million deferred acquisition payment to BMA, funded by cash set 
aside following the sell-down of Blackwater in March 2025. Following this payment, we finished FY26 with net 
debt of approximately $1.3 billion and liquidity approaching $1.0 billion. 
We successfully refinanced and extended our debt facilities during the year. The refinancing was 
underpinned by investment grade credit ratings for our senior secured debt and strong support from both 
existing and new lenders.  
Whitehaven’s inaugural US$900 million debt placement was oversubscribed by around 12 times and, together 
with a new US$600 million senior secured syndicated bank facility, extended our debt maturities, simplified 
our capital structure and substantially reduced our cost of debt, delivering annualised savings of around 
A$50 to A$55 million. 
As market conditions improve and cash flows strengthen, our financial flexibility also increases. This provides 
greater capacity to deliver returns to shareholders within our 40–60% payout range of underlying NPAT, while 
reducing net debt and moving gearing and leverage towards the lower ends of our targeted ranges. 
Confidence in our markets 
We maintain close relationships with our customers and carefully monitor evolving market conditions, 
regulatory developments and geopolitical influences. While volatility remains a feature of commodity 
markets, the Board remains confident in the long-term outlook for Whitehaven's products. 
Energy security continues to be a critical consideration for governments and electricity markets around the 
world. Global coal demand has proven more resilient than many forecasts anticipated, reflecting coal's 
enduring attributes of reliability, affordability and availability, and the continuing need for secure energy 
supply. Recent revisions to global coal demand forecasts further reinforce this reality. 
We believe these fundamentals will continue to support demand for the high-quality thermal coal Whitehaven 
supplies into key Asian markets. 
In metallurgical coal markets, global steel demand continues to underpin the need for high-quality coking 
coal, while there remains limited visibility of significant new sources of premium seaborne supply over the 
medium term. We therefore expect high-quality metallurgical coal to remain an essential input into global 
steel production for many decades to come.

Remuneration and alignment with shareholders

While base remuneration for executives was held steady in FY26 as part of cost curtailments at the bottom of

environmental responsibilities, delivery of acquisition commitments, cost discipline and the creation of long-
term shareholder value.

Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer’s Address  
Paul Flynn 
Delivering through the cycle 
Thank you Chairman, and thank you to our shareholders for joining us at Whitehaven's Annual General 
Meeting. 
FY26 was a year in which Whitehaven demonstrated the strength and quality of the business we have built 
and our ability to deliver through the cycle. 
Commodity markets remained challenging for much of the year, with both metallurgical and thermal coal 
prices well below recent peaks. In this environment, success is determined by how effectively a company 
manages the factors within its control. 
Our focus was on safety and environmental management, operational excellence, cost discipline, margin 
optimisation and cash generation. Our teams delivered excellent outcomes in FY26 and further strengthened 
the business for the future. 
 
Safety and responsible operations 
As Mark has outlined, we made further progress in safety and environmental performance during FY26, 
including reporting our lowest Total Recordable Injury Frequency Rate of 3.3. 
For me, safe, well-run operations are characterised by disciplined processes, clear accountability and people 
who are engaged in continuously improving the way we work. 
During FY26, we continued to align our approach to safety across our New South Wales and Queensland 
operations and strengthen a shared safety culture across the expanded Whitehaven business. Our 
SafeHaven conference was once again an important opportunity to bring our people leaders together, share 
experiences and reinforce the safety behaviours and standards we expect across the Group. 
Our approach to environmental management is similarly practical and operationally focused. As integration 
of the Queensland operations progressed, we continued to strengthen environmental management systems 
and compliance processes, while investing in operational improvements. This included further investment in 
water management at Blackwater and Daunia, reflecting the importance of effective water management to 
both environmental and operational performance. 
While we are encouraged by the progress made, there is always more to do. We remain focused on 
continuously improving the way we operate, with safety, environmental responsibility and operational 
discipline embedded in how we run the business every day. 
 
Delivering results in challenging conditions 
During FY26, both thermal and metallurgical coal prices hovered at cyclical low levels, particularly in the first 
half of the year but firmed in the second half of the year. In response to challenging market conditions and 
geopolitical uncertainty, our teams remained relentlessly focused on reducing Whitehaven’s cost base and 
improving productivity. 
The $100 million of annualised cost savings delivered in Queensland in FY25 continued to benefit the 
business. In FY26, we delivered a further $65 million of annualised operational savings across our New South 
Wales and Queensland operations, with more than 100 initiatives contributing to the result. These included 
contract renegotiations, optimisation of maintenance strategies, further alignment of organisational 
structure and engineering improvements. 
Further savings were achieved across mining and processing, selling and distribution, and corporate costs, as 
we maintained a disciplined focus on protecting margins in the lower-price environment.

These improvements to our cost base, combined with a higher proportion of lower-cost volumes from New 
South operations, contributed to a reduction in our unit cost of coal from $139 per tonne in FY25 to $132 per 
tonne in FY26. 
This was achieved despite ongoing inflationary pressures and a significant increase in diesel prices during the 
June quarter as a result of the Middle East conflict. Whitehaven consumes around 340 million litres of diesel 
each year, so movements in diesel prices have a meaningful impact on our cost base. Higher diesel prices 
added more than $40 million to our costs in the latter part of FY26, offsetting some of the underlying 
improvement we achieved across the business. 
Importantly, every dollar of sustainable cost reduction and productivity improvement strengthens our 
resilience and increases our leverage to higher prices when market conditions improve. 
Operationally, managed ROM production increased 3% to 40.3 million tonnes and managed sales of 
produced coal increased 8% to 32.7 million tonnes, both at the top end of our guidance range. 
Our Queensland and New South Wales operations both delivered strong production and sales outcomes. 
Managed ROM production was split evenly, with 20.1 million tonnes from Queensland and 20.2 million tonnes 
from New South Wales, while managed sales were 15.9 million tonnes from Queensland and 16.8 million 
tonnes from New South Wales.  
These outcomes were particularly pleasing given significant wet weather in Queensland during the March 
quarter, from which our operations recovered strongly in the June quarter. In addition, at our underground 
mine at Narrabri, challenging mining conditions constrained production, particularly in the second half.  
Revenue of $5.4 billion was underpinned by strong sales volumes and an average achieved coal price of $202 
per tonne, 6% lower than FY25 due to softer coal markets and adverse exchange rate movements.  
Our FY26 revenue mix was 57% metallurgical coal and 43% thermal coal1, providing diversified exposure to 
both steelmaking and energy markets. 
The strength of our diversified portfolio and disciplined focus on costs enabled Whitehaven to generate 
underlying EBITDA of $1.3 billion and underlying NPAT of $227 million. 
 
Creating long-term value  
Mining makes a significant contribution to regional Australia through employment, local procurement, 
infrastructure investment, royalties and taxes. 
Throughout FY26 we continued investing in our communities, supporting Indigenous participation, partnering 
with local suppliers and contributing to programs that strengthen the regions in which we operate. 
Whitehaven spent $2.9 billion with suppliers in regional NSW and regional Queensland in FY26, and we paid 
and collected $800 million in taxes and royalties. 
We are very proud of the role our people play in creating economic opportunity and supporting the prosperity 
of communities across New South Wales and Queensland. 
A major source of long-term value for Whitehaven is our organic growth projects. 
We continue to advance the Vickery Extension Project in New South Wales with work progressing to review 
project scope, capital requirements and funding options. At Winchester South in Queensland, the external 
approvals process continues to progress with the recent recommendation for approval by the Queensland 
Land Court being a meaningful step forward. 
Both development projects have the potential to generate significant future value for shareholders and 
continue to attract strong customer support.

FY27 outlook and beyond 
Looking ahead, we enter FY27 in a position of strength. Our business is performing well, and market 
conditions have improved, with stronger pricing across both thermal and metallurgical coal markets. 
In the first quarter of FY27, the gC Newc Index, which underpins our thermal coal prices, averaged US$135 per 
tonne compared with an average of US$118 per tonne in FY26, and the PLV HCC Index averaged US$248 per 
tonne for the September quarter compared with the 12-month average of US$214 per tonne for FY26.  
These stronger pricing conditions support higher revenues and earnings and will allow us to reduce net debt. 
The three-year contingent payment period for the BMA acquisition ends in April 2027. The first two annual 
payments were US$9 million and US$58 million, reflecting lower metallurgical coal prices. Based on current 
forward prices, we expect the final payment to be materially higher, subject to an annual cap of US$350 
million.  
That final contingent payment is to be made in July 2027, having already paid the final deferred consideration 
payment of US$100 million in April 2027. This will complete the repayment of the vendor finance for the 
Queensland acquisition. This will be an important milestone for Whitehaven, with the full benefit of future 
cash flows from the Queensland operations accruing to Whitehaven from that point onwards. 
In August, we released our FY27 production, sales and cost guidance. Our objective is to continue delivering 
improvement across the business and to perform towards the top end of our production and sales guidance 
ranges. At the same time, our guidance recognises the inherent variability of mining, including mine 
sequencing, weather and input cost volatility. 
With our first quarter of production reported two weeks ago, we have had a good start to the FY27 year. We 
have seen strong production from both Queensland and New South Wales, and sales were also solid.  This 
was despite ship loader interruptions at the RG Tanna terminal in Queensland and at PWCS in New South 
Wales resulting in some sales slipping into the December quarter. 
FY27 sees a nine-week longwall move at Narrabri, which is scheduled for the second half of the financial year. 
During this move we will complete an overhaul of the longwall hydraulics and roof-supports. Following this 
work, we expect improved longwall reliability and more consistent performance. 
Our unit cost guidance range of $132 to $147 per tonne reflects the guided production range, together with 
inflationary pressures and the expectation that higher diesel costs will continue to impact during FY27. 
Following the first quarter, unit costs are tracking at the low end of the guidance range, supported by strong 
September quarter volumes and focused cost controls. This is a good position to be in ahead of the 
Queensland wet season and ahead of the extended longwall move at Narrabri.  
Consistent with our track record, we will remain focused on the factors within our control, prioritising safety, 
maintaining strong cost discipline and optimising operational performance. 
Beyond FY27, we remain confident that high-quality coal producers with large, low-cost and long-life assets 
will continue to play an essential role in global energy and industrial systems for decades to come. We 
continue to see strong demand from customers for the premium products we supply.
