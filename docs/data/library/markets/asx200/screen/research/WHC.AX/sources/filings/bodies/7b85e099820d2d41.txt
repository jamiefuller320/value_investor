Managing Director and CEO’s message
At Whitehaven, our mission is to help the 
world progress. The coal we produce supports 
economic and industrial development, and 
our operations contribute to the prosperity of 
regional communities across Australia. These 
outcomes are built on our people, responsible 
environmental management and strong 
relationships with our communities.
FY26 was an important year for our business as we 
continued to integrate our expanded portfolio of 
operations and strengthen Whitehaven’s position as a 
leading Australian coal producer. Throughout the year, 
we remained focused on operating safely, efficiently 
and responsibly, while also creating value for our many 
stakeholders. 
This year also marks the first release of our standalone 
People, Environment & Communities Report (Report). 
This change reflects the introduction of the new
Australian Sustainability Reporting Standards, with 
Whitehaven’s Sustainability Report and climate-related 
financial disclosures now part of our Annual Report.
Our people 
Central to our success are our people. Their safety 
remains our highest priority, and we are committed to 
ensuring everyone returns home safely every day. 
During FY26, Whitehaven recorded a Total Recordable 
Injury Frequency Rate (TRIFR) of 3.3, compared with 4.6 
in FY25 and a five-year average of 4.7 across Whitehaven 
and our acquired Queensland operations for employees 
and contractors. While encouraged by this improvement, 
we remain focused on continually strengthening our 
safety culture and performance.
Our people play a vital role in shaping the culture 
and capability of our business. Employee feedback 
throughout the year reflected a strong sense of 
teamwork, pride and purpose across our operations. 
We aim to foster an inclusive workplace where 
opportunities are earned through capability and 
performance. Female representation remained above 
21% across the workforce, with women holding 27.3% of 
leadership positions. Indigenous Australians represented 
10% of our workforce, including strong representation at 
our Maules Creek and Vickery operations, at 15.6% and 
16.7%, respectively.
Environmental management 
Alongside our focus on people, responsible environmental 
management remains fundamental to how we operate. 
In FY26, we maintained a disciplined approach to 
environmental performance, with a focus on compliance, 
assurance and continuous improvement. 
We recorded no environmental enforcement action (EEA) 
events during the year, reflecting the concerted efforts 
of our employees and contractors across the business.¹ 
Communities
Beyond our operations, we recognise the important 
role mining plays in supporting local communities and 
creating opportunities in the regions where we operate.
In FY26, Whitehaven contributed approximately $2.9 billion 
to local suppliers in Central Queensland, North West New 
South Wales (NSW) and the Hunter and Newcastle regions 
through procurement. In addition, we paid $379 million 
in regional Queensland and NSW employee wages and 
salaries, and approximately $1.6 million in community 
partnerships and donations. This contribution reflects 
the important role our operations play in supporting 
jobs, local businesses and economic activity across 
regional Australia.
We also continue to work with Traditional Owners and 
Indigenous communities to support initiatives that 
contribute to stronger families, communities and 
future opportunities. In particular, we remain a national 
partner of the Clontarf Foundation and support the Stars 
Foundation mentoring program to help young Indigenous 
people stay engaged in education. 
Conclusion 
We enter FY27 with a continued focus on safety, responsible 
stewardship of the environment and helping local and 
regional communities thrive. These priorities are fundamental 
to the long-term value we create for all our stakeholders.
I thank our employees and contractors for their 
commitment and contribution throughout the year, 
and our shareholders for their continued support and 
confidence in Whitehaven.
 
Paul Flynn
Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer

Protecting the safety of our people and 
workplaces, and the communities around us is our 
first priority. We believe our people should expect 
to go home safe and healthy after work every day.
In FY26, the expanded business achieved a record safety 
performance demonstrating the positive impact of our health 
and safety initiatives. The total recordable injury frequency 
rate (TRIFR) was 3.3 – reflecting a 30% improvement 
compared with a five-year average of 4.7. We continue to 
report against a five-year average metric as it provides a 
meaningful and comparable view of performance trends 
by allowing the time needed to embed initiatives, integrate 
newly acquired sites and harmonise safety practices. 
Strengthening our safety culture through a revised Field 
Safety Leadership (FSL) program remained a key priority in 
FY26. Leaders undertook more than 74,000 field leadership 
activities, engaging directly with teams to reinforce safe 
behaviours and support positive safety outcomes.
Our operations also completed more than 700 critical 
control verifications, with approximately one in four of these 
activities identifying an opportunity to strengthen safety 
controls. This proactive identification of improvement 
opportunities is viewed positively by the organisation, as 
it enables operations to reduce the risk of incidents before 
they occur. 
In FY26, our near miss frequency rate – which measures events 
that did not result in a fatality or permanent disabling injury but 
had the potential to do so – improved by 45%. These results 
suggest that safety initiatives implemented are supporting 
our teams to better identify and avoid high-potential hazards. 
We continue to encourage reporting to capture insights.

Case study 
Field Safety Leadership program 
Whitehaven implemented a revised FSL program to 
continue to strengthen our safety performance and 
support our broader HSE strategy. The initiative was 
designed to align FSL practices, improve the quality 
of leadership interactions, and move from high 
volumes of activity to a more targeted, insight-driven 
safety focus. 
The approach focused on simplifying and 
standardising the field safety leadership processes 
across operations. Legacy safety activities were 
consolidated into two core practices, Safehaven 
Conversations and Safehaven Verifications enabling 
leaders to spend more time in the field engaging 
with workers on material risks, behaviours and 
controls. This shift improved consistency while 
reinforcing a culture of care and accountability. 
To build capability, we introduced structured training 
and onboarding initiatives, with more than 400 
leaders completing the training during the transition. 
These efforts were supported by workshops and 
on-site coaching, helping leaders develop the skills 
needed to deliver higher quality safety interactions 
and embed the new approach across all operations. 
A key enabler of the program was the 
implementation of the Whitehaven 360 digital 
platform, which centralised the recording of safety 
interactions and enhanced access to real-time data 
and insights. This allowed the organisation to shift 
from tracking activity volumes to understanding 
trends in at-risk behaviours, hazard exposure and 
corrective actions.
Early results demonstrate strong adoption and 
engagement, with 38,765 Safehaven conversations 
conducted in FY26 following commencement of 
the program on 1 October 2025. The improved data 
visibility has provided a baseline for measuring 
performance and identifying priority areas, 
particularly in relation to injury-related hazards 
and material risk behaviours.
Overall, the FSL program is strengthening safety 
culture, improving risk management and enabling 
more informed decision-making. As the program 
matures, we aim to further enhance coaching, refine 
performance metrics and link safety insights to 
broader operational outcomes.

The Safehaven Conference 
The Safehaven Conference is an annual Whitehaven event that 
strengthens collaboration across key health and safety priorities 
while supporting alignment with our HSE strategy and building 
leadership capability.
Held in September 2025 under the theme “Leadership in 
Action”, the conference brought together leaders, operational 
personnel and contract partners to share insights, reflect 
on performance, and drive continuous improvement in 
safety outcomes. It reinforced our commitment to achieving 
excellence through visible, effective leadership.
The program combined senior leadership updates, keynote 
presentations, operational case studies and interactive 
discussions to translate strategy into practical action.
Keynote speakers included Dr Ali Walker, who explored how human 
connection influences leadership and safety outcomes, and Shane 
Fitzsimmons AO AFSM, who shared insights on leadership and 
resilience drawn from large-scale crisis response.
Operational leaders showcased practical safety improvements, 
including strengthening risk assessment quality, enhancing 
contractor performance, delivering complex operations, and 
driving field-based leadership to improve culture.
The conference also featured company performance updates, 
future HSE commitments, and an executive panel discussion, 
along with opportunities for networking and cross-site collaboration.
Held in Gunnedah, the event continues to provide a valuable forum 
to connect leaders, share learnings, and reinforce accountability 
for proactive safety leadership and continuous improvement.

Governance and risk management 
Our approach to tailings storage facility (TSF) 
management is supported by sound governance 
and effective risk management. 
Although the likelihood of a TSF failure is low, we 
recognise the potentially significant consequences 
for people and the environment of such a failure. We 
take these potential consequences seriously through 
robust governance, ongoing monitoring and proactive 
management across the full TSF life cycle.
Our risk management framework is designed to ensure 
the safety and integrity of TSFs throughout all stages 
of their life cycle, including construction, operation, 
monitoring, maintenance and closure. TSF risks 
are systematically identified, assessed, controlled 
and monitored through site-specific standards 
and procedures, which establish clear roles and 
responsibilities to help ensure appropriately  
qualified personnel undertake activities.
Managing the integrity of TSFs 
Whitehaven operates eight facilities that meet the 
definition of a TSF under the GISTM, including six 
at Blackwater Mine and two at the Gunnedah Coal 
Handling and Preparation Plant (CHPP). 
Through the proactive work of our teams, Whitehaven 
has identified and formally classified the Bottom Dam 
at South Blackwater Mine as a legacy TSF. The facility 
dates back to the early Kenmare operations and predates 
BHP’s ownership, reflecting the site’s long operational 
history. Since its identification, extensive geotechnical 
investigations have been undertaken to better 
understand the volume and characteristics of material 
stored within the facility. Monitoring is underway, and 
the facility is now overseen by the Engineer of Record 
and the Independent Tailings Review Board (ITRB). Work 
is continuing to bring this legacy facility into alignment 
with the GISTM.
We operate two TSF emplacement areas at the Gunnedah 
CHPP. Each TSF area contains five dry-stack cells, where 
fine rejects are drained, consolidated, and returned to site 
to be commingled with mine waste rock.
Traditional TSFs are not used at Daunia and Maules Creek. 
Instead, we use non-storage methods that co-dispose 
mixed plant rejects with mine waste, eliminating the need 
for separate TSFs. 
Only one TSF, the North Coal Preparation Plant at 
Blackwater, has a dam failure consequence classification 
of ‘very high’ under the GISTM. All other TSFs at 
Blackwater have a significant or low rating. 
The current active TSF at Blackwater (Boonie Doon) has 
low to very low hazard categorisation. This TSF receives 
tailings via slurry discharge points into in-pit tailings. 
Our management structure at the Blackwater Mine 
aligns with the GISTM and ANCOLD guidelines, and 
includes a Dam Owner, Responsible Dam Engineer, 
and Engineer of Record, which is a specialist external 
engineer. These roles are responsible for overseeing 
the safe operation and integrity of the TSFs and report 
through the accountable executive, Whitehaven’s Chief 
Operating Officer, who reports to the Managing Director 
and Chief Executive Officer. 
The ITRB provides external oversight of the 
Blackwater TSFs. The ITRB conducts periodic 
reviews of TSF design, operation, and performance 
and provides recommendations to support the 
continuous improvement in tailings governance 
and management practices. 
In May 2026, the ITRB completed its latest review 
of the Blackwater TSFs, providing a number of 
recommendations focused on governance, monitoring 
and maintenance to support ongoing improvements. No 
critical defects were identified at the time of the review. 
Comprehensive performance monitoring programs are 
in place for each TSF at Blackwater, detailing the specific 
inspections, reviews, and monitoring requirements 
aligned to the consequence classification for each 
facility. Geotechnical, geochemical, hydrological and 
environmental monitoring data is used to inform 
operational decision-making and the development of 
Trigger Action Response Plans (TARPs). 
Our Emergency Response Plans include provisions for 
tailings-related hazards and potential failure scenarios, 
with escalating response requirements defined through 
Emergency Action Plans and TARPs. Where appropriate, 
the plans have been developed in consultation with 
external emergency service providers and are reviewed by 
the Responsible Dam Engineer and Engineer of Record. 
These plans are integrated into our Site Emergency 
Response Plan.

Tax transparency
Whitehaven strives to maintain open, honest, 
transparent, and cooperative relationships 
with all taxation and revenue authorities.
Whitehaven is listed on the Australian Securities Exchange 
and has mining operations located in New South Wales and 
Queensland. We pay various taxes to federal, state, and 
local governments, and collect various tax payments on 
behalf of federal and state governments. 
Despite having no foreign subsidiaries or operations, the 
Group is subject to the Public country-by-country (CBC) 
Reporting requirements, which first applied in FY25, with 
reporting lodged in FY26. Under the Public CBC reporting 
regime, the Group must disclose jurisdiction-specific 
information to the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) on its 
global economic activities, including revenue, profit, tax 
and business operations, to increase tax transparency. 
In addition, during FY26, the Group was subject to the 
Private CBC reporting requirements administered by the 
ATO, including the requirement to lodge a master file and 
local file for FY25. Up to FY24, the Group was exempt 
from the Private CBC reporting by the ATO; however, 
this exemption was no longer available for FY25 and 
future income years even though the Group does not 
have any foreign subsidiaries or operations.
Tax governance and approach 
We adopt a conservative approach concerning tax. 
Our culture of compliance and transparency is apparent 
in our policies, strategies, resources, procedures and 
controls, and in our constructive relationships with tax 
authorities, including the ATO, state revenue offices, 
and local governments. 
Comprehensive Board oversight of tax matters is 
provided through the Audit and Risk Management 
Committee. A tax governance and tax risk management 
framework outlines the management and resourcing of 
our tax functions across the business in respect of all 
taxes payable and collected.
The framework has been prepared with reference to the 
ATO’s public guidance, which seeks higher levels of tax 
transparency, governance, and accountability across 
all taxes for Australian taxpayers, and formalises and 
captures our prudent approach to taxation matters. 
We maintain management systems and resources to:
• actively monitor, identify and manage tax risk 
• comply with Australian taxation laws and reporting 
standards 
• lodge all taxation returns and documentation on time 
• collect and pay the calculated amounts of federal, 
state and local taxes, royalties, levies, duties, rates, 
and imposts when they fall due 
• maintain, with each revenue authority, Whitehaven’s 
standing as an organisation of integrity.
Taxes paid 
In FY26, Whitehaven and its JV partners contributed 
$0.8 billion to federal, state, and local governments in 
the form of income tax, royalties, payroll tax, and other 
taxes. Coal royalties in New South Wales and Queensland 
made up the largest component of taxes paid. 
Coal royalties to the Queensland Government represent 
the largest single component of taxes paid. Queensland 
continues to have the world’s highest coal royalties 
following the change to the royalty regime in 2022. 
Combined with income taxes, Whitehaven’s adjusted 
effective tax rate for profits derived in Queensland, 
including royalties, is approximately 62%.
Taxes collected
The largest single category of taxes we collect is on 
behalf of the Australian Government in respect of 
employees. This includes PAYG withholding tax from 
employees’ salaries and wages. In FY26, the Group 
collected and remitted $210 million in employee payroll 
taxes or PAYG to the Australian Government.
