Overview 
Genesis Minerals Limited (ASX: GMD) delivered a solid start to FY27 with quarterly production of 72,107oz at an AISC 
of A$2,966/oz. Full year “stand-alone” guidance of 270 - 300,000oz at an AISC of A$2,750 - 3,050/oz is maintained. 
Recent hallmarks of rising Genesis production, disciplined cost control and an accommodative gold price continue 
to deliver substantial cash flow, notwithstanding a strong commitment to investment in future growth.

Operations 
Genesis produced 72.1 koz for the quarter ( June quarter 70.8koz), generating A$213 m of mine operating cash 
flow.  
Net mine cash flow was A$ 162m after growth capital of A$ 51m. Key growth capital items included mine 
development and site infrastructure of A$31m at Tower Hill and A$11m at Bruno Lewis. 
Mining 
At Gwalia underground, 27.4koz at 4.9g/t was mined (June quarter 29.5koz at 6.6g/t). 
Ore delivery was 173kt ( June quarter 139kt), a 25% uplift on the previous period and highlighting Byrnecut’ s 
continued ramp up since May. Mined grade reflected increased weighting of planned lower-grade stopes in the 
mid-levels whilst the coveted “Heart of Gold” stoping sequence moved through paste fill and production drilling 
cycles in the first six weeks of the quarter. The December quarter is expected to re-align with Reserve grade. 
Total development advance for the period was 731m (June quarter 486m), delivering a 50% uplift and the highest 
lateral advance performance since 2024. 
At Ulysses underground, a record 17.4koz at 3.8g/t was mined (June quarter 12.8koz at 3.4g/t). 
Ore delivery was 144kt (June quarter 116kt), representing the highest ore tonnes since project commencement in 
March 2024. Refined narrow vein stope designs and drill and blast execution delivered improved dilution control 
and ensured consistent grade performance throughout the September quarter.

Total development advance for the period was 1,586m (June quarter 1,319m), a 20% uplift on the previous

At Admiral open pit, 15.9koz at 1.2g/t was mined (June quarter 15.1koz at 1.0g/t), with 398kt of ore mined (June

Further upside on total material movement was constrained by significant wet weather during the period. 
At Hub open pit, 9.9koz at 3.1g/t was mined (June quarter 12.9koz at 2.8g/t), with 100kt of ore mined (June quarter 
143kt) from 564 kt of total material movement ( June quarter 2, 002kt). Lower total volume is reflective of the 
narrowing nature of the mine as it approached final pit floor.  
Mining of the high-grade Hub open pit was completed ahead of schedule in early September, and closure and 
rehabilitation works immediately commenced. This enabled commencement of mobilisation of Genesis Mining 
Services ( “GMS”) fleet and pe rsonnel to Bruno Lewis open pit, which is on track to provide first ore in the 
December quarter. 
At Jupiter open pit, a record 13.1koz at 0.7g/t was mined (June quarter 11.1koz at 0.7g/t). 
The GMS team snared consecutive record performances with 550 kt of ore mined (June quarter 480kt) and total 
material movement of 3,667kt (June quarter 3,617kt). 
Processing 
The Leonora mill processed 368kt at 4.1g/t (June quarter 354kt at 4.4g/t) with a metallurgical recovery of 91.9% 
(June quarter 92.4%), producing 44.1koz.  
The Laverton mill processed 756kt at 1.2g/t (June quarter 761kt at 1.1g/t) with a metallurgical recovery of 92.7% 
(June quarter 92.8%), producing 27.9koz.  
At quarter end, closing ore stockpiles (awaiting processing) rose to 2.0Mt at 0.9 g/t for 5 7koz*, representing 
~A$313 million of latent revenue at the spot gold price and assuming 93% recovery, with sunk mining costs.

Fu
el supply commentary 
Operations remain unimpacted by diesel supply disruptions stemming from the ongoing conflict in the Middle 
East, other than bearing elevated diesel prices.  
Genesis’ diesel is sourced from major importers under long term supply contracts. 
FY27 cost guidance assumes a diesel price of A$1.60 per litre (net of rebate), representing ~5% of total costs. The 
spot price is ~A$1.76 per litre (net of rebate). AISC sensitivity is ~A$11/oz per A10c diesel price movement. 
In the event of any diesel disruption, the impact to Genesis’ production can potentially be mitigated by: 
► Leonora and Laverton mills powered by Western Australian natural gas
► Ability to prio ritise total surface ore stockpiles of >3. 4Mt (including Laverton legacy stockpile) (~ 77% of
4.4Mtpa milling capacity)
► Ability to prioritise high grade underground ore (less diesel intensive per ounce) from Gwalia and Ulysses

FY27 exploration bolstered; Results update later in December quarter 
Genesis’ FY27 A$80- 90m exploration effort commenced during the quarter , with A$19.9m invested. The 
expanded FY27 budget matches the exploration spend of the entire preceding three  years (FY24-FY26 A$85m 
combined). The significant increase is motivated by portfolio- wide drilling success in FY26; For example, 21 
intercepts exceeding 100 gram-metres were reported in the June quarter 2026 alone (ASX announcements 5th May 
2026 "Outstanding Leonora drilling paves the way for more organic growth" and 26th June 2026 "Drilling builds case

Lewis (shallow extensional opportunities ). The underexplored Chatterbox Trend also commands attention , 
specifically “first cab off the rank”  Beasley Creek (infill and extensional), the recently acquired Lady Julie North 
project (infill) plus the undrilled gap between Lady Julie North and Rumour.

APPENDIX B - Merger Accounting 
Subject to shareholder and Court approval, upon implementation of the merger with Vault Minerals by way of a 
Vault Scheme of Arrangement, Vault's earnings will be included in Genesis' consolidated financial results. 
Accordingly, based on the expected implementation date of 26 November 2026, approximately one month of Vault 
earnings are expected to be included in Genesis' financial results for the half-year ending 31st December 2026.  
The deemed consideration for the transaction will be determined with reference to the Genesis shares issued and 
cash consideration under the Scheme. The consideration is expected to exceed the carrying value of Vault's net 
assets and will therefore require Genesis to undertake a “purchase price allocation (“PPA”) process to determine 
the fair value of the acquired assets and liabilities.  
The acquisition is expected to have several impacts on Genesis' future statutory financial results, including: 
► A h igher depreciation and amortisation expense arising from fair value uplifts recognised against acquired
mine properties and plant and equipment.
► Fair value uplifts will also be attributed to acquired ore stockpiles and gold inventories, and as such, additional
non-cash inventory charges will be recognised as these are processed and sold.
► These additional non-cash charges will likely result in reduced statutory NPAT and EBIT Margins compared to
the current margins of the standalone business.
► Transaction-related expenses, including stamp duty and other acquisition-related costs, will be recognised in 
the profit or loss as the transaction will be deemed  a “Business Combination” (rather than an “Asset
Acquisition”) in accordance with Australian Accounting Standards.
Until the valuations required to determine the fair value of the acquired assets and liabilities are complete, the 
size and timing of these impacts cannot be reliably estimated. The  Company has 12 months to finalise this 
complex process, h owever, Genesis will target having  p rovisional PPA values available to incorporate into its 
financial statements for the 6 months ending 31st December 2026. 
While the provisional effects of the business combination have not yet been determined, the requirement to 
allocate the deemed consideration, will result in a significant increase in net assets of the Group and changes to 
subsequent profit or loss. Accordingly, any financial ratios and metrics prepared using Genesis’ financial 
statements will materially change as a result of the merger.
