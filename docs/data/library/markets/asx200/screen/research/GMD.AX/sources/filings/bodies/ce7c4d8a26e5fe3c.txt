Despite the slip at benchmark level, in the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers beat decliners by a modest 159 to 125. Information Technology (XIJ) (+2.4%) caught a strong bid as the AI trade reignited — Anthropic’s landmark IPO filing and Meta’s announcement of a US$95 billion stock offering to fund artificial intelligence infrastructure sparked a chipmaker rally overnight that extended into local tech stocks. Appen (APX) (+9.0%) and Data#3 (DTL) (+7.8%) were the standout movers, with Megaport (MP1) (+5.1%) and WiseTech Global (WTC) (+1.5%) also advancing. The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (+2.6%) led the session as gold stocks rallied on an overnight gain in the gold price, with COMEX benchmark gold futures rising 0.9% overnight before conceding most of that gain in Asian trade — easing back -0.8% to US$4,043/oz. West African Resources (WAF) (+5.3%) and Pantoro Gold (PNR) (+5.0%) led the advance, with Kingsgate Consolidated (KCN) (+4.8%) and Westgold Resources (WGX) (+4.5%) also advancing. Materials (XMJ) (+1.1%) advanced as AI-driven copper demand sentiment continued to support the sector — COMEX benchmark copper futures rose 1.7% overnight before going flat in the Asian session at US$6.647/lb — with rare earths and copper stocks among the strongest performers. Arafura Rare Earths (ARU) (+10.3%) and Aurelia Metals (AMI) (+10.3%) led the charge, followed by Lynas Rare Earths (LYC) (+7.3%) and Sandfire Resources (SFR) (+4.8%) . Lithium stocks also made headway — Pilbara Minerals (PLS) (+3.8%) rose after reporting record production in the September quarter, even as GFEX lithium carbonate futures in China fell -5.1% to CNY93,520/t . Today's mining sector trade started in New York and cared little about actual commodity price moves... 🤔 S&P/ASX 200 Materials Sector Index (XMJ) intraday chart Energy (XEJ) (-2.5%) was the session’s worst-performing sector as oil prices fell overnight before recovering in Asian trade — ICE benchmark Brent crude oil futures dropping 1.2% overnight before bouncing +1.6% to US$111.15/bbl in Asia — and SGX Australian Premium Coking Coal futures shed -1.7% to US$155.05/t. Oil and gas stocks bore the brunt: Woodside Energy (WDS) (-3.2%) and Santos (STO) (-1.7%) were both lower, while fuel retailer Ampol (ALD) (-3.8%) fell sharply. Coal stocks followed: New Hope Corp (NHC) (-3.9%) and Yancoal Australia (YAL) (-3.7%) gave ground. Uranium was the sole sub-sector to buck the trend, surging after the Sprott Uranium Miners ETF — a widely tracked measure of international uranium stocks — jumped 5.2% overnight on the reignited AI trade, which has boosted the long-term demand case for nuclear power. Boss Energy (BOE) (+10.0%) , Bannerman Energy (BMN) (+9.3%) , Deep Yellow (DYL) (+7.4%) , and Paladin Energy (PDN) (+4.9%) all surged. Consumer Discretionary (XDJ) (-1.5%) fell sharply on the inflation data, with traders pricing in an 87% probability of a fifth RBA rate rise to 4.85% on Melbourne Cup day — raising fears that higher borrowing costs and a slowing housing market will further dampen consumer spending. Universal Store (UNI) (-3.8%) and Super Retail Group (SUL) (-3.2%) led the falls, with JB Hi-Fi (JBH) (-2.3%) and Wesfarmers (WES) (-2.1%) also lower. S&P/ASX 200 Consumer Discretionary Sector Index (XDJ) intraday chart Utilities (XUJ) (-1.2%) were hit by a combination of the ongoing rotation out of defensives and the prospect of rising rates. APA Group (APA) (-2.2%) , AGL Energy (AGL) (-1.1%) , and Origin Energy (ORG) (-0.6%) all declined. Communication Services (XTJ) (-1.1%) tracked weakness in major defensive earner Telstra (TLS) (-1.2%) , which weighed on the sector. Consumer Staples (XSJ) (-0.7%) faced a double headwind — hot inflation raising input cost concerns and the ongoing rotation away from defensives. Metcash (MTS) (-1.2%) and Coles Group (COL) (-1.2%) led the falls, with Woolworths (WOW) (-0.5%) also lower.
