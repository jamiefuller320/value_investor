In the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers snuck past decliners by a narrow 143 to 140. The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (+4.2%) was the standout today, and the one corner of mining where the commodity genuinely backed share price moves. COMEX gold futures rose 1.4% to US$4,413.0/oz and COMEX silver futures rose 1.6% to US$52.78/oz in Asian trade. Black Cat Syndicate (BC8) (+8.9%) and Catalyst Metals (CYL) (+8.8%) led the charge. Genesis Minerals (GMD) (+6.4%) and Ora Banda Mining (OBM) (+6.3%) also rallied. Materials (XMJ) (+2.9%) was the engine room of a classic rob-Peter-to-pay-Paul session, as money pulled from banks and defensives flowed back into miners. A modest recovery in the AI trade on Wall Street on Friday revived stocks sold off last week on fears an AI infrastructure slowdown would hit copper. But investors must ask the awkward question: outside of gold, and a small gain in rare earths prices, commodities prices didn’t back today's move. S&P/ASX 200 Materials Sector Index (XMJ) intraday chart Base metals stocks were the clearest example. Aluminium, copper, tin and zinc all fell sharply on the London Metal Exchange on Friday, down 1.4%, 1.1%, 3.7% and 3% respectively, though COMEX copper futures rose 1.8% to US$6.35/lb in the Asian session. Sandfire Resources (SFR) (+5.7%) and IGO (IGO) (+4.8%) led. South32 (S32) (+3.4%) rallied. The big diversified miners did the heavy lifting for the index, even with SGX iron ore futures barely moving, up 0.1% to US$92.75/t. BHP Group (BHP) (+2.7%) and Rio Tinto (RIO) (+1.6%) were likely helped by their copper exposure. Mineral Resources (MIN) (+4.8%) outpaced both. Rare earths stocks also surged, with NdPr prices in China up just 0.3% to CNY 707,000/t, a gain that hardly explains the size of the moves in Lynas Rare Earths (LYC) (+6.8%) and Iluka Resources (ILU) (+4.6%) . Lithium stocks were the most glaring disconnect. GFEX lithium carbonate futures in China slumped 4.8% to CNY 88,400/t, yet investors piled into beaten-down energy transition plays regardless. Core Lithium (CXO) (+8.7%) and Elevra Lithium (ELV) (+7.2%) led. Liontown (LTR) (+4.7%) and Vulcan Energy Resources (VUL) (+2.7%) followed, while PLS Group (PLS) (+2.1%) also firmed. Information Technology (XIJ) (+0.9%) took its lead from Friday’s rebound in the Nasdaq, as dip buyers returned to US tech. SiteMinder (SDR) (+4.4%) and Codan (CDA) (+2.9%) led, the latter after announcing the $244 million acquisition of US-based Eventide Communications. Catapult Sports (CAT) (+2.2%) and WiseTech Global (WTC) (+1.8%) also gained, while Megaport (MP1) (+1.6%) rounded out the winners. Energy (XEJ) (+0.7%) also rose despite weaker commodity leads, with the gains concentrated in uranium and coal. Uranium stocks surged as investors hunted beaten-down AI and energy transition plays, likely tracking their US counterparts after the Sprott Uranium Miners ETF (URNM) rose 3% in New York on Friday. Paladin Energy (PDN) (+10.9%) and Boss Energy (BOE) (+10.7%) led. Deep Yellow (DYL) (+10.6%) and NexGen Energy (NXG) (+7.3%) followed, while Bannerman Energy (BMN) (+5.7%) also rallied. COMEX uranium futures were steady, down 0.1% to US$91.75/lb. Coal stocks gained even as prices fell, with globalCoal Newcastle coal futures down 2.8% to US$142/t and SGX Australian premium coking coal futures down 1.1% to US$255/t. Yancoal Australia (YAL) (+2.7%) and Whitehaven Coal (WHC) (+2.3%) led. New Hope Corporation (NHC) (+1.9%) also rose. Oil and gas stocks were mixed as ICE Brent crude futures fell 1.0% to US$97.38/bbl. Santos (STO) (+0.7%) led the majors, while Woodside Energy Group (WDS) (-0.1%) slipped. A-REIT (XPJ) (-1.3%) was the worst-performing sector as higher bond yields, after a stronger-than-expected US non-farm payrolls report, weighed on rate-sensitive stocks. Property trusts are bond proxies, prized for steady income, so rising bond yields make them relatively less attractive. The selling also unwound some of last week’s rally on hopes Australian rates had peaked. Lendlease Group (LLC) (-5.4%) was the heaviest drag after terminating its Milan project sale, forgoing $90 million in anticipated proceeds. Scentre Group (SCG) (-4.4%) , a landlord to the collapsed David Jones, also slid. Stockland (SGP) (-3.5%) and GPT Group (GPT) (-3.5%) also fell sharply. Consumer Discretionary (XDJ) (-1.1%) was also caught by higher bond yields. Cettire (CTT) (-7.9%) and Eagers Automotive (APE) (-5.0%) led the declines. Myer Holdings (MYR) (-4.0%) and Nick Scali (NCK) (-3.0%) also fell. Financials (XFJ) (-1.1%) were hit by higher yields and a stumbling bank reporting season, with bad debts creeping higher. ANZ Group Holdings (ANZ) (-3.2%) was the heaviest drag despite a 23% jump in full-year net profit, and the pullback likely pushed capital out of banks and towards resources. National Australia Bank (NAB) (-1.4%) , which disappointed last week, also fell after appointing a new chairman. S&P/ASX 200 Financials Sector Index (XFJ) intraday chart The regional lenders fared worse, with Bendigo and Adelaide Bank (BEN) (-2.1%) and Bank of Queensland (BOQ) (-1.7%) lower. Insurer QBE Insurance Group (QBE) (-1.6%) also retreated. Communication Services (XTJ) (-1.0%) lacked support in the risk-on environment, with utility-like telcos reversing some of their recent strength. Superloop (SLC) (-2.5%) and Chorus (CNU) (-2.2%) led the declines. TPG Telecom (TPG) (-1.5%) and Telstra Group (TLS) (-1.0%) also slipped. Health Care (XHJ) (-0.5%) pulled back from recent highs as defensives fell out of favour. Cochlear (COH) (-2.2%) was the biggest drag. Sonic Healthcare (SHL) (-1.4%) and ResMed (RMD) (-1.4%) also fell. CSL (CSL) (-0.6%) edged lower.
