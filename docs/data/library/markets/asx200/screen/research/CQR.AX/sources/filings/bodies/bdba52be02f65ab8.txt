This is largely driven by the V-shaped move for copper, where prices surged 8% in the last six sessions (though down 1.2% in today's session to US$6.83/lb). BHP rose 1.4% to $62.07, while most copper pure-play names rose 1-3%. Gold miners also traded firmly higher, with the All Ords Gold Index up 2.5%. Real Estate (+1.3%) locks in a three-day win streak, up 2.1% in total. Though its very much a bounce for ants, considering the index has fallen 12.5% since 5-Aug. The Aussie 10-year has slipped to a two-week low of 5.25% (from highs of ~5.4%). So a key inversely correlated metric is starting to make a move in the right direction. Healthcare (-0.6%) stocks closed at a fresh seven-month high on Tuesday, and experienced a slight pullback today. The sector has been trading relatively sideways since 20-Aug, as it digests a massive ~44% move from June lows. Leading names like CSL and Cochlear have some envious momentum, though their FY26 results point to very modest growth (a classic example of how a stock earns a re-rate by being a little less bad). Financials (-0.6%) managed to catch a bid off session lows of (-1.3%). The all-important CBA still fell 0.8%, lower than the 13-May low (this was when CBA tell 10.4% in a single day). Now trading at levels last seen in February. Tech (-0.6%) continues to be a momentum graveyard. This sector has somehow slumped ~17% since 25-Aug, at a time where a comparable index like the iShares Expanded Tech-Software ETF has gained 4.8% and trading closed to year-to-date highs. A name like Wisetech is down 53% year-to-date, still ~6% away from the lows it briefly hit on 23-Jun and 24-Jul. Yeah you could look at those points as some support, but then you look over at a name like Xero, down 48% year-to-date and undercut its Jun-Jul lows like butter. Xero tumbled 4.7% to $58.20, the lowest since May 2019. Some of the value-propositions for various software names might make sense, but boy is it a slog. Overall, not much to see here. Oil, yields and rate expectations look to be peaking and starting to move in the right direction. But the market remains choppy as it works through a seasonally weak September, and breadth has been persistently poor outside of healthcare, energy and a recent resurgence in miners.
