# PEXA Group Limited (PXA.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-09T07:21:34.114444+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
PEXA Group operates Australia’s dominant electronic lodgement network (ELN), processing roughly 90% of property transfer settlements, and is expanding in the UK while exiting its Digital Solutions segment. The quantitative screen rates PXA a buy on cheapness and balance-sheet risk metrics—P/B 1.11, debt/equity 21%, FCF yield ~6.2%—despite failing all quality, dividend and GARP models. The valuation hook is a cash-generative monopoly-like franchise trading near book value after a severe share-price de-rating driven by NSW regulator IPART’s draft proposal to cut ELNO fees. The central debate is whether improving operating cash flow and deleveraging offset a potential step-down in regulated Australian revenue and ongoing UK investment burn.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy signal rests on two families: **cheapness** (Schloss Low P/B, FCF Yield, Composite Value) and **risk** (Financial Health). These align with a business generating substantial free cash flow while reducing leverage—net debt/EBITDA fell from 2.5x (FY24) to 1.8x (FY25) and 1.4x (1H26)—and trading at P/B ~1.1x despite ~90% Australian market penetration and double-digit revenue growth.

For a value investor, the case is **asset-light cash yield at a distressed multiple**, not classic Graham quality. PXA fails 18 of 22 models: P/E 64x, ROE 1.7%, no dividend, Piotroski F-Score 4/9, negative statutory earnings in recent years, and tangible book value deeply negative due to acquisition intangibles (~$1.44bn goodwill and intangibles per Yahoo balance sheet, FY26). The screen correctly identifies balance-sheet repair and FCF conversion; it does not capture regulatory fee-setting risk, which is the primary threat to the Australian cash engine.

Peer comparison within the Technology screen set shows PXA passing fewer models than GRG.L (8) and DRX.L (7), but uniquely combining Schloss P/B with Financial Health—a profile consistent with a de-rated infrastructure-like platform rather than a growth compounder.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory (primary):** IPART’s draft ELNO service-fee review (July 2026) proposed a ~20% cut to regulated revenue. PEXA responded (18 August 2026 ASX announcement; body not extracted) and warned the approach could create an *“unsustainable”* financial profile (Kalkine, 21 July 2026). Final report timing confirmed for late 2026 (Kalkine Media, 4 September 2026). FY25 annual report notes awaiting IPART engagement on pricing review. This is the single largest threat to the Australian exchange margin (~58% EBITDA margin in 1H26).

**UK execution:** International segment remains EBITDA-negative with guided operating cash outflow of $(59–63)m in FY26. NatWest implementation on track for 4Q26, but scale-up requires sustained capex and operating spend.

**Portfolio simplification:** Digital Solutions classified held-for-sale/discontinued; $(26.1)m net impairments in 1H26. Exit reduces complexity but crystallises losses and removes diversification.

**Earnings quality:** Statutory vs core NPAT divergence is large and persistent. Piotroski F-Score 4/9 (negative net income component flagged). Heavy non-cash amortisation and periodic impairments ($78.2m FY25; interoperability and investment write-offs in FY25 annual report body).

**Cyclicality:** Revenue tied to Australian and UK property transaction volumes; sensitive to interest rates and housing turnover.

**Balance sheet:** Tangible book value negative; goodwill/intangibles dominate assets. Leverage improved but net debt ~$159m remains meaningful against statutory earnings.

**Liquidity:** Current ratio 1.13 (screen)—below conservative thresholds. No dividend; buyback ceased in favour of debt repayment (1H26 release).

**Governance/management:** CFO transition—Graham Curtin appointed effective 1 September 2026 (ASX, 28 May 2026); interim CFO Liz Warrell departing post-FY26 results.

Going-concern language in FY25 annual report and 1H26 half-year report is affirmative; no covenant breach language identified in available filing bodies.
