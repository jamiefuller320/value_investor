About PEXA 
PEXA (Property Exchange Australia) is a world-leading, digital property exchange business, listed on the 
Australian Stock Exchange. Since 2013, PEXA has facilitated more than 26 million property settlements, 
and today, 90% of all property transfer settlements in Australia are processed on the PEXA platform. In 
2022, PEXA commenced its international expansion through entry into the UK digital refinancing market, 
followed by the UK launch of PEXA’s Sale & Purchase capability in 2025.

Chair address:  
Good morning shareholders; it is my great pleasure to welcome you to our sixth annual 
general meeting. My name is Vivek Bhatia and I will be chairing the meeting today.  Our 
Board Chair, Mark Joiner, sends his apologies. He is unable to join us today due to health 
issues.  He expects to be up and about as normal shortly and we all wish him a speedy 
recovery. 
There are two very distinct stories arising out of the 2026 financial year.  
Firstly, under the decisive leadership of our CEO, Russell Cohen, the company performed 
strongly in both financial and strategic terms. Revenue, margins and free cash flow all 
improved, reflecting in part a near $20m realisation of cost savings through efficiency 
initiatives, while alignment with strategic goals were driven by the progressive exit of the 
Digital Solutions portfolio and a restructuring of the UK leadership team. The Group 
reported Net Profit After Tax of $19.2 million from continuing operations in FY26. However, 
a $35.1 million loss from discontinued operations resulted in a statutory net loss of $15.9 
million for the year.  
In FY26, our Australian Exchange customer satisfaction remained extremely high and the 
development of innovative new products and services continued. In the UK, progress with 
the major banks remained slower than we like and is influenced by factors outside our 
control. Despite this, we successfully signed NatWest to process mortgage refinancing, 
something which is delivering a step function improvement in their performance. We are 
on track for NatWest to complete their first Sale & Purchase transaction early in the new 
year. Altogether, this activity reflects a strong performance under refreshed leadership.  
In March, we were informed by our regulator, ARNECC, about their decision to conclude 
the industry Interoperability program after 6 years of effort and much cost. This is a 
decision we regard as sensible and backed by ARNECC’s independent analysis that the 
program, as it was originally conceived, could not meet its stated objectives in a cost 
effective or practical way. This has allowed PEXA and other industry participants to focus 
on core technology improvements and customer service. 
IPART, commissioned by ARNECC, continued its review of PEXA’s pricing in FY26, 
resulting in a draft report landing on 3rd July 2026 that proposed a 20% reduction in PEXA’s 
regulated revenue through reducing critical aspects of PEXA’s pricing and led to a severe 
contraction in the company’s share price.  The proposed revenue reduction was 
unprecedented in its magnitude. IPART’s draft report has disregarded the early risk taken 
by investors and assumed that PEXA was a “sure thing” when it was a start-up, despite 
the very real risks to the national digital transformation up until the COVID period.  IPART’s 
report also ignored the actual return realised by the State governments, which is a 
verifiable data point for calculating PEXA’s early returns and supports the value of our 
asset. IPART also failed to extend their modelling into future years, which would have 
shown them that the methodology they were adopting could, in the absence of 
management intervention, turn PEXA’s free cash flows negative in just 7 years’ time, 
putting its long-term solvency at risk.

In addition, IPART’s draft ignored the voice of our many investors and customers, who 
engaged constructively with the IPART consultation process through numerous 
submissions, raising concerns with the methodology and real-world implications of limiting 
PEXA’s ability to invest and serve its customers.  
I would emphasise that, in our view, the methodology IPART used was not appropriate for 
a digital business. 
If IPART’s draft recommendation is adopted, this will create the kind of sovereign risk that 
undermines Australia’s credentials as an attractive destination for foreign capital. 
Following receipt of the final recommendation, which is expected very soon, we will 
continue to engage with our regulator and reinforce our position that accepting the view 
of IPART, without its own analysis and consideration of broader factors, is problematic. 
IPART has clearly used a model that is not fit for purpose and has unintended 
consequences. 
IPART submitted its final report to the NSW Government on 30 October on a confidential 
basis. We expect the Government to refer it to ARNECC and publish it within the coming 
week. Depending upon where this all lands, we at PEXA will be forced to respond in 
whatever ways we can. If there is minimal change between the draft and final 
recommendation, the inevitable casualty will likely be investment in innovation. Having 
been declared critical infrastructure by the federal government, PEXA must remain vigilant 
amid rising cyber threats, including those enabled by Artificial Intelligence. We will of 
course prioritise the safety of our customers through strong cyber security measures. 
The Board’s focus is to protect the resilience, security and service quality of Australia’s 
Exchange while responding to the regulatory outcome. We will maintain disciplined capital 
allocation across the Group, with oversight of UK adoption and delivery, the development 
of PEXA Clear and the capital-light New Zealand pilot. We will continue to assess 
investment, risk and progress towards sustainable returns across each of these priorities. 
FY26 also saw important governance changes. We welcomed John Hooper, chairman of 
our UK businesses, to the Group board, and appointed Janelle Hopkins, formerly CFO at 
tech platform business REA. Both bring considerable relevant experience to the board. 
Reflecting her heavy commitments elsewhere, Helen Silver resigned from the board and 
we thank her for her contributions over the many years.  
At our 2025 AGM, I announced the commencement of a Board refresh, and these changes 
form part of that program. We will continue to take a phased approach to that transition, 
ensuring the Board collectively has the skills and experience needed to guide the 
company through this stage of its journey.  
I also want to acknowledge the feedback shareholders provided on the proposed Share 
Price Recovery Incentive. Following further consideration and shareholder feedback, the 
Board withdrew resolution 8(b). No replacement arrangement is proposed at this stage. 
The Board will continue to consider how best to support CEO retention and align 
incentives with long-term shareholder value, taking account of shareholder feedback and 
the regulatory outcome.

CEO and Group Managing Director address: 
I’d also like to welcome everyone joining us in person and online, and thank you for your 
ongoing interest and support. It’s a privilege to speak on behalf of our team and the 
customers we serve across Australia and the UK. 
The Chair has spoken about PEXA’s FY26 performance and the broader issues facing the 
Group. I’ll focus on what our teams delivered across the business and how we’re 
approaching the year ahead. 
I’m proud of the progress our team made in FY26. In Australia, record transaction volumes 
and the cost efficiency measures we implemented helped drive strong operating leverage 
and an expansion in Group EBITDA margin. 
In the UK, we delivered NatWest’s remortgage implementation three months ahead of 
schedule. Since going live in March, the platform has operated successfully with a major 
lender. That is an important proof point as we continue to engage with other lenders and 
conveyancers to drive broader adoption of our UK platform. 
We also made clear choices about where to focus the business. We completed the 
strategic review of Digital Solutions and have subsequently exited the portfolio. We 
launched PEXA Clear to help real estate agents and conveyancers meet their new 
AML/CTF obligations. Lastly, we prepared for the launch of a phased pilot in New Zealand, 
and Phase 1 of that pilot is under way across the Tasman as I speak to you now.  
Our Core Group results from continuing operations show the strength of the underlying 
business. Group revenue exceeded $400 million for the first time, increasing 7% on FY25. 
EBITDA increased 12%, and the Group EBITDA margin expanded to 37.3%. 
Statutory NPAT from continuing operations was $19.2 million, compared with a loss in 
FY25. The Australian Exchange also continued to generate strong cash flow, with free 
cash flow up 39%. 
These results reflect a disciplined approach to costs while continuing to invest in the 
security, reliability and resilience of our platform. 
Focusing now on Australia; our Australian business had a strong year, supported by record 
transaction volumes, particularly in the second quarter of the financial year. We now serve 
customers in every state and territory and we continue to focus on increased document 
capacity across all jurisdictions. Remortgages launched in the Northern Territory in August 
2025, and we are working with the NT Land Registry to launch transfer capabilities 
imminently.

We saw advancements in digitisation in Tasmania, including the removal of the paper title 
requirement from 1 September. I want to acknowledge the land registry and other industry 
participants who worked with us to make eConveyancing available in the state. 
We invested $35 million in our Australian Exchange during FY26. This supported platform

development of PEXA Clear.  
In the UK, the NatWest implementation for remortgages showed that our platform can 
operate successfully with a major lender. We’ve also received positive feedback from 
NatWest and their customers using the platform, providing encouraging evidence for other 
lenders considering adoption of our UK technology. 
Lender adoption in the UK has taken longer than we wanted. However with the technology 
built and operational, our team fully formed and having all the necessary regulatory 
approvals and licences, our focus now is on working with lenders and conveyancers to 
address the practical barriers to adoption and grow transaction volumes. 
There is also growing momentum across government and industry to improve the UK 
home-buying and selling process. This gives us an opportunity to engage with lenders on 
how digital processes can support a more connected transaction. 
With refreshed PEXA UK leadership, we are sharpening our focus on execution, customer 
adoption and leveraging our technology and capability amid broader industry shifts. 
We have refreshed our FY27 strategy, and I’ll take you through it now on the next page.  
Our strategy starts with our purpose: connecting people to place. 
For shareholders, that means attracting, and retaining, the best people to PEXA, inspiring 
them to operate world-class digital infrastructure and deliver customer service to the 
highest standards, all in service of protecting the integrity of property lodgement and 
settlements. Successfully executing on this objective means growing with discipline and 
seeking returns from our investments that shareholders benefit from over time. For our 
customers, it means making property transactions simpler, faster and more secure. For 
our team, it means creating an environment where they can do their best work and 
understand how their contribution supports the business. 
That purpose connects our work across the Group. It guides how we strengthen and 
innovate our technology, support customers through new requirements and pursue 
opportunities in the UK and New Zealand.

As we noted in our 1st quarter of FY27 trading update released this morning, we made a

We’ll continue to manage PEXA for the long term, with disciplined cost control and careful

Vivek has outlined the regulatory uncertainty currently facing PEXA. I know shareholders 
will be asking what actions we could take if the final IPART recommendation is materially 
similar to the draft released in July. 
We are acutely aware of our fiduciary obligations to shareholders. Based on our current 
analysis, a reduction in revenue of the scale proposed would require significant changes 
to our cost base, including our workforce, if we were to maintain similar margins. As a 
technology company headquartered in Victoria, many of the roles affected would be at our 
Melbourne head office.  
This would have real consequences for Victoria and its tech industry. PEXA is a world-first 
innovation, with a platform that settles property transactions across Australia. Having run 
platforms internationally, I know how difficult it is to build and scale a successful technology 
business of this kind. I hope the real-world implications of IPART’s analysis are taken 
seriously, alongside the theoretical economic modelling. 
While we await ARNECC’s decision on any changes to our service fees, we have prepared 
for a range of possible outcomes. Our planning includes identifying additional revenue 
opportunities, reducing investment in innovation, and seeking efficiencies in parts of the 
business where we currently deliver above required standards. 
Before considering any long-term structural changes, we will need to assess the final 
valuation methodology and the service fee outcome determined by ARNECC. These will 
shape the options available to us and the actions that may be appropriate for the business. 
Whatever the outcome, we remain focused on managing PEXA for the long term, 
balancing near-term headwinds with the investment and capabilities needed to serve our 
customers and sustain the business. In the meantime, we will continue to advocate for an 
appropriate outcome for our customers, our shareholders, our employees and the broader 
industry.
