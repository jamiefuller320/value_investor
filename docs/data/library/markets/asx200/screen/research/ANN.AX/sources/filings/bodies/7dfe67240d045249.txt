About Ansell 
Ansell (ASX:ANN) is a global leader in safety solutions and an integrated manufacturer of personal protection equipment for 
healthcare and industrial workplaces. Each day, over 10 million workers in more than 100 countries trust their safety to Ansell 
brands such as HyFlex, Ringers, MICROFLEX, TouchNTuff, GAMMEX, Kimtech, KleenGuard, and AlphaTec. Driven by a vision 
to lead the world to a safer future, the company continuously pursues new product and service innovations that predict, prevent, 
and protect against workplace risk while promoting sustainable sourcing and manufacturing. 
Information on Ansell and its products can be found at www.ansell.com. #AnsellProtects

AGM is an important opportunity to report on the company’s performance, explain

Financial Performance 
Financial year 2026 was a year of strong performance for our company. The Board is 
pleased with the financial outcomes, which included growth in sales, expanded 
margins and strong cash generation. These results were delivered in challenging 
external conditions, including a higher tariff environment in the US and the conflict 
in the Middle East. Against this backdrop, delivery of Adjusted Earnings Per Share at

discipline and resilience of the Ansell business. Nathalie will discuss the results in

September, who is with us in the room this morning. 
Leadership transitions are an important test of Board stewardship. They require us to 
preserve the strengths of the business while ensuring that new leadership has the 
mandate and accountability to challenge established assumptions and deliver the 
next phase of performance. The Board’s focus through this period has been on

Sustainability 
As a Board we recognise that Ansell’s long-term success depends on more than 
financial performance. It depends on the resilience of our operations, the responsible 
management of our environmental and social impacts, and the trust of the 
customers, employees, suppliers and communities connected to our business. The 
Board’s role is to ensure these considerations are embedded in the company’s 
strategy, risk management and decision-making.

Transparent reporting is an important part of that responsibility. It enables 
shareholders to understand how Ansell is managing material risks, where progress is 
being made and where further work is required. It also provides a basis for 
meaningful engagement with shareholders on the issues that matter to the long-
term performance and resilience of the business. 
This year, Ansell published its first climate-related financial disclosures under AASB 
S2, further developing the way we assess and report on climate-related risks and 
opportunities. We also continued our work to strengthen human rights due 
diligence and responsible sourcing across our global supply chain. 
Labour rights and responsible sourcing remain an important area of Board 
oversight. The experience at Mediceram, a Malaysian supplier of ceramic formers 
used in the manufacture of single-use gloves in one of our manufacturing facilities 
that represented less than 0.1% of our total global supplier spend has brought into 
further sharp focus the challenges of identifying and addressing labour rights risks 
across complex supply chains. 
I want to be clear that what happened to workers at Mediceram should not have 
happened. We recognise the seriousness of the issues. 
Ansell was one of a number of customers of Mediceram. While most of these 
customers immediately ended their relationship with Mediceram, Ansell worked 
collectively with another Mediceram customer, together with the Responsible 
Business Alliance , to understand the issues, investigate the circumstances and 
support remediation for affected workers. 
Consistent with the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, we chose 
to remain engaged with the company to use our commercial influence to seek to 
improve outcomes for affected workers, rather than simply walk away. 
To support these efforts, Ansell made advance payment for orders covering more 
than 12 months of supply and expedited payment for goods delivered. This 
contributed to more than US$1 million being repaid to migrant workers for 
recruitment fees they had incurred.
ANSELL LIMITED AGM 2026 | Chair Address  
Ansell stayed engaged with Mediceram for as long as there appeared a genuine 
prospect of improving outcomes for workers. However, following Mediceram's 
decision to terminate workers and its failure to take appropriate corrective actions, 
Ansell suspended the supplier relationship. With Mediceram then moving into 
liquidation, Ansell continued working, through the Responsible Business Alliance, to 
support affected former workers, including assistance with repatriation and access 
to food and other basic necessities where required. We invested in these efforts 
because we believed it was the right thing to do. 
We recognise there have been calls for more to be done and understand why there 
are strong feelings about this issue. The focus of our work with the Responsible 
Business Alliance, and other stakeholders, was helping workers receive direct 
remediation of the fees they had paid and assistance with repatriation. We consider 
that this was, and is, the most direct and effective way of supporting workers and 
their immediate needs.  
This matter provided important learnings for Ansell and reinforced the importance 
of continuous improvement and ongoing strengthening of our due diligence 
approach. Our due diligence framework already assessed a broad network of 
suppliers against a range of risk factors. However, the Mediceram experience 
showed that labour rights risks can also arise with smaller, specialised suppliers, 
where they may be less visible. 
As a result, during FY26 we reviewed the scope and prioritisation criteria of our 
Supplier Management Framework and we expanded engagement with ceramic 
former suppliers. This also informed our broader approach to identifying suppliers 
associated with potential elevated labour rights risks, with an even greater focus on 
industry characteristics, workforce composition and recruitment practices. 
What we know is that there is no single audit, standard or control that can 
guarantee the identification of every labour rights issue across a complex supply 
chain. This is why it is important Ansell has continually evolving systems in place that 
enable the Company to effectively assess for risk, respond to issues and identify 
appropriate remediation when gaps are exposed. We will continue to assess the

importance of continued constructive engagement with shareholders and other 
stakeholders. 
Ansell also recognises that labour rights challenges persist across global supply 
chains and that addressing them requires engagement and action across suppliers, 
buyers, industry groups, governments and worker organisations, an effort to which 
your Board and management remains committed.

CEO ADDRESS 
Good morning to all shareholders joining us today both in Melbourne and online. It is 
a privilege to address you for the first time as Chief Executive Officer.  
Since I commenced this role in February, I have spent my time where it matters 
most: with our customers, and our teams around the world. Two things have stood 
out. 
The first is how essential our products are to our customers’ operations, and how 
much they value partnering with us.  
That is a real strength, and I want us to build on it. 
The second is the passion, talent and commitment of our people. As Nigel touched 
on earlier, we faced significant external challenges in FY26. The way our teams 
responded, with clarity and decisiveness, helped deliver our strong FY26 results.  
Our Purpose: Leading The World to a Safer Future 
But before I discuss our financial performance, I want to remind you of our purpose: 
leading the world to a safer future.  
It is a powerful purpose. It is who we are.  
Across our 130-year history, Ansell has established itself as the global leader in safety, 
innovating solutions that safeguard lives and enable businesses to thrive.  
To our customers, Ansell is more than just a supplier of PPE. We are a trusted safety 
partner.  
We want to build on that reputation through deeper partnerships. That includes 
using our insights to tailor solutions to the specific challenges of our customers.  
I would like to give you a recent example. Earlier this year, we launched a new 
TouchNTuff single use glove. It is the first in the market to be resistant to acetone for 
at least 15 minutes. It gives workers exposed to hazardous chemicals the protection 
they require, without having to layer multiple gloves or use heavy-duty, more 
cumbersome products.  
This is customer-led innovation in action, and we will continue to focus on solving 
our customers’ most challenging problems.

FY26 Performance 
Now I would like to talk about our performance in FY26. It was a year of strong sales 
and earnings growth, delivered against a backdrop of significant external challenges, 
including new US tariffs and the Middle East crisis. 
We responded decisively to these challenges. What was in our own hands, we acted 
on. We implemented targeted pricing actions to recover higher costs. We 
accelerated our sourcing and supply chain productivity initiatives, and we stayed 
close to our customers. 
Our sales were up 5.0 per cent on an organic basis, increasing to 5.7 per cent on an 
adjusted basis. Growth accelerated significantly in the second half, including higher 
volumes. We also had strong momentum in key strategic markets, with US sales 
growing 10 per cent, and global sales of Cleanroom solutions also growing 10 per 
cent.  
It was particularly encouraging to see sales growth translate into improved earnings. 
Adjusted EBIT was up 14.9 per cent on an organic basis, and our Adjusted EBIT 
margin increased to 15 per cent. Earnings grew faster than sales, reflecting the 
benefits of improved product mix and productivity initiatives.  
Our strong earnings growth translated to good cash flow, with cash conversion 
rising from 91 per cent in FY25 to 113 per cent in FY26, ensuring our balance sheet 
remains very healthy.  
And finally, we delivered Adjusted Earnings Per Share of 148.6 cents, up nearly 18 per 
cent. This allowed us to increase our full year dividend by 35.7 per cent to 68.1 cents.  
Sustainability Progress 
I’ll now touch on the progress we have made against our safety and sustainability 
commitments. Our responsibility does not end with the products we make. It 
extends to the people who make them, the suppliers we work with and the 
resources we use.  
Safety is central to everything we do at Ansell. It was very pleasing to see our Total 
Recordable Injury Frequency Rate fall by 32 per cent in FY26, and a strong increase 
in employee safety observations.

We continue to progress toward our target of net zero by 2045. Scope 1 and 2 
emissions are now 36 per cent below our FY20 base year. 
Responsible sourcing is a key part of our social responsibility. As Nigel mentioned 
earlier, during FY26, we expanded our supplier management framework and 
continued to enhance how we identify and address labour rights risks. 
For management, the focus is on embedding our standards in supplier selection and 
engagement, improving our understanding of risk and working to address issues 
when they are identified. We also continue to work with industry partners and other 
stakeholders to improve visibility and address challenges that extend beyond any 
one company. 
This is an ongoing responsibility, and one that requires continued attention. 
Working With NGOs To Respond To The Ebola Outbreak In Africa 
As a global leader in safety, Ansell has a long history working with health 
organisations to help protect communities and frontline workers from viral 
outbreaks.  
Today we are partnering with NGOs to support the response to the current Ebola 
crisis in Africa, where the impact on affected communities is extremely high and 
over 10,000 protective suits are being used every day. 
We have quickly ramped up production to fill critical supply chain gaps so that 
frontline efforts are not disrupted. Our work also includes fitting frontline workers 
with the right PPE and helping develop the next generation of infection prevention 
products.  
We are proud of the role we play in keeping workers and communities safe, and 
partnering with NGOs to provide the safety solutions needed as they respond to 
health crises around the world. 
 
Strategic Priorities 
Next, I would like to discuss our strategic priorities.  
Our FY26 results demonstrate Ansell is a resilient company.

Our focus now is to accelerate profitable sales growth and get closer to our 
customers.  
We will do that through three levers.  
Two of those levers drive growth: commercial excellence, and a focus on our most 
attractive markets.  
The third lever funds that growth: operational excellence that simplifies our business 
and enhances our ability to meet customer needs at scale. 
We are already seeing progress with these strategies. We saw faster growth across 
our top five brands in FY26, while they also contributed to better margins. In the US – 
our largest market and central to our growth plans – sales grew at 1.8 times the 
group rate, led by premium industrial and scientific segments.  
We will build on the strength of these brands and markets and continue to assess 
where our product range and commercial resources can deliver the greatest value. 
Our customer relationships also show how we deliver value beyond the product 
itself. Let me give you one example: our partnership with Amazon.  
Where Amazon has deployed the Ringers impact protection glove we co-developed 
with them, hand injuries have fallen 65 per cent. With around 1.5 million warehouse 
workers, this is a significantly positive impact for Amazon and an excellent 
illustration of what happens when we match the right solution to a specific risk.  
We also need a simpler, more focused business.  
In FY26, our Accelerated Productivity Investment Program achieved its recurring 
saving target of US$50 million. We will now focus on further reducing complexity 
across our product and brand portfolios, supply chain and ways of working.  
These priorities are connected.  
Stronger positions in attractive markets. More focused commercial investment. And 
a simpler product portfolio.  
Together this will grow sales more profitably and convert more of that growth into 
earnings.  Our task now is to deliver those improvements consistently.

Trading Update 
Now I’ll provide a short update on trading so far in FY27. 
While macroeconomic uncertainty persists, end market conditions have been 
largely as expected, with continued strong demand in the US supporting good 
constant currency sales growth in the first quarter. 
Margins have been trending positively, supported by supply chain productivity and 
operational excellence initiatives, including select organisational changes to 
implement more customer focused ways of working. While key input costs have 
retreated from peak levels reached in late FY26, they remain elevated. We continue 
to retain the flexibility to adjust prices when required. 
As mentioned in our August results briefing, we are prioritising capex towards our 
higher growth business segments, with spend still expected to be in the range of 
$45 million to $55 million for the full year.  
I am also pleased to report we have successfully completed the first of our 
commercial ERP system upgrades, with the North America region going live on the 
upgraded system in early October.  
So based on these factors, we are maintaining our guidance range for FY27 Adjusted 
Earnings Per Share of 158 to 170 US cents.

To Nigel and the Board, thank you for your support. And to you, our shareholders, 
thank you for your trust. 
We have made real progress this year, but we are just getting started.
