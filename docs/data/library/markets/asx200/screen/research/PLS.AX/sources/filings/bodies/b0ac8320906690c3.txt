Fund flows: Still the one ✅ Sure, the tech sector bounced today, but the key takeaway from today's rally for me is that the Resources sector was strong again . For those who follow my ChartWatch *LIVE* Webinars , you'll be aware of my Relative Strength Comparative (RSC) indicator. Not to be confused with the Relative Strength Indicator (RSI) – which has zero usefulness as a technical indicator – the RSC helps illustrate the relative performance of one security against another. I use the RSC to compare stocks and sectors to a benchmark, in our case, the ASX 200. If the stock/sector is outperforming the ASX 200, my RSC indicator will be rising, and the opposite is true for underperformance. Readings above/below zero indicate sustained outperformance/underperformance over the last 3-months (i.e., 63 candles on a daily chart hence "RSC-63"). S&P/ASX 200 Resources Index with "RSC-63" ( click here for full size image) As with any technical indicator, the RSC only tells us what has happened and never what will be . So, understand that the excellent RSC showing for the XJR in the chart above could change tomorrow . For now, though, it demonstrates to me that the big fund managers are still rotating into the sector . So, the price action was decent ✅. The volume was slightly below average – a fairly regular day in that regard – also suggesting that the demand in the system didn’t encounter major supply-side resistance. Benign. Speaking of potential supply-side resistance, I note that the long term trend ribbon – now neutralised and now potentially a zone of dynamic supply – also didn’t pose any problems. Again, decent. But not spectacular! As in, compare today's candle to Tuesday’s wipe out, or Friday’s tumble before that. Add in the fact that: The short term trend is unequivocally down The price action is sweeping down phases into falling troughs vs shallow rallies into falling peaks There’s a predominance of supply-side candles (i.e., black-bodied and or upward pointing shadows) And one might assume there’s still a long way to go in terms of moving back to even just a “balance between demand and supply” here. Nope, the supply-side is still in control of the Old Tin Pot's price as far as I'm concerned! I’d like to see at least a couple more candles like today (i.e., long white-bodied and or long downward pointing shadows, a close back above the long term trend ribbon , and potentially even a higher trough formed – before I’d contemplate calling “equilibrium”… let alone before calling “demand-side control”. View Today's price action is a decent step in the right direction – but more needs to be done to confirm the demand-side has grappled back control of the XJO's price. Therefore, I remain comfortable at 1/3RP in my Risk Bucket 🪣 for now . (i.e., my personal allowable capital allocation limit (i.e., R isk P osition) for my investments in Australian stocks is 33%) . Key levels 8421 is the closest point of demand. The long term trend ribbon is the closest zone of supply (presently 8543-8685 ). The short term downtrend ribbon is, however, the critical zone of supply (presently 8745-8795 ). In the absence of other confirming demand-side control signals, the XJO must close back above this point to demonstrate the demand-side is moving back into control.
