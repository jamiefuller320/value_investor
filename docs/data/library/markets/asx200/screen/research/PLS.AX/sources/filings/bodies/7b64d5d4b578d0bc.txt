Pilgangoora Ore Reserve increases 31% in contained lithium 
PLS Group Limited (ASX: PLS) (PLS or the Company) is pleased to announce a substantial increase in the 
Ore Reserve and an updated Mineral Resource for its 100% -owned Pilgangoora Operation, following 
completion of the P2000 Feasibility Study (FS), P2000 final investment decision (FID) and Underground Pre -
Feasibility Study (Underground PFS) announced concurrently today. 
The P2000 FS has materially enhanced the development strategy for the Pilgangoora orebody. Improved 
metallurgical understanding and processing capability (including the new Jimmagunya plant) enable a greater 
proportion of mineralised material to be economi cally recovered, supporting a materially larger Ore Reserve. 
In parallel, the Underground PFS has established a maiden Underground Ore Reserve beneath the existing 
open pits. 
The updated Ore Reserve further strengthens the long-term production base for Pilgangoora and supports the 
increased processing rates under the P2000 expansion. The Mineral Resource has also been updated 
following a revised assessment of reasonable prospec ts for eventual economic extraction (RPEEE) including 
open pit and underground components, with the underlying geological model and estimation unchanged.  
The reference point for Ore Reserve reporting has been changed from the Run-of-Mine (ROM) stockpile to the 
Coarse-Ore-Stockpile (COS) after crushing-and-sorting reflecting the feed for the wet processing plants.

SUMMARY OF ORE RESERVE ESTIMATE AND REPORTING CRITERIA 
As per ASX Listing Rule 5. 9 and the 2012 JORC reporting guidelines, a summary of the material information 
used to estimate the Ore Reserves is detailed below (for more detail please refer to Table 1, Section 4 included 
in Appendix 1). 
Ore Reserve classification 
The Ore Reserves consist of 6% Proved and 94% Probable. Measured or Indicated Mineral Resources have 
been converted to Ore Reserves by the application of Modifying Factors. Proved Ore Reserves have been 
derived from Measured Mineral Resources only. Probable Ore Reserves have been derived from Indicated 
Mineral Resources only. Stockpile Ore Reserves have been classified into both Proved and P robable. 
The Ore Reserve is the economically mineable part of the Measured and Indicated Resource. It includes 
allowances for mining dilution and ore losses during mining,  and excludes ore sorting ejects . Appropriate 
assessments and studies have been carried out and include consideration of and modification by realistically 
assumed mining, metallurgical, economic, marketing, legal, environmental, social and governmental factors. 
These assessments demonstrate at the time of reporting that extraction can reasonably be justified. 
Near surface oxidised material above the cut -off grade was treated as waste for the purposes of the Ore 
Reserve estimate but has potential to be added to the mining inventory with further metallurgical testing.  
The Underground Ore Reserves are 100% Probable tonnes by classification.  
Mining methods, assumptions and estimation methodology 
Open Pit 
The model used as an input to the Ore Reserve estimate was the same model used for the Mineral Resource 
estimate announced in this release,  depleted to end of June 2026. This was converted to a diluted mining 
model using industry standard regularisation to estimate ore loss and dilution with regularisation resulting in 
7% loss of ore (equivalent to 93% ore recovery). With the P2000 Jimmagunya ore sorting plant capable of 
treating material containing up to 35% waste, the pegmatite % cut-off grade for ore has been reduced to 25% 
improving overall ore recovery and resulting in 27% dilution of pegmatite to the ROM. This ore loss and dilution 
is considered appropriate by the Competent Person with FY26 reconciliation supporting the modifying factors 
used for determination of the Ore Reserve. 
The diluted mining model was subsequently used for the generation of optimi sed pit shells and defining 
economic mining envelopes, based on various inputs, including geotechnical domains, costs and sales prices.  
The transition cross over point for moving from Open Pit to Underground mining was determined as part of 
the Underground scoping study to identify the point where the economic extraction of the known Resource of 
each optimised pit shell provided superior economic benefit when mined from underground as shown in 
Figure 1 below.

Selected optimal pit shells were then used as the basis for detailed stage and ultimate pit designs with revenue 
factors averaging 0.64 (~US$980/dmt SC6) for deposits extending below the transition point and 1.00 for the 
smaller deposits that did not. Additionally, sensitivity analysis to price, grade, recovery, costs (mining, 
processing and capital), and overall wall angles was undertaken at levels of +/- 20% using Whittle optimisation 
software with the results confirming robust economic outcomes. 
Mining operations at the Pilgangoora Operation commenced in October 2017 and are well understood.  The 
current Open Pit mining method is conventional drill-and-blast on 5 metres (m) to 10m nominal bench heights 
and load-and-haul on flitch heights of nominally 2.5m to 4.5m . Mining is undertaken with 200t class hydraulic 
excavators in backhoe configuration loading 150t nominal class rear dump haul trucks to separately mine ore 
and basalt and ultramafic waste. 
Future increased mining requirements will include the addition of 400t and 600t class hydraulic excavators 
loading 240t nominal class haul trucks.  
Presplit and production blast hole drilling is undertaken using both tracked and platform rigs enabling access 
from surface contour to pit bottom. The drill-and-blast, load-and-haul mining method is considered appropriate 
for the deposit geometry  and production rates having been in use since 2017. Access is available to all 
designed pits, waste dumps and stockpiles and the minimum mining width for pushbacks is 40m.  
Ore containing 5%  to 75% waste is currently stockpiled for processing through the ore sorting facilities. 
Following commissioning of the Jimmagunya plant, this material will be scheduled as direct plant feed and 
existing stockpiles will be progressively reclaimed.  
The geotechnical slope parameters used for pit designs are based on geotechnical slope domains or design 
sectors derived from multiple geotechnical investigations and incorporate evaluation of existing wall 
performance by on-site geotechnical engineers and geotechnical consultants. 
Batter face angles range from 60° to 85° in fresh (unweathered) rock and from 60° to 65° in weathered rocks 
(transition and oxide) dependent on orientation of wall aspect and foliation within each design sector.  
Berm widths are variable within each geotechnical domain ranging from between 7.5m and 11.0m to achieve 
the inter-ramp design angles for the design sector. 
Additional width rock catch berms based on operational requirements are incorporated into designs where 
required.

Underground 
AMC Consultants Pty Ltd (AMC) was engaged to assist PLS to undertake the Underground PFS to assess the 
potential for an underground operation at Pilgangoora and  support the declaration of the Underground Ore 
Reserve.  
The Underground PFS completed for the potential underground operation at Pilgangoora identified overhand 
longhole stoping with pillars and waste rock backfill as the preferred mining method. Thicker areas (above 
20m) are planned to be mined using transverse stoping and narrower areas (20m and below) using longitudinal 
stoping. 
The underground Ore Reserve is based on a detailed pre-feasibility level mine design (Figure 2) and schedule 
generated in Deswik CAD and Scheduler considering all applicable costs, operational considerations and 
geotechnical constraints. Underground fleet productivity assumptions are based on P50 productivities sourced 
from AMC’s benchmarking databas e. Estimated fleet and personnel numbers are based on mine schedule 
physicals, delivered at those productivity rates. 
Underground mining costs have been built up from first principles using AMC’s database of mining owner and 
contractor costs from recent Western Australian projects. These have been supplemented with infrastructure 
capital costs provided by PLS based upon P2000 Project studies.

Ore Sorting 
Ore sorting is treated as a Modifying Factor in this Ore Reserve estimate. The reference point for Ore Reserve 
reporting is at the point of delivery to the coarse ore stockpile (COS), after crushing and ore sorting, as feed 
to the wet processing plants  with the ore sorting waste and ore loss ejects excluded from the Ore Reserve  
estimate. The COS reference point will be the basis for Ore Reserve reporting going forward. This is a change 
from previous Pilgangoora Ore Reserve estimates , including the FY26 Annual Report, which reported ore 
delivered to the ROM before crushing and sorting. 
This change has been adopted following approval of the P2000 Project and the corresponding Jimmagunya 
flowsheet enabling the processing of material containing up to 35% waste.  With the additional Jimmagunya 
plant ore sorting capability combined with the existing Pilgan plant ore sorting capacity, the majority of ore 
mined and processed will pass through ore sorting at either the Jimmagunya or Pilgan plants.  The change of

reporting basis from before to after crushing- and-sorting will enable the Ore Reserve to be reconciled more 
accurately to measured plant feed. Two effects of ore sorting are accounted for as Modifying Factors. The first 
is the removal of host rock from the sorted fraction, which reduces tonnes, increases Li 2O and Ta2O5 grades 
and reduces the Fe2O3 grade of COS ore relative to ROM ore. The second is the loss of pegmatite to the ejects 
stream through mis -ejection, which is accounted for as an additional  ore loss. Neither of these effects is 
included in the processing plant recoveries described under "Processing assumptions" below, which are 
applied to COS wet plant feed ore. Ore sorting performance is applied to each parcel of ore in the mine 
schedule using relationships  derived in the P2000 FS from the waste content of the ROM ore and the 
proportion of crushed feed presented to the sorters, informed by the operating performance of the Pilgan ore 
sorting facility, which has been in operation since August 2024. All undergr ound ore is scheduled to the 
Jimmagunya plant and is subject to the same Modifying Factor s for Ore Reserve reporting . As the Ngungaju 
plant has no ore sorting facility and treats clean pegmatite ore only, no ore sorting adjustment is applied to 
Ngungaju feed. 
For the purposes of this report, the following terms are used: 
• ROM ore (ore mined) – ore delivered from the open pit, underground or stockpile reclaim to the ROM 
pad or primary crusher, before crushing and ore sorting, inclusive of mining dilution and after mining 
ore loss (the basis of Table 2). 
• Ore sorting ejects – material rejected by the ore sorters, comprising host rock and a small proportion 
of mis-ejected pegmatite, treated as mine waste and excluded from the Ore Reserve.  
• COS ore (Ore Reserve) – ore after crushing and ore sorting, delivered to the coarse ore stockpile as 
wet plant feed, being the reference point at which the Ore Reserve is reported (the basis of Table 1).  
• Ore processed – COS ore wet plant feed for concentration, to which the processing recoveries are 
applied. 
Processing assumptions 
The 5.1Mtpa Pilgan plant comprises crushing with integrated ore sorting, high pressure grinding rolls (HPGR), 
heavy media separation, ball milling, magnetic separation, spirals, flotation, and concentrate filtration to 
produce spodumene concentrate and tantalite by -product. The 1.6Mtpa Ngungaju Plant comprises crushing, 
HPGR, heavy media separation, ball milling, flotation, magnetic separation and filtration to produce 
spodumene concentrate only. The existing Pilgan Plant and Ngungaju processing plants , including the ore 
sorting facility at the front end of the Pilgan plant , all utilise conventional, widely used and well -tested 
metallurgical techniques which have now been operationally proven at Pilgangoora for over 18 months. 
The 6.3Mtpa Jimmagunya plant flowsheet comprises crushing with integrated ore sorting, Semi-Autogenous 
Grinding (SAG) mill, Ball mill, and Crusher  (SABC), magnetic separation, spi rals, flotation and concentrate 
filtration to produce spodumene concentrate and tantalite by -product. The flowsheet is designed to accept 
ROM ore containing up to 35% waste. 
Pegmatite lost to ore sorting ejects through mis-ejection is accounted for as additional ore loss and is 
excluded from the plant recoveries quoted, which are applied to COS feed.
