# Regis Resources Limited (RRL.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T19:22:31.959933+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Regis Resources Limited (RRL.AX) is a mid-tier Australian gold producer operating Duketon (100%) and Tropicana (30% JV), with McPhillamys as a long-dated growth option. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy on cheapness (P/E 8.9, P/B 2.3), quality (ROE 29.2%), dividend yield (3.2%), and FCF yield, with 16 of 22 models passing. Yahoo-sourced FY25 accounts show a sharp earnings recovery (net profit A$254m on revenue A$1.65bn) and balance-sheet repair (cash A$505m after A$300m debt repayment), while recent secondary headlines cite record FY26 production of 379koz and cash plus bullion of ~A$1.18bn. The central debate is whether standalone Regis — after declining to match Genesis Minerals’ superior bid for Vault Minerals — can redeploy capital into McPhillamys and organic growth quickly enough to justify a re-rating, or whether the failed merger, rising all-in sustaining costs, and a flagged reporting issue cap upside despite cheap headline multiples.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen flags Regis as a strong buy with a composite score of 78% (sector-relative 79%), conviction of 74% (stable, four weeks at signal), and five factor families passed: cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk. Sixteen models clear, including Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Lynch/Neff PEG variants, Buffett Quality, Magic Formula, Piotroski F-Score, and Financial Health — an unusually broad pass set for a single-name gold miner.

The hook for a value investor is low absolute valuation combined with improving fundamental quality. FY25 ROE of ~29% (screen), current ratio 2.63 (screen; Yahoo balance sheet consistent at ~2.6×), and FY25 free cash flow of A$545m (Yahoo) versus A$201m in FY24. Revenue has grown from A$1.02bn (FY22) to A$1.65bn (FY25), and net income swung from a A$186m loss (FY24) to A$254m profit (FY25), suggesting the earnings trough tied to weaker gold realisations and elevated operating charges may have passed.

Regis also screens as a dividend compounder (3.2% yield; Dividend Growth model pass) with net-cash optionality after debt repayment. For GARP-oriented value investors, Lynch/Neff PEG passes imply growth is not fully priced at sub-9× earnings — provided FY26–27 production guidance converts to sustained margins. The May 2026 merger presentation body (classified “other” in the filing index) showed Regis standalone with A$1.13bn cash, no debt, and FY26 guidance of 350–380koz at AISC of A$2,610–2,990/oz, supporting the screen’s quality and risk passes.

Caveat: no annual report or interim results body is present in the filing index; screen-to-filing reconciliation cannot be completed in this pack.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Strategic / M&A

- Regis declined to match Genesis Minerals’ US$3.9bn rival bid for Vault Minerals (“Regis Resources Won’t Match Genesis Bid For Vault Minerals”, WSJ via yfinance, 12 July 2026). The indexed Vault filing body (13 July 2026) confirms Regis will not counter; Vault intends to accept Genesis. A break fee of approximately A$50.7m is payable to Regis upon Vault’s termination of the scheme implementation deed. Standalone Regis loses the pro forma >700koz/year scale-up, tax synergies (>A$500m anticipated), and senior-producer re-rating envisaged in the May 2026 merger presentation — a material strategic setback even with compensation.
- Capital allocation uncertainty: how Regis deploys cash/bullion (McPhillamys, dividends, buybacks, or renewed M&A) is now the key governance question. Management stated the Vault counter-bid “did not meet the value and return thresholds” (Market Index, 24 July 2026).

Cyclical / operational

- Gold price and A$/US$ exposure drive revenue; macro context shows AUD/USD at 0.698 (25 July 2026) — relevant for USD gold receipts, not used to override the screen signal.
- Cost inflation: “ASX gold producer Regis is cashed up, but costs are rising” (Market Index, 24 July 2026). Q4 AISC missed estimates; FY27 AISC guidance is higher. FY24’s spike in other operating expenses (A$231m vs A$39m in FY23, Yahoo) illustrates margin vulnerability when costs run ahead of price.
- McPhillamys development risk: the May 2026 merger presentation body states that due to a Section 10 Declaration, the previous definitive feasibility study “cannot be relied upon”; an alternative tailings storage facility solution requires extensive new investigations with “no certainty of a viable alternative being realised”.
- Production risk at Duketon (transition to higher-cost underground ounces) and grade mix at Tropicana (30% JV, AngloGold-operated).

Governance / disclosure

- No annual or interim filing bodies in the research pack — cannot review going-concern statements, contingencies, covenant language, or director remuneration from primary statutory sources.
- “Gold Producer Regis Resources (ASX: RRL) Faces Investor Questions After Reporting Issue” (Kalkine, 17 July 2026) — unverified without the underlying ASX announcement body; unresolved red flag.
- CEO compensation scrutiny in press (“We Think Shareholders Will Probably Be Generous With Regis Resources Limited's (ASX:RRL) CEO Compensation”, simplywall.st, 14 November 2025) — immaterial financially but worth monitoring at AGM.

Balance-sheet / other

- Long-term provisions of A$172m (rehabilitation) — standard but material; closure-cost assumptions sensitive to discount rates and regulatory change.
- Lease obligations (~A$119m total debt per Yahoo, largely capital leases) remain.
- Pension/post-retirement obligations are small relative to equity.
- No going-concern or covenant breach language appears in any indexed filing body; the sole primary Regis-related extract is a third-party Vault transaction update.
