# Regis Resources Limited (RRL.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T12:58:28.996242+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Regis Resources is a mid-tier Australian gold producer whose quantitative profile — P/E 8.9, 3.2% yield, 29.2% ROE, and strong FCF yield — passes 16 of 22 value models and reads as a classic cyclical-quality compounder at a trough multiple. Operationally, Yahoo-sourced FY25 data show a sharp earnings recovery (net profit A$254m on revenue A$1.65bn) and balance-sheet repair (cash A$505m, debt cut by A$300m), while recent headlines cite record FY26 production of 379koz and cash plus bullion of ~A$1.18bn. The central debate is whether standalone Regis — after declining to match Genesis Minerals’ superior bid for Vault Minerals — can redeploy capital into McPhillamys and organic growth fast enough to justify re-rating, or whether the failed merger and rising cost commentary cap upside despite cheap headline multiples.

For GARP-oriented value investors, the Lynch/Neff PEG passes imply growth is not fully priced at sub-9× earnings — provided FY26–27 production guidance (headlines cite lifted FY27 outlook) converts to sustained margins.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Strategic / M&A**
- Regis **declined to match** Genesis Minerals’ US$3.9bn rival bid for Vault Minerals (WSJ/yfinance, 12 July 2026). The indexed filing body (Vault announcement, 13 July 2026) states Vault intends to terminate the Regis scheme and accept Genesis; a **~A$50.7m break fee** is payable to Regis. Standalone Regis loses the pro forma >700koz/year scale-up and tax synergies envisaged in the May 2026 merger — a material strategic setback even with compensation.
- Capital allocation uncertainty: how Regis deploys cash/bullion (McPhillamys, dividends, buybacks, or renewed M&A) is now the key governance question.
