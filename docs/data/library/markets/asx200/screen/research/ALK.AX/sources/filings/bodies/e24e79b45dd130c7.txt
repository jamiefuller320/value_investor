Alkane Drilling Confirms and Extends Gold-Antimony 
Mineral System at Nagambie, Victoria  
Perth, Western Australia - Alkane Resources Limited (ASX: ALK; TSX: ALK; OTCQX: ALKRY) (‘Alkane’) is pleased 
to announce the initial exploration results from an earn-in period in which Alkane has agreed to initially spend a total 
of $12.5M on exploration and evaluation activities to earn a 60% interest in the Nagambie property over three years, 
with the right to move to 80% with further expenditure.1 
 
Program Summary 
 Drilling commenced at Nagambie in June 2026 targeting the north-south trending “C” vein orientation, which is 
interpreted to contain gold-antimony mineralisation. 3 holes (1,868m) have been drilled, logged and sampled at 
the time of this announcement. 
 Initial assays received to date for the first of three new Alkane holes, confirm the highly mineralised nature and 
continuity of the epizonal Au-Sb system.  
 Standout grades have been encountered in the C3 and C2 veins that support previous drilling results that informed 
the initial Mineral Resource2 estimated by Nagambie Resources in 2024. Additionally, the C2 zone has been 
intercepted a further 115m below the previously known extent, highlighting the open nature of the resource.  
 Further to the concentrated high-grade mineralisation intercepted (Costerfield analogue), the drilling has also 
intercepted wide mineralised zones of up to 32m in estimated true width (ETW), containing significant amounts of 
both gold and antimony.

Nagambie Gold-Antimony Deposit 
Nagambie Resources Ltd 100%, Alkane Resources Earn-In. 
The Nagambie Gold-Antimony deposit was discovered during systematic exploration for bulk-tonnage, low-grade gold 
mineralisation in the mid-1980s by East Union Pty Ltd (later renamed Perseverance Mining Pty Ltd). Subsequent 
geological prospecting and rock-chip sampling identified anomalous gold associated with intense silicification and 
quartz veining at Hill 158 in 1984, where a resistant outcrop marked the position of the mineralised structure.

the East and West pits to exploit the oxide gold mineralisation. Mining continued until 1994, with a total of 123,000 
ounces of gold produced from the operation.3 
Exploration for primary sulphide mineralisation began in 2006, when Panaegis Gold Mines Ltd (later renamed 
Nagambie Resources Ltd) drilled beneath historic pits. The programme intersected gold-antimony mineralisation, 
including high-grade stibnite-bearing veins beneath the West Pit in an RC hole. However, the geometry of the 
mineralised structures was not well-established, and so follow-up drilling did not test this intercept. 
In 2018 Nagambie Resources Ltd renewed investigations, including diamond drilling and induced polarisation 
geophysics. Re-interpretation of the earlier drilling in 2022 suggested that the high-grade gold-antimony veins could 
have a north-northwest orientation, and in April 2022, the company targeted diamond drilling beneath the West Pit to 
test for Costerfield-style gold-antimony veins (C-suite).  
The program identified the C1 lode system in 2022, followed by C2 and C3 in 2023. Drilling also discovered the N1 
lode, which strikes east-west. These discoveries established multiple gold-antimony lode systems beneath the historic 
oxide workings.  
The discoveries supported a maiden JORC Inferred Mineral Resource announced by Nagambie Minerals Ltd on 20 
May 2024 titled ‘Maiden JORC Resource at the Nagambie Mine’. Nagambie Minerals Ltd released an announcement 
on 15 November 2024 titled ‘Replacement Announcement of Gold-Antimony Resource Drilling’ advising a resource 
update to 539,000 tonnes at 3.3 g/t gold and 3.9% antimony, containing approximately 58,000 ounces of gold and 
20,800 tonnes of antimony. The estimate relates to mineralisation beneath the West Pit and remains open to the east, 
west and at depth.  
In April 2026, Nagambie Resources entered into an earn-in agreement with Alkane Resources Limited, through its 
wholly owned subsidiary Mandalay Resources Costerfield Operations Pty Ltd, covering the Nagambie Mine and 
surrounding exploration license. The agreement provides for Alkane to sole-fund exploration and evaluation activities, 
with the potential to earn a 60% or 80% interest in the tenements. Alkane commenced diamond drilling at the 
Nagambie Mine in June 2026, targeting extensions to the C1, C2, C3 and N1 lodes. 
 
Deposit Geology  
The Nagambie Gold-Antimony Deposit is hosted by folded Silurian-Lower Devonian marine sedimentary rocks within 
the Melbourne Structural Zone of central Victoria, approximately 7 km east of Nagambie. Gold-antimony mineralisation 
is hosted by folded Silurian-Lower Devonian marine sedimentary rocks comprising sandstone, siltstone and shale. 
Younger cover obscures parts of the underlying bedrock geology.  
The deposit is associated with a broadly east-west -trending, doubly plunging anticlinal structure. Principal east-west 
structural features include the Nagambie Mine Thrust (NMT), the Central Anticline, and the 303 Zone. The north-
dipping NMT is associated with the historically mined oxide gold mineralisation, while the underlying antimony-sulphide 
system comprises several interpreted lode systems with distinct orientations and spatial distributions (Figure 5).  
The distribution of veining and sulphide mineralisation appears to be influenced by lithological and rheological 
contrasts within the sedimentary sequence. Sandstone-rich intervals are commonly silicified, fractured and host 
extensive veining, whereas finer-grained shale and siltstone units locally host bedding-parallel laminated quartz 
structures and associated sericite alteration. Marker shale units identified in the detailed stratigraphic log provide 
constraints for interpreting the folded sequence and its relationship to mineralised structures. These observations 
suggest that host-rock competency, stratigraphic position and structural architecture collectively influence the 
distribution and style of mineralisation.  
Lodes are numbered from east to west as C1, C2 and C3 (north-south striking) and N1 (east-west striking). C1 (green) 
strikes north-south, and its southern extent remains unconfirmed by the drilling shown in Figure 3. C2 (orange) also 
strikes north-south and is supported by confirmed drilling intersections in ADD001, ADD006 and ADD013. C3 (red) 
comprises steeply west-dipping, north-south-striking mineralisation and includes the high-grade intercept in ADD001 
illustrated in Figure 7. N1 (blue) is an east-west-striking lode that dips south.

Gold-antimony mineralisation occurs in a range of quartz, quartz-carbonate and stibnite-bearing breccias and 
stockwork networks. Observed vein and mineralisation styles include: 
• 
Quartz-matrix-supported breccias: Brecciated host rock within a quartz matrix, locally containing 
contrasting grey and translucent quartz. These breccias transition into crackle breccias at their margins, where 
closely spaced fractures are infilled by translucent quartz (Figure 8).  
• 
West-dipping, south-plunging faults: Composite quartz-filled structures displaying laminated, brecciated 
and puggy textures and associated stockwork veining, stibnite and locally strong arsenopyrite and pyrite. 
Figure 7 illustrates a high-grade intercept of this style.  
• 
Bedding-parallel laminated quartz faults: Quartz-filled structures parallel to bedding, locally associated with 
moderate to intense sericite alteration.  
• 
Stibnite-bearing tension and shear veins: Steeply-dipping veins associated with shearing or extension, with 
stibnite forming a prominent component of the vein fill. 
• 
Stibnite-carbonate tension veins: Veins containing stibnite and carbonate, locally with stibnite concentrated 
along the vein center. 
• 
Vuggy, open space veins: Veins preserving open-space textures and late mineral growth, including quartz 
crystals, acicular stibnite and calcite. 
Stibnite is the principal antimony-bearing mineral, with disseminated pyrite and arsenopyrite occurring in association 
with mineralised structures. Pyrite exhibits a range of textures, including coarse-grained cubic and dodecahedral 
crystals, fine-grained aggregates, nodular or framboidal forms and vein-hosted occurrences. Quartz and carbonate 
assemblages vary between veins, with multiple carbonate generations recognised in core and sphalerite observed 
locally.  
The diversity of vein textures, sulphide assemblages and carbonate generations indicates a complex mineralisation 
history consistent with episodic fracture opening and fluid flow. The relative timing and genetic relationships of the 
different vein styles remain under investigation.

Geology 
• 
Deposit type, geological setting and style of mineralisation. 
The Nagambie Gold-Antimony Deposit is a structurally controlled gold-antimony system hosted 
by folded Silurian-Lower Devonian marine sedimentary rocks within the Melbourne Structural 
Zone of central Victoria. Mineralisation occurs in quartz, quartz-carbonate and stibnite-bearing 
veins, breccias and stockwork networks, with stibnite the principal antimony-bearing mineral 
and pyrite and arsenopyrite also present as a halo of disseminated grains.  
Mineralisation is associated with an east-west -trending, doubly plunging anticlinal structure and 
a network of east-west and north-south structures. The north-dipping Nagambie Mine Thrust is 
associated with the historically mined oxide gold mineralisation, while the underlying sulphide 
system comprises north-south -striking, steeply west-dipping C-system lodes and the east-west -
striking, south-dipping N1 lode.  
The distribution and style of veining appear to be influenced by structural architecture and 
rheological contrasts within the sedimentary sequence. Sandstone-rich intervals are commonly 
silicified, fractured and extensively veined, while finer-grained shale and siltstone units locally 
host bedding-parallel quartz structures and sericite alteration. Brecciation, stockwork veining 
and open-space vein textures are consistent with episodic fracture development and fluid flow. 
The relative timing and genetic relationships of the different mineralisation styles remain under 
investigation.

Arsenic is present in the Nagambie Project in form of arsenopyrite and arsenian pyrite and is 
likely to have a close relationship to a portion of the gold mineralisation. Gold deportment is 
currently being studied. In the absence of this data the mineralisation of Costerfield is 
considered a close analogue and so recovery information stated within this document is based 
on the performance of Costerfield ore.
