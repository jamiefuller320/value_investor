ANNUAL REPORT  ABOUT ALKANE
About Alkane
We are an Australia-based resources group with interests in gold, 
antimony and copper and three operating mines across Australia  
and Sweden.
Alkane Resources Ltd is the parent 
entity of the Alkane group, a gold and 
antimony producer in Australia and Sweden. 
Our wholly owned producing assets are 
the T omingley open pit and underground 
gold mine southwest of Dubbo in Central 
West New South Wales (around 310km 
northwest of Sydney), the Costerfield 
gold and antimony underground mining 
operation northeast of Heathcote in 
Central Victoria (around 105km north 
of Melbourne), and the Björkdal 
underground gold mine northwest of 
Skellefteå in Sweden (approximately 
750km north of Stockholm).
Alkane also owns the very large gold-
copper porphyry Boda-Kaiser Project 
in Central West New South Wales and 
holds several highly prospective gold and 
copper tenements in Central West New 
South Wales.
Alkane is headquartered in Perth, 
Western Australia. The company was 
incorporated in 1969 and merged with 
Mandalay Resources in August 2025.

ANNUAL REPORT  MESSAGE FROM THE CHAIR
Message from the Chair
It is my privilege to present my first Chair’s Full-Year Statement  
to you, marking the conclusion of a truly transformational year 
for Alkane Resources.
Dear Shareholder,
The year ended 30 June 2026 was the year in which Alkane 
came of age — evolving from a single-mine Australian gold 
producer into a genuinely multi-asset, multi-jurisdictional 
gold and antimony company with three high-quality operating 
mines, a very strong balance sheet, and for the first time in the 
company’s history, a dividend in the hands of shareholders.
A merger of equals, delivered
On 5 August 2025, Alkane completed its merger of equals with 
Mandalay Resources Corporation, bringing together Alkane’s 
Tomingley gold mine in New South Wales with Mandalay’s 
Costerfield gold-antimony operation in Victoria and Björkdal 
gold mine in Sweden.
The all-share transaction saw former Mandalay shareholders and existing Alkane shareholders emerge 
owning approximately 55% and 45% of the combined company respectively. The enlarged Alkane 
began trading on the ASX under its existing listing and, shortly after, on the Toronto Stock Exchange, 
broadening our access to North American capital markets.
I was honoured to be appointed Non-Executive Chair on completion of the merger, and I want to 
record at the outset my thanks to the Boards, management teams and employees of both legacy 
companies for an integration that has, by any measure, exceeded expectations. Rarely does a “merger 
of equals” deliver so quickly on its stated rationale of scale, diversification and balance sheet strength 
— but that is precisely what FY2026 demonstrated.
Record operating and financial performance
The numbers speak for themselves. For the year ended 30 June 2026, Alkane delivered:
• Net profit after tax of A$228.7 million, an increase of 592% on the prior year
• Revenue of A$935.8 million, up 257% year-on-year
• Basic earnings per share of 17.69 cents, up 224%
• Gold-equivalent production of 162,440 ounces, up 134.5%, with gold-equivalent sales of 164,878  
ounces, up 136%
• Cashflows from operating activities of $460.3 million for the year, and
• Cash, bullion and listed investments of A$453.9 million at year end, with the company remaining  
effectively debt-free.
Each of our three mines pulled its weight. Tomingley, our flagship New South Wales operation, 
exceeded its production guidance, delivering 82,973 ounces of gold against a target range of  
75,000 to 80,000 ounces, and successfully progressed the Newell Highway realignment project

Costerfield continued its long track record of consistent, high-margin production, whilst discovering 
further high-grade deposits such as Brunswick South. Björkdal, meanwhile, has invested in major 
underground development that will result in significant productivity improvements going forward. The 
operation is also preparing a satellite open pit mining area aimed at lowering its overall cost profile in 
2027 and developing plans for opening a major new underground development to Storheden that is

Underpinning all of this was a gold price environment without recent precedent. The gold price rose from 
around US$3,300/oz at the start of the financial year to briefly exceed US$5,500/oz intraday in January 
2026 — an extraordinary, if volatile, rally — before settling around the US$4,000/oz level by year end.
In Australian dollar terms, the price we realised on our sales rose steadily through the year, reaching 
over A$6,300/oz by the March quarter and averaging A$5,664/oz for the full year. Combined with firm 
antimony prices, averaging over A$30,000 per tonne through the second half, this provided a powerful 
tailwind to our margins — one that our larger, multi-asset platform was well positioned to capture in full.
Returning capital to shareholders
A particular highlight of the year, and one I know will be welcomed by many of you, is that the Board 
declared Alkane’s maiden dividend — a fully franked final dividend of 2.0 cents per share, payable on 
1 October 2026. This reflects both the strength of FY2026 earnings and the Board’s confidence in the 
durability of the company’s cash generation.
Alongside the dividend, the Board approved an on-market share buy-back of up to A$50 million, to be 
conducted at the company’s discretion over the next 12 months.
Together, these represent Alkane’s first direct capital returns to shareholders and an important 
milestone in the company’s evolution: Alkane has moved from being purely a growth and exploration 
story to a company that can fund its growth internally while also directly rewarding our shareholders.
These decisions reflect the three capital allocation priorities the Board has now formally adopted — 
reinvestment in the business, growing shareholder returns, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. 
We will continue to balance direct returns to shareholders against the compelling organic growth 
opportunities within our own portfolio.
Growing the resource base
Exploration success in FY2026 gives me particular confidence in the years ahead. At Costerfield, 
extension and infill drilling at the Brunswick South deposit — located just 200 metres from existing 
underground infrastructure — returned some outstanding high-grade gold and antimony intercepts, 
including grades in excess of 50g/t gold with substantial antimony credits. Capital development 
toward Brunswick South is scheduled to begin in the first quarter of FY2027, with an updated Mineral 
Resource and initial Ore Reserve to follow.
At Björkdal, near-mine exploration through the year continues to extend mine life on attractive 
economics, and we announced a further increase to the Storheden resource just after year end. In New 
South Wales, work continued to advance the Boda-Kaiser porphyry project through environmental 
studies towards a future project approval application — a longer-dated but potentially significant 
growth option for the company.
Safety, risk and sustainability
All of this activity must occur safely and responsibly, and that principle sat behind the operational 
decisions the Board and management took through the year. Safety remains, and will always remain, 
a very high priority across all three sites, and integrating the safety cultures and systems of two 
previously separate organisations into a single, consistent standard was a significant undertaking that 
the team managed well through FY2026 and will continue to develop throughout FY2027.

We also used the year to materially strengthen the company’s risk management framework, 
appropriate to the scale and complexity Alkane now operates at as a multi-jurisdictional producer.
In addition, Alkane published its first Sustainability Report focused on climate-related financial

Board and governance
The Board was strengthened through the year to reflect the scale and complexity of the enlarged 
company. Frazer Bourchier and Brad Mills, formerly CEO/President and Chair of Mandalay Resources

former Chair of Alkane, ensuring valuable continuity of knowledge across the newly combined asset base.

experience. The Board’s oversight of these areas has already been sharpened, and I am grateful for the 
rigour she has brought to that role in a short space of time.
Just after year end, I was also delighted to welcome Dr Jennifer Purdie and the Hon. Gabrielle Upton 
to the Board, further deepening our capability in operations, capital markets and governance as Alkane

Outlook
We enter FY2027 from a position of genuine strength: three producing mines, a resource base that 
continues to grow through the drill bit, no debt, a growing cash position, and now a dividend-paying 
track record.
The company has announced FY2027 guidance of 163,000 to 177,000 ounces gold-equivalent 
production, broadly consistent with the platform we have now established, alongside growth capital 
expenditure of A$160–190 million and exploration spend of A$55–65 million. In parallel, we continue 
to evaluate disciplined, tier-one jurisdiction merger and acquisition opportunities that meet our strict 
criteria for value accretion. 
The maiden dividend and buy-back give shareholders a first, tangible sense of what continued success 
on this scale can mean directly for them, and the Board intends this to be the beginning of a growing, 
sustainable pattern of capital returns rather than a one-off. Alkane’s stated ambition to consolidate 
further within the mid-tier gold and antimony space remains firmly in place, underpinned by the 
balance sheet strength this transformational year has given us.

former Mandalay employee across Australia and Sweden for the safe, disciplined operational delivery 
that made these results possible.
Finally, I thank you, our shareholders, for your continued support and patience through a period 
of significant corporate change — support that has been reflected in the company’s share price 
performance over the year, and one which the Board does not take for granted.
I look forward to reporting further progress in the year ahead.

Ian Chalmers retires
Long-serving former Executive Director, Ian Chalmers, announced 
his retirement from Alkane earlier this year. We extend gratitude and 
recognition for his immense contributions to the company and broader 
mining industry over decades.
After more than three decades with Alkane, 
inspirational former director Ian Chalmers has retired 
from the company.
Ian’s considerable expertise, passion, tenacity and 
drive have fuelled Alkane’s successes over many years. 
He has been formational in setting Alkane’s culture 
of technical discipline and calm confidence that has 
allowed the company to thrive, and his presence will 
be sorely missed.
His association with Alkane began over 30 years ago, 
initially as a consultant and Technical Director, then 
Managing Director from 2006 to 2017. He stepped 
back to the role of Technical Director in September 
2017 and retired from the Board following the merger 
with Mandalay in August 2025.
Over three decades, Ian steered Alkane’s minerals 
exploration and development efforts across Australia. 
His extensive geological knowledge and experience, 
combined with tremendous instincts and optimism, 
means Ian has often seen opportunities where others 
did not. One of his more strategic moves in the 
early 1990s was to focus exploration on the Lachlan 
Fold Belt of NSW, which at the time was relatively 
unexplored. He acquired several key tenements 
being relinquished that have proven highly fertile 
(including Boda-Kaiser, Tomingley and McPhillamy’s) 
and recognised the significance of the Dubbo Project 
(divested by Alkane in 2020) for Australia’s critical 
metals industry.
Throughout his distinguished career, Ian demonstrated 
a strong commitment to education and knowledge 
sharing to help train the next generation of geologists 
and broaden the geological knowledge base. He also 
drove initiatives to minimise the environmental impact 
of mining.
Ian’s substantial legacy will help sustain Alkane for 
many years to come. We wish him all the best in his 
retirement and future endeavours.

Calculation of gold equivalent
Gold equivalent ounces are calculated by multiplying quantities of gold and antimony in period by respective average market price of commodities in period, adding the 
two amounts to get ‘total contained value based on market price’ and dividing that total contained value by the average market price of gold in period; i.e., AuEq = 
((Au produced x Au $/oz) + (Sb produced pre-payability x 70% payability x Sb $/t)) / (Au $/oz). The average market prices for the June quarter were A$6,349/oz Au 
(being the average of the daily PM price, sourced from www.lbma.org.uk) and A$30,675/t Sb (being the average Shanghai Metal Market Price sourced from 
www.metal.com), using an AUD:USD exchange rate of 0.7098. Average market prices for the March, December and September quarters of FY2026 were A$7,015/oz 
Au and A$29,449/t Sb; A$6,299/oz Au and A$30,245/t Sb; and A$5,283/oz Au and A$33,508/t Sb respectively, using AUD:USD exchange rates of 0.6946, 0.6565 
and 0.6544. Metallurgical recoveries for gold and antimony are well established through current and historical plant performance, with actual annual recoveries set 
out for the statutory reporting period on page 16 (also page 110) and the full-year period on page 111. It is Alkane’s opinion that all the elements included in the metal 
equivalent calculation have a reasonable potential to be recovered and sold.

(a) Revenue
The group recognises revenue from gold sales when it satisfies the performance obligation of transferring control 
of the gold inventory to the customer. The group’s assessment is that this generally occurs once the sales contract 
has been entered into and the customer has taken physical possession of the gold, as this is the point at which the 
customer obtains the ability to direct the use of, and obtain substantially all of the remaining benefits from, the 
asset. The transaction price is determined based on the agreed price and the number of ounces delivered. Payment 
is due upon delivery under the sales contract. The group generates revenue from the sale of gold bullion and metal 
concentrates.
Tomingley sells gold to two customers, Macquarie Bank and ABC Refinery, with revenue from these customers 
(excluding derivative options) of $106.8m and $313.0m respectively in 2026 (2025: $91.6m and $171.4m). 
Costerfield’s revenue is materially derived from two customers, CPG Group ($166.4m) and China National Gold 
Group ($64.2m). Björkdal’s revenue is derived from two customers, Aurubis ($198.5m) and Boliden ($51.6m).
Gold bullion sales
Revenue from gold bullion sales is recognised at a point in time when control of the bullion is transferred to the 
customer. Control is generally transferred in accordance with the terms of the sales agreement and occurs when the 
customer obtains the ability to direct the use of and obtain substantially all of the remaining benefits from the gold.
Concentrate sales (gold and antimony) 
Revenue from concentrate sales is recognised when control of the goods is transferred to the customer at an 
amount that reflects the consideration to which the company expects to be entitled in exchange for those goods. 
The company has generally concluded that it is the principal in its revenue contracts because it typically controls 
the goods or services before transferring them to the customer. Concentrate sales are typically provisionally priced 
at the date of sale, with subsequent price adjustments recognised as set out in (b) below.
(b) Provisional pricing adjustments
Sales of certain commodities (gold and antimony) are provisionally priced such that the price is not settled until a 
predetermined future date based on the market price at that time. Revenue on these sales is initially recognised 
at the prevailing market price. The receivables relating to provisionally priced sales are marked to market at each 
reporting date using the forward price for the period equivalent to that outlined in the contract and by using the 
current market price at the end of each reporting period. This mark to market adjustment is recognised in revenue 
but is not considered to be revenue from contracts with customers.
