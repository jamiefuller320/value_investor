Statement Summary 
 Mineral Resources and Ore Reserves have been estimated for the underground operations of the Tomingley Gold 
Mine as at the 30th of June 2026.  
 For the Tomingley Project, Total Ore Reserves have increased by 3.2% to 641,000 ounces of gold. 
 The increase in Ore Reserve is net of a depletion of 95 koz through site record production of 80 Koz. 
 Growth of the Resources and Reserves is due to a combination of exploration and grade control processes within 
Roswell undertaken during FY26 as well as rising gold price assumptions. 
 Mineral Resources and Ore Reserves of the San Antonio open cut pit have  not been updated due to incomplete 
grade control drilling. The continuation of this drilling is pending the completion of the Newell Highway realignment. 
 Peak Hill Mineral Resource also remains as previously estimated 1 due to no further significant exploration. 
 Mineral Resources for Boda and Kaiser have not been updated due to insufficient further drilling and are not 
described within this document. For details on Boda and Kaiser see the October 2025 ASX release2.

Tomingley Gold Operations (TGO) 
The Tomingley Gold Operation (TGO) is located on the Newell Highway, two kilometres south of the town of 
Tomingley.  Tomingley is 54km southwest of Dubbo and 67km north of Parkes, in the Central West region of New 
South Wales. TGO’s mining operations are currently focussed on the Roswell and Caloma deposits , and this forms a 
small portion of the Tomingley Gold Project (TGP) exploration licences (Figure 1). 
The Tomingley gold deposits are located within a tightly folded Ordovician volcano-sedimentary sequence named the 
Mingelo Volcanics that has been altered to a sericite- carbonate-albite-quartz-pyrite-arsenopyrite assemblage, typical 
of orogenic lode- style gold deposits. Mineralised fluids are interpreted to have been focused by differential strain in 
and around andesitic volcanics due t o the rheological competency contrast between the volcanics and the bounding 
volcaniclastic sediments. The brittle nature of the volcanics often leads to the development of shear -hosted sheeted 
quartz vein and breccia deposits within and adjacent to the volcanic bodies. Separately, thin carbonaceous mudstone 
strata appear to have been a focus for shearing and a chemical trap for gold.  
Development at Tomingley has been based on the Wyoming One, Wyoming Three, Caloma One, Caloma Two and 
Roswell gold deposits. To date, mining occurred underground at Wyoming One, Caloma One, Caloma Two and 
Roswell deposits. Roswell stope ore production came on stream in April 2024 (ASX Announcement 22 April 2024) via 
an approximately 3 km decline from the Wyoming One open cut. Undeveloped gold resources exist at the San Antonio 
and McLeans deposits.

All underground orebodies are accessed using declines driven from the Wyoming One Pit. Three mining methods are 
predominately used for mining the underground portion of the Roswell and Caloma resource being Longhole Open 
Stoping (LHOS) with full paste fill, cemented rockfill or no fill using pillars. The choice of mining method is determined 
by value of the resource, orebody width, proximity to the pastefill plant and geotechnical factors.

Stoping configurations are predominantly single or double lift stoping (25m vertical interval) with strike length of 20-
30m. The stoping method (as illustrated in Figure 2) involves establishing a slot using conventional long-hole drill and 
blast techniques and then the stopes mined in a retreat sequence along strike to the central access. The stopes are 
filled or a pillar left prior to the adjacent stope being mined. The installation of brow cables and the use of a concurrent 
strike-retreat blasting sequence, and use of fill or adequately designed pillars control ground stability.

Electrical infrastructure servicing TGO can deliver 15MW. The site currently uses 10MW; this falls within the current 
15MW peak allowance. Underground mining currently uses 4.5MW, power is reticulated to Wyoming One, using a 
1.3km high voltage cable from the mill. Power to the Caloma orebodies is provided by a further 600m extension of the 
high voltage cable along the access drive. 
Tailings are deposited into Stage 1 of RSF2 (tailing storage facility) which has a capacity of ~3.4Mt and adequate for 
processing until mid-2027 at current production rates. An additional 10.5Mt of tailings storage capacity can be realised 
by raising RSF2 to it full design height.  
At Tomingley Gold Operations, ore is trucked to the Processing Plant ROM pad adjacent to the Wyoming Three pit, 
where it is processed through a mobile two- stage crushing circuit, which feeds a fixed two- stage crushing circuit to 
provide feed to the ball mill. The ground slurry is classified using hydrocyclones, with free gold recovered via a gravity 
circuit incorporating a Knelson concentrator. Gravity concentrates are treated separately through intensive leaching,

while cyclone overflow is processed through flotation. The flotation concentrate undergoes ultrafine grinding before 
being recombined with the flotation tailings. The combined slurry is then treated in a carbon- in-leach (CIL) circuit for 
gold extraction. Loaded carbon is processed through elution, electrowinning, and smelting to produce doré bars from 
both the gravity and leaching circuits. Finally, the tailings stream undergoes cyanide detoxification before being either 
deposited to the tailings storage facility or processed into cemented paste fill for underground backfilling.

Hill Gold Mine (PHGM) was a fully operational open pit gold mine that is currently under care and maintenance with
