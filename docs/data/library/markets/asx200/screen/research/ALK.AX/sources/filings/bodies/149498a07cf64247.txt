Despite the recovering in the benchmark index during the session, in the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers lagged decliners miserably by 96 to 180. The market breadth kinda tells the story today. Well, the market breadth and the mix of sectors that added / recovered enough points to skew the benchmark towards a "respectable" decline. "Defensives" are sectors that tend to see resilient earnings regardless of the state of the economy or stage of the business cycle. They deal in essentials, like healthcare, consumer staples, utilities, etc. Arguably, the big banks are defensive bloc in our market, given their size and track record for delivering stable fully franked dividends. Look at some of the sectors that did well today (in a down market): Consumer Staples (XSJ) (+1.1%) Health Care (XHJ) (+0.9%) Utilities (XUJ) (+0.5%) S&P-ASX 200 Health Care Sector Index (XHJ) intraday chart Sure, Financials (XFJ) (-0.5%) were down, tracking a 0.8% decline in their US counterparts overnight. But consider that they climbed 0.84% from their lows... that's a bunch of important index points added back... S&P-ASX 200 Financials Sector Index (XFJ) intraday chart It's great that defensive stocks are strong right now, particularly if you happen to own them, but also due to the support their strength provides the index. But consider that there's usually something not quite right in the local or global economy if investors are allocating capital with a defensive mindset. We know that a pile of cash from superannuation guarantee flows into the itsy-bitsy-by-global-standards Australian share market each day. It's a pile of cash with few opportunities (and its shrinking with all the takeovers this year!). Yet the cash must go somewhere. So, we often get days like this where defensives are up and the more growth-oriented areas of the market are weak (i.e., due to all the bad stuff that's troubling those same investors who are diverting cash into defensives!). Take Materials (XMJ) (-1.2%) for example 👇 S&P-ASX 200 Materials Sector Index (XMJ) intraday chart And Energy (XEJ) (-1.3%) , although arguably, this is a sector prone to plenty of volatility given current world events. Consider that if you sell some growth stocks, you end up with cash. That kinda defeats the goal of allocating all that capital coming in. So, you kinda have to buy even more defensive stocks. You're effectively robbing Peter to pay Paul. Alas, such is the nature of the Old Tin Pot! 🤦 S&P-ASX 200 Energy Sector Index (XEJ) intraday chart Now, if you've been suffering through my rantings here in the Evening Wrap long enough, you may have started to pick which sectors are likely to prosper or falter on a day depending on which way the winds of global risk appetite are blowing. I hope this is the case! Consider that stock picking is important, but even the best stock in the worst sector could find itself underperforming an average stock in the best sector. So, it's important to know which sectors the big fund managers are being forced to buy / favouring, and which sectors they're being forced to sell / avoid.
