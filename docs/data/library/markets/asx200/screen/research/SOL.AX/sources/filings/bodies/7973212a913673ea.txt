Brent is hovering around breakeven near US$103 a barrel. There were few US-Iran developments during our session, with the latest being President Pezeshkian's refusal to allow freedom of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz while sanctions and the US blockade remain in place Healthcare (+0.25%) has traded in an extremely tight range, aside from a 3% skid between 9 and 11 September that it recovered over the following three sessions. It makes you wonder whether the sector, and CSL in particular, can break out again after doubling from its early June lows Tech (+0.25%) and Discretionary (+0.1%) had a somewhat encouraging session, with both reversing steep early declines. Both have tumbled around 15% from their early to mid-August highs, so this is nothing exciting, just a reversal and a possible bottoming process (if they've bottomed at all) Financials (-0.8%) are looking quite bearish, trading around 2% from a fresh 16-month low. CBA has already started to roll over, undercutting its May low to close at its lowest level since 4 February Materials (-1.4%) staged a small recovery from a session low of -2.2%, but never stood a chance after copper and gold fell 1.5% and 1.6% overnight. The US Dollar Index also rose 0.5% to 101.11, its highest since 29 July. The Materials sector has whipsawed all year, and despite a 4% bounce in recent days, is on the backfoot again Real Estate (-1.9%) snapped a three-day winning streak in which it gained 2.1%, following a roughly 14% skid since 5 August. The sector is now trading at its lowest since January 2024, weighed down by soaring yields, the Bathla collapse and falling property prices. It's hard to have conviction here until bond yields show clearer signs of topping As for interesting stocks today, a few results-driven movers, including: Soul Patts (+6.2%) opened 0.9% lower at $45.08 before closing at a record $48.33, taking its year-to-date gain to 31.4%. FY26 post-tax NAV rose 31.4% to $14.5bn, net cash flow from investments climbed 11.5% to $572m and dividends grew for a 28th straight year Premier Investments (+7.0%) slid 5.2% in early trade on a better-than-feared FY26 result. Premier Retail EBIT of $175.9m was in line with ests, while full-year dividends of 81cps beat 73.6cps ests Tuas (+0.2%) briefly bounced 9.2%, this follows a 23% results-day selloff on Wednesday amid M1 regulatory concerns.
