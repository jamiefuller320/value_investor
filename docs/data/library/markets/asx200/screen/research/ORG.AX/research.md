# Origin Energy Limited (ORG.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T12:26:32.165589+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Origin Energy is an integrated Australian energy company spanning retail energy markets, LNG (via equity-accounted APLNG), and international retail through Octopus Energy. The quantitative screen rates the stock a buy on cheapness (P/E 13.3, P/B 2.1), dividend yield (~5.0%), quality (ROE 15.7%), and moderate leverage (D/E 49%), though a dividend overlay adjusts the headline signal to Hold given weak FCF yield (3.9%) and earnings-quality failures. The investment case rests on a recovering earnings base, a fully franked interim dividend of A$0.30, and retail cash generation offsetting cyclical LNG weakness. The central debate is whether statutory profitability and the 5% yield are sustainable when reported free cash flow turned sharply negative in FY25 and net debt rose materially. Primary filing coverage is thin—only a dividend announcement is indexed—so financial trends below rely on Yahoo fallback data and should be verified against the full-year and half-year reports before trading.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Origin presents a classic “quality at a reasonable price” profile within Australian utilities: nine of 22 screen models pass, spanning cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families, with high data quality (19/20 metrics). Graham Enterprising, Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Buffett Quality, High Dividend Yield, and Financial Health all pass—suggesting the market is not fully pricing a business that has rebuilt statutory earnings from the FY22 impairment trough (Yahoo: net loss A$1.43bn) to FY25 profit of A$1.48bn.

Valuation appears undemanding versus sector peers (sector-relative composite 59% vs stock 54%). At P/E 13.3 and yield ~5.0%, the stock offers income plus modest growth (earnings growth ~1.2% on screen inputs), without deep-value distress pricing. ROE of 15.7% and debt/equity of 49% indicate acceptable balance-sheet health, though not fortress-like (Graham Defensive fails on current ratio 1.15 and P/B).

The buy case is reinforced by recent operating momentum: news flow (August 2026) reports profit rising despite weaker LNG-linked earnings, with retail energy performance and cash generation carrying the result—consistent with a diversified integrated model rather than pure LNG exposure. The August 2026 ASX dividend announcement confirms management’s willingness to return capital: a fully franked A$0.30 interim dividend for the half-year ended 30 June 2026, payable 2 October 2026, with DRP satisfied via on-market purchase.

What keeps this from a clean deep-value pass is cash conversion. The screen fails FCF Yield, Earnings Quality, and Piotroski F-Score (5/9): operating cash flow did not exceed net income, leverage is not declining, and gross margins are not improving. These are precisely the metrics that matter for dividend sustainability. The quantitative buy signal is therefore a starting point for accumulation, not a blind entry—particularly given absent primary financial statements in the research pack.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and policy:** Origin’s Octopus Energy expansion faces UK regulatory change; April 2026 news reports a sharply reduced FY26 Octopus earnings outlook citing regulatory changes, higher costs, and weather impacts. Australian energy retail remains subject to price caps, market intervention, and political scrutiny—particularly relevant given May 2026 political headlines (One Nation by-election), though direct Origin impact is indirect.

**Cyclical LNG exposure:** A material share of earnings comes from equity-accounted APLNG income (A$750m in FY25, down from prior years). LNG prices and production volumes drive volatility; March 2026 quarter news flagged lower LNG production and revenue. This cyclicality is not fully captured by static value screens.

**Cash flow and dividend sustainability:** FY25 negative FCF, rising net debt, and low cash (A$161m) against nearly A$1bn annual dividends create a leverage/liquidity tension. Screen failures on FCF Yield and Earnings Quality are corroborated by Yahoo data. Without interim or annual filing bodies, covenant headroom and going-concern assessments cannot be performed.

**Capital intensity:** FY25 capex nearly doubled to A$1.47bn. If elevated spend persists (renewables, grid, Octopus), FCF recovery may lag earnings recovery.

**Accounting and earnings quality:** Piotroski F-Score 5/9; OCF did not cover net income in the screen assessment. Large equity-accounted LNG earnings and A$658m “other income/expense” in FY25 (Yahoo) complicate statutory-to-cash analysis. Impairments recurred in FY22–FY25 (though smaller in FY25).

**Governance / strategic:** No governance-specific filing language available. Failed 2023 Brookfield/EIG takeover (outside this news window) remains a backdrop for capital-allocation debate.

**Filing gap risk:** The research pack lacks annual and interim financial report bodies; any investment decision should treat this memo as incomplete on balance-sheet covenants, contingencies, and segment detail.
