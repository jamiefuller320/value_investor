1. FY27 GUIDANCE 
Catalyst consolidated the Plutonic Belt in July 2023  with the objective of transforming the scale, operating 
performance and long-term growth potential of the operation. Effecting this plan, over the following two years, 
Catalyst transformed its business –  turning around Plutonic’s operations around, acquiring an asset, selling an 
asset and securing A$250m in capital to fortify its balance sheet.   
By September 2025, due to the extent of the changes to the Plutonic Belt , Catalyst sought to provide investors 
with an indication of the Belt’s longer-term potential  and released 10-year guidance. As with any long-term 
guidance for an underground mine, it is general in nature.  
Since then, the team  continued to refine its understanding of the projects along the Plutonic Gold  Belt and 
develop a more detailed nearer term production plan.  This work included: delineating a 2Moz Reserve, grade 
control drilling  leading to a more detailed understanding of Trident , Cinnamon discovery, Old Highway and 
Cinnamon permitting timelines , and an improved  understanding of the Plutonic processing plant’s operating 
performance and potential throughput capacity.  This led to Catalyst communicating to investors in July 2026 
that adjustments to the September 2025 general guidance were anticipated. 
The September 2025 guidance suggested annual production in FY27 of 130koz to 150koz. This included within it, 
production from Old Highway and Trident. Since th en, a number of factors have seen a change to FY27 .  These 
include:  
• Delays to Old Highway’s permitting pushing estimated production into the next financial year; and  
• Trident’s delayed underground start  and slower development ramp up. Further, while grade control 
drilling increased the overall grade contained within the Trident Resource, the drilling did not deliver the 
same improvements at the extremities of the deposit .  This reinforces the importance of undertaking 
detailed grade control drilling and mine planning ahead of production. 
Beyond FY27, internal strategic decisions have resulted in a change to the development approach of new mines.  
These decisions are made possible because of Catalyst’s balance sheet .  It allows the Company to prioritise 
detailed mine development over accelerating production to meet previously indicated timelines. 
Accordingly, Catalyst has adopted a measured approach to the development of its underground deposits , in 
particular prioritising mine development over production so as to adequately separate development and 
production work areas . Management considers this approach to be in the best long- term interests of the 
Company and its shareholders, notwithstanding the impact on nearer-term production expectations. 
While Catalyst sees no material change to the underlying Reserves and  its ±200koz production vision, it sees a 
deferral of Trident and Old Highway’s FY27 production into FY28  for the reasons outlined above and a re -
organisation of the organic growth to 200koz.  Whether the extent of this re-organisation is material is not yet 
clear.  Work is ongoing to further understand the nearer-term production schedule. 
The Company does not expect gold production for FY27 to be linear from quarter to quarter. Gold production 
will vary throughout the year as management deal with mine plan variability and day -to-day operational 
challenges. Furthermore, expenditure will vary during the year depending on the availability of contractors, lead 
times on orders and timeliness on the completion of capital projects.

2.2 Operations 
In the September 2026 quarter, Plutonic produced 22,231oz of gold  at an AISC of A$4,267/oz ( prior quarter 
31,886oz produced at A$2,666/oz).  Multiple mines across the Plutonic Gold Belt continue to deliver ore to the 
central processing plant: Plutonic Main, Plutonic East and Keillor.  Stockpiled Trident Open Pit ore will continue 
to be processed over the remainder of the CY26 year albeit its contributions continue to reduce as stockpiles are 
diminished. 
Production for the quarter was hampered primarily by low staffing levels. An extended processing plant shut 
down period, for the purpose of improving long term throughput rates , also had an impact –  see processing 
section below. 
Lower staffing levels arose for several reasons including a  material change in flight times causing considerable 
disruption to the workforce and an increasingly competitive labour market in Western Australia. 
The consequence of these events were lower material movements across Plutonic East and Main and with their 
inherent fixed costs base, unit costs rose.  
Significant steps were taken during the quarter to address these issues with the establishment of a new internal 
recruitment function, staff performance monitoring and securing of new and more appropriate FIFO flight times. 
The results of these steps have been positive with vacancies being filled and production numbers returning to a 
more appropriate level.

production rate from ±100koz to ±200koz2.  The six ore sources to achieve this production rate are Plutonic Main,

the Trident Open Pit.  With three mines now in commercial production (Plutonic Main, Plutonic East and Keillor),

3.1 Trident Gold Project 
1.1Moz, high-grade underground Resource.  Underground development progressing. 
Trident is located 30km north-east of the underutilised Plutonic processing plant.  The underground decline is 
currently being developed to allow mining to commence in CY27. 
Trident’s Resources sits at 1.1Moz at 5.4 g/t Au and a Reserve of 524koz at 4.2g/t Au. Mine life beyond this 
would include the Trident 448koz Inferred Resource3. Trident’s inferred Resources have historically converted 
to Reserves at approximately 75%.  
The development of the Trident underground contemplated a small open pit above the underground orebody, 
acting as the boxcut/portal.  Catalyst completed mining of this small open pit during CY26 and has commenced 
construction of the underground portal and decline.  
Development rates have been slower than desired. With the single heading, rates have advanced 
approximately ±100m per month. Slower development early in the mine’s establishment is not uncommon 
given the more oxidised material closer to the surface, single heading nature of the mine and employees 
familiarising themselves with the new area.  More headings are expected to open in the coming months with 
development rates expected to improve. To date, var ying ground conditions have been experienced with 
different ground support techniques used to address  this.  These experiences give encouragement as to how 
the mine will perform in the future.

3.5 Processing plant improvements 
Catalyst is targeting an annual throughput rate of 2Mtpa from the centrally located processing plant (PP1).  A 
capital works program is underway and will continue through FY27 aimed at increasing the reliability and 
throughput of the PP1 plant. This program includes installation of a new crushing circuit (complete), upgrade to 
the elution circuit, new tails thickener, tails screen and pumps, control system and various structural 
improvements and conveyor belt upgrades. 
FY27 activities 
The activities over the last three years led to a works program being designed for FY27. The program spans 29 
projects costing ±A$30m. Some of these projects relate to throughput, some to rehabilitation of old structural 
works, and some to improved management, or modernisation, of the plant.  
Catalyst, like others, has adopted the operating philosophy that the mill should be the site’s bottleneck. That 
is not currently the case  and this give s our team encouragement that the processing target of 2Mtpa and 
beyond is achievable after various projects are completed. 
The focus within the business is to take the time in FY27 to set up the processing plant up for the long term, 
making up for years of underinvestment. In parallel to this, we work with our operating team to continually lift 
performance to new daily maximums, tighten up shut times and improve day-to-day maintenance procedures. 
This approach, we believe, will put us in good stead to cater for the excess ore to be delivered over the coming 
years.

Cinnamon is hosted in one of several repeating conglomerate units. Resource definition drilling was largely

Drilling during the quarter sought to test the repeating conglomerate units for mineralisation.  Results received

The Cinnamon trend (being a sedimentary conglomerate) sequence is distinct from other known host units on

along the belt has largely been focussed on these known host units – the Cinnamon discovery opens up 
alternative, new exploration horizons for Catalyst to target. 
 
4.3 Victorian Gold Exploration

of 70,000 at 26g/t Au10. It also includes numerous gold prospects, four of which are Boyd’s Dam, Hayanmi, Pickles 
and the Iris Zone (Figure 11 and Figure 12).   
Over the last few years, Catalyst has been gradually putting together the key elements needed to demonstrate 
a realistic and commercially viable gold project at Four Eagles. After delineating a maiden high -grade gold 
Resources in 2023, significant milestones were reached in 2025: 
- In March 2025, Catalyst secured a 17-year option to buy a 50% interest in the Maldon Processing Facility, a 
permitted and fully operational 200,000t pa processing plant 100km south of Catalyst’s high grade Four 
Eagles Gold Project; 
- In October 2025, approval was obtained from the Victorian government for the Works Plan related to the 
exploration tunnel at the Four Eagles “Boyd’s Dam” project; and 
- In November 2025, Catalyst acquired the remaining 49% interest in the Tandarra project and consolidated 
its ownership on the Bendigo Belt. 
This provides a pathway for Catalyst to create value for shareholders in an asset that was not being valued by 
the broader market. 
Drilling programs will be planned for FY27  and will target the identification of additional high -grade gold 
Resources across the belt.
