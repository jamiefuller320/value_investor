Sounds like a ripper come back, right? Well, consider that In the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers lagged decliners by a sizeable 118 to 171. You might say: “Um, that’s a bid odd… how’s that possible?” 🤔 Okay, do you want the AFR-style "this went up that much and this went down that much" ASX market wrap? Or do the want the heavily biased, cynical " Well that's just how the Old Tin Pot works ladies and gentlemen " ASX market wrap? I'll do both, because the cynical version only requires two charts (two pictures telling a thousand words!): S&P-ASX 200 Financials Sector Index (XFJ) intraday chart (top), and S&P-ASX 200 Materials Sector Index (XMJ) intraday chart (bottom) You all know what really happened today! Look at the pop in the banks in the closing price auction... Juicy! 🧃😉 Consumer Discretionary (XDJ) (+2.6%) was the session’s standout sector, driven almost entirely at the top by Temple & Webster (TPW) (+33.4%) , which surged after its trading update revealed the online furniture retailer had returned to revenue growth. Nick Scali (NCK) (+5.2%) also rebounded sharply after yesterday’s heavy sell-off, while blue-chip retail stalwart Wesfarmers (WES) (+3.8%) added further ballast to the sector. Financials (XFJ) (+2.2%) acted as the index’s safe-haven anchor, attracting capital withdrawn from the mining sector across the session. A closing auction surge helped consolidate the gains. Commonwealth Bank of Australia (CBA) (+4.3%) led the majors, with National Australia Bank (NAB) (+3.2%) , Westpac Banking Corp (WBC) (+3.1%) , and ANZ (ANZ) (+0.9%) all advancing. Communication Services (XTJ) (+1.2%) was anchored by Telstra (TLS) (+1.9%) , which also drew defensive flows as investors sought the reliability of a utility-style earnings profile amid the day’s broader risk-off tone. Utilities (XUJ) (+0.8%) also attracted defensive buying, with AGL Energy (AGL) (+2.5%) the standout and Origin Energy (ORG) (+0.7%) also advancing. Health Care (XHJ) (+0.7%) joined the defensive cohort, with Cochlear (COH) (+4.4%) the clear leader, followed by ResMed (RMD) (+3.2%) and Pro Medicus (PME) (+2.6%) . Consumer Staples (XSJ) (+0.1%) edged marginally higher, with Inghams (ING) (+0.9%) and Endeavour Group (EDV) (+0.9%) the modest gainers. The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (-5.6%) was the session’s worst performer as COMEX benchmark gold futures fell -2.2% overnight. A wave of September quarter updates compounded the damage, with the emerging theme of rising cost pressures weighing heavily. Ora Banda Mining (OBM) (-16.5%) and Black Cat Syndicate (BC8) (-15.8%) were the hardest hit, with Alkane Resources (ALK) (-10.9%) and Catalyst Metals (CYL) (-9.2%) also falling sharply. Materials (XMJ) (-3.8%) suffered a broad sell-off as ICE benchmark Brent crude oil futures hit their highest level since the Iran conflict began, rising +1.2% to US$114.30/bbl in Asian trade after surging 4% overnight. A double headwind for miners: diesel is their largest direct cost input plus higher energy prices create a drag on global economic growth. Capital rotated decisively out of the sector with South32 (S32) (-5.3%) the heaviest faller among the larger names. BHP (BHP) (-3.6%) and Rio Tinto (RIO) (-2.7%) were also lower. Battery and specialty materials stocks were caught in the same sell-off. PLS Group (PLS) (-8.1%) and IGO (IGO) (-7.4%) led lithium names lower, while Lynas Rare Earths (LYC) (-5.9%) fell sharply and Mineral Resources (MIN) (-4.3%) dropped despite posting record lithium sales in the September quarter (a reminder that strong operational results offer little shelter on a day when the whole sector is being de-risked!) In other commodities moves, COMEX benchmark copper futures fell -2.8% overnight — hitting their lowest level in more than five weeks as investors worried the AI infrastructure spending slowdown could crimp demand for key industrial metals — before partially recovering to US$6.469/lb, up +1.1% in Asian trade. COMEX benchmark gold futures rebounded +0.7% to US$4,034.10/oz in the Asian session after the overnight rout.
