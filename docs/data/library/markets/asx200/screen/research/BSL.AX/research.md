# BlueScope Steel Limited (BSL.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-09T07:20:47.042000+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
BlueScope Steel is a diversified steelmaker whose FY2026 earnings rebound was driven chiefly by North Star BlueScope in the United States, with record Southeast Asia performance and a capital-return step-up offsetting weak Australian spreads. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cheapness (P/E 17.2, P/B 1.3), dividend yield (~4.2%), and balance-sheet health (debt/equity ~16%), but quality and cash-conversion screens fail. The central debate is whether FY2026’s $1.27bn underlying EBIT and planned ~$3/share annual returns reflect a through-cycle upgrade or a cyclical peak in US spreads, with Australia still exposed to Chinese export pressure. Valuation looks reasonable on headline multiples, but not deep value; conviction is tempered by thin FCF yield, sub-12% ROE, and absent interim filing bodies in the research pack.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, BSL offers a contrarian cyclical entry where the screen’s **cheapness**, **dividend**, and **risk** families align: moderate P/E and P/B, a rebased payout policy (target ≥75% of free cash flow), and conservative leverage despite a rising net-debt profile through peak capex. Business quality is mixed. FY2026 underlying EBIT ROIC of **10.7%** (up from 6.2% in FY2025) and a $200m cost programme delivered in-year, plus a further ~$150m targeted for FY2027, support the screen’s financial-health pass. However, the name fails quality, FCF yield, deep value, earnings quality, and Piotroski (4/9) screens — consistent with a cyclical steelmaker rather than a compounder.

The buy case rests on: (1) **portfolio diversification** — North America contributed ~81% of FY2026 underlying EBITDA ($1,268m of $1,966m group underlying EBITDA per supplementary materials); (2) **capital discipline inflection** — ~$2.5bn major-project spend largely behind peak, with management guiding capex ramp-down and higher returns; (3) **shareholder returns** — completion of a $3.00/share CY2026 return plan and a repeat plan for CY2027 ($1.30 ordinary + $1.70 via buy-backs/specials). Against that, ROE remains modest (**8.1%** on screen inputs; underlying NPAT $851m on ~$10.9bn equity ≈ 7.8% per filing balance sheet), and earnings are highly spread-sensitive. The screen’s buy signal is directionally supported by the earnings and returns trajectory, but not by quality or cash-yield metrics.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclicality and spread exposure.** Management explicitly cites “sustained low Asian steel spreads, off the back of record Chinese steel exports” weighing on Australia (ASX release). North Star’s FY2026 outperformance (+101%) reflects materially stronger US spreads; 1H FY2027 guidance embeds spread assumptions that may not persist. Steel despatches fell 1% YoY despite higher EBIT — margin, not volume, drove the result.

**Geographic concentration risk.** ~81% of FY2026 underlying EBITDA originated in North America (supplementary materials). A downturn in US construction, auto, or data-centre demand — or compression in tariff-supported spreads — would disproportionately hit earnings.

**Capital programme and leverage.** Net debt rose from **$28m to $600m** during FY2026 as borrowings increased **$919m** net (cash-flow statement) while ~$1.5bn was spent on capex. ~$519m of major-project spend remains. Management lifted the net-debt target to **up to $1.5bn**; liquidity is ample ($2.8bn), but leverage is trending up through the investment cycle.

**Cash conversion / earnings quality.** Underlying NPAT of **$851m** versus net cash flow after capex of **~$240–408m** (depending on definition) explains screen failures on FCF yield, earnings quality, and Piotroski (OCF > NI failed). This is a material red flag for dividend sustainability if spreads normalise before capex falls.

**Regulatory / trade policy.** Presentation notes US tariffs “continue to support robust spreads”; conversely, news flow references Australia challenging a **12.5% US tariff** on Australian goods (July 2026). Tariff policy shifts in the US or Asia could alter the earnings bridge.

**Safety and governance.** Contractor fatality at Port Kembla in November 2025 (results presentation); TRIFR stable but potential-severity rate rising. CEO transition completed February 2026 (Tania Archibald succeeding Mark Vassella). Board rejected SGH/Steel Dynamics **A$32.35/share (~A$13.2bn)** best-and-final takeover (February 2026), removing a floor but leaving strategic uncertainty.

**Accounting / adjustments.** FY2026 underlying adjustments include **$79.8m** restructuring, **$57.0m** JV sale gain (Tata BlueScope, December 2025), and climate-investment costs. FY2025 included a large impairment write-back/charge. Underlying metrics are management-defined non-IFRS measures; investors should reconcile to reported NPAT.

**Pension.** Retirement benefit obligations **$0m** at 30 June 2026 per balance sheet — not a current red flag. Contingency detail is referenced in sustainability supplement but full annual-report body text is **not** in the research pack.

**Competitive.** Chinese overcapacity, Middle East conflict impacts on raw-material costs (noted in Australia segment), and coated-products turnaround risk (BCP North America) remain operational headwinds.
