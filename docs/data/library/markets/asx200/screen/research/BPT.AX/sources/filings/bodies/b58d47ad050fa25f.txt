It will be no surprise to regular readers that the gain in the benchmark was not reflected in the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) , as advancers lagged decliners by 134 to 140. Financials (XFJ) (+0.3%) is worth a note. The sector entered the session well-bid — up as much as 1.5% in the late morning — before the 11:30am jobs print triggered a sustained rotation out of rate-sensitive domestic stocks. Banks fell steadily through the afternoon and into the closing auction, the first session in several weeks where the sector did not receive the post-market buying order that has repeatedly cushioned its late-session performance. ASX 200 Financials (XFJ) intraday chart The major banks' gains were trimmed sharply: National Australia Bank (NAB) (+1.4%) , Westpac (WBC) (+0.8%) , ANZ (ANZ) (+0.6%) , and Commonwealth Bank (CBA) (+0.4%) all finished positive but well below intraday highs. Macquarie Group (MQG) (-0.5%) fell after CEO Shemara Wikramanayake announced her retirement in November, with head of banking and financial services Greg Ward appointed as her successor. Information Technology (XIJ) (-3.3%) was the session's worst performer, hammered by two simultaneous blows — Alphabet's after-hours result disappointed the market and sent its shares down 4% in US trading, casting doubt on near-term AI advertising spend, while the domestic jobs shock pushed Australian benchmark bond yields to 4.97%, lifting the discount rate applied to long-duration tech earnings and compressing their present value. WiseTech Global (WTC) (-7.0%) , Xero (XRO) (-5.0%) , and Technology One (TNE) (-3.7%) all fell sharply. ASX 200 Information Technology (XIJ) intraday chart Health Care (XHJ) (-1.4%) also bore the bond yield pressure — its largest constituents carry premium valuations justified by long-duration earnings growth, and higher risk-free rates are the sector's most direct valuation headwind. CSL (CSL) (-2.3%) , Cochlear (COH) (-2.2%) , and Pro Medicus (PME) (-1.6%) were all lower. ASX 200 Healthcare (XHJ) intraday chart Materials (XMJ) (+1.4%) was the session's strongest performer and the one sector that kept most of its pre-11:30am gains almost intact — commodity price leads were firm enough, and the jobs data is not an immediate negative for mining earnings, so the sector kept its bid. SGX iron ore futures added 0.6% to US$98.20/t and COMEX copper futures edged up 0.2% to US$6.505/lb, consolidating recent gains. ASX 200 Materials (XMJ) intraday chart James Hardie Industries (JHX) (+6.1%) was the standout individual mover after upgrading its June-quarter sales guidance to US$1.449–1.475 billion, well ahead of market expectations. Sandfire Resources (SFR) (+3.6%) — which reported record quarterly revenue, stronger copper-equivalent production, and a US$353 million net cash position — and South32 (S32) (+2.4%) , BHP (BHP) (+1.5%) , and Fortescue (FMG) (+1.0%) were also firmer. The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (+1.3%) also proved resilient despite a morning shock from the jobs data — gold futures had rallied 1.9% overnight and, while COMEX gold futures gave back 0.8% to US$4,120/oz in Asian trade, the sector entered the session with enough momentum to absorb the bond yield surge without surrendering its gains. Northern Star Resources (NST) (+1.9%) , Pantoro Gold (PNR) (+1.9%) , Ramelius Resources (RMS) (+1.9%) , and Evolution Mining (EVN) (+1.8%) all advanced. Energy (XEJ) (+1.0%) firmed as ICE Brent crude futures gained 2.9% to US$96.80/bbl in Asian trade after surging 3.4% overnight, following Iran-backed Houthi militants' reported attacks on two Saudi Arabian tankers in the Red Sea and raising the spectre of a broadening conflict beyond the Strait of Hormuz. Brent is now up roughly 30% across July. Karoon Energy (KAR) (+3.5%) — which reported US$116.4 million in June-quarter sales revenue from 1.08 million barrels of production — and Beach Energy (BPT) (+1.1%) led the oil and gas names, alongside Woodside Energy (WDS) (+0.5%) . Coal stocks tracked oil's momentum — Whitehaven Coal (WHC) (+2.2%) , Yancoal Australia (YAL) (+1.5%) , and New Hope Corp. (NHC) (+1.3%) were all firmer, with globalCoal Newcastle Coal futures gaining 1.2% to US$137.60/t, now up 9.6% since the latest Middle East escalation began. Core Lithium (CXO) (+6.3%) , Liontown Resources (LTR) (+3.6%) , IGO (IGO) (+3.1%) , Mineral Resources (MIN) (+2.2%) , and Pilbara Minerals (PLS) (+1.9%) all advanced.
