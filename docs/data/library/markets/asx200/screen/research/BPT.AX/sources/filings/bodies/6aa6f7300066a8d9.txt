Slide 4 – Beach’s role in Australia 
I want to start with why this company matters. 
Beach supplies around a fifth of the gas used on Australia’s east coast, and all the gas we produce there goes 
to Australian customers. That gas powers Australian industry and supports Australian households. Gas is what

The Government’s Future Made in Australia policy needs more than gas. It needs a domestic gas sector that

Slide 5 – Strategy in focus 
Our ambition is to become Australia’s leading, low-cost, returns-focused energy producer. While we have made 
progress on that strategy this year, we recognise there is more we need to do. Most of all, in putting our 
balance sheet and capability to work. 
Safety is a foundation of our strategy. In FY26, for the first time since 2011, no one working on a Beach 
operation suffered a recordable injury during the year. That is the result that matters most, and I thank everyone 
at Beach and our contractors who made it happen. 
We have taken operating costs out of the business and worked with our customers on new markets and more 
flexible contracts, lifting the price we receive for our gas. 
We are also seeing progress at Waitsia, operated by our partner Mitsui, which is now producing as it should, 
after a slower start. Brett will take you through the detail. 
But that progress does not yet make up for the shortfalls across the business. Production in FY26 was 2% lower 
than the year before, and we produced more than we added to our reserves. 
As an energy company, we are focused on growing our reserves, but that growth must also generate accretive 
returns. That is why we divested Artisan instead of investing to develop the field. It is also why we have invested 
in new regions for Beach, such as the Taroom Trough.  
Last month we agreed to farm into Elixir’s core Taroom acreage. On completion, Beach will operate a material 
position in this emerging basin. Replacing and growing our reserves with a focus on improving the quality of 
our assets is the central task in front of us. 
To address that, we have rebuilt our pipeline of opportunities. Low-cost onshore gas close to infrastructure we 
already own. Near-field exploration that can refill our plants. And offshore prospects of real scale, where we 
will bring in a partner to share the risk. 
We will also look at acquisitions, but only where they deliver value to our shareholders. 
We will back the opportunities that earn their return, and say no to the ones that don’t. And we want to control 
more of our own future, which is why operatorship mattered to us in the Taroom.

Slide 6 – Capital Management Framework 
How we allocate your capital is how you should judge us. 
The order is simple. First, we fund what it takes to run the business safely and reliably. Then we invest in growth 
that clears our hurdle rates, while keeping the balance sheet strong. Then we return surplus capital to 
shareholders through dividends. 
We are always balancing investing for growth with returning capital to you. 
The dividend for FY26 is 3 cents a share. It was a deliberate choice, because the Board believes the best use of 
that capital today is to fund Beach’s growth. 
Every dollar we keep has to earn a return that justifies keeping it. If it can’t, we will return it. With this approach 
we can transform Beach into a higher quality business with higher returns. 
Slide 7 – Getting the policy settings right 
I want to turn now to policy, because it will determine how much of this country’s gas is developed. 
Australia does not have a gas resource problem. It has a supply problem, and supply only comes from 
investment. 
We support the Government’s objective. Australians should have affordable gas, and this country has the 
resources to provide it. Frankly, that is what Beach has been delivering for decades. Where we differ is the 
mechanism to achieve that. 
The Domestic Gas Reservation Bill is now before the Senate, and its committee reports today. As drafted, it 
gives the Government discretion to require LNG exporters to supply up to 20% of their exports to the domestic 
market. Reservation moves gas. It does not make it. And discretion creates uncertainty that can stop new supply 
being developed. 
Forcing exporters to oversupply the domestic market undercuts the companies that have always supplied it. 
That punishes the domestic gas sector, the very sector Future Made in Australia depends on.  
If there is to be a reservation, it should be rules-based, prospective, and sized to fill the gap in wholesale 
demand, not to oversupply the whole market. The Bill requires producers to deliver 110% of the market. That 
is a standard we usually reserve for the football field. That oversupply will deliver nothing more than a short-
term sugar hit in prices. It is not a lasting solution. The only way to get more gas to market is to encourage 
investment, not mandate sales.   
Capital is mobile. It will not sit where returns can be legislated away. Beach is a proud Australian company, but 
our obligation is to you, our shareholders. We will not commit your capital to new supply where the balance 
of risk and reward is not right. 
We also need policy settings that recognise the role of gas in keeping electricity reliable and affordable. As 
coal retires, the system needs power that is there when Australians demand it, not only when the weather 
allows. The cheapest electron is not necessarily the cheapest power. We need to consider the full cost of 
keeping the lights on.

CEO’s Address 
Slide 8 – CEO’s Address 
Thank you, Ryan, and good morning everyone. 
FY26 was a year of progress for Beach. Two years on from the strategic reset, we operated safely, strengthened 
margins, generated positive free cash flow and positioned the business for disciplined growth. 
Across the portfolio, we translated that strategy into execution: supporting the Waitsia ramp-up, safely 
completing the offshore drilling and decommissioning campaign, restarting Western Flank drilling after nearly 
three years and further embedding an owner’s mindset across the organisation. 
I am proud of what our people achieved during the year and look forward to sharing the highlights of the year 
with you this morning. 
Slide 9 – FY26 headline results 
Our headline results demonstrate the resilience of our operating model. Commercial execution and cost 
discipline helped offset production headwinds and supported strong cash generation during a period of 
significant project activity. 
Production was 19.4 million barrels of oil equivalent, 2% below the prior year, primarily due to flooding in the 
Cooper Basin and a slower ramp-up at Waitsia. However, all operated assets delivered above their FY26 targets 
and Waitsia is performing strongly into FY27.  
Sales volumes were 22.9 million barrels of oil equivalent, reflecting lower production. Revenue was $1.8 billion, 
supported by six LNG cargoes and a 7% increase in our average realised gas price to $11.50 per gigajoule, 
reflecting the continued value of our targeted gas marketing strategy. 
Underlying EBITDA was $1.0 billion and pre-growth free cash flow was $458 million, representing a free cash 
flow yield of around 44%. This was a strong cash outcome in a year that included major project delivery. 
We finished the year with $983 million of total liquidity and low gearing, giving Beach the financial flexibility 
to fund disciplined organic investment and assess value-accretive inorganic opportunities. 
As Ryan mentioned, the Board declared a fully franked full year dividend of 3 cents per share to balance 
shareholder returns with the need to preserve capital for the next phase of growth. 
Slide 10 – FY26 milestones 
Those financial outcomes were supported by tangible operational and commercial milestones across the 
portfolio, which are summarised on Slide 10.

In Western Australia, the Waitsia Gas Plant commenced production in December and achieved its nameplate 
capacity of 250 terajoules per day in April.  
We have worked closely with operator Mitsui to improve plant performance and following the first statutory 
shutdown in early October, the plant is now producing reliably near nameplate capacity. 
On the East Coast, we recommenced Western Flank drilling, completing nine development wells and achieving 
a 100% success rate throughout the year.  
Moomba CCS continued to perform strongly. Around 1.3 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent were captured and 
stored during FY26, taking the total safely injected since commissioning to approximately 2.3 million tonnes. 
We took disciplined steps to high-grade the portfolio and strengthen future returns. The sale of our 60% 
operated interest in VIC/L35 monetised the Artisan discovery while preserving exposure through a production 
royalty and allowing more than $500 million of near-term capital to be redirected to higher-margin 
opportunities. 
Our six LNG cargoes generated $343 million of revenue, while domestic marketing broadened our customer 
base and lifted realised pricing. 
We have also expanded and refreshed our inventory with acreage awards and a more recent farm-in agreed in 
the Taroom Trough, and we have better assessed and defined opportunities in Perth Basin tight-gas, Western 
Flank oil, and Otway Basin exploration. 
Together, these achievements demonstrate a stronger operating platform, a high-graded portfolio and the 
financial capacity to pursue disciplined growth. I am proud of the efforts to reach this position. 
Slide 11 – Health, safety and environment 
Delivering this level of activity required an unwavering focus on health, safety and the environment, and FY26 
was an outstanding year for Beach. 
Our teams safely executed more than 1.5 million work hours across complex offshore activity, active onshore 
drilling programs and day-to-day operations. Beach was recordable-injury free for the year, delivering a total 
recordable injury frequency rate of zero. This level of personal safety performance was last achieved in 2011, 
when Beach operated only in the Cooper Basin. We also recorded no Tier 1 process safety events during the 
year and only one Tier 2 event. 
I thank our people and contractors for the planning, risk management and frontline discipline behind this 
exceptional result. 
Unfortunately, as disclosed in our first quarter result, a contractor sustained a hand injury in October which 
required medical treatment, ending Beach’s recordable injury-free period at 21 consecutive months.  
We will not be complacent. Maintaining the highest safety standards will remain our first priority as activity 
increases across our core hubs in FY27. 
Slide 12 – An owner’s mindset ingrained in our operations 
Slide 12 shows how the owner’s mindset introduced through the strategic reset is now ingrained across Beach. 
An owner’s mindset makes every asset team accountable for safe delivery, cost discipline, capital efficiency and 
the value of every molecule, making decisions as owners of capital, not simply operators.

Operated field costs have reduced by 18% on an absolute basis since FY24, falling to $244 million in FY26 
despite the addition of Waitsia Gas Plant operations from December. 
Unit operated field costs were $11.40 per barrel of oil equivalent. This was slightly above our target, reflecting 
lower production from a slower Waitsia ramp-up and flood-related disruption in the Western Flank, but the 
underlying cost trajectory remains clear. 
Commercially, we increased exposure to spot and short-term gas markets when pricing was advantageous. As 
policy uncertainty affected the forward market, we shifted to flexible contracts attracting premium pricing, 
including agreements with manufacturers and data centres and contracts linked to electricity spark spreads 
and LNG prices. Managing uncontracted volumes through these diverse routes to market supported a 29% 
increase in our average realised gas price since the first half of FY24. 
Most importantly, this discipline is translating into cash outcomes. We have maintained a free cash flow 
breakeven oil price well below US$30 per barrel and kept sustaining capital expenditure below our $450 million 
per annum operating principle. 
This is the owner’s mindset in practice: safe and reliable operations, lower structural costs, higher realised value, 
disciplined capital deployment and stronger cash generation. 
Slide 13 – Active work programs across core hubs 
With this stronger operating model in place, as Slide 13 shows, our priorities are clear: execute multiple drilling 
campaigns to sustain the base business, upgrade and simplify our infrastructure, and expand the resource base 
for the next phase of growth. 
In the Cooper Basin Western Flank, we will commence an eight-well exploration campaign later this month 
that aims to unlock new exploration horizons to add production and extend field life.  
In the Cooper Basin Joint Venture, exploration, appraisal and development drilling will continue, including 
appraisal of the Granite Wash opportunity.  
Alongside these drilling programs, we are upgrading and simplifying our infrastructure. The Moomba Central 
Optimisation project will modernise and centralise facilities and power generation, improving recoveries and 
reducing field operating and sustaining capital requirements by approximately $400 million net over the life of 
the asset. At the Otway Gas Plant, we’ve worked to optimise shutdowns and now expect to complete the 28-
day integrity shutdown on a six-yearly cycle instead of four. At Waitsia, we’re progressing inlet compression to 
improve and sustain production over the longer-term. 
Then, as we start to pivot towards growth: 
In Queensland’s Taroom Trough, a two-well exploration campaign is currently underway in ATP 2081 and the 
Omega-led joint venture is working to develop the next phase of accelerated activity. Our recently agreed 
farm-in to Elixir’s core acreage will, on completion, give us an operated interest and complement our existing 
portfolio. Together, these positions give Beach exposure to an emerging gas and liquids play close to 
established markets and infrastructure. 
In the Otway Basin, we recently reached a final investment decision on a two-well nearshore exploration 
campaign targeting Enterprise East and Selje. In a success case, these prospects could provide low-cost backfill 
through existing Enterprise and Otway infrastructure. 
On the West Coast, two conventional exploration wells planned in the Perth Basin’s L1 and L2 permits will 
target near-field resources for potential future production and backfill through existing infrastructure.

In the deeper-water T/30P and T/50P permits, reprocessed 3D seismic and advanced geophysical work has 
identified a deep inventory of prospects and leads, several within potential tie-back distance of the Thylacine 
platform. We are high grading this material opportunity and progressing a partnership to bring capability and 
capital, share risk and retain upside. Interest is strong, and I hope to update you in due course. 
The Perth Basin tight gas inventory offers another important longer-dated growth opportunity with additional

Together, these organic opportunities provide a disciplined pathway to growth across our core hubs. Our 
strong balance sheet also gives us the capacity to consider inorganic growth, but the standard is clear: any

framework.

As we look forward to FY27, we reiterate our production and capex guidance provided to the market in August. 
I am pleased to note however that August was the strongest month of production realised by Beach for five

Slide 15 – Beach value proposition 
To conclude, I would like to reinforce the value proposition for Beach. 
We have a material domestic energy portfolio across the East and West Coasts, supported by strategic 
infrastructure, diverse routes to market and a disciplined operating model. 
With a strong balance sheet, we will sustain the base business, progress organic growth across our core hubs 
and assess acquisitions that extend reserve and resource life and improve shareholder returns. 
Our approach will remain disciplined. Safety and operational excellence come first. Capital will be directed to 
opportunities that meet our return thresholds. We will continue to maximise the value of every molecule and 
preserve the financial flexibility required to grow through the cycle. 
Through domestic energy supply, jobs, investment, taxes and royalties, we support Australia’s energy security 
as we pursue our vision of becoming Australia’s leading, low-cost, returns-focused energy producer. 
I am confident in the platform we have built and excited about the opportunities ahead.
