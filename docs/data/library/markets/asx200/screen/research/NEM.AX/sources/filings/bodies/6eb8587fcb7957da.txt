In the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers beat decliners by a solid 178 to 92. The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (+3.7%) delivered its second consecutive session gain above 3%, extending a two-day rally that now ranks among the sector's best since 2020. COMEX gold futures surged 3.7% overnight to US$4,314/oz before adding a further 0.25% in Asian trade, while COMEX silver futures rose 3.4% overnight before easing 0.25% to US$62.12/oz. The tailwinds are compounding: falling oil prices ease diesel costs for gold producers even as the gold price itself climbs, and lower benchmark bond yields reduce the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding bullion. Pantoro Gold (PNR) (+10.0%) , Westgold Resources (WGX) (+5.4%) , Evolution Mining (EVN) (+3.8%) , and Newmont (NEM) (+3.7%) all continued their sharp recovery from July's lows. Gold Futures (Front month, back-adjusted) COMEX chart Materials (XMJ) (+1.1%) was today's more nuanced session — the diversified major miners were essentially flat while pure-play copper and iron ore names outperformed. COMEX copper futures rose 1.3% overnight before easing 0.21% to US$6.713/lb in Asian trade, and copper's reset of its all-time record in both New York and London added a fundamental underpinning to the move. Capstone Copper (CSC) (+2.7%) and Sandfire Resources (SFR) (+1.0%) led, while BHP (BHP) (+0.4%) barely moved, and Rio Tinto (RIO) was flat after Glencore confirmed it would pursue an ASX secondary listing following its failed merger approach with the miner earlier this year. Champion Iron (CIA) (+3.5%) and Fortescue (FMG) (+1.4%) led the iron ore cohort as SGX iron ore futures gained 1.4% to US$95.80/t. ASX 200 Materials (XMJ) intraday chart In other commodities moves, GFEX lithium carbonate futures in China fell 1.9% to CNY 140,480/t in the Asian session — lithium stocks largely held up despite the commodity price fall, with Core Lithium (CXO) (+1.9%) , Liontown Resources (LTR) (+1.9%) , and Mineral Resources (MIN) (+1.8%) all advancing. Uranium stocks gave back a portion of recent gains without a clear news catalyst — Silex Systems (SLX) (-5.3%) , Bannerman Energy (BMN) (-2.9%) , Deep Yellow (DYL) (-2.5%) , Paladin Energy (PDN) (-1.6%) , and Boss Energy (BOE) (-1.1%) were all lower. ASX 200 Financials (XFJ) intraday chart Financials (XFJ) (+0.4%) also advanced modestly, with two distinct catalysts providing the lift. AMP (AMP) (+6.0%) — in its biggest session in months — posted a 57% jump in first-half profit and announced a $150 million buyback. ASX Ltd (ASX) (+3.1%) and Netwealth (NWL) (+2.2%) were both firmer, while Commonwealth Bank (CBA) (+0.9%) was the major bank standout. Health Care (XHJ) (+0.8%) continued to attract bargain hunters drawn to last year's most brutally sold-off large caps. Cochlear (COH) (+3.3%) , Telix Pharmaceuticals (TLX) (+2.2%) , Ramsay Health Care (RHC) (+1.8%) , and CSL (CSL) (+1.3%) all advanced in what looks increasingly like a sustained sector rerating rather than a dead-cat bounce. Energy (XEJ) (+0.1%) managed to inch fractionally positive despite ICE Brent crude futures holding near US$79.65/bbl — the sector closed 1.6% above its session lows, suggesting active buying on dips. The session was split along familiar sub-sector lines: coal stocks led — Yancoal Australia (YAL) (+2.6%) , Whitehaven Coal (WHC) (+2.5%) , and New Hope Corp. (NHC) (+1.0%) — while oil and gas names were mixed, with Santos (STO) (+0.5%) advancing and Woodside Energy (WDS) (-0.2%) and Beach Energy (BPT) (-0.7%) — the latter after a 21% decline in full-year underlying profit — both easing. Real Estate (XPJ) (-0.9%) and Utilities (XUJ) (-0.4%) were simply overlooked — on a day when mining stocks commanded investor attention, there was little appetite for bond-proxy income sectors. Goodman Group (GMG) (-1.7%) , Scentre Group (SCG) (-1.3%) , and Charter Hall (CHC) (-1.2%) were the laggards.
