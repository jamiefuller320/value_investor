Delivering on Newmont's Consistent Capital Allocation Framework
Newmont's consistent capital allocation framework is designed to be sustainable through the commodity and investment cycles 
while maximizing total return of capital to shareholders, maintaining a flexible and resilient balance sheet, and focusing on high-
return capital investments for long-term value creation. The capital allocation uses below are presented in order of priority.1 
Newmont is consistently delivering on these priorities, supported by the robust free cash flow generated to date in 2026. 
Ongoing Sustaining Capital Investment in World-Class Portfolio
Newmont expects to spend $1.95 billion in 2026 in sustaining capital through targeted investments in critical infrastructure, 
including tailings solutions, as detailed in the '2026 Guidance Expectations' section below. As of the end of the third quarter, 
Newmont has invested $1.33 billion of sustaining capital in 2026, with full-year 2026 guidance remaining unchanged.2 
Sustainable Through the Cycle Cash Dividend
Newmont is committed to returning capital to shareholders through a sustainable cash dividend of $1.1 billion per year. Central 
to this framework is a dividend structured to grow on a per-share basis without increasing Newmont's financial commitment, as 
share repurchases executed through the cycle permanently lower the outstanding share count. The annual total per share 
dividend target will be calculated annually in February based on the current number of shares issued and outstanding. The 
dividend payment will be divided into four equal payments rounded up to the nearest $0.01, to be paid out on a quarterly basis, 
subject to quarterly approval by Newmont's Board of Directors1. In line with this commitment, a dividend of $0.26 per share for 
the third quarter of 2026 has been declared payable on December 21, 2026, to holders of record of such common stock at the 
close of business on November 25, 2026. This equates to an indicated total annualized dividend of $1.04 per share, with 
continued per-share dividend increases expected as share repurchases continue.
Disciplined Approach to Development Capital Reinvestment
Newmont expects to invest $1.4 billion of development capital in 2026 as it advances the highest-return free cash flow generative 
projects, while continuing to study, evaluate and define the future growth profile of its portfolio. As of the end of the third 
quarter, Newmont has invested $878 million in its current development projects in 2026, with full-year 2026 guidance remaining 
unchanged.2
Maintaining an Optimized Capital Structure Through the Cycle
Newmont is focused on maintaining a resilient balance sheet, anchored by a $1 billion net cash target3, with flexibility of plus or 
minus $2 billion depending on market conditions. During strong commodity price environments, Newmont intends to further 
optimize its balance sheet by actively managing gross debt, while maintaining a minimum cash balance of $5 billion through the 
cycle. Newmont ended the third quarter of 2026 with a cash balance of $8.8 billion and a net cash balance of $3.2 billion3, before 
the planned payment of $1.95 billion to Barrick under the terms of the amended NGM agreement, which is expected to be paid 
in the fourth quarter.
Ratable Share Repurchase Program
Since the last earnings call, Newmont executed $1.6 billion of share repurchases, with $2.8 billion remaining under the current 
repurchase authorization of $6.0 billion. Newmont intends to request additional approval from its Board of Directors as the 
current authorization approaches completion, consistent with the Company's disciplined and repeatable approach to returning 
excess cash to shareholders.

Third Quarter 2026 Production and Financial Summary
Attributable gold production1 increased slightly by 1 percent to 1,302 thousand ounces from the prior quarter, driven by 
higher production at Cadia after the recovery from the seismic event in the second quarter, higher production at Brucejack due 
to ongoing productivity improvements and increased development rates, and higher throughput, grades and recovery rates at 
Peñasquito. These benefits were largely offset by lower production at Lihir and Boddington due to planned mine maintenance 
and at Yanacocha due to mine plan sequencing.  Consolidated gold sales were 1,211 thousand ounces for the quarter.
Copper production increased 35 percent to 23 thousand tonnes compared to the prior quarter, driven by higher production at 
Cadia after the recovery from the seismic event in the second quarter. Silver production increased 57 percent to 11 million 
ounces, lead production increased 22 percent to 22 thousand tonnes and zinc production increased 68 percent to 67 thousand 
tonnes compared to the prior quarter, driven by higher co-product grades at Peñasquito. 
Average realized gold price was $4,317 per ounce, a decrease of $97 per ounce from the prior quarter. Average realized gold 
price includes a gross sales price of $4,291 per ounce and a favorable mark-to-market adjustment of $26 per ounce on 
provisionally-priced sales.

Costs Applicable to Sales (CAS)2 allocated to gold totaled $1.8 billion for the quarter, with an additional $389 million allocated to

Gold by-product AISC per ounce3 decreased 10 percent to $1,454 for the quarter. Building from CAS per ounce, the decrease

Consolidated cash from operations before working capital5 increased 11 percent from the prior quarter to $3.3 billion

realized gold and silver prices and slightly higher CAS.

unfavorable change in working capital of $475 million. Working capital was a net use of cash of $565 million in the third quarter, 
reflecting an unfavorable accounts receivable movement of $318 million, continued cash spend for previously accrued 
reclamation activities of $256 million and normal course inventory and stockpile builds of $172 million. These impacts were 
partially offset by a favorable change in other accrued liabilities of $165 million primarily related to the timing of employee profit 
sharing payments in Mexico and Peru, and accrued tax liabilities of $29 million.
Income and mining cash tax paid decreased 21 percent from the prior quarter to $858 million due to the timing of annual tax 
payments accrued in 2025.
Free cash flow7 decreased 13 percent from the prior quarter to $1.9 billion primarily due to a decrease in net cash provided by 
operating activities and higher capital investment.
Balance sheet and liquidity remained strong in the third quarter, ending with $8.8 billion of cash and cash equivalents, with 
$12.8 billion of total liquidity; ended the quarter in a net cash position of $3.2 billion.8  The balances as of the end of the third 
quarter are before the planned payment of $1.95 billion to Barrick under the terms of the amended NGM agreement, which is 
expected to be paid in the fourth quarter of 2026.
