_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T17:25:11.239168+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ASX Limited operates Australia's dominant listed-market infrastructure — cash equities, derivatives, clearing, settlement, and data — earning quasi-monopoly economics on domestic trading activity. The quantitative screen rates the stock a buy on moderate multiples (P/E 20.2, P/B 2.7, yield 3.8%, ROE 13.6%, D/E 12%) with passes across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families. The value hook is a recovering earnings base after FY2023 impairment noise, a capital-light fee model, and a dividend that screens attractively relative to peers. The central debate is whether regulatory remediation, technology transformation (post-CHESS), and a CFO departure are transient headwinds or evidence that the moat is narrowing — a question this filing pack cannot fully resolve.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, ASX offers a rare combination: essential infrastructure, high recurring revenue, low balance-sheet leverage on an equity basis, and a screen profile that clears eight models including Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Quality Value, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, and Financial Health. Composite score of 56% sits modestly above the ASX 200 sector-relative benchmark (55%), with five of five metric families passing and data quality rated 19/20.

The business quality case rests on ASX's structural role as the primary venue for Australian capital formation. Revenue and net income have rebounded from the FY2023 trough (Yahoo fallback: net income A$317.3m) to FY2025 (A$502.6m), with EPS rising from A$1.64 to A$2.59 over the same period. Operating margins remain robust — FY2025 operating income of A$643.4m on revenue of A$1,128m implies ~57% operating margin — consistent with a toll-bridge exchange model. The screen's FCF Yield pass aligns with FY2025 free cash flow of A$302.9m (Yahoo), though this is down from A$480.3m in FY2024.

What the screen does not capture is the reputational and execution risk from years of CHESS replacement delays, ASIC enforcement, and leadership turnover. The buy signal is therefore a *relative-value* call on a franchise asset, not a deep-moat quality compounder — Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Earnings Quality, and Piotroski F-Score (5/9) all failed. Conviction on the screen itself is low at 33%, with the signal only one week old and improving.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory.** *ASX Settles ASIC Legal Proceedings* (15 Jun 2026) is indexed but without body text — settlement terms, penalties, and ongoing undertakings are unknown. Yahoo Finance (15 Dec 2025) reported a "A$99 million regulatory bombshell" contributing to an eight-year share-price low; magnitude and status post-settlement cannot be confirmed from primary sources here.

**Technology and execution.** CHESS replacement failures remain the defining operational risk. The screen fails Economic Moat and Earnings Quality; Piotroski scores 5/9 with failures on operating cash flow, gross margin, and current ratio trends. Without annual report language on going concern, contingencies, or capex commitments, these risks are flagged but unquantified.

**Leadership.** Three articles in the past week (Kalkine, 24 Jul 2026; simplywall.st, 25–26 Jul 2026) highlight CFO retirement and question whether management can execute the transformation strategy. This is a governance and continuity risk not captured by quantitative metrics.

**Competitive and structural.** Secondary coverage notes ETF industry growth toward A$500bn (AFR, 24 Jul 2026) — a volume tailwind — but also rising capital-markets competition and periodic market-structure reviews. The AFR piece on listing economics (26 Jul 2026) signals ongoing scrutiny of ASX's role in capital raising.

**Cyclical.** ASX revenues correlate with market activity; a softer FY2026 for listings and trading volumes (AFR, 25 Jul 2026: "Why the last financial year may have been a warning for ASX investors") could pressure fee income. Macro context (ASX 200 at 8,772, AUD/USD 0.70) is neutral colour only.

**Pension.** Non-current pension provisions are immaterial in Yahoo balance-sheet data (A$2.6m); no filing-body language on defined-benefit exposure is available.
