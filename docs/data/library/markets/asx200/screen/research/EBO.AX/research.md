## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
EBOS Group is Australasia’s largest healthcare and animal-care wholesaler, operating Terry White Chemmart, Symbion, and related distribution platforms across Australia and New Zealand. The quantitative screen rates the name **Strong Buy** (7/22 models; composite 73%), anchored on cheapness (P/E 18.5, P/B 1.4), a 4.8% dividend yield, and passes across all four factor families — cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk. The valuation hook is a large-cap defensive distributor trading at modest multiples after a multi-year drawdown, with FY2025 free cash flow holding up better than reported earnings. The central debate is whether FY2025’s earnings contraction and the April 2026 FY26 guidance trim (fuel costs) reflect transitory headwinds — partially offset by H1 FY2026 revenue recovery — or structural margin pressure, goodwill impairment risk, and persistently weak returns on capital on an acquisitive, intangible-heavy balance sheet. **Primary ASX regulatory filings are absent from the research library** (`filings_index.json`: zero annual, interim, or other entries; no body extracts under `filings/bodies/`); all financial figures below fall back to Yahoo Finance.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy rests on a coherent value profile. Seven models pass, including Graham Enterprising, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Composite Value, and Financial Health. Composite score is 0.73 (sector-relative 0.71), data quality is high (19/20 metrics), conviction is 55% (persistent, four weeks at signal, stable trend), and key thresholds are met: P/E below 25, P/B below 3, debt/equity 68% (below 100%).

For a value investor, EBOS combines **scale, recurring wholesale revenues, and income** at sub-market multiples. Revenue exceeded **A$13.2bn** in FY2025 (Yahoo fallback), the Terry White Chemmart franchise and Symbion wholesale platform provide defensive exposure to pharmacy and hospital channels, and the 4.8% yield supports the dividend screen. FY2025 free cash flow of **A$396m** (Yahoo) rose **38%** versus FY2024 (**A$288m**) despite net profit falling **22%** to **A$232m**, suggesting the market may be penalising accounting earnings while cash generation remains resilient.

Business quality is mixed. Operating margins are thin (typical for distribution), ROE is a modest 9.0% (screen), and the balance sheet carries **A$3.0bn** of goodwill and intangibles — driving negative tangible book value (**−A$75m** at FY2025). The risk-family pass likely reflects moderate leverage (net debt **A$989m**, roughly 1.7× EBITDA on Yahoo figures) rather than pristine balance-sheet quality. H1 FY2026 news points to **13% revenue growth to A$6.8bn** with a steady dividend, which — if sustained through the full year — would support a re-rating narrative that the quantitative signal is capturing early, though the subsequent FY26 guidance cut tempers that optimism.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Primary filing gap:** Without ASX/NZX annual or half-year report bodies, going-concern statements, litigation contingencies, debt covenants, and segment commentary **cannot be verified**. This is a material limitation for a leveraged, acquisitive distributor.

**Earnings and guidance:** Yahoo FY2025 shows a sharp earnings decline; news flags an **FY2025 EPS miss (~15%)** (*Earnings Miss: EBOS Group Limited Missed EPS By 15% And Analysts Are Revising Their Forecasts*, 30 Aug 2025). In **April 2026**, EBOS **trimmed FY26 earnings guidance**, citing elevated fuel costs (*EBOS Group trims FY26 earnings guidance as fuel costs bite*, 22 Apr 2026; *EBOS Group Revises FY26 Earnings Outlook Amid Elevated Fuel Costs*, Sharecafe, 22 Apr 2026). A broker subsequently lowered its outlook (*Why did this broker just lower its outlook on this ASX 200 stock?*, 23 Apr 2026).

**Returns on capital:** Commentary flags weak returns (*Here's What's Concerning About EBOS Group's (NZSE:EBO) Returns On Capital*, 23 Oct 2025). ROE of 9% (screen) is modest for a goodwill-heavy franchise.

**Balance sheet / M&A:** Negative tangible equity and **A$3.0bn** of intangibles create impairment and integration risk. FY2022–FY2025 cumulative acquisition outlays exceed **A$2.0bn** (Yahoo cash-flow). Failed or overpriced deals would pressure equity.

**Leverage and interest:** Net debt **A$989m**; interest expense **A$96m** (FY2025). Covenant terms and headroom are **unverified** without filings; rising rates could constrain dividends or further M&A.

**Index and liquidity:** EBOS was **removed from the ASX 200** in the March 2026 rebalance (*3 ASX shares slide after being cut from the ASX 200*, 16 Mar 2026), which may have triggered passive selling and reduced index-tracker demand — a risk not captured by value screens.

**Cyclical / regulatory:** Healthcare wholesale faces government pricing and reimbursement pressure (PBS, hospital contracts) and customer concentration in pharmacy/hospital channels.

**Governance / ownership:** Retail investors hold ~56% (*While institutions own 38% of EBOS Group Limited (NZSE:EBO), retail investors are its largest shareholders with 56% ownership*, simplywall.st, 11 Oct 2025), which can amplify volatility. CEO **Adam Hall** received **13,274 ordinary shares** under a sign-on performance rights award (*EBOS Group Issues 13,274 Ordinary Shares to CEO Adam Hall Under Sign-On Performance Rights Award*, 2 Jul 2026) — routine but worth monitoring for incentive alignment.

**Pension:** ~**A$116m** combined pension obligations (Yahoo); immaterial relative to scale but worth monitoring when filings become available.

**Share price:** News notes **−33% over three years** (*Investors in EBOS Group (NZSE:EBO) have unfortunately lost 33% over the last three years*, 2 Jan 2026) — the value case may be a trap if earnings do not normalise.
