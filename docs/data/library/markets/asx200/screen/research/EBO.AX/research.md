# EBOS Group Limited (EBO.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-09T07:42:40.931976+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
EBOS Group is Australasia’s largest diversified healthcare and animal-care distributor, spanning community pharmacy wholesale, TerryWhite Chemmart and other retail pharmacy banners, institutional healthcare, medical technology, contract logistics, and pet brands. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy, though the current screening snapshot carries no populated model or metric detail (data quality score 0.0), limiting automated conviction. The value case rests on a defensive, essential-services franchise trading at depressed levels after a sharp share-price fall, a dividend yield that broker commentary puts near 5%, and a FY27 cash-flow inflection as the ~$360m distribution-centre renewal programme completes. The central debate is whether revenue momentum (FY26 revenue reportedly +9.9% to ~$13.5bn) can translate into margin and earnings recovery, or whether competitive wholesale pressure, lost Chemist Warehouse volume, elevated leverage, and transition costs keep underlying NPAT flat to down.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, EBOS offers a high-quality, quasi-defensive compounder at a potential inflection point. The business owns leading positions in pharmacy wholesale (Symbion, ProPharma), Australia’s largest health-services-focused pharmacy network (TerryWhite Chemmart, now 782 stores including MediAdvice), hospital medicines distribution, Southeast Asian medical technology, and ANZ animal-care brands and vet wholesale. Revenue is structurally supported by ageing demographics, pharmaceutical utilisation (including GLP-1 and other high-value medicines), and stable government funding frameworks — the FY25 annual report notes a new five-year, $4.2bn Community Services Obligation agreement.

The screen’s buy rating aligns with a name that, in an earlier populated snapshot (July 2026), passed seven of 22 models across cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk families, with P/E ~18.5, P/B ~1.4, dividend yield ~4.8% and ROE ~9.0%. That profile suits a value-oriented healthcare distributor: not deep cyclical value, but a franchise trading below historical esteem after a ~48% one-year share decline (per news coverage). Management reaffirmed FY26 EBITDA guidance through HY26, completed the largest DC (Kemps Creek) on time and on budget, and guides to ~30% lower capex and deleveraging from FY27 — a credible path to free-cash-flow recovery that screens typically reward.

Offsetting the thesis: underlying NPAT has lagged revenue (HY26 underlying NPAT −4.3%; FY26 full-year headlines cite −3.1%), ROCE has drifted lower (12.9% at HY26 vs 13.3% pcp), and leverage rose to 2.2x at HY26 (within the 1.7–2.3x covenant target). The loss of the Chemist Warehouse Australia contract — normalised out of FY25 comparatives — and Sigma Healthcare’s reported win of a ~$3bn Chemist Warehouse supply contract (October 2025) underscore persistent competitive risk in community pharmacy wholesale. The buy case therefore depends on execution through the DC transition and margin recovery, not on passive mean reversion alone.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Competitive.** Loss of the Chemist Warehouse Australia contract distorts FY24/FY25 comparatives; Sigma’s reported ~$3bn Chemist Warehouse supply win (October 2025, smallcaps.com.au) signals intensifying wholesale competition. HY26 filings note “competitive dynamics in Community Pharmacy” and GOR margin compression from low-margin, high-value medicine mix.

**Leverage and liquidity.** HY26 leverage 2.2x sits at the top of the 1.7–2.3x target. Total debt rose (Yahoo: ~$2.31bn FY26 vs ~$1.75bn FY25). April 2025 A$200m placement diluted ~5.9m shares. HY26 operating cash flow was sharply lower; WC build of $84m in H1. Covenants (interest coverage, leverage, EBITDAF) are reported compliant, but deleveraging is deferred to FY27.

**Regulatory / funding.** EBOS depends on Community Services Obligation and pharmacy wholesale funding frameworks. A modest CSO increase began H2 FY25 with further uplift from FY27; regulatory change or delayed funding would affect margins.

**Execution / capex.** The ~$360m, eight-site DC renewal programme (FY23–FY26) creates duplication and ramp-up inefficiencies; benefits are guided for FY27–FY28. Failure to deliver productivity gains would extend margin pressure.

**Governance / key person.** CEO transition: John Cullity retired 30 June 2025; Adam Hall appointed from 1 July 2025 (19 February 2025 announcement). New leadership execution risk during a capital-intensive transition.

**Customer concentration.** Community pharmacy wholesale remains competitively contested; key customers were “retained” in HY26 per filing, but CWA loss demonstrates concentration risk in large accounts.

**Cyclical.** Animal Care faces subdued discretionary pet spending; FY25 noted consumer headwinds on discretionary categories.

**Accounting.** Underlying vs statutory divergence is material (HY26: underlying NPAT −4.3% vs statutory +13.0%), driven by M&A costs, restructuring, and acquisition-related amortisation. No going-concern qualification in available extracts; auditor review conclusion on HY26 interim was unqualified.
