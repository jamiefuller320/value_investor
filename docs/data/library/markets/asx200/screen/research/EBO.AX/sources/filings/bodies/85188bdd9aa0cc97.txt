Chair’s Address to Shareholders 
Turning now to our highlights for the year.  
We will begin with a short video.  The video captures the breadth of our operations, the 
dedication of our people and the key role we play in supporting healthcare and animal 
care customers and communities across New Zealand, Australia, Southeast Asia and 
Hong Kong. 
(A copy of the video will be made available on the EBOS website) 
As you have just seen, FY26 was another year of significant activity and progress across 
the Group. 
The year was particularly important as we refreshed our strategic direction, sharpening 
our focus on the higher-growth and higher-return segments within Healthcare and 
Animal Care and further positioning EBOS for long-term sustainable growth. 
At its core, EBOS is built on a clear and repeatable model grounded in care, productivity 
and partnership. These principles guide how we operate, how we invest and how we 
create value. Our Care based portfolio has been curated over many years and is defined 
by our four operating divisions. 
Symbion & Healthcare Distribution supplies medicines, consumables and logistics 
services to pharmacies, hospitals, government and healthcare partners across 
Australia and New Zealand.  
Retail Pharmacy Brands helps independent pharmacies grow by providing store brands, 
services, digital tools and retail support across a large network.  
Medical Technology works in hospitals and alongside surgical teams to support 
specialist medical technology and biologics solutions across Australia, New Zealand, 
Southeast Asia and Hong Kong.  
And Animal Care makes and markets pet food, and distributes vet products and animal 
health brands through retail and wholesale channels. 
Importantly, each of these businesses holds a leading position in its market. In most 
cases, we are number one or number two. That scale and leadership is a real strength, 
and it underpins the resilience of the EBOS portfolio. 
Together, our businesses connect people, pets, and communities to care, anywhere. 
FY26 was a year of solid growth for EBOS, with an 8% increase in statutory EBITDA and a 
5% increase in Underlying EBITDA. 
For personal use only
It also marked an important milestone. Over recent years, the Board has backed a 
significant program of investment to modernise our network and strengthen our 
portfolio. That facility investment cycle is now complete. 
As a result, EBOS goes into FY27 a stronger business. We have modern facilities, a 
balance sheet with capacity, and the flexibility to keep investing in growth. 
The Board is confident there are further opportunities to build value for shareholders, 
both through our existing businesses and through disciplined, complementary 
acquisitions. 
Adam will take you through the results and our priorities in more detail shortly. 
Over the long-term, EBOS has delivered an approximately 10% EBITDA compound 
annual growth rate, supported by a balanced contribution from organic expansion and 
carefully selected acquisitions aligned with our strategy.  
We have complemented this growth with a strong capital allocation framework, 
generating attractive returns from bolt-on investments while maintaining stable 
dividends for shareholders, guided by our longstanding payout ratio target. 
Our balance sheet remains robust and our strong operating cash flows continue to 
provide EBOS with the capacity to reinvest in its businesses, pursue growth 
opportunities with discipline, and deliver returns to shareholders. 
Turning now to sustainability, where our focus during the year was on practical action 
and clear reporting. 
We published our third Climate Statement in mid-September, with a refreshed 
assessment of our climate-related risks and opportunities and their potential impacts 
on the Group.  
We continued to invest in renewable energy and energy efficiency. Our grid-purchased 
electricity efficiency improved, largely reflecting the opening of newer, more efficient 
facilities. Rooftop solar installations increased our renewable electricity generation, 
and planning progressed on two large-scale ground-mounted solar arrays at our Parkes 
facility. In New Zealand, we have matched all of our electricity consumption with 
renewable energy certificates since FY24. 
Our contribution to our long-standing partner Greenfleet increased again this year, 
taking our support since 2007 to more than $2.9 million supporting its work in restoring 
native, biodiverse forests across Australia and New Zealand.  
In rural Victoria, we have also partnered with Greenfleet on a reforestation project at a 
former dairy farm. Approximately 98,000 native trees and shrubs were planted, with 
native wildlife already returning to the area.   
For personal use only
We are equally committed to an inclusive and supportive workplace, because we 
believe that people do their best work when they feel safe, respected and valued.  In 
FY26 we refreshed our Diversity, Equity & Inclusion Policy to ensure it aligned with that 
commitment and with our broader strategy. 
During FY26, we continued to identify and strengthen safeguards in our warehouse 
operations through targeted engineering improvements and investment in critical risk 
management, which continues into FY27.  We also progressed the rollout of the EBOS 
Life Savers program, reinforcing a consistent Group-wide framework for the 
management of high-risk activities. 
Through our safety leadership walks, your Board and the Enterprise Leadership Team 
engage directly with employees to understand how risks are managed and identify 
opportunities to strengthen safety performance.  
Together, these initiatives reinforce the importance of visible leadership, accountability 
and open communication - all of which support a strong safety culture. This 
commitment is reflected in the simple but important Safety Matters mantra: ‘work safe 
to get home safe’.  
We also introduced new training, including our Respect at Work module, giving our 
people practical tools to look after one another and to foster safe, respectful and 
inclusive workplaces across the Group. 
The success of EBOS is made possible by the contribution of our people, and their 
health, safety and wellbeing is a fundamental priority for the Board. 
Retaining and motivating our people also remains a key priority for the Board. In FY26 
we completed a comprehensive review of our executive remuneration framework. The 
changes, which apply from FY27, strengthen the link between executive reward and 
EBOS’s medium- and long-term performance. They include deferring part of the short-
term incentive into equity, adding market-priced options alongside performance rights 
in the long-term incentive, and introducing a minimum shareholding requirement for 
executives. 
The Board has also adopted a minimum shareholding policy for non-executive 
directors, and each of us bought EBOS shares during FY26.  
Together, these steps strengthen the alignment of your Board and executives with you 
as shareholders. 
Our responsibilities extend beyond our commercial operations to the community. 
As you can see from this slide, EBOS businesses continued to support a range of 
partnerships and initiatives that improve health and wellbeing outcomes in the 
communities we serve. These contributions include fundraising, strategic partnerships, 
employee participation and the provision of products and services. 
For personal use only
They are closely aligned with our purpose of connecting people, pets and communities 
to care, anywhere, and reflect the values of our employees, many of whom contribute 
their own time and energy to supporting local organisations and causes. 
Turning now to shareholder returns, your Board declared a final dividend of NZ 61.5 
cents per share, which was paid to shareholders last month. 
This brings the full-year dividend to NZ 118.5 cents per share, representing a payout 
ratio of 84.5 per cent of underlying net profit after tax. 
In determining dividends, the Board carefully balances the importance of providing 
shareholders with a reliable income stream against the need to maintain financial 
flexibility and invest in attractive growth opportunities.  
The final dividend reflects the Board's confidence in the resilience of EBOS' earnings, 
the strength of its cash-flow generation and the Group's long-term growth outlook. Our 
approach will continue to be guided by the sustainability of earnings and cash flows, 
the strength of the balance sheet and the long-term interests of shareholders.  
On behalf of the Board, I would like to thank Chief Executive Officer, Adam Hall, the 
Enterprise Leadership Team, and all EBOS employees across New Zealand, Australia, 
Southeast Asia and Hong Kong for their leadership, commitment and contribution 
throughout the year. Their dedication, expertise and care underpin our success and 
enable us to continue serving our customers and communities. 
I also thank our customers, suppliers, pharmacy partners, healthcare professionals 
and other business partners for the trust they place in EBOS and to our shareholders for 
your continued support and confidence. 
We are proud of what we achieved in FY26 and look forward to building on that 
momentum as we deliver on our refreshed strategy and create long-term value for all 
stakeholders. Together, we will continue to build a stronger, more resilient EBOS – 
delivering meaningful outcomes for the communities we serve. 
I will now invite Adam to provide a more detailed review of the Group’s operational 
performance and outlook. 
Thank you.

CEO’s Address to Shareholders 
Thank you, Liz, and good afternoon. Thank you for joining us, whether you are here in the 
room or online. 
I would like to cover three things this morning. First, our FY26 performance. Second, 
how FY27 has begun across each of our four divisions. And third, the opportunity to 
bring those divisions closer together and create further value across the Group. 
Let me start with FY26. Revenue increased 9.9% to $13.5 billion, while underlying 
EBITDA - our measure of operating earnings - increased 5% to $614 million. 
I want to acknowledge the bottom line. Our Underlying net profit was $250 million, 
down 3.1%, and underlying earnings per share declined 7.3%. This largely reflects the 
depreciation and interest costs associated with our distribution centre investment 
program. Importantly, as of 30 June 2026, the major facility investment cycle of recent 
years is now complete with all 8 facilities operational. Those investments have 
strengthened the Group’s capabilities and capacity, and our focus is firmly on 
execution - improving productivity, realising the benefits of that investment and 
converting our structural advantages into stronger returns and sustainable growth. 
Following completion of this once in a generation major facility investment cycle, we 
have an immediate 1/3rd stepdown in our capital expenditure in FY27, and that tracks 
with what we have seen to date this year. 
Our balance sheet remains in good shape. Leverage was 2.1 times, comfortably within 
our target range of 1.7 to 2.3 times, and return on capital employed was 12.8%. 
While we made important operational and strategic progress during the year, the Board 
and executive team are very aware that EBOS’s share price performance has been 
disappointing for shareholders. We recognise that progress within the business must 
ultimately translate into sustainable earnings growth and long-term shareholder value.   
As we demonstrate daily operational performance and post consistent ﬁnancial results, 
we believe that shareholders will appreciate the value of a high-quality EBOS portfolio 
that grows with our aging population and the wealth of new medicines and medical 
technology coming to market.  
Part of consistency is delivering on our guidance.  In FY26 we achieved our financial 
guidance metrics - EBITDA, capex, depreciation and amortisation, net finance costs 
and leverage. This was despite elevated fuel and broader energy costs, which led us to 
update EBITDA guidance soon after the Iran conﬂict began. 
The divisions also delivered against the growth priorities set out at our Investor Day. 
Symbion grew in line with its industry, Retail Pharmacy Brands delivered high single-
digit growth, Medical Technology delivered high single-digit growth, and Animal Care 
delivered mid-to-high single-digit growth. As noted in the full year result, macro 
headwinds such as foreign exchange rates and fuel costs, directly impacted earnings. 
For personal use only
Pleasingly, we were proactively able to mitigate some fuel costs across our various 
businesses.  
Let me now turn to the first quarter of FY27. 
The key message is that positive growth has continued to build through the quarter and 
we are re-affirming our existing FY27 guidance, despite persistent FX and fuel cost 
headwinds. As previously indicated, we continue to expect earnings growth to be 
weighted to the second half. 
What is particularly pleasing is that the growth momentum is broad based. Kemps 
Creek is ramping up in Symbion, and the Chemist Warehouse New Zealand contract 
has been extended. Retail Pharmacy Brands is expanding its store network and owned-
brand sales. In Medical Technology, our supplier partnerships continue to grow and our 
recent acquisitions are  performing. In Animal Care, Black Hawk and VitaPet sales 
increased mid to high single digits, and Kiwi Kitchens has made a promising start in the 
United States. 
There is also an important change in our financial position. As I mentioned, our 8 new 
facilities are now operational at the end of FY26 thanks to the hard work and 
commitment of the teams leading these large capital projects.  Capex was 
approximately $145 million in FY26 and remains on track to step down to around $100 
million in FY27. That lower capital intensity supports stronger free cash flow. Within our 
target leverage range, it also creates approximately $150 million of capacity for 
disciplined bolt-on acquisitions. 
At the same time, we are beginning to unlock the beneﬁts of operating more effectively 
as one Group. We expect these initiatives to provide incremental ﬁnancial beneﬁts from 
FY28 as well as organisational opportunities, and I’ll discuss this further shortly. 
I will now talk to each division, then outline the Group-wide synergy opportunity and 
finish with our FY27 guidance. 
Let me begin with Symbion and Healthcare Distribution. 
The priority here is clear – it is to put recently installed capacity to work. We have spent 
several years renewing the distribution network. With that program complete, the focus 
is now on using those assets to lift productivity and support growth, without further 
major investment. 
Kemps Creek is a great example. Warehouse productivity is already approximately 20% 
higher than at the former Greystanes site. We expect the improvement to reach around 
30% at the end of FY27 as automation benefits are realised and volumes continue to 
ramp up. The first-quarter run rate is on track. 
Community pharmacy also has supportive structural tailwinds. Although competition 
to serve community pharmacy remains elevated, the Government's Community Service 
For personal use only
Obligation funding pool increased from 1 July, with earnings tracking in line with our 
expectations. Demand for GLP-1 and high value medicines continues to remain strong 
and consistent with our growth outlook. 
In contract logistics, the team secured 20 new principals in FY26. And our new Perth 
facility is now live and serving customers, enabling full Australia-wide coverage. 
In New Zealand, the CWNZ wholesaling contract has been extended by agreement and 
will now ramp down progressively through to the end of FY28. This agreement 
represents a modest component of Group earnings, and the extended timeframe gives 
us the opportunity to adjust the cost base in a disciplined way. Three sites have already 
been exited to date, with a fourth transition, the Dunedin site into Christchurch, now 
announced. Importantly, the remainder of our New Zealand business continues to 
perform well, and we recently renewed a separate major customer contract as well. 
Turning to Retail Pharmacy Brands, the priority is to drive store dollars. Put simply, 
when our pharmacy partners perform well, we perform well. We have seen a 
meaningful challenge to customer spend as the cost of living impacts their wallet.  As a 
result, our investment is therefore focused on helping stores attract more customers, 
expand the services they provide and increase sales. We see five practical levers to 
help achieve this. 
First, network growth. We now support approximately 790 stores, following around 10 
net additions in the quarter. This includes TerryWhite Chemmart, Cincotta and 
MediAdvice, in which we acquired a majority interest during FY26. A larger network 
creates further scale across our brands, buying and services. 
Second, broadening care. 61% of Australians live with an ongoing medical condition, 
and 38% of Australians live with two or more. These figures are expected to increase as 
the population ages. Against this backdrop, TWC commitment to care continues to 
resonate with customers.  During the FY26 flu season, the network delivered 24% of all 
pharmacy flu vaccinations. This is not just a measure of vaccination volumes. It reflects 
the expanding scope of practice in community pharmacy and its increasingly important 
role as a front door to healthcare - creating more opportunities to support customers 
with their health over time. 
Third, digital ecosystem. Units sold through TerryWhite Chemmart's online channels 
increased 25%. Customers increasingly expect to engage with us both in store and 
online, and the team is enhancing that capability. 
Fourth, partnering with consumer brands. Campaigns delivered through our in-store 
screens generated a 35% uplift in unit sales for promoted products. That creates 
immediate value for supplier partners and pharmacy owners, while also developing a 
higher-margin income stream for the Group. 
For personal use only
And fifth, owned brands. Retail sales increased at a low double-digit rate. Owned 
brands provide customers with good value and support stronger margins for pharmacy 
owners and the Group. 
These levers reinforce one another. More stores create more opportunities for health 
services, online engagement and owned-brand sales. Greater customer traffic also 
increases the value of our retail media platform. There is always more to be done, but 
the division has solid momentum as we move through FY27. 
In Medical Technology, our priority is to expand scope and reach. 
It is worth briefly explaining the business model. We connect leading medical 
technology manufacturers and donor networks with the hospitals, clinics and 
healthcare practitioners who use their products. We have more than 400 supply 
partnerships, serve more than 4,500 hospitals and clinics, and engage approximately 
2,000 practitioners each year through professional education programs. 
This is not just a distribution business. It is a trusted partnership. We believe we are the 
leading independent distributor in Australia and New Zealand and in South-East Asia, 
and the largest biologics provider in Australia. 
We strengthened that platform in FY26 through four acquisitions, each continuing to 
build out our scale therapy area presence: 
• 
Precision Surgical, a spine and orthopaedic distributor on the East coast of 
Australia 
• 
AlphaXRT, which provides oncology radiation solutions in New Zealand and 
Australia 
• 
K-Talyst, which works in aesthetics and reconstruction across Southeast Asia 
• 
And finally by consolidating Origin, a US based allograft joint venture in which we 
have a majority interest 
Each of these broadens the range of procedures, customers and clinicians we can 
support.  As with FY26 there remains continued pressure from forex and fuel charges, in 
addition to customer and market dynamics that we must respond to.  
Overall, Medical Technology provides exposure to attractive long-term growth in 
specialist healthcare. It is also a natural destination for the disciplined bolt-on 
investment our balance sheet can support. 
Finally, Animal Care, where the focus is new product and customer momentum. 
The branded portfolio is performing well in the current climate of elevated cost of living 
concerns. Across July and August, Black Hawk and VitaPet delivered mid-high single-
digit value growth compared with the prior corresponding period, ahead of the category. 
While cost inﬂation and the phasing of marketing investments is expected to subdue 
ﬁrst half EBITDA growth, pricing actions will accelerate second half performance. At a 
For personal use only
time when household budgets remain under pressure, this performance demonstrates 
the strength of the brands and the trust of pet owners. 
The second growth engine is international expansion – particularly export of Kiwi 
Kitchens to the USA. Sales of our Kiwi Kitchen product continue to accelerate in the 
United States, albeit off a relatively small base right now. The brand is now ranged 
across California, and the ambition of expanding our presence in more regions remains 
on track. The United States is the world's largest pet food market, so even a modest 
position represents meaningful opportunity over time. 
Our Branded Animal Care business has two distinct sources of growth - strong brands, 
with manufacturing capability in its home markets and increasing reach internationally. 
Whilst not mentioned on the slide, our leading Vet Wholesale business also continues 
to perform well, supported by the structural growth in pet ownership and an ageing pet 
population. As pets live longer, they typically require more frequent and increasingly 
sophisticated care, supporting sustained demand across veterinary medicines, 
consumables and other clinical products. With our broad range, established 
distribution network and trusted customer relationships, Vet Wholesale remains well 
placed to support veterinary practices as that demand continues to develop.   
We are also seeing more and more opportunity to swap best practices between human 
and animal health, particularly in distribution and warehousing.  These practical 
examples point to a much broader opportunity across the group – bringing together the 
capabilities, expertise and scale that already exist across EBOS. 
This now brings me to an important value frontier for EBOS - the opportunity to continue 
to work more efficiently across the Group. 
EBOS has four strong divisions that have historically operated with a high degree of 
independence. Over the past year, our leadership team has established a shared 
purpose and a common Group strategy. That work has identified three practical 
opportunities to collaborate to lower our cost base, each at a different stage of 
development.  
First, combined support office costs. This work is underway across finance and IT, with 
a disciplined focus on centralising, standardising and automating activity, and 
partnering where appropriate. These changes have already begun and we expect 
modest run-rate benefits beginning by the end of FY27, which will then positively 
impact FY28 onwards. 
Second, scaled negotiation. Group-wide visibility and analysis of our external spend 
has identified opportunities to use our scale to drive better negotiating leverage. Value 
creation starts to kick in as legacy contracts renew towards the end of FY27 and in 
FY28. 
Third, supply chain efficiency. This opportunity is at the scoping stage. It involves 
evaluating options on how our network best serves our customers across all our 
For personal use only
divisions, building on the network we have just renewed. This will be a multi-year 
program, carefully sequenced to protect customer service, with an opportunity for 
proof of concept in FY27. 
Together, these initiatives are expected to deliver incremental financial benefits from 
FY28 and unlock opportunities in the organisation. We will provide further detail, 
including the size of the opportunity, with our half-year results in February. 
Turning to the outlook, the resilience of the divisional performance we have outlined is 
particularly important given the signiﬁcant foreign exchange and fuel headwinds 
affecting the business. The Australian dollar has continued to appreciate against the 
New Zealand dollar, up another circa 2% in the period, while the average diesel price 
increased by approximately 20%. Together, these movements have created a material 
headwind to earnings and distribution costs. We expect both drivers to remain volatile 
throughout FY27. Despite this, I am pleased to reaffirm our FY27 guidance. We continue 
to expect: 
• underlying EBITDA of $635 to $655 million, representing mid-single-digit organic 
growth; 
• capex of around $100 million, following the completion of the investment 
program; 
• depreciation and amortisation of $152 to $162 million; and 
• net finance costs of $138 to $148 million. 
As noted, similar to last year EBITDA and NPAT growth are expected to be weighted to 
the second half. Importantly, the confidence in our guidance is supported by the 
Group’s resilient market positions, disciplined execution and the benefits of the 
investment program completed in recent years. 
Let me close by bringing the key themes together. 
First, approximately 85% of Group EBITDA is generated by businesses with number one 
or number two market positions. Second, we have redeployed approximately $2 billion 
of capital over the past five years into higher-growth areas, including Medical 
Technology and Animal Care. Those higher-growth businesses now represent more 
than 70% of Group earnings. 
Third, the major investment cycle is complete. Each division has a clear growth agenda, 
and we are beginning to realise the benefits of operating more effectively as one Group. 
We also have the balance sheet capacity to continue investing, while maintaining the 
discipline on returns that shareholders should expect from us. 
None of this is possible without our people. I would like to thank our teams across 
Australia, New Zealand and Asia for the care and commitment they show every day, and 
our customers and partners for their continued trust. Thank you also to our 
shareholders for your support. 
I will now hand back to Liz.

Ms Julie Tay was appointed to the EBOS Group Limited Board in May 2023. 
Julie is currently a director of Sonova, a global hearing care solutions company, headquartered in Switzerland and 
listed on the Swiss stock exchange. 
She has over 30 years’ experience in international leadership roles across consumer healthcare, medical devices 
and digital healthcare. Julie was most recently Senior Vice President and Managing Director, Asia Pacific and 
member of the global Executive Management Committee for Align Technology. 
Prior to this time, she was regional head of Bayer Healthcare (Diabetes Care) in Asia Pacific and also previously 
held senior executive roles in Asia at Johnson Diversey and Johnson & Johnson.
