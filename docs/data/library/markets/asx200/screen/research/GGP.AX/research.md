# Greatland Resources Limited (GGP.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-09T07:20:07.348242+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Greatland Resources Limited (GGP.AX) has completed its first full financial year as owner of the Telfer gold–copper mine, delivering record production, earnings and cash generation. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy (10/22 models; composite 73%), supported by earnings yield, FCF yield, Lynch PEG (0.14), quality and financial-health factors. The valuation hook is a mid-single-digit P/E on a producer with ~A$1.29bn net cash, optional A$475m undrawn debt capacity, and a funded Havieron development pipeline. The central debate is whether FY26’s profit peak and low AISC (A$2,179/oz) are sustainable into FY27, when guidance implies materially higher costs (A$2,900–3,330/oz), lower volumes (260–300koz), and A$750m–850m of growth capital, against full commodity-price exposure.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, GGP offers a rare combination of current earnings power and optionality on reserve growth and Havieron, rather than classic asset-based deep value. The screen’s buy signal rests on cheapness versus reported earnings (P/E 9.1; E/P 11.0%), FCF yield (~6.5% on screen TTM FCF of ~A$503m), and growth-adjusted valuation (statutory EPS growth ~67%; Lynch PEG 0.14). Quality screens pass (Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple), consistent with FY26 ROE of 48.4% and a balance sheet that shifted from pre-production explorer to net-cash operator in one year.

Business quality has improved materially under Greatland ownership: FY26 gold production of 328,987oz beat guidance; group ore reserves rose to ~5.0Moz Au after Telfer drilling; Havieron received environmental approvals, FID, and a A$500m syndicated facility (undrawn for working-capital tranches at year-end). Telfer integration, processing recoveries and reserve replacement support the screen’s quality and risk-family passes. The name fails dividend and Graham-style deep-value models (no dividend; P/B 3.5), so the case is GARP/quality-value on operating cash conversion, not net-net or income investing.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Commodity cyclicality:** Revenue and FY26 profitability are highly sensitive to gold (~326,859oz sold at ~A$6,223/oz per results release) and copper prices. Management acknowledges macro uncertainty and potential supply-chain/cost impacts. The company maintains gold put options for partial downside protection but full upside exposure.

**Cost and execution:** FY27 AISC guidance is 33–53% above FY26 actuals, while production guidance is lower. Havieron pre-production capex is estimated at A$1,065m (Feasibility Study, June 2025 base), with expansion capex of A$673m largely self-funded—schedule and cost overrun risk is material as FID has been taken and development accelerates.

**Leverage and capital structure:** Reported debt/equity ~1.0 in the screen reflects lease and facility structures despite net cash; A$500m facilities introduce future covenant and refinancing considerations if drawn for Havieron. Capital commitments (A$82.5m) and rehabilitation provisions (sensitivity disclosed in notes; ±5-year timing shifts can move provisions by tens of millions) add closure-cost uncertainty.

**Acquisition overhang:** A$107.4m deferred contingent consideration to Newmont remains a cash outflow risk tied to production and gold price.

**Governance and key personnel:** COO Simon Tyrrell resigned (18 February 2026); Nick Strong appointed COO from 5 October 2026, with Otto Richter moving to CTO. Non-executive director Clive Latcham retired (March 2026). Independent director share sales were reported (September 2026). Management transition during a major capex cycle warrants monitoring.

**Screen red flags:** Piotroski F-Score 5/9 (failed: operating cash flow test, OCF > net income, no share dilution, gross margin trend). Earnings Quality model failed (weak FCF conversion vs earnings in screen methodology—partially at odds with filing FCF of A$737m vs NPAT A$862m, but worth reconciling before sizing). No dividend; Graham-style models fail on P/B and yield.

**Regulatory:** Primary Havieron environmental approvals received (May–June 2026); secondary approvals and ongoing compliance remain execution dependencies.
