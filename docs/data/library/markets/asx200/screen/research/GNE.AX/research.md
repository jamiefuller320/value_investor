# Genesis Energy Limited (GNE.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T12:51:50.202757+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Genesis Energy Limited is a vertically integrated New Zealand electricity and gas generator/retailer, dual-listed on NZX (GNE) and ASX (GNE.AX), with the New Zealand government holding a controlling stake. The quantitative screen rates the name a strong buy on cheapness (P/B 0.9, FCF yield 8.1%), dividend quality (5.6% yield, dividend growth), GARP, and balance-sheet metrics (D/E 48%), with a composite score of 86% versus 83% sector-relative and conviction at 64% (stable, four weeks at signal). Yahoo FY2025 data shows revenue and net profit recovery, but earnings remain below FY2022–23 peaks, ROE is modest at 6.4%, and free cash flow fell sharply in FY2025. The central debate is whether discounted book value and an improving operating trajectory adequately compensate for state-controlled governance, a March 2026 NZ$400m equity raise, and capital-intensive renewable transition — with almost no primary filing body extracts available to validate covenants, contingencies, or going-concern language.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen passes four of four factor families — cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk — on 10 of 22 models, including Schloss Low P/B, FCF Yield, Lynch/Neff PEG variants, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Financial Health. At P/E 15.4, P/B 0.9, and yield 5.6%, the market prices Genesis below tangible book (NZ$2.68bn per Yahoo FY2025) despite a regulated utility asset base with growing customer receipts (NZ$3.78bn cash from customers in FY2025). FY2025 net profit rose 29% to NZ$169.1m and operating income more than doubled to NZ$210.4m, suggesting operational inflection after a weak FY2024 (net profit NZ$131.1m). For a value investor, the hook is a cash-generative franchise trading below book, returning capital via dividends (NZ$115.8m paid in FY2025), with GARP screens satisfied by earnings recovery off a depressed base. Business quality is mixed rather than exceptional: ROE of 6.4% sits below a typical cost of equity, returns on capital have not structurally improved per secondary commentary, and the fixed-asset revaluation reserve (NZ$2.18bn) inflates equity — making the sub-1.0 P/B partly an accounting artefact rather than deep distress. Recent news of a FY26 guidance upgrade (April 2026) supports the recovery narrative, but the completed NZ$400m capital raise (March 2026) introduces dilution and signals management prioritising balance-sheet strength over pure equity yield metrics.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Governance and ownership:** News coverage (10 September 2025) notes state/government control of ~51% of shares versus 41% individual investors — minority shareholders have limited influence over strategy, dividend policy, and capital raises. The NZ$400m rights offer (March 2026) was executed under this ownership structure; filing bodies would be needed to assess related-party terms or government policy alignment.

**Regulatory and sector:** As a NZ integrated generator/retailer, earnings are exposed to wholesale electricity price volatility, gas supply constraints (referenced in the indexed FY24 Q4 headline), Tiwai Point aluminium smelter contract dynamics, and climate/renewable policy. Without filing bodies, specific regulatory contingencies, price-path assumptions, and hedge book disclosures cannot be reviewed.

**Capital structure:** Rising net debt (NZ$1.41bn, +11% YoY) and declining cash (NZ$81m year-end) reduced liquidity headroom prior to the March 2026 raise. The equity raise addresses leverage but dilutes existing holders (shares reportedly fell 3.8% on completion, 23 March 2026). Post-raise capital structure cannot be verified from primary filings in this pack.

**Earnings quality and returns:** ROE of 6.4% is low for a leveraged utility; secondary articles (October–November 2025) question whether Genesis has accelerated returns on capital. FY2024 operating income collapse and FY2025 partial recovery suggest commodity/mark-to-market sensitivity not fully captured by static value screens.

**Balance sheet accounting:** The large revaluation reserve (NZ$2.18bn) means P/B < 1 may overstate economic margin of safety if asset values are reassessed downward. Long-term provisions (NZ$203m) and impairment charges (NZ$0.9m FY2025; NZ$0.5–4.0m prior years) hint at asset-retirement and capital obsolescence risks. Derivative liabilities of NZ$38m (Yahoo FY2025) cannot be contextualised without hedge disclosures from filings.

**Capital allocation and dividend sustainability:** Renewable growth ambitions (H1 FY2026 earnings commentary, February 2026) imply elevated future capex; FY2024–25 capex already ran NZ$135–152m versus NZ$88–93m in FY2022–23. FCF compression in FY2025 is a yellow flag for dividend sustainability if capex escalates, notwithstanding the screen’s Dividend Growth pass and September 2025 dividend increase headlines.

**Filing gaps:** Absence of primary filing bodies prevents assessment of going-concern statements, debt covenants, contingent liabilities, and related-party transactions. This is a material research limitation for a leveraged utility that has recently raised equity.
