# Genesis Energy Limited (GNE.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T19:16:53.642787+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Genesis Energy Limited is a vertically integrated New Zealand electricity and gas generator/retailer, dual-listed on NZX and ASX, with the New Zealand government holding a controlling stake (~51% per news coverage). The quantitative screen rates GNE.AX a strong buy on cheapness (P/B 0.9, FCF yield 8.1%), dividend quality (5.6% yield, dividend growth), GARP, and balance-sheet metrics (D/E 48%), with an 86% composite score held persistently for four weeks. The valuation hook is a cash-generative utility franchise trading below tangible book, returning capital via dividends, with FY2025 earnings recovery (net profit +29% YoY per Yahoo fallback) and a recent FY26 guidance upgrade. The central debate is whether discounted book value and an improving operating trajectory adequately compensate for state-controlled governance, the March 2026 NZ$400m equity raise, rising thermal generation and emissions amid gas/hydro constraints (evident in the FY24 Q4 filing), and a capital-intensive renewable transition — with no annual report body available to validate covenants, contingencies, or going-concern language.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen passes four of four factor families — cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk — on 10 of 22 models, including Schloss Low P/B, FCF Yield, Lynch/Neff PEG variants, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Financial Health. At P/E 15.4, P/B 0.9, and yield 5.6%, the market prices Genesis below tangible book (NZ$2.68bn per Yahoo FY2025) despite a regulated utility asset base with growing customer receipts (NZ$3.78bn cash from customers in FY2025, Yahoo fallback).

FY2025 net profit rose 29% to NZ$169.1m and operating income more than doubled to NZ$210.4m (Yahoo), suggesting operational inflection after a weak FY2024 (net profit NZ$131.1m). The FY24 Q4 filing confirms retail momentum — 496,596 customers (+2.7% YoY), electricity netback of NZ$150/MWh (+5.3%) — even as wholesale operations were strained by gas shortages and low hydro storage. For a value investor, the hook is a cash-generative franchise trading below book, returning capital via dividends (NZ$115.8m paid in FY2025, Yahoo), with GARP screens satisfied by earnings recovery off a depressed base.

Business quality is mixed rather than exceptional: ROE of 6.4% sits below a typical cost of equity, returns on capital have not structurally improved per secondary commentary, and the fixed-asset revaluation reserve (NZ$2.18bn, Yahoo) inflates equity — making the sub-1.0 P/B partly an accounting artefact rather than deep distress. Recent news of a FY26 guidance upgrade (April 2026) supports the recovery narrative, but the completed NZ$400m capital raise (March 2026) introduces dilution and signals management prioritising balance-sheet strength over pure equity yield metrics.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Governance and ownership:** News coverage (10 September 2025) notes state/government control of ~51% of shares versus 41% individual investors — minority shareholders have limited influence over strategy, dividend policy, and capital raises. The NZ$400m rights offer (March 2026) was executed under this ownership structure.

**Fuel security and emissions (filing body):** The FY24 Q4 report flags ongoing gas shortages, hydro storage at 85% of long-run average, and heavy reliance on Huntly Rankine coal generation (99% coal-fuelled in Q4 FY24). Generation emissions rose 244% in Q4 and 128% YTD FY24. Coal stockpile fell to 231 kt from 962 kt pcp; imported coal was required. Reserve Rankine operation is contingent on staffing, unit reliability, and fuel availability — operational dependencies explicitly stated in the filing.

**Regulatory and sector:** As a NZ integrated generator/retailer, earnings are exposed to wholesale electricity price volatility, gas supply constraints, Tiwai Point contract dynamics, and climate/renewable policy. Without annual filing bodies, specific regulatory contingencies and hedge disclosures cannot be reviewed.

**Capital structure:** Rising net debt (NZ$1.41bn, +11% YoY, Yahoo) and declining cash (NZ$81m) reduced liquidity headroom prior to the March 2026 raise. The equity raise addresses leverage but dilutes existing holders (shares reportedly fell 3.8% on completion, 23 March 2026). Post-raise capital structure cannot be verified from primary filings.

**Earnings quality and returns:** ROE of 6.4% is low for a leveraged utility. FY2024 operating income collapse and FY2025 partial recovery suggest commodity and fuel-cost sensitivity not fully captured by static value screens. Gross customer churn rose to 22.5% in Q4 FY24 (from 20.3% pcp, filing body).

**Balance sheet accounting:** The large revaluation reserve (NZ$2.18bn) means P/B < 1 may overstate economic margin of safety if asset values are reassessed downward. Long-term provisions (NZ$203m) and impairment charges (NZ$0.9m FY2025) hint at asset-retirement risks. Derivative liabilities of NZ$38m (Yahoo) cannot be contextualised without hedge disclosures.

**Dividend sustainability:** Renewable growth ambitions imply elevated future capex; FY2024–25 capex ran NZ$135–152m versus NZ$88–93m in FY2022–23. FCF compression in FY2025 is a yellow flag if capex escalates.

**Filing gaps:** Absence of annual filing bodies prevents assessment of going-concern statements, debt covenants, contingent liabilities, and related-party transactions.
