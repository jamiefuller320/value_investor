## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Orora Limited (ASX:ORA) is an Australasian packaging group spanning beverage cans, closures and premium glass (Saverglass), following a major portfolio reshaping: the FY2024 Saverglass acquisition, a large FY2025 divestiture (Yahoo reports ~A$907m discontinued-operation gain), and material deleveraging. The quantitative screen rates the stock a **strong buy** (9/22 models, composite 94%, sector-relative 85%), driven by cheapness (P/E 14.3, P/B 0.6), a 7.0% dividend yield, modest gearing (D/E ~29%), and passes across cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk families. The valuation hook is asset-backed cheapness on a cleaner balance sheet after FY2025; the key debate is whether that supports accumulation, or whether weak continuing returns (ROE 4.4%), dividend cover below free cash flow, and an April 2026 Saverglass earnings reset amid Middle East disruption mark a value trap. **No Orora annual or interim filing bodies are indexed** (`filings_index.json`: 0 annual, 0 interim, 0 downloadable bodies; indexed items are mis-tagged third-party pages for other ASX tickers), so financial analysis below relies on Yahoo fallback data and must be treated as unverified pending primary filings.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Orora fits a post-transformation deep-value and income profile that aligns closely with the screen’s strong-buy signal. The stock passes Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value and Financial Health — spanning all four model families (cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk) with high data quality (19/20 metrics). Sub-1.0 P/B (0.64) on positive tangible book (Yahoo: A$1.41bn FY2025) and net debt cut from A$1.72bn to A$228m after FY2025 suggest the market is pricing execution and portfolio risk rather than balance-sheet distress. Screen conviction has risen to 68% (persistent, four weeks at signal, stable), reinforcing the quantitative case.

Business quality, however, is mixed and only partly captured by the screen. Continuing operations are thin: FY2025 continuing net income was A$66m on A$2.90bn equity (ROE ~4.4% per screen), while reported net income of A$973m was dominated by divestiture gains. The bull case rests on (1) a simplified, lower-leverage core focused on cans and packaging, (2) capital return (A$127m buyback and sustained dividends in FY2025 per Yahoo), and (3) mean-reversion if cans momentum (H1 FY2026 news) offsets glass weakness. The bear case — low recurring returns, dividends exceeding FCF, goodwill-heavy equity, and geopolitical exposure at Saverglass — is not fully penalised by static cheapness metrics. Net: the screen correctly identifies **asset-backed cheapness and income**; deep research supports the quantitative signal on valuation and leverage, but not on earnings quality or dividend durability without filing verification.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
1. **Earnings reset and cyclicality** — April 2026 guidance cut: news reports Saverglass FY26 underlying EBIT trimmed to €63–68m (excluding direct conflict effects), triggering a ~19% share-price fall. Continuing ROE of 4.4% suggests the market may be discounting sustained weak returns, not temporary noise alone.

2. **Geopolitical / operational (Saverglass)** — Middle East tensions halted bottle production at Saverglass’s Ras Al Khaimah (UAE) facility (news, Apr 2026). Without filing contingency or going-concern language, exposure size, insurance and recovery timelines cannot be verified; glass remains a strategic pain point despite reported cans strength.

3. **M&A and capital allocation** — FY2024 Saverglass acquisition (A$2.16bn) followed by FY2025 large discontinued gain implies a rapid buy-then-sell cycle that raises governance and capital-allocation questions; event-driven gains obscure underlying packaging economics.

4. **Dividend sustainability** — ~7% screen yield is attractive, but dividends exceeded FCF in the last two reported years (Yahoo). News (Apr 2026) reports the **buyback was paused** alongside the outlook cut. Franking, payout-ratio commitments and dividend-policy statements are unverified without annual-report notes.

5. **Interest, leases and covenants** — FY2025 interest expense A$66m (down from A$97m) plus capital lease obligations A$168m still matter relative to A$66m continuing net income. **Covenant headroom cannot be assessed** — no filing bodies with debt-facility or covenant disclosures.

6. **Intangibles and impairment** — A$1.48bn goodwill/intangibles against A$2.90bn equity; any Saverglass or glass impairment would hit book value and weaken the P/B thesis.

7. **Pension / employee benefits** — Yahoo shows non-current employee benefit liabilities A$31m and current pension/post-retirement A$74m — modest versus equity but **unverified** against actuarial assumptions in filings.

8. **Governance / going concern** — No primary filing bodies available; going-concern, litigation, contingencies and related-party disclosures **cannot be reviewed**. This is a material research gap for a verify-before-trade pack.

9. **Screen blind spots** — Low ROE, earnings volatility and event-driven reported earnings are poorly captured by static cheapness/dividend models; timing signal is insufficient_data (RSI/SMA200 unavailable).
