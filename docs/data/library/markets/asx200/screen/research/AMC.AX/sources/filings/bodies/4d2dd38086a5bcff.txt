Results for the half year ended 30 September 2026 First results as a listed company: Strong first half performance, on track to deliver FY'27 guidance Interim dividend of 6.4 US cents per share declared London, 26 October 2026 – Airtel Mobile Commerce N.V. ('Airtel Money' or the 'Group', LSE: AMC), one of Africa's leading digital financial services platforms, today announces its results for the half year ended 30 September 2026, its first as a listed company. Operating highlights Customer base grew by 18.7% to 56.7 million, driving mobile money penetration of telco customer base to 42.9%, up 3.2pp from the prior year. Our app customer base increased by 64.3% to 4.1m, reflecting continued digital adoption, deeper customer engagement and higher transaction activity. These strong operating trends have supported 36.4% constant currency growth in annualised total processed value (‘TPV’) to USD 270bn for Q2’27. ARPU grew by 5.1% in constant currency, reflecting an increase in transaction value per customer to USD 397 per month, an increase of 14.9%. Our commitment to delivering an affordable solution through a highly scalable technology stack is supported by a continued focus on expanding our distribution network to reach more users and drive financial inclusion. Active agents grew by 26.3% to 2.4 million, while the active merchants increased to over 587,000, an increase of 85.5%. Financial performance Revenue of USD 830m continued to benefit from increased scale and higher levels of engagement, delivering growth of 34.0% in reported currency and 25.1% in constant currency. Currency appreciation over the prior period resulted in higher revenue growth in reported currency than in constant currency, mainly due to appreciation in the Zambian kwacha. The constant currency revenue growth reflects the consistent execution of our strategy and continued strong momentum across the product portfolio, with wallet services up 27.6%, payments and transfers up 29.4% and financial services up 44.8%. Revenue growth of 25.1% was impacted by the changes to intra-group agreements (IGA) mainly related to retention revenue from July 2025 onwards. Gross profit of USD 499m increased by 31.3% in reported currency and 22.0% in constant currency, from USD 381m in the prior period. Gross margin at 60.2% of revenue compared with 61.4% in the prior period, impacted largely by the change to the intra-group agreements. Underlying EBITDA grew by 26.4% in reported currency to USD 398m with the underlying EBITDA margin at 48.0%, compared with 50.8% in the prior period, driven primarily by the impact from the change to the intra-group agreements with effect from July 2025 and additional levies in one of the markets. Profit after tax of USD 228m improved from USD 188m in the prior period, primarily reflecting the strong revenue growth over the period. Operating free cash flow up 27.1% to USD 378m driven by higher underlying EBITDA, pre-tax conversion ratio at 94.9%, up 54 bps from prior period (from 94.4%). Basic EPS of 7.9 US cents, up from 6.5 US cents in the prior period, predominantly reflects the growth in operating profit. Excluding exceptional items relating to IPO expense recorded during the current period, EPS before exceptional items at 8.1 US cents, up from 6.5 US cents in the prior period. The Board has declared an interim dividend of 6.4 US cents per share, in line with dividend policy. The interim dividend represents 81% of profit attributable to owners of the company. The dividend is consistent with the Group’s stated policy of targeting a minimum payout of 80% of profit attributable to owners of the Company. We reiterate our near-to-mid-term guidance. For FY’27, the Group has guided to revenue growth of mid-20s% in constant currency, with underlying EBITDA margins expected to moderate by up to c.2 percentage points relative to FY’26. Over the medium term, constant currency revenue growth is expected to trend upwards in line with historical constant currency growth. Refer note 4c of back half. Financial review for the half year ended 30 September 2026 Revenue Revenue in reported currency increased by 34.0% to USD 830m, with constant currency growth of 25.1%. Reported currency revenue growth is higher than constant currency growth, reflecting currency appreciation, mainly in the Zambian kwacha. Constant currency revenue growth was supported by growth in the customer base and transaction value per customer. At a segment level, East Africa showed constant currency growth of 20.5%. Francophone Africa grew by 40.2%, mainly driven by a strong performance in the DRC. Revenue growth of 25.1% was impacted by the changes to intra-group agreements (IGA) from July 2025 onwards. Revenue growth was accompanied by ongoing product diversification, with the continued revenue growth of financial services reflecting the successful execution of our strategy to deepen ecosystem engagement and expand higher-value offerings. Wallet services revenue grew by 27.6% in constant currency, payments and transfers grew by 29.4% and financial services grew by 44.8%. Gross profit Gross profit of USD 499m, up 31.3% in reported currency (22.0% in constant currency) from USD 381m in the prior period, reflecting the growth in revenue, with the gross profit margin at 60.2% of revenue (61.4% in the prior period). The decline in gross profit margin largely resulted from the change to the intra-group agreements (IGA) from July 2025. Underlying EBITDA Reported currency underlying EBITDA grew by 26.4% to USD 398m, while in constant currency, underlying EBITDA increased by 16.1%. Reported currency growth was ahead of constant currency growth, reflecting currency appreciation, mainly in the Zambian kwacha. Underlying EBITDA growth was partly offset by the impact from the change to the IGA, which came into effect in July 2025. The underlying EBITDA margin for the period was 48.0%, a decline of 286 bps from the prior period, due primarily to the IGA changes, which impacted margins by 270 bps and had corresponding impact on underlying EBITDA growth. Furthermore, an additional levy in DRC impacted the underlying EBITDA margin by 60 bps. The first half margin reflects the greater concentration of the IGA impact in H1. As this effect moderates, we expect the margin to strengthen in H2, leaving us on track to deliver our full-year guidance. Intra-group agreement change In July 2025, upon expiry of the existing lock-ins and agreements that were established at the time of the minority investors’ investment in Airtel Mobile Commerce N.V., Airtel Africa renegotiated the terms of intra-group agreements between the mobile services and mobile money segments. The revised agreements continue to be based on arm’s length pricing and reflect evolving market dynamics. Effective July 2025, the primary amendment relates to retention revenue, currently classified as “Others” within the mobile money segment, which will be gradually phased out by March 2027. The revised agreements also encompass recharge revenue, collections revenue services provided to Airtel Africa and sales force cross ‑ charges, SMS and USSD costs, and IT services and management support services received from Airtel Africa. As the revised intra-group agreements took effect from July 2025, the first half of the current year had an incremental impact on underlying EBITDA, impacting margin by 270 bps in H1’27 over H1’26. Airtel Money is addressing this through a digital-first, mobile-led platform. Our focus remains on growing our customer base, scaling digital adoption, expanding our distribution network and diversifying our product ecosystem (including merchant and enterprise payments). Further grow the Group's existing large customer base The Group has a large, unpenetrated captive customer base opportunity comprising over 75 million telco subscribers across its footprint, representing meaningful headroom for growth. By focusing on high-quality telco-linked customers, the Group benefits from a structurally lower customer acquisition cost due to distribution synergies with the telco network. Customer acquisition is accelerated through targeted campaigns, differentiated recharge products, referral and incentive models, and ecosystem marketing. The flywheel effects of the Airtel Money platform from the emergence and adoption of new product use cases will also serve to accelerate the Group's customer base growth. Drive App usage and deepen customer engagement The Group is focused on deepening customer engagement by accelerating adoption of its smartphone-based app and creating a seamless user experience. Our app customer base increased by 64.3% to 4.1m. App users generate higher ARPU than featurephone users; therefore, the Group is prioritising app adoption and engagement through several measures including seamless onboarding, gamification, bonus and cashback rewards. The digital-first approach is expected to improve monetisation and increase retention through greater wallet stickiness. Extend distribution network A strong distribution footprint remains the cornerstone of the Group's strategy. As of 30 September 2026, the Group had a network of over 2.4 million agents and over 43,000 exclusive retail touchpoints (AMBs and Kiosks). The Group's exclusive retail touchpoints and scaled agent network provide in-person onboarding, education and support, improving retention. Agents also act as brand ambassadors, providing visibility and confidence using Airtel Money branding and signage. Accelerate merchant network The Group is committed to scaling its merchant acceptance network to enable everyday digital payments and drive ecosystem digitisation. This includes deployment of interoperable QR codes, merchant codes, app and POS systems, tap-and-pay, and payment links, supported by partnerships with global payment networks. By embedding merchant payments into daily life, the Group aims to increase transaction frequency, drive platform stickiness, and deepen customer engagement across both capital cities and regional markets. Build enterprise payments The Group is poised to introduce a suite of significant features designed to further enhance the enterprise payments portal. These upcoming developments include registration changes, new capabilities for vendor payments, the introduction of payment links, improvements for transaction reversals, user interface enhancements as well as the acceleration of biller migration to the enterprise portal for existing partners across all markets. Expand product ecosystem The Group intends to continue evolving from a transactional mobile wallet into a fully-fledged digital financial services platform. Product expansion areas include merchant acquiring, e-commerce, lending, insurance, savings and wealth management, and virtual and physical cards. The breadth of the ecosystem is designed to increase customer lifetime value, reinforce platform stickiness, and diversify revenue streams. The consistent execution of our strategy resulted in higher revenue contribution from financial services by 0.6pp (increased to 4.7% as compared with 4.1% in prior period). Summary of principal risks The Group continually monitors its external and internal environment to identify risks which could impact its operations and the achievement of its objectives. Strategic growth and competition: The Group operates in highly competitive mobile money and digital financial services markets. Failure to innovate, respond to changing customer needs, successfully execute strategic initiatives or maintain competitive market positions could adversely impact growth, customer engagement and financial performance. Technology resilience and cyber security: The Group depends on resilient technology platforms, infrastructure and third-party systems to deliver services to customers. Technology failures, service disruption, cyber-attacks or information security incidents could adversely affect operations, customer trust and financial performance. Relationship with Airtel Africa: The Group has significant commercial arrangements with Airtel Africa Group, including access to its telco subscriber base, distribution network for customer onboarding, brand, technology and shared services. Any disruption, discontinuation or material change to these arrangements, or to Airtel Africa’s telco operations in the Group’s markets, could adversely affect customer acquisition, cost structure, profitability and cash flows. Third-party dependencies and distribution network: The Group relies on key commercial arrangements, technology providers, distributors, agents, merchants and other strategic partners to support its operations and growth. Failure of these relationships, or deficiencies in the performance of third parties, could impact service delivery, customer experience and operational effectiveness. Brand and reputation: The Group's reputation and the strength of the Airtel Money brand are critical to its success. Operational failures, service disruptions, regulatory actions, financial crime incidents or adverse publicity could damage stakeholder confidence and adversely affect business performance. Financial resilience: The Group is exposed to foreign exchange volatility, exchange control restrictions, liquidity constraints, credit risk from various counterparties and challenges associated with the repatriation of funds in certain markets. These factors may adversely affect operating costs, profitability, cash flows and financial position and flexibility. Business resilience: The Group operates in markets that may experience political, economic, regulatory or civil instability. Such events may disrupt operations, affect customer activity and impact the Group's ability to execute its strategy and deliver services. Legal and regulatory risk: The Group operates in a highly regulated environment and is subject to licensing, regulatory and legal obligations across multiple jurisdictions. Changes in laws, regulations, licensing conditions or governmental policy, as well as adverse legal or regulatory proceedings, could affect the Group's operations, reputation and financial performance. Financial crime risk: The Group is exposed to risks arising from money laundering, terrorist financing, proliferation financing, sanctions breaches, fraud, bribery and corruption. Failure to effectively prevent, detect or respond to such risks could result in financial loss, regulatory action and reputational damage. Data privacy and information protection: The Group processes significant volumes of personal and transactional data. Failure to comply with data protection requirements, or a significant data privacy or information security breach, could result in regulatory sanctions, financial penalties, litigation and loss of customer confidence. Exceptional items Management exercises judgement in determining the adjustments to apply to IFRS measurements in order to derive APMs, which provide additional useful information on the underlying trends, performance and position of the Group.
