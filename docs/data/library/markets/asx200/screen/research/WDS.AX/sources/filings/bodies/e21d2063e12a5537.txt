Despite the dour close at benchmark level, in the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers breezed past decliners by a margin of 100 (187 to 87). For the week, the XJO finished down 86.4 points or 0.95% lower, 1.3% from its intraweek high and 0.8% from its intraweek low. The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (+1.1%) was the best sector for a second consecutive day as COMEX gold futures rallied 2.8% overnight (but fell 0.6% to US$4,514/oz in Asian trade). The catalyst was a second day of easing in US benchmark bond yields, reducing the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold. Ora Banda Mining (OBM) (+5.6%) , Bellevue Gold (BGL) (+3.3%) , Pantoro Gold (PNR) (+3.2%) , and Emerald Resources (EMR) (+3.0%) all advanced. Gold Futures (Front month, back-adjusted) COMEX Information Technology (XIJ) (+1.0%) and Communication Services (XTJ) (+1.0%) shared precisely the same lower-yield tailwind, as these sectors' long-duration earnings structures benefit from the lower discount rates that flow from lower risk-free yields. Nuix (NXL) (+5.3%) , Life360 (360) (+4.1%) , Megaport (MP1) (+4.1%) , and WiseTech Global (WTC) (+2.5%) all advanced within technology. The classifieds names led Communication Services — Seek (SEK) (+2.4%) , REA Group (REA) (+2.3%) , and CAR Group (CAR) (+2.3%) . Financials (XFJ) (+0.1%) and Health Care (XHJ) (-0.1%) were both essentially flat — the major banks and large-cap healthcare names holding ground as capital that had flowed to them on Wednesday's risk-off move rotated back toward technology and cyclicals. Within Financials, the four major banks were fractionally mixed: Westpac (WBC) (+0.1%) barely gained while ANZ (ANZ) (-0.2%) and CBA (-0.1%) barely lost — a sector in equilibrium rather than in motion. Within Healthcare, Cochlear (COH) (+1.3%) , Sonic Healthcare (SHL) (+1.2%) , and Pro Medicus (PME) (+0.7%) advanced while CSL (CSL) (-0.6%) gave back a portion of its recent recovery. Energy (XEJ) (-1.2%) was the session's worst sector, but the headline decline is substantially explained by ex-dividends rather than any fundamental deterioration. Ampol (ALD) (-6.0%) and Viva Energy (VEA) (-3.7%) both went ex-dividend today — accounting for the lion's share of the sector-level damage. ICE Brent crude futures added 0.4% to US$95.91/bbl in Asian trade, approaching what would be their highest close since 19 May, so the underlying oil price environment remains supportive. Still, Woodside Energy (WDS) (-1.2%) and Santos (STO) (-0.8%) eased — likely reflecting some profit-taking before the weekend. Materials (XMJ) (-0.8%) was the other weak sector, with a familiar paradox at its core — COMEX copper futures gained 1.1% overnight before easing just 0.1% to US$6.655/lb in Asian trade, and SGX iron ore futures added 0.5% to US$99.40/t, yet the sector fell. The explanation is largely BHP (BHP) (-2.4%) — the mining giant extended a losing streak (it went ex-dividend yesterday FYI), suggesting possible repositioning within the sector, or perhaps even, valuation concerns. Rio Tinto (RIO) (-0.7%) also fell. Offsetting the BHP and RIO drag, copper specialists found buyers in line with the overnight LME gains: Firefly Metals (FFM) (+4.8%) , Aurelia Metals (AMI) (+4.5%) , and Aeris Resources (AIS) (+2.0%) all surged. Elsewhere, Fortescue (FMG) (+1.6%) was the standout among iron ore plays. Lithium Carbonate Futures (Benchmark month, back-adjusted) GFEX In other commodities moves, lithium stocks fell again as GFEX lithium carbonate futures plunged 5.3% to CNY 141,940/t — breaking through a key technical support level at CNY 148,320/t that had provided a floor for the past several sessions. With that level now breached, the next significant technical support sits at CNY 136,260/t — roughly 4% below where the contract closed. Wildcat Resources (WC8) (-3.8%) , Pilbara Minerals (PLS) (-2.1%) , Core Lithium (CXO) (-1.4%) , Mineral Resources (MIN) (-1.4%) , and IGO (IGO) (-1.3%) were all lower. Uranium stocks extended Thursday's rally for a second day — this time with confirmation from the US market, where the Sprott Uranium Miners ETF jumped 4.1% in New York overnight. COMEX uranium futures held flat at US$89.45/lb, but the rally in the ETF move provided all the momentum the local names needed. Paladin Energy (PDN) (+3.9%) , Boss Energy (BOE) (+3.8%) , NexGen Energy (NXG) (+2.7%) , and Bannerman Energy (BMN) (+1.2%) all advanced. Gap… run… high close… good stuff. 👍 And consider: about average candle (i.e., strong, but not extraordinary with respect to the regular price action)… and below average volume (i.e., only modest demand side engagement in a supply side vacuum). Conclusion: It looks like the demand side came creeping back in (the bond yield bogeyman didn’t get much better, but at least it didn’t get much worse — and the natural state of the Comp is to rise! 🫒) It also looks like the supply side took a rain check — probably waiting for a better price inside 26701-2719 . When I look at a candle like Thursday’s, within the broader technical framework, my brain naturally says: I’d expect another strong demand side candle next . But we saw a very similar candle 5 candles ago, and it was followed by an upward pointing shadow stopping precisely at the supply zone. It will be super interesting to see if that supply remains active in the system, or if it's found to be conspicuous by its absence ! 🤔 Can I go so far as to say: Thursday’s candle sets the Comp up for one of its most important sessions in months … If we see another strong demand side candle, that closes deep inside the 26701-27191 supply zone, then it almost certainly must trigger a wave of FOMO and HOFU throughout the demand-supply environment. Volume will be interesting as well. It’s September now. Markets should be emerging from August’s typical malaise. Let’s see some engagement so we can make more confident decisions here. As for risk… You know that I hate being less than FRP on the Comp ! It’s a beast. It can make you look very, very conservative (read as stupid! 😉) very quickly. Still, I’d much rather have a strong bull phase than the herd of zebras / chop-chop-chop price action we’re stuck in right now... So come on Comp — do your worst (best!) View 1/2RP . ( RP = R isk P osition — it reflects my personal allowable capital allocation limit for my investments in US stocks. So 1/2RP is 50%, 2/3RP is 67% and FRP is 100%). A long white candle, high close, and preferably on some volume — to signal the demand side has what it takes to overcome what is clearly a very credible supply side that remains in the system . So, let's just play it cool until we see such a signal. Today was a great opportunity for one to occur... but it just wasn't meant to be!
