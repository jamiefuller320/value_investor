Reflecting the positivity at benchmark level, in the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers beat decliners by an impressive 232 to 52. Information Technology (XIJ) (+4.5%) delivered the session's biggest sectoral rebound and the logic is clean — high-P/E, long-duration growth stocks are the assets most acutely sensitive to benchmark bond yields, and oil's 10% two-day collapse removed the inflation premium that had been forcing yields higher. Lower yields mean lower discount rates applied to future earnings, which mechanically lifts the present value of technology stocks with earnings weighted far into the future. ASX 200 Information Technology (XIJ) intraday chart WiseTech Global (WTC) (+7.0%) , Xero (XRO) (+6.0%) , Technology One (TNE) (+5.8%) , and Life360 (360) (+5.5%) all surged in a reversal that recouped a meaningful portion of the past two weeks' losses. The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (+3.9%) recovered sharply for the same bond yield reason — plus an additional and direct benefit: lower diesel costs as oil retreats are one of the largest input cost improvements a gold miner can receive. COMEX gold futures gained 0.5% to US$4,089/oz and COMEX silver futures gained 1.1% to US$59.55/oz. Ora Banda Mining (OBM) (+6.7%) , Pantoro Gold (PNR) (+6.5%) , Westgold Resources (WGX) (+6.1%) , and Catalyst Metals (CYL) (+6.0%) led the recovery. Evolution Mining (EVN) (+2.0%) separately agreed to acquire Carnaby Resources in a $213 million scrip deal, adding the Greater Duchess copper-gold project in Queensland to its portfolio — Carnaby Resources (CNB) (+57.3%) surged on the news. ASX 200 Materials (XMJ) intraday chart Materials (XMJ) (+2.4%) tracked the same logic — mining sector input costs fall when oil falls, since diesel is among the largest operating expenses for most extraction operations. COMEX copper futures eased just 0.1% to US$6.352/lb and SGX iron ore futures were also flat at US$98.00/t, meaning the commodity price leads were minimal. The equity gains ran well ahead of underlying price moves — a classic short-covering and positioning reversal after weeks of relentless selling. Sandfire Resources (SFR) (+3.9%) , Capstone Copper (CSC) (+3.2%) , Rio Tinto (RIO) (+2.2%) , and BHP (BHP) (+2.1%) all advanced. Real Estate (XPJ) (+1.7%) and Health Care (XHJ) (+1.4%) were the other notable best-sector performers. Both are bond-proxy or long-duration sectors that benefit from falling yields — the XPJ through improved relative income appeal as risk-free rates fall, and XHJ through lower discount rates applied to premium healthcare multiples. Lendlease (LLC) (+3.2%) , Goodman Group (GMG) (+3.2%) — noting HMC Capital's +3.6% among the broader real estate-adjacent financials — and 4DMedical (4DX) (+5.5%) , Cochlear (COH) (+2.6%) , ResMed (RMD) (+2.1%) , and CSL (CSL) (+1.9%) all recovered ground lost through July's yield surge. ASX 200 Financial XFJ) intraday chart Financials (XFJ) (+1.2%) participated in the rally but lagged the pace of the more yield-sensitive sectors — the major banks benefit from lower rates as they reduce near-term credit quality concerns, but the effect is less mechanical than on bond proxies or growth stocks. National Australia Bank (NAB) (+1.4%) , Westpac (WBC) (+1.5%) , ANZ (ANZ) (+1.2%) , and Commonwealth Bank (CBA) (+1.2%) all gained. Perpetual (PPT) (+3.1%) was lifted by EQT Partners increasing its takeover proposal to $22.50 per share, valuing the business at approximately $2.65 billion. ASX 200 Energy (XEJ) intraday chart Energy (XEJ) (-3.0%) was, unavoidably, the session's worst performer — the oil price mechanism that had made the sector one of the only winners through July's volatility reversed with equivalent brutality. ICE Brent crude futures fell 9.6% to US$87.45/bbl after having already declined 4.1% on Friday — a two-day fall of more than 13% from recent highs. Karoon Energy (KAR) (-7.8%) bore the heaviest losses, with Yancoal Australia (YAL) (-4.8%) , Beach Energy (BPT) (-3.9%) , New Hope Corp. (NHC) (-3.8%) , and Santos (STO) (-3.6%) all retreating sharply. Santos separately marked the export of the first condensate cargo from its Barossa gas project — a positive operational milestone — though it did little to offset the sector's oil price headwind. Lithium stocks rebounded alongside the broader mining rally, with GFEX lithium carbonate futures gaining 1.1% to CNY 145,860/t. Core Lithium (CXO) (+6.3%) , Liontown Resources (LTR) (+3.4%) , IGO (IGO) (+2.9%) , Pilbara Minerals (PLS) (+2.7%) , and Mineral Resources (MIN) (+2.2%) all advanced. Rare earths stocks also firmed — NdPr in China rose 1.0% to CNY 765,000/t, with Lynas Rare Earths (LYC) (+0.5%) edging higher after separately signing binding agreements with South Korea's LS Eco Energy for mutual ~$29 million convertible instrument investments.
