Reflecting the neither-here-nor-there nature of today’s trade, in the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers and decliners were nearly dead-even at 139 to 140! You've heard me use the idiom " Robbing Peter to pay Paul " here for, well, as long as you've been reading my Evening Wraps! It turns out (after a little Googling) that its really an old proverb that dates back to the late 14th century, to works by English theologian, Catholic priest, and Oxford professor John Wycliffe in which he uses the Apostles Saint Peter and Saint Paul to comment on medieval shifts in church funds and assets. That's the first thing you learned today. You're welcome. Because the next comment is something you already knew: The OTP is a Rob Peter to Pay Paul kind of market , and it's been like that since at least the 1300's! 😭 Here are the only two charts you need to know right now (um, two lots of two actually). Exhibits A & B: S&P/ASX 200 Financials Sector Index (XFJ) daily & intraday charts S&P-ASX 200 Financials Sector Index (XFJ) intraday chart (top), and daily chart (bottom) Exhibits C & D: Materials Sector Index (XMJ) daily & intraday charts S&P-ASX 200 Materials Sector Index (XMJ) intraday chart (top), and daily chart (bottom) It hasn't always been this way... because for much of the back-half of 2025-and that start of this year it was the exact opposite — mining stocks were Paul and the big banks were Peter. And because they're roughly the same size in terms of market capitalisation — and because the OTP is a market capitalisation-weighted index — the net result of Peter down vs Paul up is, was, and probably forever shall be : roughly nil. 🤦 As for other stuff that happened today, I also note that another theme seemed to play out yet again. "Defence wins because capital still must be allocated in an uncertain investing environment". The reliable, recession-proof earnings streams of Consumer Staples (XSJ) (+0.4%) , Health Care (XHJ) (+0.1%) , and Utilities (XUJ) (+0.1%) joined Financials (XFJ) (+0.4%) in the winners column. Apart from Materials (XMJ) (-2.0%) , other growth sectors like Consumer Discretionary (XDJ) (-1.7%) were also hit today, this time on the back of Nick Scali (NCK) (-15.3%) warning FY27 profits will fall sharply due to "challenging" retail conditions. Then you get stuff like Energy (XEJ) (-2.4%) as the worst performing sector today, even as brent crude oil is probing its highest close since the war with Iran began. There are some quarterly updates floating around ( Woodside Energy (WDS) (-3.5%) yesterday and Santos (STO) (-1.3%) today) — but can I just say this is a rather tricky little market to navigate!
