Despite the gain at headline level, in the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers lagged decliners by 122 to 154. S&P-ASX 200 Energy Sector Index intraday chart – firing on all cylinders today. Energy (XEJ) (+1.8%) was Monday's dominant sector as ICE Brent crude futures gained 1.3% to US$97.50/bbl and globalCoal Newcastle Coal futures surged 1.8% to US$154.05/t. Thermal coal is now up more than 10% across the past nine sessions, while coking coal has surged more than 32% since the start of August. Whitehaven Coal (WHC) (+7.0%) led the sector, with New Hope Corp. (NHC) (+4.1%) , Yancoal Australia (YAL) (+3.9%) , and Stanmore Resources (SMR) (+3.3%) also popping. Oil and gas producers were solid, too, as Karoon Energy (KAR) (+2.3%) , Santos (STO) (+1.7%) , Woodside Energy (WDS) (+1.6%) , and Beach Energy (BPT) (+1.2%) all advanced. Materials (XMJ) (+0.4%) was carried almost entirely by iron ore stocks as SGX iron ore futures gained 0.8% to US$100.70/t — their first foray back above the US$100/t psychological threshold since 17 June. Fortescue (FMG) (+3.2%) led, with Champion Iron (CIA) (+1.4%) , Deterra Royalties (DRR) (+1.2%) , BHP (BHP) (+1.1%) , Mineral Resources (MIN) (+1.0%) , and Rio Tinto (RIO) (+0.8%) all firmer. SGX Iron Ore futures chart The sector was dragged lower elsewhere — as gold stocks weighed, and base metals stocks were generally lower as COMEX copper eased 0.3% to US$6.661/lb. S&P-ASX 200 Financials Sector Index intraday chart (just a little bit of juicing at the end there! 🧃😉) Financials (XFJ) (+0.1%) held near flat — its now-characteristic anchoring role (dare we say "balancing" role!?) in a market still searching for direction. Commonwealth Bank (CBA) (+0.7%) and National Australia Bank (NAB) (+0.5%) were the positive movers among the major banks, while ANZ (ANZ) (-0.1%) barely moved. Information Technology (XIJ) (-2.6%) was Monday's worst sector by a wide margin — US benchmark bond yields remain near three-year highs and the mathematical impact on long-duration growth stock valuations is unrelenting. The discount rate applied to earnings weighted far into the future rises with each basis point of yield, compressing present values with precision. Xero (XRO) (-3.9%) , Technology One (TNE) (-3.9%) , WiseTech Global (WTC) (-3.8%) , and Life360 (360) (-3.5%) were all sharply lower. S&P-ASX 200 Information Technology Sector Index intraday chart. Computer says no... The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (-1.2%) continued its retreat as COMEX gold futures fell 0.8% to US$4,441/oz after having already fallen 1.4% on Friday — the precious metal's extended decline reflecting the same higher-yields, higher-opportunity-cost dynamic that hammered technology. Pantoro Gold (PNR) (-5.9%) , Catalyst Metals (CYL) (-4.1%) , Newmont (NEM) (-2.3%) , and Ora Banda Mining (OBM) (-2.2%) were all lower. Utilities (XUJ) (-1.0%) was another textbook bond-proxy casualty — when risk-free yields rise, the stable but modest income streams of utilities face direct competition from government bonds and their relative appeal diminishes. Origin Energy (ORG) (-1.1%) , APA Group (APA) (-0.9%) , and AGL Energy (AGL) (-0.6%) were all softer. Health Care (XHJ) (-0.8%) let's go ditto — the same bond yield pressure that hit technology applied here, compressing the premium multiples of high-P/E healthcare names. Profit-taking after the sector's extraordinary August recovery may have also added a further dimension to the selling. Pro Medicus (PME) (-3.1%) went ex-dividend $0.37 per share fully franked today, but Artrya (AYA) (-4.3%) , 4DMedical (4DX) (-2.2%) , and Cochlear (COH) (-1.6%) had no excuses! In other commodities moves, lithium stocks fell as GFEX lithium carbonate futures eased 0.2% to CNY 141,640/t and Australian spodumene concentrate fell 0.8% to US$2,255/t. Elevra Lithium (ELV) (-4.6%) , Galan Lithium (GLN) (-3.7%) , Liontown Resources (LTR) (-3.3%) , Pilbara Minerals (PLS) (-3.1%) , and Core Lithium (CXO) (-2.9%) were all lower. On a positive note, rare earths stocks advanced despite NdPr prices in China easing 0.1% to CNY 735,000/t. Arafura Rare Earths (ARU) (+5.4%) , Lynas Rare Earths (LYC) (+2.0%) , and Iluka Resources (ILU) (+1.8%) all surged, with the buying appearing to reflect strategic demand expectations and supply chain positioning rather than any commodity price lead.
