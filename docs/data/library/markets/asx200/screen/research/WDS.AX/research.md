## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags WDS.AX as a deep-value energy compounder rather than a pure commodity momentum play. At P/B 1.11 (Schloss Low P/B), PEGY 0.03 (Neff PEGY), and 5.6% yield (High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth), the stock offers tangible-asset backing and income appeal uncommon among ASX200 energy peers. D/E of 34% passes Financial Health screens, and ROE of 7.2%—modest in absolute terms—is tolerable for a capital-intensive LNG builder in a heavy investment phase. Magic Formula and Dreman Contrarian passes suggest the market is pricing below-normal earnings power relative to asset base.

Business quality supports the screen: Woodside is Australia's largest independent oil and gas producer, with integrated LNG export capacity, domestic gas supply (including assumed Gippsland Basin operatorship), and a multi-year growth pipeline. Per Yahoo (FY2025 fallback), construction-in-progress exceeds A$22.9 billion, consistent with Scarborough, Louisiana LNG, and ammonia investments. The screen's negative FCF metric (−A$314 million) reflects growth capex, not operational failure—operating cash flow was A$7.19 billion in FY2025. News flow confirms record FY2025 production with dividend maintained (24 February 2026). For a value investor, the hook is buying a strategic LNG asset base near book with a 5.6% cash return whilst the market discounts near-term earnings cyclicality and capex drag.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Cyclical earnings exposure. Net income has fallen from A$6.5 billion (FY2022) to A$2.7 billion (FY2025) as commodity prices normalised. ROE of 7.2% is below typical cost-of-equity thresholds; further oil/gas price weakness would compress margins and dividend headroom.

Negative free cash flow during peak capex. FY2025 FCF of −A$782 million, with construction-in-progress at A$22.9 billion, signals a capital-intensive phase. Execution risk on major projects (Scarborough, Louisiana LNG, Browse) is the primary operational threat; cost overruns and schedule delays cannot be assessed from filing contingency disclosures as none are available.

Rising leverage. Total debt increased A$2.1 billion year-on-year to A$13.7 billion. Net debt/EBITDA remains manageable (~0.7x), but continued debt-funded capex alongside ~A$2 billion annual dividends could stress the balance sheet if commodity prices weaken. Without primary filing bodies, covenant headroom and maturity profiles are unverified.

Impairment history. A$1.92 billion impairment in FY2023 and A$143 million in FY2025 indicate asset write-down risk persists in a lower-price environment.

Governance and pension. Yahoo balance sheet shows employee benefit liabilities of A$220 million (non-current) and A$449 million (current pension/post-retirement); detailed pension funding status requires annual report note disclosure, which is missing. CEO departure was flagged in market commentary (18 December 2025: "WDS losses its CEO"); Tony O'Neill resigned as non-executive director effective 1 July 2026.

M&A, regulatory, and geopolitical overhang. Reported Exxon Mobil acquisition interest (June 2025) created share-price volatility; Woodside publicly denied active takeover discussions. Portfolio complexity is rising—Tellurian and OCI Clean Ammonia acquisitions (2024), Greater Angostura divestiture to Perenco (March 2025), Louisiana LNG partnership and sell-down to Stonepeak (2025)—each carrying integration and regulatory scrutiny. Geopolitical LNG supply disruption (Middle East tensions, Strait of Hormuz-related shocks cited repeatedly in news) affects realised prices and export volumes.

Screen-specific red flag. The screen flags negative FCF (−A$314 million), inconsistent with dividend sustainability if capex remains elevated without offsetting project cash flows.
