# Woodside Energy Group Ltd (WDS.AX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-11T07:44:30.453620+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Woodside Energy Group Ltd (WDS.AX) is Australia’s largest independent LNG producer, combining a high-reliability operated base (Pluto, North West Shelf, Gulf of Mexico) with a heavy growth pipeline: Scarborough (first LNG targeted Q4 2026), Louisiana LNG (FID taken April 2025), Trion and Beaumont ammonia. The quantitative screen flags a buy, though underlying metric detail in the snapshot is sparse (no model pass breakdown or valuation ratios populated). The value hook is a disciplined ~80% underlying-NPAT dividend payout, record production (198.8 MMboe in FY2025), and optionality as Scarborough ramps. The central debate is whether elevated capex, gearing at the top of — or marginally above — the 10–20% target, and commodity cyclicality are adequately priced, versus the cash-return profile of a maturing LNG franchise.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Woodside offers a large-scale, low-cost hydrocarbon platform with visible shareholder returns rather than speculative exploration upside. Filings show consistent dividend policy: fully franked payouts at roughly 80% of underlying NPAT (H1 2024 interim 69 US cps; FY2025 full-year 112 US cps; H1 2026 interim 57 US cps). Since the 2022 BHP petroleum merger, the company reports ~US$11 billion returned via dividends through FY2025, supporting the screen’s income-oriented buy signal.

Business quality is evidenced by operated LNG reliability (98%+ at key assets), Sangomar at nameplate (~100 Mbbl/d), and active portfolio management: Scarborough sell-downs to JERA and LNG Japan (~US$2.3 billion combined), Stonepeak/Williams partnering at Louisiana LNG (Woodside’s share of capex reduced to ~US$9.9 billion vs US$17.5 billion total project cost), and Greater Angostura divestment. Underlying earnings have been more stable than statutory NPAT: FY2023 underlying NPAT US$3,320 million vs reported US$1,660 million (impairments); FY2025 underlying US$2,649 million vs reported US$2,718 million.

The screen’s buy rating aligns with a business generating strong operating cash flow (US$7,192 million in FY2025) and improving free cash flow as major projects advance (FY2025 FCF US$1,889 million after a FCF deficit in FY2024). The caveat is that value here is cyclical and capex-intensive: realised prices averaged US$60.2/boe in FY2025 (down 5% YoY) while net PPE and construction-in-progress remain large. This is a quality cyclical at a potential inflection — not a classic deep-value asset play with minimal reinvestment needs.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Regulatory and climate: Shareholders rejected the Climate Transition Action Plan at the 2024 AGM (58.36% against, per H1 2024 report). H1 2026 briefing indicates retirement of Scope 3 targets and continued reliance on carbon credits for net Scope 1 and 2 targets — elevating transition and reputational risk. Scarborough faced legal challenge (dismissal announced August 2024); Browse pre-emption exercised June 2026, implying further capital commitment. Senegalese tax assessment disputed at Sangomar (H1 2024 report).

Cyclical: Realised prices drive earnings (US$60.2/boe FY2025; US$74.0/boe H1 2026). FY2024 free cash flow was negative despite strong underlying profit; dividend sustainability in a prolonged price downturn is a recurring market concern (e.g. “Woodside dividends still at risk…”, Market Index, 16 Oct 2024).

Leverage and capex: Louisiana LNG FID at ~US$17.5 billion total project cost; gearing expected above target during investment cycle (flagged at H1 2024). H1 2026 gearing 20.6% confirms pressure. FY2025 Yahoo cash-flow data shows capex ~US$7.97 billion and negative reported FCF before the filing’s restated positive US$1,889 million — methodology matters for dividend coverage analysis.

Governance and execution: Meg O’Neill resigned as CEO during Q4 2025; Liz Westcott appointed Acting CEO then permanent CEO (March 2026 per briefing transcript). Leadership transition during Scarborough start-up and Louisiana LNG execution adds key-person risk. Beaumont ammonia operational review announced August 2026 (“everything on the table” per news).

Other filing risks (H1 2024/H1 2026): Principal risk categories include health/safety, environment, strategy/climate, project delivery, commodity price, cyber, and social integrity — no material change to going-concern status noted; contingent liabilities referenced in H1 2026 notes (Chevron asset swap contingent consideration US$175 million). No covenant breach flagged; filing states no new covenants or material changes to debt facilities in H1 2026.
