## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags WDS.AX as a deep-value energy compounder rather than a pure commodity momentum play. At P/B 1.11 (Schloss Low P/B), PEGY 0.03 (Neff PEGY), and 5.6% yield (High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth), the stock offers tangible-asset backing and income appeal uncommon among ASX200 energy peers. D/E of 34% passes Financial Health screens; ROE of 7.2% is modest in absolute terms but tolerable for a capital-intensive LNG builder in a heavy investment phase. Magic Formula and Dreman Contrarian passes suggest the market is pricing below-normal earnings power relative to asset base.

Business quality supports the screen. Woodside is Australia's largest independent oil and gas producer, with integrated LNG export capacity, domestic gas supply (Gippsland Basin operatorship assumed July 2026), and a multi-year growth pipeline. The February 2024 reserves statement (primary filing) confirms top-quartile proved reserves life of 12.2 years at 2,450.1 MMboe 1P and 3,757.1 MMboe 2P, with 158% 2P production replacement in 2023. Per Yahoo fallback, construction-in-progress exceeds A$22.9 billion, consistent with Scarborough, Louisiana LNG, and ammonia investments. Operating cash flow was A$7.19 billion in FY2025 despite negative FCF (−A$782 million). News flow confirms record FY2025 production with dividend maintained (24 February 2026). For a value investor, the hook is buying a strategic LNG asset base near book with a 5.6% cash return whilst the market discounts near-term earnings cyclicality and capex drag.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Cyclical earnings exposure. Net income has fallen from A$6.5 billion (FY2022, Yahoo) to A$2.7 billion (FY2025) as commodity prices normalised. ROE of 7.2% is below typical cost-of-equity thresholds.

Negative free cash flow during peak capex. FY2025 FCF of −A$782 million, with construction-in-progress at A$22.9 billion, signals a capital-intensive phase. RNS forward-looking disclaimers consistently reference project timing, expenditure, and commodity price risks detailed in the Annual Report—none extractable here.

Rising leverage and dividend tension. Total debt increased A$2.1 billion year-on-year. October 2024 market commentary flagged dividend risk despite strong output. Covenant headroom unverified.

Impairment history. The February 2024 RNS guided ~A$1,500 million post-tax impairments for FY2023; Yahoo records A$1,917 million pre-tax in FY2023 and A$143 million in FY2025. RNS notes Shenzi goodwill impairments are not reversible.

Governance uncertainty. Tony O'Neill appointed June 2024 (RNS) but resigned effective 1 July 2026. Market wrap flagged CEO departure (18 December 2025). Leadership continuity during peak capex is a watchpoint.

Pension and provisions. Yahoo shows employee benefit liabilities of A$220m (non-current) and A$449m (current); detailed funding status requires annual report notes, which are missing.

M&A and geopolitical overhang. Exxon Mobil acquisition speculation (June 2025) denied by Woodside. Portfolio complexity rising across US LNG, ammonia, and divestitures. Middle East supply disruption affects realised prices.

Screen-specific red flag. Negative FCF (−A$314 million TTM) is inconsistent with dividend sustainability if capex remains elevated.
