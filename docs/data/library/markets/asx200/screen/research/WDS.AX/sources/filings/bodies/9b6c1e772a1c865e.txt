An early 0.34% gain quickly faded to a (0.36%) decline before a late-session bid brought things off worst levels Energy closed at its highest since 8-Apr a nd now sits within 3% of the 7-Apr high. The sector, spanning oil and gas, coal and refiners, looks very strong amid this Hormuz deadlock. Brent is trading around breakeven today, at US$99.4, but with prices holding such elevated levels you can get pretty bullish on near-term sector earnings Materials also finished at session highs , largely off the back of further copper momentum, which lifted key names like BHP (+3.2%) and Rio Tinto (+1.9%). The copper ascent comes on poor production guidance from most major miners, a massive shift of refined metal into the US ahead of potential tariffs, and expectations of a demand pickup in China as its market enters a seasonal high for manufacturing activity. The small-to-mid end is also looking spicy, with Marimaca (+10.9%) rallying off drill results, Hot Chilli (+6.7%) at a two-month high, Kantra Copper (+4.6%) at the highest since May-24 and 29Metals (+2.7%) near a five-month high. Most charts are taking shape after a shallow pullback. Healthcare took a breather after the S&P 500 healthcare sector dipped 2.55% overnight, where Novartis (-10.8%) and its failed cholesterol trial weighed on peers like Amgen and Eli Lilly. Nothing to do with local names, but the read-across still hit the sector, with CSL down 2.0% to a two-week low of $171.21 (but still up ~28% since its FY26 result on 17-Aug) Financials are down 2.7% across the last two sessions and on the cusp of undercutting the 27-Aug low to hit a three-month low. Today featured a broad-based pullback for the banks, with CBA down 2.1% and now 3.3% lower YTD Discretionary is on a three-day skid, having traded lower in 18 of the last 24 sessions, and is down 14.5% since 6-Aug. Some very downbeat stuff. Tuesday's consumer confidence index fell 5.2% month-on-month to 84.4, wiping out the recent bounce to 88.9 that had marked the highest read since March 2026. UBS reckons a few more months of soft sentiment would slow real consumption growth over coming quarters, and could even see it "weaken sharply towards flat" Policy-sensitive Aussie 3-year yield up 1 bp to 4.82%, just shy of a fresh 15-year high. The 10-year is up 1 bp to 5.21%, also fractionally shy of a 15-year high Overall, the path of least resistance remains lower against an uncomfortable backdrop of high oil prices (on the cusp of US$100 Brent), elevated yields as per the 15-year highs above, and softening consumer data. That doesn't mean the market is void of opportunity, with the energy complex and select resources continuing to perform.
