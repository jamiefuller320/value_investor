In the broader-based S&P/ASX 300 (XKO) advancers lagged decliners by a disappointing 94 to 193. 😭 Bond market-ageddon! 🦖🌠 Information Technology (XIJ) (-3.4%) was the biggest casualty. When benchmark risk-free yields rise, the rate used to discount future earnings rises with them. For a conventional business earning profits today, the impact is modest. For a high-P/E, high-growth technology business whose earnings are weighted heavily toward future years, the discounting impact is severe — each additional basis point of yield compresses the present value of those distant earnings disproportionately. NextDC (NXT) (-3.9%) , Weebit Nano (WBT) (-4.2%) , WiseTech Global (WTC) (-5.2%) , Siteminder (SDR) (-3.4%) , and Life360 (360) (-4.8%) were all savaged. US 10-Year T-Bond Yield chart The Gold Sub-Index (XGD) (-3.4%) received the same higher-yield treatment — gold produces no income, requires storage and insurance costs, and therefore competes directly with risk-free government bonds for capital. When benchmark bond yields surge, that competition intensifies and gold loses. COMEX gold futures fell 1.9% overnight before easing a further 0.5% to US$4,376/oz in Asian trade. Kingsgate Consolidated (KCN) (-6.2%) , St Barbara (SBM) (-5.8%) , Ora Banda Mining (OBM) (-6.1%) , Westgold Resources (WGX) (-5.6%) , and Newmont (NEM) (-3.3%) were all sharply lower. Materials (XMJ) (-3.1%) bore the commodity-price and growth-concern combination — higher US yields strengthen the US dollar, which makes dollar-priced commodities more expensive for non-US buyers, compressing demand. COMEX copper futures fell 1.3% overnight before easing a further 0.7% to US$6.554/lb in Asian trade. Nickel Industries (NIC) (-4.7%) , Pilbara Minerals (PLS) (-5.3%) , Champion Iron (CIA) (-3.4%) , Sandfire Resources (SFR) (-3.0%) , and BHP (BHP) (-3.3%) were all lower. Real Estate (XPJ) (-1.2%) is another sector that detests higher risk-free bond yields. Its earnings are considered to be bond-like, so if you can get a better yield in the risk-free versions — then the returns in this sector look far less attractive... Goodman Group (GMG) (-3.0%) , HMC Capital (HMC) (-1.9%) , and Lendlease (LLC) (-1.7%) were among the worst casualties. Consumer Discretionary (XDJ) (-0.4%) was another casualty — high market rates flow directly into higher mortgage and personal borrowing costs, reducing household disposable income and compressing consumer spending capacity. Lovisa (LOV) (-4.3%) , Universal Store (UNI) (-3.5%) , Breville (BRG) (-2.9%) and Temple and Webster (TPW) (-2.1%) were all sharply lower. But it wasn't a complete wipeout. There were some VERY STRONG MOVES TODAY. And apart from energy, they were in the most defensive, non-discretionary segments of the market you can get... Health Care (XHJ) (-0.1%) was largely spared the rod — a sector that generates revenues irrespective of the rate cycle and is therefore largely insulated from the bond yield shock that hammered cyclical and tech plays. Telix Pharmaceuticals (TLX) (+2.8%) , Cochlear (COH) (+1.6%) , and CSL (CSL) (+0.9%) all advanced. Energy (XEJ) (+0.0%) was flat even as ICE Brent crude futures surged 4.6% overnight before adding a further 0.4% to US$95/bbl in Asian trade — approaching the psychologically significant US$100 mark as US strikes on Iranian oil infrastructure raised fresh fears about regional supply disruption. Woodside Energy (WDS) (+1.2%) and Santos (STO) (+0.4%) , advanced but coal stocks pulled back from recent highs. Utilities (XUJ) (-0.2%) appeared to barely hold positive ground, but add back constituent dividends today and it was actually 1.1% higher. The sector's defensive nature provided a shield against selling elsewhere. AGL Energy (AGL) (+0.8%) and APA Group (APA) (+1.5%) both firmed, Origin Energy (ORG) (-1.7%) was down 20 cents, but went ex-dividend 30 cents fully franked. Financials (XFJ) (+0.3%) was fractionally positive but outperformed the benchmark substantially — and the driver was sector-specific and elegant. Higher benchmark bond yields are toxic for most asset classes, but for insurers they are directly beneficial — insurance companies park premium inflows in short-duration fixed income securities, and when yields rise, those portfolios earn more. Insurance Australia Group (IAG) (+5.4%) , Suncorp (SUN) (+2.4%) , QBE Insurance (QBE) (+1.3%) and NIB Holdings (NHF) (+1.0%) all gained — and along with the Big 4 banks which were flat-to-slightly-higher — provided enough index weight to stem some of the day's losses. Consumer Staples (XSJ) (+0.8%) was the best defensive sector. Primary producers and agricultural companies had a strong session — as the prices of agricultural commodities surged overnight. GrainCorp (GNC) (+11.0%) , Select Harvests (SHV) (+6.2%) , Inghams (ING) (+2.4%) , The A2 Milk Company (A2M) (+2.4%) , and Elders (ELD) (+2.5%) all advanced. Woolworths (WOW) (+0.7%) and Coles (COL) (+0.9%) also rose — flexing their defensive credentials.
