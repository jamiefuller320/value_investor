Q3 2026: double-digit growth in sales and business EPS
FY 2026: guidance upgraded
October 30, 2026
Q3 sales growth of 14.3% at CER1 and business earnings per share (EPS)2 of €3.55
• Pharma launches sales increased by 31.4%, reaching €1.4 billion, driven by ALTUVIIIO, Ayvakit, and Rezurock
• Dupixent sales increased by 34.8% to €5.7 billion, above €5 billion per quarter for the second time
• Vaccines sales decreased by 6.6% to €3.2 billion, impacted by the anticipated phasing in respiratory prevention
• Research and Development expenses reached €1.8 billion, broadly stable
• Selling and general expenses reached €2.3 billion, down by 0.9%, reflecting cost discipline
• Business EPS was €3.55, up by 19.6% at CER; 22.0% at actual exchange rates; IFRS EPS €2.52
Pipeline update
• Two regulatory approvals; in rare diseases and vaccines
• One regulatory submission acceptance and two regulatory designations (priority review and PRIME)
Guidance upgraded
• 2026 guidance is upgraded: sales are expected to grow by a low-teens percentage at CER. Business EPS at CER is expected 
to grow by a mid-teens percentage.3
Belén Garijo, Chief Executive Officer:  “Sanofi achieved another strong quarter with double-digit sales growth of 14.3%, mainly 
driven by our new medicines and Dupixent. Business EPS increased by 19.6%, driven by cost discipline. The pipeline milestones 
included two regulatory approvals, one regulatory submission, and two additional regulatory designations. Our strong 
commercial and financial momentum to date leads us to raise our 2026 guidance: sales growth is now expected at a low-teens 
percentage and business EPS growth at a mid-teens percentage, underscoring our sustainable, profitable growth.
In the third quarter, we accelerated Sanofi’s transformation by expanding our long-standing alliance with Regeneron, which 
opens up further opportunities in immunology. We also continued to simplify our portfolio through a partnership with 
Cheplapharm covering a number of legacy medicines4.
We are confident about our ability to pursue our strong commercial and financial performance in the short and medium term. 
The whole organisation is focused on managing Sanofi’s post-Dupixent trajectory over the next decade. Our priority is to 
protect earnings through Dupixent’s loss of exclusivity. We have already begun taking action to improve R&D productivity. 
Business development and M&A will be critical to complement this transformation. We remain committed to our long-term 
capital allocation policy, including our progressive dividend.”

In Q3 2026, sales were €14,444 million and increased by 14.3%. Exchange rate movements had a positive effect of 1.9 percentage 
points (pp); therefore, at actual exchange rates, sales increased by 16.2%. The divestments of medicines/portfolio streamlining 
had a negative impact of 0.4pp on sales growth. In YTD 2026, sales were €36,550 million and increased by 15.2%. Exchange rate 
movements had a negative effect of 2.1pp; therefore, at actual exchange rates, sales increased by 13.1%. The divestments of 
medicines/portfolio streamlining had a negative impact of 0.4pp on sales growth.

Business operating income
In Q3 2026, business operating income (BOI) was €5,430 million and increased by 19.7% (22.2% at actual exchange rates) from 
€4,445 million in Q3 2025. The ratio of BOI to net sales was 37.4% and increased by 1.7pp (37.6% at actual exchange rates, up by 
1.9pp). The increase was mainly driven by growth in business gross profit and operating leverage partly offset by the increase in 
other operating expenses from higher profit sharing to Sanofi’s collaboration partner Regeneron. In YTD 2026, BOI was €11,688 
million and increased by 21.1% (19.2% at actual exchange rates) from €9,808 million in YTD 2025. The ratio of BOI to net sales was 
31.9% and increased by 1.6pp (32.0% at actual exchange rates, up by 1.7pp).
Business development
In September, Sanofi and Cheplapharm announced the intention to create a new strategic partnership in mature medicines 
under which Cheplapharm would take over from Sanofi a selection of 20 mature medicines and three manufacturing sites 
worldwide. In return, Sanofi will receive a 26.4% equity stake in Cheplapharm, building on a collaboration that started in 2014. 
The commercial transfer of the medicine portfolio is planned to begin in the first quarter of 2027, followed by the transfer of the 
sites, subject to information and consultation procedures with employee representatives, regulatory approvals and customary 
closing conditions. The transaction is expected to be fully completed by the third quarter of 2027. The medicines being divested 
from Sanofi include Lovenox/Clexane (enoxaparin), excluding in the US. Lovenox/Clexane had 2025 sales of €822 million. 
Additional financial details are expected to be provided at a later stage.

longstanding antibody collaboration, adding multiple next-generation, long-acting antibodies to the alliance. Under the

Immunology
Tzield/Teizeild (teplizumab)
The European Medicines Agency (EMA) accepted Teizeild for regulatory review to delay the onset of stage 3 type 1 diabetes 
(T1D) in patients aged one to seven years diagnosed with stage 2 T1D. Teizeild is already approved for this indication in adults 
and children aged eight years and older.
lunsekimig (IL13xTSLP Nanobody® VHH)
At the European Respiratory Society Congress 2026 in Barcelona, Spain, positive phase 2 data from two separate studies 
showed that lunsekimig, targeting thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP) and interleukin-13 (IL13), led to clinically meaningful 
improvements in patients with asthma and CRSwNP.
• The AIRCULES phase 2b dose-ranging study (clinical study identifier: NCT06102005) evaluated the efficacy, safety, and 
tolerability of lunsekimig in addition to standard of care therapy over 48 weeks in adult patients with moderate-to-severe 
asthma who had one or more exacerbations in the preceding year, compared with placebo. Lunsekimig 300 mg every four 
weeks (Q4W) achieved a statistically significant and clinically meaningful reduction in the annual asthma exacerbation rate

over 48 weeks compared to placebo, regardless of biomarker status, meeting the study’s primary objective. The study also 
met the key secondary endpoint, demonstrating statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements in lung 
function at week 48, as measured by pre-bronchodilator forced expiratory volume in 1 second compared to placebo, which 
was observed as early as week 4 and sustained through week 48. In addition, patients reported improvements in scores for

Rare diseases
Ayvakit (avapritinib)
The US FDA approved a label update for Ayvakit including a 50 mg daily dose option in adults with indolent systemic 
mastocytosis (ISM), for use as clinically indicated after at least six months of treatment at 25 mg daily, which remains the 
recommended starting dose. ISM is defined by a broad spectrum of disease activity that varies by patient, highlighting the 
importance of providing flexibility to healthcare providers to address these diverse medical needs. This label update was based 
on observed data for Ayvakit 50 mg from the long-term, open-label extension portion of the PIONEER clinical trial (clinical study 
identifier: NCT03731260).
Vaccines
MenQuadfi (meningococcal disease)
The EU approved an extension of the indication for MenQuadfi (Meningococcal Group A, C, W and Y conjugate vaccine) to 
include infants from six weeks of age to help protect against invasive meningococcal disease caused by Neisseria meningitidis 
serogroups A, C, W, and Y. MenQuadfi was previously approved in the EU for use in individuals aged 12 months and older.
Invasive meningococcal disease is a life-threatening bacterial infection that can progress rapidly, with patients potentially 
deteriorating from the onset of symptoms to death within 24 hours. Infants are among the populations at highest risk for invasive 
meningococcal disease, facing high rates of mortality and severe long-term complications. Although serogroup A is now rarely 
reported in the EU, serogroups C, W, and Y remain responsible for a significant share of invasive meningococcal disease cases.
Beyfortus (nirsevimab)
The Chinese National Medical Products Administration granted a priority review to Beyfortus for the regulatory submission to 
extend the indication to children up to 24 months of age during their second respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) season. Beyfortus 
is currently approved for the prevention of RSV lower respiratory tract infection in neonates and infants entering or during their 
first RSV season.
SP0087 (rabies)
The EMA’s Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) issued a positive opinion for SP0087 for pre- and post-
exposure prophylaxis against rabies in all age groups. This next-generation vaccine is highly purified, free from human and 
animal-origin materials, with enhanced thermostability and longer shelf-life.
SP0289 (influenza H5 pandemic)
The CHMP granted PRIME eligibility to the second generation structurally designed mRNA influenza H5 pandemic vaccine. This 
pandemic preparedness vaccine is intended for prevention of influenza in case of an officially declared pandemic. The CHMP 
acknowledged the vaccine's potential to address unmet medical need, noting the mRNA platform enables rapid manufacturing 
and adaptation to emerging virus variants or novel pandemic strains.

Sustainability update
Acting for nature: reducing dependencies, building resilience
Biodiversity is a key focus area in the efforts to limit the impact on nature and reduce to a minimum the use of natural resources.
Sanofi has completed a nature-related risk and dependency analysis across its operations and value chain. The analysis 
identified four critical nature-based commodities: horseshoe crab blood, palm oil, cattle, and pig derivatives. Although the 
projected cost impacts are financially immaterial, Sanofi is proactively strengthening its long-term resilience through targeted 
adaptation measures.
Horseshoe crab blood is essential for testing the safety of injectable medicines and vaccines, as required by regulators. Sanofi is 
accelerating its transition to synthetic alternatives and has an action plan with the following targets to be achieved by the end of 
2030: (1) 70% of endotoxin tests performed without horseshoe crab reagents in all Sanofi quality control laboratories; (2) All new 
launches submitted with synthetic method.
This plan reinforces Sanofi's commitment to reducing its impact on nature, while safeguarding uninterrupted access to 
medicines for patients worldwide.

Appendix 8: Definitions of non-IFRS financial measures
Sales at constant exchange rates
References to changes in net sales “at constant exchange rates” (CER) means that the effect of changes in exchange rates is 
eliminated. The effect of exchange rates is eliminated by recalculating net sales for the relevant period at the exchange rates 
used for the previous period.

Business gross profit
Business gross profit is a non-IFRS financial measure that excludes from gross profit as reported under IFRS the impact of 
expenses arising from the fair value remeasurement of inventories following acquisitions that are either (i) a business 
combination under IFRS 3 or (ii) part of a group of assets within the meaning of paragraph 2b of IFRS 3.
Business operating income
Business operating income is a non-IFRS financial measure which is derived from operating income as reported under IFRS, after 
eliminating:
• amortisation and impairment losses charged against intangible assets (other than software and other rights of an industrial or 
operational nature);
• fair value remeasurements of contingent consideration relating to business combinations or business divestments;
• expenses arising from the remeasurement of inventories following business combinations, or acquisitions of groups of assets 
that do not constitute a business within the meaning of paragraph 2b of IFRS 3;
• restructuring costs and similar items; and
• gains and losses (including gains and losses on major divestments), and costs and provisions related to litigation, as reported 
within “Other gains and losses, and litigation” under IFRS.
In addition:
• the share of profits/losses from investments accounted for using the equity method is added, to the extent that this relates (i) 
to joint ventures or (ii) to associates with which Sanofi has entered into R&D agreements and/or whose operations are 
managed as an integral part of Sanofi’s business activities; and
• net income attributable to non-controlling interests related to continuing operations, and excluding the effects of the above 
reconciling items, is deducted.
Business net income
Business net income is a key non-IFRS financial measure. It is defined as net income attributable to equity holders of Sanofi as 
reported under IFRS, excluding:
• net income from discontinued operations;
• amortisation of intangible assets;
• impairment of intangible assets;
• fair value remeasurement of contingent consideration related to business combinations or to disposals;
• expenses arising from the impact of acquisitions on inventories;
• restructuring costs and similar items (comprising transaction, integration, and separation costs in relation to major 
acquisitions and disposals)1;
• other gains and losses (including gains and losses on disposals of non-current assets1);
• costs or provisions associated with litigation1;
• financial (income)/expense related to liabilities carried at amortised cost, other than net debt;
• tax effects related to the items listed above as well as effects of major tax disputes;
• the share of profits/losses from investments accounted for using the equity method, except to the extent that this relates (i) 
to joint ventures or (ii) to associates with which Sanofi has entered into R&D agreements and/or whose operations are 
managed as an integral part of Sanofi’s business activities; and,
• net income attributable to non-controlling interests related to the items listed above.
Business EPS
Sanofi also publishes business earnings per share (business EPS), a non-IFRS financial measure obtained by dividing business net 
income by the weighted average number of shares outstanding.

Effective tax rate
The effective tax rate is a non-IFRS financial measure calculated on the basis of business operating income, minus net financial 
expenses and before (i) the share of profit/loss from investments accounted for using the equity method and (ii) net income 
attributable to non-controlling interests.
Free cash flow 
Free cash flow is a non-IFRS financial measure which is reviewed by management, and which management believes provides 
useful information to measure the net cash generated from Sanofi’s operations that is available for strategic investments 1 (net of 
divestments1), for debt repayment, and for payments to shareholders. “Free cash flow” is determined from the business net 
income after adding back (in the case of expenses and losses) or deducting (in the case of income and gains) the following items: 
depreciation, amortisation, and impairment, share of undistributed earnings from investments accounted for using the equity 
method, gains and losses on disposals of non-current assets, net change in provisions (including pensions and other post-
employment benefits), deferred taxes, share-based expense, and other non-cash items. It also includes net changes in working 
capital, capital expenditures and other asset acquisitions 2 net of disposal proceeds 2, and payments related to restructuring and 
similar items. “Free cash flow” is not defined by IFRS, and it is not a substitute for “Net cash provided by/(used in) operating 
activities” as reported under IFRS.
