Paris, 5 November 2026 
PRESS RELEASE 
Very strong nine-month 2026 Group results 
Group full-year COPA now expected to increase slightly 
 
© Gregory de Leeuw 
  
 
In a particularly volatile macroeconomic and geopolitical environment, the Group turned in a very 
strong performance in the first nine months of 2026. 
• Group sales were stable at €41.6bn at constant exchange rates (down 0.1% year-on-year1), with 
growth in the third quarter (up 1.9% at constant exchange rates2). 
• Increase in Group COPA3 year-on-year to €1,858m (up €44m year -on-year), driven by Equans 
and, to a lesser extent, by Bouygues Construction. 
• Net profit attributable to the Group impacted by a €569m impairment on Bouygues SA’s stake in 
TF1, with no effect on the Group’s cash position or investment capacity. 
• Very significant improvement in Group net debt confirmed: €5.6bn at end -September 2026, 
improving by €2bn year -on-year, in keeping with the trends observed in the three previous 
quarters. 
• Very strong increase in Equans’ margin from activities to 5.5%, up 1.4 points year-on-year, with 
a continued improvement in sales in the third quarter. Full -year margin from activities target 
raised again to “at least 5.5%”. 
• Backlogs at record high levels at end -September: €27.7bn at Equans (up €1.9bn year-on-year) 
and €32.5bn for the Construction Division (up €0.4bn year-on-year).

Outlook for the Group  
Bouygues operates in promising markets, the diversity of its business segments enables the Group to grow over 
the long term and demonstrate sustained resilience. In a highly uncertain macroeconomic and geopolitical 
environment, the Group will remain agile in adapting to developments in its markets. 
 
For 2026, the Group is aiming for: 
• stable sales at constant exchange rates; 
• a slight increase in current operating profit from activities (COPA). Previously the Group was aiming for 
COPA maintained at a record high level, after several years of significant improvement.  
The improvement in Equans' COPA and, to a lesser extent, in Bouygues Construction’s COPA  will more 
than offset the expected decline in TF1's COPA, due to the anticipated tensions in the linear TV 
advertising market, in Bouygues Telecom's COPA, due to the expected increase in depreciation and 
amortisation, and in Bouygues Immobilier ’s COPA  due to challenging conditions in the real estate 
market. 
 
The Group remains very vigilant regarding the changing global economic environment, particularly amid 
heightened uncertainty in France. 
 
Outlook for Equans 
Equans continues to roll out its strategic Plan. For 2026, Equans confirms it is targeting: 
• stable sales versus 2025, at constant exchange rates; 
• a cash conversion rate (COPA-to-cash flow1) before working capital requirement (WCR) of between 80% 
and 100%. 
 
Equans has raised its full-year margin target again and is now targeting a margin from activities of at least 5.5% 
(following an initial upward revision to at least 5.2% in first-half 2026). As a reminder, the initial target set at the 
Capital Markets Day in February 2023 was to reach a margin from activities of 5.0% in 2027.  
 
Equans will hold another Capital Markets Day on 25 February 2027, starting at 10.30am (CET).  
 
Outlook for Bouygues Telecom  
Bouygues Telecom confirms its guidance for 2026, and is targeting:  
• sales billed to customers and EBITDA after Leases  close to the level of 2025, showing modest growth 
versus 2023 excluding La Poste Telecom; 
• gross capital expenditure of close to €1.3 billion (excluding frequencies), confirming a decline after the 
peak in capex over the last 5 years; 
• free cash flow (FCF) before working capital requirement (WCR) 2 of around €600  million excluding La 
Poste Telecom, and before impact of the income tax surcharge for large companies in France. FCF before 
WCR, including La Poste Telecom and the income tax surcharge, will be around €500  million. This 
outlook does not factor in the potential impacts of the proposed acquisition of SFR.

Outlook for the TF1 group 
The TF1 group’s 2026 targets are maintained in a context of continued limited visibility. Capitalising on its 
strategy, on its new digital initiatives and on its solid financial position, the TF1 group’s targets are as follows:  
• strong double-digit revenue growth in digital1 in 2026; 
• aim for a growing dividend policy in the coming years; 
• against a backdrop of rapidly changing consumption habits and a persistently unstable macroeconomic 
and political environment, the linear advertising market remains under strong pressure. During this 
digital transition phase, the TF1 group intends to maint ain a mid -to-high single digit margin from 
activities before capital gains in 2026, subject to the evolution of the linear market. 
 
DETAILED ANALYSIS BY SECTOR OF ACTIVITY 
 
Construction Division 
At end-September 2026, the backlog in the Construction Division (Colas, Bouygues Construction and Bouygues 
Immobilier) reached a record level of €32.5 billion at end-September, providing visibility on future activity. This 
represented an increase of 1% year-on-year (stable at constant exchange rates and excluding principal disposals 
and acquisitions), and a sharp increase of 18% versus end-September 2022.  
At end-September 2026, the backlog was driven by commercial activity in the international excluding Europe 
geography (up 8% year -on-year), and was down in France (down 2% year -on-year) and in Europe excluding 
France (down 4% year-on-year).  
 
The share of backlog at end-September 2026 to be executed within 15 months was up €1.1 billion year-on-year, 
driven by Bouygues Construction.  
 
• The backlog at Colas stood at €13.9 billion, down 2% year -on-year (stable at constant exchange rates 
and excluding principal disposals and acquisitions). The Roads backlog was up 2% year -on-year, 
decreasing in France (down 12% year -on-year), while rising 9% year -on-year internationally. The Rail 
backlog was down 7% year-on-year, reflecting a high basis of comparison.  
 
Colas recorded an order intake of €9.5 billion in the first nine months of 2026. 
Order intake in Roads declined slightly year -on-year: in France the decline was due to the electoral 
period, as expected. As a result, Colas France is continuing to diversify its activities. The strong basis of 
comparison in international markets is masking the positive trend seen in several geographies, such as 
Northern Europe, North America and Western Europe. 
Rail order intake was down sharply year -on-year due to a very tough basis of comparison, following 
significant contracts signed in the United Kingdom and Morocco in the first nine months of 2025. The 
underlying commercial momentum remained strong, as demonstrated by several major contract awards 
since the start of the year, including one in Chile.

• Bouygues Construction’s backlog stood at €18.0 billion at end-September 2026, up 4% year-on-year (up 
1% at constant exchange rates and excluding principal disposals and acquisitions). It was driven mainly 
by France Building and International Building (including Vannoy Construction), where backlogs increased 
by 4% and 37% respectively year -on-year. The backlog at Civil Works decreased by 17% year -on-year

Bouygues Construction’s order intake was €7.9  billion in the first nine months of 2026, supported by 
good momentum in the normal course of business (contracts of less than €100 million), which remained 
at a good level, representing 56% of total order intake over the first nine months. In third-quarter 2026, 
Bouygues Construction also booked several contracts worth more than €100  million, of which a major 
contract for the expansion of a data centre campus contract worth around €1.2 billion in Australia.  
• Bouygues Immobilier  continues to face a challenging market environment. The backlog stood at 
€0.6 billion, down 13% year -on-year (down 13% at constant exchange rates and excluding principal 
disposals and acquisitions). As a reminder, Bouygues Immobilier’s backlog is secured,  since it only 
includes sales outstanding from notarised sales. 
In terms of commercial performance, the sudden deterioration in residential property market conditions 
in France in September has led to a sharp increase in the cancellation rate, as a result of weaker 
consumer confidence and rising interest rates. Over third -quarter 2026, unit residential property 
reservations in France nonetheless stood at €591  million, up 11% year -on-year in value terms, while 
block reservations decreased to €207  million (down 44% year -on-year) due to a different seasonal 
nature of sales compared to 2025. Sales activity in the commercial property remained at a standstill. 
 
The Construction Division reported sales of €20.7 billion1 in the first nine months of 2026, stable year-on-year, 
and up 1% year-on-year like-for-like and at constant exchange rates. 
 
• Sales at Colas were €11.5 billion, down 3% year-on-year (down 2% like-for-like and at constant exchange 
rates). Sales in Rail increased 5% year-on-year, while declining 4% year-on-year in Roads, of which down 
6% in France and down 2% internationally. 
 
• Bouygues Construction’s sales rose 7% year-on-year to €8.5 billion (up 5% like-for-like and at constant 
exchange rates). The increase was driven across all three activities: France Building (up 6%), 
International Building (up 3%) and Civil Works (up 13%).  
 
• Sales at Bouygues Immobilier were down 13%2 year-on-year at €0.8 billion (down 10% like-for-like and 
at constant exchange rates). The decrease in the sales figure partly reflects the disposal of activities in 
Poland in July 2025 and the low number of transactions contributing to sales. 
 
In the first nine months of 2026, current operating profit from activities (COPA) in the Construction Division 
was €585 million, slightly down by €6  million year-on-year. The Construction Division COPA margin decreased 
by 0.1 points year-on-year to 2.8%. 
 
• COPA at Colas was €329 million in first nine months of 2026, rising by €12  million year-on-year. The 
margin from activities was 2.9%, an increase of 0.2 points year-on-year.

Equans 
Equans’ backlog improved sharply to a record €27.7  billion at end-September 2026, rising by €1.9  billion year-
on-year (up 7%).  
 
Order intake was €15.3 billion in the first nine months of 2026, marking a record for a nine-month period. Order 
intake on contracts worth less than €5 million was up slightly year-on-year, representing around 65% of the total 
order intake. Order intake on  contracts worth more than €5  million increased very sharply year -on-year, 
representing around 35% of the total order intake, with major new orders booked across several specialist 
segments such as data centres, solar & storage and energy systems. 
Further significant contracts are also expected in fourth-quarter 2026.  
Part of the order intake worth more than €5  million booked since the start of 2026 will contribute to Equans’ 
sales from the end of 2027 onwards.  
 
Equans posted sales of €13.5  billion in the first nine months of 2026, down 2% year -on-year (down 1% at 
constant exchange rates). Third-quarter 2026 sales were up 2%, confirming the gradual improvement in activity 
expected over the course of the year and helping to progressively offset the soft start to the year.  
 
Equans’ profitability continued to improve significantly. COPA at Equans was €739  million in first nine months 
of 2026, rising by €174  million year-on-year. The margin from activities posted strong growth year -on-year at 
5.5% (up 1.4 points year-on-year). 
 
In the first nine months of 2026, Equans completed ten acquisitions , representing full -year sales of around 
€315 million. 
 
Bouygues Telecom 
The strong commercial momentum in Bouygues Telecom’s Fixed operations continued, supported by a quality-
based strategy focused on customer satisfaction, which translated into a steady decline in churn in the first nine 
months of 2026. 
 
At end-September 2026, FTTH customers totalled 5.0 million, following 298,000 new adds during the first nine 
months of 2026, of which 114,000 in the third quarter. The Fixed customer base was 5.7  million, following 
219,000 new adds in the first nine months  of 2026, of which 103,000 in the third quarter – a record level for a 
third quarter in the past nine years.  
The share of Fixed customers subscribing to a FTTH line continued to increase, reaching 88% versus 85% one 
year earlier. Bouygues Telecom continued to boost FTTH coverage while all the time strengthening network 
quality to enhance customer experience.
7/20 
 
 
In third-quarter 2026, Fixed ABPU decreased by €0.3 year-on-year to €33.1 per customer per month. The end of 
massive repricing initiatives and targeted “more for more” campaigns, in addition to the success of the new 
Fixed offers – which are driving new customer acquisitions – are weighing on ABPU. 
 
Bouygues Telecom reported a sustained commercial performance in Mobile in a market impacted by ongoing 
price pressure. This performance was driven by the good match between its brands and the BiG strategy, 
initiated in late 2024, which helps reduce churn and boost the number of convergent customers. Mobile plan 
customers excluding MtoM totalled 19.0 million, following 359,000 new adds in the first nine months of 2026, 
of which 187,000 in the third quarter, a record quarterly performance since the third quarter of 2019. 
In third-quarter 2026, Mobile ABPU was €16.8 per customer per month, which was €0.5 lower than in third -
quarter 2025 – negatively impacted by continued low prices for new customers, particularly in the low -end 
segment. In third -quarter 2026, Mobile ABPU al so benefited from a favourable seasonal effect from roaming 
compared with the second quarter. 
 
Sales billed to customers were €4.9  billion, up 1% year -on-year, as the positive contribution from Fixed offset 
the decline in Mobile. In total, Bouygues Telecom’s sales were up 2% year -on-year, driven by the increase in 
Other sales (up 6% year-on-year), which mainly consist of Handset, Accessories and Built-to-suit sales.  
 
EBITDA after Leases came to €1,500  million at end-September 2026, which was stable year-on-year. Bouygues 
Telecom continued efforts to control costs. EBITDA after Leases margin was 31.0%, a decrease of 0.3 points year-
on-year.  
 
Bouygues Telecom’s COPA was €469  million, down €40  million year -on-year. This expected decline resulted 
from the continuing increase in depreciation and amortisation in line with the high level of investments made 
over recent years.  
 
Gross capital expenditure excluding frequencies was €904  million at end -September 2026, down €132  million 
year-on-year.  
 
TF1 
Despite intense competition (especially from the Winter Olympics and the FIFA World Cup) in the first nine 
months of 2026, the TF1 group retained its leadership among its commercial targets (women under 50 who are 
purchasing decision-makers and individuals aged 25-49). In September, it further strengthened its leadership, 
delivering a record start to the autumn season and achieving a record audience share of 36.9% among women 
under 50 who are purchasing decision-makers (WPDM<50). During the first nine months of 2026, the audience 
share was 33.3% among WPDM<50 (down 0.5 points year -on-year) and 29.7% among individuals aged 25 -49 
(down 1.0 point year-on-year). TF1+ confirmed its popularity with the public, with 42  million average monthly 
streamers in the first nine months of 2026 (its second best month ever in September reaching 43.5 million). The 
number of hours watched on TF1+ rose by 13% year -on-year, with growth accelerating to 25% in the third 
quarter benefiting from the partnership with Netflix. Nearly one in two Netflix subscribers has watched at least 
one TF1+ programme since the launch. 
 
Media sales were €1,236 million at end-September 2026, down 11% year-on-year (down 8% like-for-like). 
• Linear advertising revenue declined against a backdrop of a still challenging market, impacted by an 
unfavourable geopolitical, economic and regulatory environment. TF1+ maintained its strong growth 
momentum (up 19% year-on-year), confirming its appeal for advertisers.

• Media sales excluding advertising were down, reflecting scope effects (divestments of My Little Paris 
and Play 2 in first-half 2025) and the termination of certain distribution contracts. 
Sales at Studio TF1 were €218 million at end-September 2026, up 3% year-on-year. 
 
COPA was €110 million, down €81 million year-on-year, as expected. As a reminder, COPA at TF1 benefited from 
a €17 million capital gain on disposals at end-September 2025. Stripping out this base effect, the change in COPA 
was mainly related to the decline  in linear advertising revenue. This was partly offset by adjustments to 
scheduling, particularly during the FIFA World Cup, and by tight control of other costs. In the first nine months 
of 2026, the margin from activities was 7.6%, in line with the released full-year guidance range. 
FINANCIAL SITUATION  
At €16.9 billion, the Group maintained a very high level of liquidity, which comprised €5.3  billion in cash and 
equivalents, supplemented by €11.5 billion in undrawn medium- and long-term credit facilities.  
 
Net debt at end -September 2026 was €5.6 billion, versus €7.6 billion at end-September 2025, representing an 
improvement of €2 billion in year-on-year.  
 
The change in working capital requirements and other was a negative €1,485 million in the first nine months of 
2026. 
 
Net gearing1 was 39% at end-September 2026, an improvement versus end-September 2025 (53%).  
 
At end-September 2026, the average maturity of the Group’s bonds was 5.6 years, and the average coupon was 
3.00% (average effective rate of 2.25%). The debt maturity schedule is well spread over time. 
Following the signing of the Memorandum of Understanding for the acquisition of SFR, Moody’s announced on 
16 June 2026 that the outlook on Bouygues’ long-term rating was unchanged at stable. On 3 July 2026, Standard 
and Poor’s placed Bouygues’ long-term rating under CreditWatch negative. 
SIGNIFICANT EVENTS IN THIRD-QUARTER 2026 
The Bouygues group recorded an impairment on its stake in TF1 amounting to €569  million, corresponding to 
the full amount of goodwill relating to this stake. The carrying amount of TF1 in the Group’s consolidated balance 
sheet will therefore be reduced to €2.0 billion as of 30 September 2026, representing €9.67 per share (compared 
with €15.36 before the impairment). This charge, which is not tax -deductible, impacts Bouygues’ net profit 
attributable to the Group.  
 
The impairment will not affect the TF1 group’s consolidated financial statements. It will not impact the 
Bouygues SA parent company financial statements or distributable earnings either. 
 
The impairment follows TF1’s announcement of a sharp decline in linear advertising revenue for the fourth 
consecutive quarter, confirming that the linear TV advertising market is shrinking faster than anticipated. This 
decline is the result of structural trends observed globally, characterised by rapid changes in the way televisual 
content is consumed and the increasing shift of advertising spend towards on -line platforms. This situation is

SIGNIFICANT EVENTS AFTER 30 SEPTEMBER 2026 
Colas 
Following the completion of the review process under US antitrust regulations (the Hart-Scott-Rodino Antitrust 
Improvements Act) and the successful conclusion of discussions with the US competition authorities, Colas Inc., 
the US subsidiary of Colas, compl eted on 20 October 2026 the acquisition of 100% of the shares of Suit -Kote 
from the Suits family. 
Specialising in the distribution of liquid asphalt, the production of emulsion blends, and pavement construction 
and preservation services across several northeastern US states, Suit -Kote generated approximately 
USD 460 million in sales in 2025. The acquis ition was completed on the basis of an enterprise value of 
USD 480 million. Following completion of the acquisition on 20  October 2026, Suit -Kote’s results will be 
consolidated in the Group’s financial statements as of 31 December 2026. 
Colas will implement post -closing remedies and related measures required under the antitrust clearance 
process. 
This acquisition is in line with Colas’ international development strategy in targeted geographies, particularly 
the United States, Northern Europe and Germany. It strengthens the Group’s industrial footprint in North 
America and notably enhances its position in the asphalt distribution market in the region. 
 
Colas 
Given the deterioration in its business outlook  in France, Colas France initiated a mutually -agreed resignation 
scheme in early October 2026, based exclusively on voluntary departures, to gradually adjust its workforce while 
maintaining its agility and safeguarding profitability. In this context,  like in each one of these situations,  the 
Bouygues group is rolling out  its internal job mobility initiatives to support the employees concerned and 
facilitate their redeployment within the Group. 
 
Equans 
On 20 October, Equans signed a purchase agreement with a view to acquiring Jiliti , a company that specialises 
in IT  infrastructure lifecycle management, particularly for data centres, in France and internationally. Jiliti 
operates in 17 countries and has a global reach through a network of partners. It generated sales of around 
€150 million in 2025.  Completion of the transaction is expected by the end of 2026, subject to the necessary 
approvals.

GLOSSARY 
ABPU (Average Billing Per User): 
• In the mobile segment, it is equal to the total of mobile sales billed to customers (BtoC and BtoB) divided 
by the average number of customers over the period. It excludes MtoM SIM cards and free SIM cards. 
• In the fixed segment, it is equal to the total of fixed sales billed to customers (excluding BtoB) divided 
by the average number of customers over the period. 
Available cash: the aggregate of cash and cash equivalents and the positive fair value of hedging instruments. 
 
BtoB (business to business): when one business makes a commercial transaction with another. 
 
Backlog: 
• Colas, Bouygues Construction, Equans: the amount of work still to be done on projects for which a firm 
order has been taken, i.e. the contract has been signed and has taken effect (after notice to proceed has 
been issued and suspensory clauses have been lifted). 
• Bouygues Immobilier: sales outstanding from notarised sales. 
Under IFRS 11, Bouygues Immobilier’s backlog does not include sales from notarised sales taken via 
companies accounted for by the equity method (co-promotion companies where there is joint control). 
Book-to-bill: order intake over a 12-month rolling period divided by sales over a 12-month rolling period. 
Business segment : designates each one of the Bouygues group’s six main subsidiaries, namely  
Colas, Bouygues Construction, Bouygues Immobilier, Equans, Bouygues Telecom and TF1. 
 
Change in sales like-for-like and at constant exchange rates: 
• At constant exchange rates: change after translating foreign-currency sales for the current period at the 
exchange rates for the comparative period. 
• On a like-for-like basis: change in sales for the periods compared, adjusted as follows: 
▪ For acquisitions, by deducting from the current period those sales of the acquired entity that have 
no equivalent during the comparative period. 
▪ For divestments, by deducting from the comparative period those sales of the divested entity that 
have no equivalent during the current period. 
 
Churn: refers to the loss of subscribers or customers over a given period. It is closely linked to the concept of 
customer loyalty, and is used in particular by telecoms operators to refer to the rate of customers who have 
switched operator. 
 
Construction Division: Colas, Bouygues Construction and Bouygues Immobilier. 
 
Current operating profit/(loss) from activities (COPA) : current operating profit from activities equates to 
current operating profit before amortisation and impairment of intangible assets recognised in acquisitions 
(PPA).
19/20 
 
 
EBITDA after Leases: current operating profit after taking account of the interest expense on lease liabilities, 
before (i) net charges for depreciation, amortisation and impairment losses on property, plant and equipment 
and intangible assets, (ii) net charges to provisions a nd other impairment losses and (iii) effects of losses of 
control. Those effects relate to the impact of remeasuring retained interests. 
 
EBITDA margin after Leases (Bouygues Telecom): EBITDA after Leases as a proportion of sales from services. 
 
Energies & services: Equans. 
 
Free cash flow: net cash flow (determined after (i) cost of net debt, (ii) interest expense on lease liabilities and 
(iii) income taxes paid), minus net capital expenditure and repayments of lease liabilities. It is calculated before 
changes in working capital requirements (WCR) related to (i) operating activities and (ii) non-current assets used 
in operations. 
 
FTTH (Fibre to the Home): optical fibre from the central office (where the operator’s transmission equipment is 
installed) all the way to homes or business premises (Arcep definition). 
 
FTTH premises secured : premises for which the horizontal is deployed, being deployed or ordered up to the 
concentration point. 
 
FTTH premises marketed: the connectable sockets, i.e. the horizontal and vertical deployed and connected via 
the concentration point. 
 
Group (or the Bouygues group) : designates Bouygues SA and all the entities that are controlled directly or 
indirectly by Bouygues SA as defined in Article L. 233-3 of the French Commercial Code. 
 
Liquidity: the aggregate of available cash, the fair value of hedging instruments and undrawn, confirmed 
medium- and long-term credit facilities. 
 
MtoM: machine to machine communication. This refers to direct communication between machines or smart 
devices or between smart devices and people via an information system using mobile communications 
networks, generally without human intervention. 
 
Net surplus cash/(net debt):  the aggregate of cash and cash equivalents, overdrafts and short -term bank 
borrowings, non-current and current debt, and the fair value of financial instruments. Net surplus cash/(net 
debt) does not include non-current and current lease liabilities. A positive figure represents net surplus cash and 
a negative figure represents net debt. The main components of change in net debt are presented in Note  7 to 
the consolidated financial statements at 30 September 2026, available at bouygues.com.  
 
Order intake (Colas, Bouygues Construction, Equans) : a project is included under order intake when the 
contract has been signed and has taken effect (the notice to proceed has been issued and all suspensory clauses 
have been lifted) and the financing has been arranged. The amount recorded corresponds to the sales the 
project will generate.
20/20 
 
 
Reservations by value (Bouygues Immobilier) : the € amount of the value of properties reserved over a given 
period. 
• Residential properties: the sum of the value of unit and block reservation contracts signed by customers 
and approved by Bouygues Immobilier, minus registered cancellations. 
• Commercial properties: these are registered as reservations on notarised sale. 
For co-promotion companies: 
• If Bouygues Immobilier has exclusive control over the co-promotion company (full consolidation), 100% 
of amounts are included in reservations. 
• If joint control is exercised (the company is accounted for by the equity method), commercial activity is 
recorded according to the amount of the equity interest in the co-promotion company. 
 
Sales from services (Bouygues Telecom) comprise:  
• Sales billed to customers, which include: 
▪ In Mobile: 
- For BtoC customers: sales from outgoing call charges (voice, texts and data), connection fees, 
and value-added services. 
- For BtoB customers: sales from outgoing call charges (voice, texts and data), connection fees, 
and value-added services, plus sales from business services. 
- Machine-To-Machine (MtoM) sales. 
- Visitor roaming sales. 
- Sales generated with Mobile Virtual Network Operators (MVNOs). 
▪ In Fixed: 
- For BtoC customers: sales from outgoing call charges, fixed broadband services, TV services 
(including Video on Demand and catch-up TV), and connection fees and equipment hire. 
- For BtoB customers: sales from outgoing call charges, fixed broadband services, TV services 
(including Video on Demand and catch -up TV), and connection fees and equipment hire, plus 
sales from business services. 
- Sales from bulk sales to other fixed line operators. 
• Sales from incoming Voice and Texts. 
• Spreading of handset subsidies over the projected life of the customer account, required to comply with 
IFRS 15. 
• Capitalisation of connection fee sales, which is then spread over the projected life of the customer 
account. 
 
Other sales (Bouygues Telecom): difference between Bouygues Telecom’s total sales and sales from services. 
It comprises: 
• Sales from handsets, accessories and other. 
• Roaming sales. 
• Non-telecom services (construction of sites or installation of FTTH lines). 
• Co-financing of advertising. 
 
Wholesale: wholesale market for telecoms operators.
