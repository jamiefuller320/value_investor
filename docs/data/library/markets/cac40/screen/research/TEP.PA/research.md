## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Teleperformance SE (TEP.PA, now trading as “TP”) is a global customer-experience and business-process outsourcing group whose shares screen as a deep-value strong buy: P/E 6.7, P/B 0.8, dividend yield 8.0%, and ROE 11.5%, with 14 of 22 quantitative models passing across cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk families. The hook is extreme market pessimism after a multi-year de-rating—news flow references the share price having fallen roughly threefold and exit from the CAC 40—set against a business still generating over €10bn of revenue and €1.4bn of free cash flow (Yahoo, FY2025). The central debate is whether today’s multiples reflect a cyclical trough and successful pivot to AI-augmented services, or a structural impairment of the outsourced contact-centre model as clients substitute automation for labour. Primary regulatory filings were not retrieved in this research pack, so conviction rests heavily on secondary data and news.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags Teleperformance as a strong buy (composite score 93%, sector-relative 84%; conviction score 44%, signal improving, one week at signal). The name passes classic value screens—Graham Enterprising, Deep Value, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Low P/E + High Yield, Acquirer’s Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, and Composite Value—alongside dividend models (High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth) and GARP proxies (Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY). At a trailing P/E of 6.7 and P/B of 0.8, the market prices the equity below book despite mid-teens return on equity, implying either earnings collapse or permanent impairment of the franchise.

For a value investor, the case rests on cash, not reported earnings alone. Yahoo-sourced FY2025 free cash flow of €1.36bn exceeds net income of €497m by a wide margin, supporting FCF Yield model passage and suggesting the asset base still monetises. The proposed €4.50 dividend (approved at the May 2026 AGM, payable 28 May 2026) equates to a high cash yield at current depressed prices, and management has executed bond refinancing in 2026, signalling balance-sheet access. The company remains a scale leader in CX/BPO with geographic diversification and ongoing public-sector wins (e.g. expanded UK Crown Commercial Services framework, per yfinance, November 2025).

However, the screen also fails quality and moat filters—Graham Defensive, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Quality Value, Financial Health, and Piotroski F-Score (6/9)—which is consistent with a “cheap but contested” rather than “cheap and compoundable” profile. Leverage is not declining (Piotroski fail), ROA is not improving, and gross margins are not expanding. The investment case is therefore a contrarian deep-value play on cash generation and capital returns at distressed multiples, not a high-quality compounder. That aligns with the screen’s cheapness/dividend/GARP/risk clustering while explaining why conviction remains moderate despite the strong buy label.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Structural / competitive: The outsourced contact-centre model faces acute AI disruption risk. June 2026 sector selloffs—Bloomberg’s “Call Center Stocks Fall on Worry AI Makes Them ‘Uninvestible’” and Barron’s on Concentrix’s weak guidance dragging Teleperformance down 11.5%—highlight correlated de-rating across peers. Les Echos (2 March 2026) framed the question explicitly: slow decline versus rerating. This is not captured by backward-looking value screens.

Earnings and guidance: Four-year net income erosion, FY2025 EBITDA decline, and February 2026 management commentary on lower 2026 cash flow (BFM Bourse, ABC Bourse, en-contact.com) challenge the sustainability of current dividend and FCF-yield metrics. August 2025 interim disappointment on growth and margins (BFM Bourse) reinforces the trend.

Balance sheet and accounting: Failed Financial Health and negative tangible equity mean equity is largely goodwill-dependent; impairment risk is real (FY2025 already recorded €97m special charges). Rising net debt and interest burden (Piotroski leverage-decline fail) reduce flexibility if clients compress volumes or pricing.

Governance and strategy: Founder-CEO Daniel Julien departed in February 2026; an AI specialist was appointed to lead turnaround, alongside a strategic portfolio review announced on the FY2025 earnings call (Yahoo, 27 February 2026). Leadership transitions at inflection points add execution risk. May 2026 governance changes—including appointment of Sheikha Hanadi Al Thani as Lead Independent Director (Yahoo, 24 May 2026)—improve oversight but do not resolve the business-model question.

Index and sentiment: News references CAC 40 exit and a share price “divided by three” (BFM Bourse, June 2026), implying forced selling and thin sell-side support; Zonebourse analyst-consensus piece (24 July 2026) suggests ongoing scepticism.

Regulatory / filing gaps: With no annual report, universal registration document, or interim release bodies in the research pack, covenants, litigation contingencies, going-concern statements, and segment disclosures cannot be assessed. This is a material verify-before-trade gap.
