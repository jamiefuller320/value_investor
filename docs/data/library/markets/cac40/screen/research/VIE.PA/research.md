# Veolia Environnement SA (VIE.PA) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T11:59:20.233035+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Veolia is a global environmental-services platform spanning water, waste, and energy, rated **buy** by the quantitative screen on a blend of cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk factors (6/22 models; composite 65%). The value hook is a mid-teens P/E, ~4.3% yield, and sustained free cash flow against a post-Suez asset base with recurring municipal and industrial contracts. Yahoo-sourced accounts show earnings and EBITDA rising through 2025 despite modest revenue drift, but leverage, negative tangible book, and weak liquidity are material offsets. The key debate is whether integration synergies, contract wins (India, UK, data centres), and raised 2026 guidance justify the balance-sheet risk — a question filings would normally settle but cannot here.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen flags Veolia as a buy with five factor families passing: cheapness (P/E 20.3, P/B 2.6), quality (ROE 13.4%, Piotroski F-Score 7/9), dividend (4.3% yield, Dividend Growth model pass), GARP (Neff PEGY pass), and risk (Earnings Quality pass). Graham Enterprising and FCF Yield also pass, pointing to cash-backed earnings rather than deep net-net territory.

For a value investor, the case rests on a quasi-utility franchise — long-dated water and waste concessions with inflation-linked pricing — trading below many pure-play peers on earnings yield while returning cash (Yahoo: €1.28bn dividends paid in 2025; FCF €5.15bn). Post-2022 Suez consolidation, reported net income has compounded from €716m (2022) to €1,217m (2025) and diluted EPS from €1.00 to €1.54, with EBITDA up from €5.34bn to €6.28bn. Operating cash flow has held above €5bn for three consecutive years.

The screen’s failures are instructive: Financial Health, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Lynch PEG (3.62), and deep-value screens all fail on leverage (debt/equity ~250%), current ratio 0.86, thin margins, and P/B above 1. This is a **quality-at-a-reasonable-price** name, not a distressed asset play. Business quality is supported by scale, contract backlog expansion (news), and management’s July 2026 guidance upgrade, but the balance sheet limits margin of safety relative to classic Graham criteria.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Without filing bodies, the following are inferred from screen metrics and Yahoo financials; **going concern, covenant, pension, and contingency disclosures could not be reviewed.**

1. **Leverage and liquidity:** Debt/equity ~250%; current ratio 0.86; negative working capital €4.2bn. Screen fails Financial Health, Buffett Quality, and Economic Moat on debt and liquidity. Rising net debt in 2025 (€10.8bn vs €8.4bn in 2024) despite strong FCF warrants monitoring.

2. **Intangible-heavy balance sheet:** Negative tangible book (−€8.9bn) reflects Suez-era goodwill. Impairment risk if concession renewals or tariff settlements disappoint; no impairment language available from filings.

3. **Revenue stagnation:** Top line down ~2% from 2023 peak. Earnings growth is margin- and cost-driven; sustained revenue pressure could cap further rerating.

4. **Interest burden:** €876m interest on €20bn debt; rate and refinancing sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment (macro context: EUR/USD 1.15; not used to veto the signal).

5. **Recurring restructuring:** Special charges and write-offs each year (2022–2025); integration may not yet be fully complete (Cleaners acquisition per news).

6. **Regulatory and contract risk:** Municipal water/waste concessions face tariff politics, ESG scrutiny, and renewal risk — inherent to the model but not quantified here.

7. **Screen-specific flags:** Lynch PEG 3.62 (growth expensive vs earnings); EV/EBIT 14.9 above Acquirer’s Multiple threshold; earnings yield 4.9% below deep-value hurdles.
