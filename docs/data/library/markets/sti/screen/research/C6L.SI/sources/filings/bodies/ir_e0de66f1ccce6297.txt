The SIA Group posted  a record half-year revenue of $11,297 million for  
FY2026/27, 16.8% or $1,622 million higher than a year ago. This was driven by robust 
demand for both passenger travel and air cargo.    
Passenger flown revenue grew by $1,308 million (+16.8%) to 
$9,095 million, with SIA and Scoot carrying a half-year record of 21.8 million passengers, 
up 5.0% year-on-year. Group passenger load factor (PLF)  was 86.8%, 0.9 percentage 
points lower, as capacity expansion of 4.9% outpaced traffic growth of 3.8%. Passenger 
yields grew 12.1% to 11.1 cents per revenue passenger-kilometre. 
Cargo revenue grew by 30.2% (+$323 million) to $1,394 million on a 25.4% 
improvement in yields and a 2.0-percentage point increase in the cargo load factor (CLF) 
to 58.5%. The higher CLF was driven by cargo loads increasing 4.0% while capacity 
remained flat.  
Group expenditure rose by  $1,856 million (+20.9%) to $10,728 million. 
This was mainly due to the $2,233 million (+90.3%) rise in fuel cost before hedging, with 
average jet fuel prices for the six months  at US$171 per barrel,  86.7% higher year -on-
year amid the ongoing conflict in the Middle East. Fuel hedging reversed from a loss last 
year to a gain this year (-$806 million), partially mitigating the cost increase. As a result, 
net fuel cost grew $1,427 million (+56.0%) to $3,974 million. Non-fuel expenditure rose 
$429 million (+6.8%) due to overall capacity expansion and inflationary cost increases.   
Consequently, the Group registered an operating profit of $569 million in 
the first half of FY2026/27, down $234 million (-29.1%) from a year earlier. The weaker 
first quarter performance  (-$299 million; -73.8%) was the primary drag on the first half 
operating profit  with improved second-quarter performance (+$65  million; +16. 3%) 
partly mitigating the impact.   
The Group’s first half net profit fell  $105 million (-43.9%) to $134 million. 
This was largely attributable to the decline in operating profit (-$234 million), and partially 
offset by the lower share of losses from associated companies , mainly Air India  
(+$68 million), and lower tax expenses (+$70 million).   
Second Quarter FY2026/27 – Profit and Loss 
Group operating profit for the second quarter rose by $65 million (+16.3%) 
year-on-year to $463 million, underpinned by the Group’s record second quarter revenue 
of $5,583 million (+$698 million; +14.3%). Passenger flown revenue increased 15.0% to 
$4,513 million, supported by 12.2% yield growth and 2.3% higher passenger carriage. 
Cargo revenue grew 26.9% to $ 686 million, with cargo yields 22.3%  higher and loads 
increasing 3.9%.  
Group expenditure rose $633 million  (+14.1%) due to a higher  fuel cost  
before hedging (+$805 million; +63.3%), driven by a spike in prices amid the Middle East 
conflict. Inclusive of  hedging, net fuel cost climbed $436 million (+33.9%). Non -fuel 
expenditure rose $197 million (+6.2%) mainly due to increased capacity and infl ation.

Balance Sheet 
 
 The Group holds one of the strongest balance sheets in the airline industry. 
As of 3 0 September 2026, Group shareholders’ equity was  $16.45 billion, down  
$815 million from 31 March 2026.  
 
 The Group’s total debt decreased  by $637 million, mainly due to the 
maturity of a US$500 million ($6 66 million) fixed rate bond, and debt repayments of 
$338 million, partially offset by the issuance of an offshore CNY1.50 billion ($282 million) 
five-year fixed rate bond. As a result, the Group’s debt-equity ratio fell from 0.62 times to 
0.61 times.  
 
 Cash and bank balances grew by $19 1 million to $8.12 billion. The Group 
generated $2.50 billion of net cash from operations, and $911 million in proceeds from 
the maturity of fixed deposits placed for tenors longer than 12 months. These were partly 
offset by capital expenditure ($1.11 billion),  repayments of the fixed rate bond and 
borrowings ($994  million), and the payment of ordinary and special dividend s  
($915 million).  
 
 The Group’s total cash and liquidity stood at $8.98 billion,  comprising  
$8.12 billion of cash and bank balances and $ 855 million in fixed deposits  placed for 
tenors longer than 12 months, classified as other short-term assets. In addition, the Group 
had access to $3.23 billion in committed lines of credit, all of which remain ed undrawn.  
 
FLEET AND NETWORK DEVELOPMENT 
 
 As of 30 September 2026, SIA operated 152 passenger aircraft1 and seven 
freighters, while Scoot operated 65 passenger aircraft 2. The Group’s operating fleet 
comprised 224 passenger and freighter aircraft, with an average age of seven years and 
eleven months. During the quarter, SIA took delivery of two Boeing 737-8 aircraft, while 
Scoot took delivery of two Airbus A321neo aircraft. The Group has 58 aircraft on order3. 
 
 SIA and Scoot  continue to strengthen the Group’s global network and 
enhance connectivity for customers. For the period April to September 2026, despite the 
headwinds from  the Middle East conflict on the global airline industry, the Group’s 
passenger capacity grew 4.9%. This reflects its ability to respond with agility and capture 
market opportunities amid an uncertain operating environment.

served 84. Of these, 58 were operated exclusively by Scoot, extending the Group's reach, 
particularly in fast- growing Asia-Pacific markets. The cargo network 4 spanned  
139 destinations in 36 countries and territories. 
  
 In October 2026, Scoot further expand ed its footprint in mainland China 
with the launch of three-times-weekly services to Guiyang. This is a new direct link from 
Singapore Changi Airport, making Guiyang Scoot's 16th destination in mainland China 
and the Group's 23rd destination in this growth market. To meet higher demand during 
the year-end holiday season, Scoot will also boost capacity between October 2026 and 
January 2027 across destinations in the Asia -Pacific region. This includes additional 
services to Clark (the Philippines), Kota Kinabalu (Ma laysia), Okinawa (Japan), Phu Quoc 
(Vietnam), Tiruchirappalli (India), and Hat Yai and Phuket in Thailand. 
  
 For the Northern Winter 2026 operating season (25 October 2026 to  
27 March 2027), SIA will increase frequencies to selected destinations, including Adelaide 
(Australia), Auckland (New Zealand), Milan (Italy), Munich (Germany), Ho Chi Minh City 
and Da Nang (Vietnam). Seasonal supplementary flights to Hokkaido (Japan) will also be 
mounted from 30 November 2026 to 24 January 2027 to cater to peak winter travel 
demand. SIA will launch two new destinations during the season: five-times-weekly 
services to Madrid (Spain) via Barcelona , and daily flights to the new Western Sydney 
International Airport (WSI) in Australia. Together with SIA’s four -times daily services to 
Sydney’s Kingsford Smith Airport, this will bring SIA’s total services to Sydney to five daily 
flights.

suspended. The launch of SIA's services to Riyadh (Saudi Arabia) has been deferred to 
March 2027. The Group will continue to monitor developments closely and adjust its 
schedules as appropriate. 
 
STRATEGIC INVESTMENTS AND PARTNERSHIPS 
 
 The Group’s 25.1% strategic stake in the Air India Group  is a key pillar of 
its multi-hub strategy. This direct participation in one of the world’s largest and fastest -
growing aviation markets  complements the SIA Group’s main hub in Singapore,  and 
broaden access to new traffic flows.

 Air India has made tangible progress in  its multi -year transformation, 
including enhancements to customer experience and operational performance. This has 
been accompanied by improving customer sentiment , as reflected in the positive 
trajectory of its Net Promoter Score, and  external recognition, including being named 
Best Airline in India at the 2026 Skytrax World Airline Awards. 
 
 SIA, and its partner, Tata Sons, recognise that a transformation of this scale 
will take time . Both partners continue to work closely to support Air India ’s j ourney 
towards profitable growth, including strengthening structural cost efficiencies, unlocking 
greater synergies between Air India and Air India Express, sharpening its commercial 
strategy to navigate current external challenges elevating its customer proposition.  
 
 SIA continues to deepen commercial partnerships with like-minded carriers 
in key markets to enhance network connectivity and deliver greater value to customers. 
  
 Following the signing of their Commercial Cooperation Framework 
Agreement in January 2026, Air India and SIA launched the first phase of joint initiatives 
in October 2026. These include additional flight options between Singapore and eight 
Indian cities through the airlines' respective booking channels , greater flexibility and a 
more seamless booking experience. Enhance d reciprocal loyalty programme benefits 
were introduced in April 2026, with more being planned in the second quarter of 2027.  
 
 Malaysia Airlines and SIA introduc ed a joint fare produc t in June 2026,  
providing customers with greater choice across their combined network. A memorandum 
of understanding (MoU) between Air China and SIA  will allow the airlines to, s ubject to 
regulatory approvals, coordinate schedules and fares, expand codeshare services, and 
improve frequent flyer programme benefits . In May 2026, SIA and  Vietnam Airlines 
signed an MoU to explore reciprocal frequent flyer privileges for eligible members. 
 
 SIA and DHL Express extended their Boeing  777 Freighter Partnership  
agreement. SIA pilots will continue to operate the f ive Boeing 777 freighters that are 
based at Singapore Changi Airport on behalf of  DHL Express , and oversee the 
maintenance of the aircraft. This will strengthen air cargo links between the Asia -Pacific 
region and global markets, and reinforce Singapore's role as a leading air freight hub. 
 
 Adoption of sustainable aviation fuels (SAF) remains critical lever to  the SIA 
Group’s goal of achieving net zero carbon emissions by 2050. In the third quarter of 
FY2025/26, the Group signed a three -year offtake agreement with Aether Fuels to 
procure up to  4,800 tonnes of next -generation SAF from 2028. As the first airline to 
purchase SAF from Aether’s new Singapore facility, SIA is supporting lower -carbon fuel 
production and the transition to more sustainable air travel.

for FY2026/27 be paid on 11 December 2026, to shareholders as of 26 November 2026.

OUTLOOK 
 
 Passenger demand remains healthy , supported by seasonal travel flows . 
Cargo demand has been firm , driven by semiconductor and cloud computing -related 
flows, as well as shipments shifting to air  freight amid geopolitical and  weather-related 
disruptions to maritime shipping.  
 
 The airline industry operate s in an uncertain environment, with volatile  
geopolitical developments, including the conflict in the Middle East, adding complexity  
across operations and supply chains . Jet fuel prices remain elevated relative to   
pre-conflict levels, and continue to place significant pressure on costs. While the Group 
has adjusted air fares and cargo rates, these measures do not fully offset the impact  of 
sustained high fuel prices. An extended conflict could  also have spillover effect s on 
broader macroeconomic conditions, potentially leading to further inflationary cost 
pressure and affecting demand for passenger travel and air freight.  
 
 The Group is in a strong position to navigate this volatility. Its diversified 
global network and broad mix of traffic flows help to cushion shifts in demand, while the 
complementary positioning of Singapore Airlines and Scoot enables the m to serve 
different markets and customer segments, and adjust capacity and schedules with agility, 
as the Group has consistently demonstrated. 
 
 The Group will maintain disciplined cost management  to reinforce its  
long-term competitive position. Supported by its strong foundations – a robust balance 
sheet, industry-leading digital capabilities,  and a talented and resilient workforce  – the 
Group will continue to invest strategically in both SIA and Scoot, a dvance its multi-hub 
strategy through its investment in Air India  and deepen win-win partnerships with like-
minded carriers in key markets . At the same time, it will maintain its focus o n 
strengthening the pillars of its brand promise: network connectivity, product leadership, 
and service excellence.

About Singapore Airlines 
 
The Singapore Airlines (SIA) Group’s history dates back to 1947, with the maiden flight of Malayan Airways. 
The airline was later renamed Malaysian Airways and then Malaysia -Singapore Airlines (MSA). In 1972, 
MSA split into two airlines, one of which becam e Singapore Airlines. Initially operating a modest fleet of 
10 aircraft serving 22 destinations in 18 countries, SIA has since grown into a world -class international 
airline group that is firmly committed to the continuous enhancement of the three main pillars of its brand 
promise: Service Excellence, Product Leadership, and Network Connectivity. 
 
 Singapore Airlines is the world’s most awarded airline. In September 2026, SIA was named the  
World's Best Airline  at the Skytrax World Airline Awards, marking the sixth time it has received the 
accolade. SIA was again named among Fortune magazine’s list of the 50 Most Admired Companies in the 
world in 2026. It was one of only two Asian companies in the top 50, the only Singapore-based company 
on list, and ranked first among its peers in the airline industry. For more information, please visit  
www.singaporeair.com.

GLOSSARY   
   
Revenue passenger-km = Number of passengers carried x distance flown (in km) 
Available seat-km = Number of available seats x distance flown (in km) 
Passenger load factor = Revenue passenger-km expressed as a percentage of available seat-km 
Passenger yield = Passenger revenue from scheduled services divided by revenue passenger-km 
Revenue per available seat-km = Passenger revenue from scheduled services divided by available seat-km 
Passenger unit cost = Passenger operating expenditure divided by available seat-km 
Passenger unit cost ex-fuel = Passenger operating expenditure less fuel cost, divided by available seat-km 
Passenger breakeven load factor = Passenger unit cost expressed as a percentage of passenger yield. This is the theoretical load 
factor at which passenger revenue equates to the operating expenditure of passenger operations 
   
Cargo load  = Cargo and mail load carried (in tonnes) x distance flown (in km) 
Gross capacity = Cargo capacity production (in tonnes) x distance flown (in km) 
Cargo load factor = Cargo and mail load (in tonne-km) expressed as a percentage of gross capacity (in tonne-km) 
Cargo yield = Cargo and mail revenue from scheduled services divided by cargo load (in tonne-km) 
Cargo unit cost = Cargo operating expenditure divided by gross capacity (in tonne-km) 
Cargo breakeven load factor = Cargo unit cost expressed as a percentage of cargo yield. This is the theoretical load factor at 
which cargo revenue equates to the operating expenditure of cargo operations
