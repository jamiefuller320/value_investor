The portfolio spans 15 states and over 3.1 million square feet (288,000 square metres), with a weighted average lease term of 6.8 years. Most of the properties are leased to co-location providers under triple-net arrangements, according to exclusive advisor JLL. “While the properties are being offered as a portfolio, the owner is open to selling certain assets one-off or in smaller subsets to multiple buyers,” the consultancy said this week in a release . The campaign comes ahead of a leadership handover at MIT’s manager, which is controlled by Temasek-owned Mapletree Investments. CEO Lily Ler is set to step down on 1 October , with Anand Tze Ming Chandran, Mapletree’s Asia Pacific data centre head, taking over the top job. Three Buying Waves The 22 assets had an aggregate appraised value of $1.24 billion as of March, or $1.07 billion based on MIT’s proportionate ownership, according to Mingtiandi’s tally of figures in the trust’s annual report . The largest are Atlanta’s 180 Peachtree Street and 250 Williams Street, valued at $268 million and $217 million respectively, with no guide price disclosed for the campaign. MIT’s Lily Ler is marketing data centres in her final act as CEO Sixteen of the properties were part of MIT’s $1.32 billion acquisition of 29 US data centres from Sila Realty Trust in 2021. The assets include facilities in Atlanta, Charlotte and Indianapolis. Four more came from a $1.37 billion purchase of 13 North American data centres from Digital Realty in 2019. The facilities in Arizona, suburban Denver and Lithia Springs outside Atlanta remain held through a 50:50 joint venture of MIT and trust sponsor Mapletree. The remaining properties — 180 Peachtree Street and 5150 McCrimmon Parkway in North Carolina’s Raleigh area — trace their origins to a $750 million acquisition of 14 US data centres from Carter Validus in 2017. MIT took full ownership of that portfolio in 2020 by paying $210.9 million for its sponsor’s remaining 60 percent interest. MIT’s earlier US disposals include the $10 million sale of a Michigan facility in 2022 and last year’s $11.8 million divestment of 2775 Northwoods Parkway in Georgia. In June, the trust sold 2000 Kubach Road in Philadelphia for $14.5 million , representing a 4.3 percent premium to its appraised value. Responding to unitholder questions in July, the manager said enterprise tenants had vacated some facilities as they consolidated their businesses, with certain properties not efficiently configured for full data centre use. MIT announced the divestment programme in April to shed underperforming or non-core North American facilities. While remaining positive on the region, the manager plans to recycle the proceeds into data centres backed by long-term leases to cloud, hyperscale and co-location operators in core Asian and European markets, with the funds available to reduce debt pending redeployment. Digital Veteran Takes Helm MIT’s manager announced last week that Ler would relinquish her roles as CEO and executive director to become group chief financial officer of sponsor Mapletree. Chandran’s appointment as her successor is subject to regulatory approval. Ler became CEO in July 2024 after serving as the manager’s CFO from November 2011. The Nanyang Technological University graduate began her Mapletree career in 2001 and later served as head of treasury and investor relations at the manager of Mapletree Logistics Trust. Chandran has served as Mapletree’s head of data centres for Asia Pacific since February and brings more than 16 years of experience in corporate finance, investment banking, business development and digital infrastructure. Before joining Mapletree, Chandran was CFO of Robert Kuok’s K2 Strategic, a digital infrastructure platform, where he oversaw capital and liquidity strategy, corporate governance, financial planning and acquisitions. He holds economics degrees from the London School of Economics and the University of Oxford, as well as a master’s degree in professional accounting from Singapore Management University.
