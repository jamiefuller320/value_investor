# Mapletree Industrial Trust (ME8U.SI) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T17:51:28.298377+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Mapletree Industrial Trust (MIT) is a Temasek-sponsored industrial and data-centre REIT with S$9.1 billion AUM across Singapore, North America, and Japan. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness (P/B 1.1, FCF yield 13.3%), dividend yield (6.5%), and balance-sheet quality (Piotroski 7/9), but operating momentum has turned: FY2026 DPU fell 6.3% to 12.71 cents and North American occupancy slipped to 87.8%. The investment case rests on a data-centre pivot (55% of AUM), active portfolio recycling (S$547m divestments in 2Q FY2026), and a 6%+ yield at modest book value — offset by expiring rate swaps, FX headwinds, and margin compression that the screen’s earnings-growth models miss. Key debate: whether FCF-rich asset recycling and Japan/Singapore rental reversions (+6–9%) can stabilise distributions before further NA vacancy erosion.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
MIT fits a value-income profile rather than a quality-growth one. The screen passes six of 22 models across five families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with composite score 58% (sector-relative 53%). Standout metrics are FCF yield of 13.3% (FCF ~S$731m on screen; Yahoo FY2026 operating FCF S$434m), Neff PEGY of 0.04, dividend yield 6.5%, and Magic Formula inclusion, signalling the market prices in earnings decline (-11.6% growth) more than asset-backed cash generation. Piotroski F-Score 7/9 confirms positive net income, operating cash flow exceeding earnings, declining leverage, and no dilution — though ROA and gross margin are not improving.

Business quality is mixed but directionally strategic. Data centres now dominate the portfolio (55.3% of AUM; 41.7% of gross revenue per FY2024/25 annual report summary), with sponsor Mapletree Investments actively expanding DC exposure in Japan, Korea, and Europe. Singapore industrial assets remain resilient (93.2% occupancy, +9.2% rental reversion in FY2024/25). Management is recycling capital at premiums — 22.1% above cost on Singapore divestments, 18.6% above valuation on a Georgia data centre — and deleveraging (aggregate leverage 37.3% post-divestments vs 40.1% at FY2024/25 year-end). For a value investor, the buy case is a high-yield, sub-book REIT with sponsor backing and visible FCF, bought during an earnings trough rather than for dividend growth (Dividend Growth model failed).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**North American occupancy and lease rollover.** NA occupancy fell to 87.8% (2Q FY2026) from 90.3% (FY2024/25), with known non-renewals in Philadelphia and Tempe. Management flagged headwinds and is evaluating divestments, early tenant engagement, and repositioning — but no filing body contains covenant or going-concern language; leverage at 37–40% remains well below the 50% MAS regulatory cap.

**Distribution and interest-rate exposure.** *Mapletree Industrial Trust warns of DPU hit amid expiring rate swaps and vacancies* (reitsweek, 25 June 2026) signals further near-term DPU pressure as hedges roll off into a higher-rate environment (context: US 10-year ~4.68% per macro markers). Perpetual securities (S$300m QDS tax ruling secured, The Globe and Mail, 27 June 2026) add structural financing complexity.

**Earnings quality and margin compression.** FY2026 net income fell 36% (Yahoo) despite property sales gains (gain on investment securities S$127.6m in cash-flow statement). Q4 FY2026 loss and Q1 FY2027 loss (Yahoo quarterly) challenge the data-centre growth narrative (*Mapletree Industrial Trust Q4 Loss Challenges Bullish Data Centre Growth Narrative*, simplywall.st, 30 April 2026; *Margin Compression That Tests Data Center Growth Story*, simplywall.st, 24 July 2026).

**Screen failures as red flags.** Failed models include Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Dividend Growth, Financial Health, Graham Defensive, and Deep Value — consistent with low ROE (4.2%), declining earnings, and REIT-typical capital intensity. P/E 27.6 is not cheap on reported earnings.

**Sponsor and concentration.** Temasek-backed Mapletree Investments holds most data-centre assets through MIT; sponsor strategy shifts (logistics focus, student-housing fund wind-down per Business Times, 2 June 2026) could affect pipeline and fee dynamics. Sponsor article notes MIT holds most of Mapletree’s data-centre assets.

**FX and geographic mix.** USD weakness hurt S$‑reported revenue in 2Q FY2026; ~40% of portfolio in North America creates translation and operational exposure.

**Governance.** FY2024/25 annual report summary notes 42% female board representation and proposed auditor change (Appendix A referenced but body not available). No material regulatory actions identified in sources.
