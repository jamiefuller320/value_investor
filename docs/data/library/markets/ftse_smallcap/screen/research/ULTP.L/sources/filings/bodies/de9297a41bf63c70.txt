According to its market research, nearly 80% of UK households own at least one of the Group's products. The Group sells to over 300 retailers in over 30 countries - spanning discounters, supermarkets and general retailers, and ranging from large national and international multi-channel retailers to smaller retail chains. Its products are also available on salter.com and beldray.com , as well as major third-party online marketplaces. The Group specialises in four product categories: Small Domestic Appliances; Housewares; Laundry; and Audio. Other UP brands include Progress (bakeware, cookware and kitchen electrical), Kleeneze (laundry and floorcare), Petra (small domestic appliances), George Wilkinson (cookware and kitchen electrical) and Intempo (audio). Founded in 1997, Ultimate Products is headquartered in Oldham, Greater Manchester, where it has design, sales, marketing, buying, quality assurance, support functions and warehouse facilities across two sites. Manor Mill, the Group's head office, includes a 20,000 sq ft showroom that showcases each of its brands. In addition, the Group has an office and showroom in Guangzhou, China and Paris, France. Ultimate Products employs over 300 staff and is certified as a Great Place to Work®. A significant number of its employees joined via the Group's Graduate Development Scheme, one of the biggest in the North West. Please note that Ultimate Products is not the owner of Russell Hobbs. The Group currently has licence agreements in place granting it an exclusive licence to use the "Russell Hobbs" trademark for cookware and laundry (NB this does not include Russell Hobbs electrical appliances). These have included the planned transition of Simon Showman and Andrew Gossage to non-executive roles after many years as the driving forces behind the Group, as well as the transformation of our commercial function. Throughout, however, there has been continuity in the purpose, values and culture that have long defined the business. Central to that culture is a commitment to continuous improvement, reflected in the constant desire to be better today than we were yesterday, and better tomorrow than we are today. That sometimes requires difficult choices and meaningful change. The decisions we have taken during FY26 have been made in that spirit, seeking to strengthen the business and its prospects while remaining true to the Ultimate Products identity. Continuity of Purpose Ultimate Products is the Home of Brands, and our purpose is to provide beautiful products for every home. Over the past decade, the Group has transformed from a sourcing and trading business into an owner of brands and a strategic partner to retail customers, with a portfolio of products that resonate with consumers. It is this model that has been the engine of our growth over the past ten years, as demonstrated by the table above. Sales of our proprietary brands have increased from £51.3m in FY17 to £128.4m in FY26. That growth has not always been linear, with changing customer order patterns impacting FY18 and the air fryer boom in FY23, which subsequently unwound in FY24. However, we firmly believe that our brands will drive the long-term growth of the business and are key to increasing shareholder returns. While our brands, particularly Salter and Beldray, are central to our long-term strategic success, they have not always been given the commercial primacy they required. This was particularly evident during FY24, when widespread industry overstocking led to a greater availability of clearance parcels. Ultimate Products began life as a clearance and sourcing business, and the skills associated with those activities remained deeply embedded in our commercial function as the business evolved, but it became clear that their strategic drawbacks outweigh their short-term tactical benefits. We therefore took the difficult decision to close the Group’s clearance division. Third-party clearance sales are typically higher-margin, but they are one-off, volatile and ultimately proved to be cannibalistic rather than additive to our top line. They also distracted both our commercial function and our retail customers from developing long-term brand-led relationships. While exiting this activity marked a departure from UP’s historic roots, it was a necessary step to sharpen our strategic focus on branded growth and to support our ambition to become the ‘Home of Brands’. Continuity of Values Throughout the changes made during the year, we have remained guided by our seven core values: We always strive to do the right thing We love our brands We are passionate about product We go the extra mile for our customers We invest in our people We care about our community We care about the environment These values have been a defining feature of Ultimate Products since the business was founded by Simon Showman, who led the Group for 27 years. His entrepreneurial approach and sharp commercial instincts have shaped the business and the fast-moving culture that underpins it. Together with Andrew Gossage, Simon has been fundamental to UP's development from a founder ‑ led sourcing business into the multi ‑ brand homewares group it is today. They have overseen the development of a diversified portfolio of brands, the establishment of deep and enduring retailer relationships, and the Group’s international expansion, all while remaining true to the shared values that have guided the business from the outset. The first of our values is that we always strive to do the right thing. Sometimes the right things are difficult, and this has been particularly true during the transformation of our commercial function. While sales of our proprietary brands have continued to grow, this has been driven by our international customers, whereas in the UK we have seen a disappointing fall in sales. Although challenging trading conditions have undoubtedly played a part, we believe that we can do better. We have therefore made important improvements to how we operate commercially, strengthening the foundations of the business to better position us to maximise future opportunities and return to top-line growth. Because we are passionate about product, we must be proactive in identifying the consumer trends that make our branded products desirable to both consumers and retail customers alike. Product development is where we commit the greatest proportion of our resources and capital, so it is vital that the process is as effective as possible. All decisions and activities relating to product development now sit under Katie Maxwell, our Chief Product Officer, allowing for a more coordinated approach and clearer ownership of product outcomes. Under the previous siloed structure, buying teams developed products based more on instinct. Decisions are now grounded in data-led analysis of what our consumers want on their countertops and, in turn, what our retail customers want on their shelves. However, it is in our approach to sales, under the leadership of Duncan Singleton, our Chief Commercial Officer, that we have seen the most significant changes to the commercial function. Given the origins of our business, the primary skills of our sales function were previously geared towards selling third-party clearance and white-label products rather than our UP proprietary branded products. This approach was not aligned with our value of loving our brands and the Ultimate Products of today. Building long-term commercial partnerships with retail customers requires a deep understanding of their needs, supported by detailed data gathering and analysis. The focus should not simply be on the sale made today, but on developing shared, long-term plans for the years ahead, including product pipelines and brand progression, and going the extra mile to develop products and ranges that work for both our customers and their consumers. With this in mind, during the year we restructured and strengthened the sales function to ensure it has the capabilities and skills to better understand and anticipate customer needs. This has been a major challenge and a source of significant change during FY26, but in the medium term it will support sustainable sales growth and enable the business to build long-term value. Continuity of Culture Ultimate Products has always had a culture of continuous improvement, driven by the energy and ability of our people. We take pride in being a talent-led business that gives colleagues a multitude of opportunities to develop across all areas of the organisation. Our workforce is unafraid to challenge the status quo and the way things are done, and this is something we actively encourage because it allows us to nurture our culture of continuous improvement. Our Graduate Development Scheme sits at the heart of that. It brings the best and brightest talent into the business and provides them with an industry-leading training programme that is both collegial and intellectually stimulating. The scheme was the brainchild of Andrew Gossage and is one of the key legacies of the extraordinary contribution he has made to Ultimate Products over more than two decades with the Group. Our culture drives us to challenge how we work and is increasingly visible in the productivity gains being delivered across the business. During the year, we automated 355 different tasks, saving an annualised 33,000 hours. These enhancements have driven significant productivity improvements within the business and have helped mitigate recent increases in the National Living Wage and employer National Insurance contributions, while allowing colleagues to spend more time on higher-value activities. Automation is now embedded in the way we operate, with more than 1,500 automated processes live across the Group. Other productivity gains have been driven by the teams closest to the work, with colleagues submitting 760 improvement ideas during the year, in keeping with our culture of continuous improvement. Separately, artificial intelligence has moved from experimentation into everyday usage and is increasingly embedded within our core platforms rather than bolted on, thereby providing further opportunities to improve productivity. Our Product Information Management (PIM) platform, Salsify, is now the single source of truth for product data and the engine of our digital shelf. The discipline, data integrity and automation created through PIM provide an important foundation for Project Catalyst, the ongoing programme to replace our core ERP system and our most significant transformation programme to date. The new cloud-based platform will bring multiple disconnected systems together and create a nucleus around which all Group operational data will flow. For a business of our complexity, the opportunity is substantial. A unified platform will give us the operational leverage to grow without proportionate increases in cost or headcount, provide real-time analytics and customisable dashboards directly to decision-makers and support faster, better-informed decisions across the Group. Taken together, PIM, robotic process automation, AI, and the new ERP system will form the digitally-enabled operating model that will underpin our productivity and profitability for years to come. Productivity is therefore not a one-off initiative but a core driver of long-term profitability. By continuing to work more efficiently, we are helping to protect margin against rising costs, reinvesting the savings into price, quality and growth, and building a business fit for the future. The changes we have made during FY26 have therefore been underpinned by continuity in our purpose, values and culture of continuous improvement. In appointing Simon Harrison, a key priority was that he shared those foundations. He also brings outstanding leadership qualities, strong operating discipline and considerable commercial experience. We are confident that he has the right credentials to take Ultimate Products forward into its next chapter as we work to fulfil our purpose of providing beautiful products for every home. Finally, I know I speak for everyone at Ultimate Products in thanking Andrew and Simon for their exceptional contribution, dedication and loyalty to the Group. We are delighted that both will remain with Ultimate Products as Non-Executive Directors. Financing and cash flow The Group generated £13.0m of cash from operating activities (FY25: £9.6m), representing an operating cash conversion of 130% (FY25: 77%), as working capital reduced by £4.1m, due to the reduced level of trading of the Group. As a result, at the period end, the Group had a net bank debt/adjusted EBITDA ratio of 0.9x (FY25: 1.1x), and net bank debt of £8.6m (FY25: £14.1m). The Group sees significant movements in its working capital requirement during the year due to the timing of customer orders. As such, a longer view can be helpful when considering the level of gearing within the business, with the 12-month rolling average ratio of net bank debt/adjusted EBITDA being 1.4x (FY25: 1.3x). Capital Allocation Policy It is the Board's intention to maintain the net bank debt/adjusted EBITDA ratio at around 1.0x, with debt being used to fund the Group’s working capital. The Board believes that this level of leverage is an efficient use of the Group's balance sheet and allows for further returns of capital to shareholders. The Board also intends to continue investing in the business for growth while returning around 50% of post-tax profits to shareholders through dividends, and to supplement this with share buybacks pursuant to its policy of maintaining the net bank debt/adjusted EBITDA ratio at around 1.0x. In line with this policy, the Board is pleased to propose a final dividend of 1.80p per share (FY25: 2.15p per share) taking the total dividend for the year to 2.7p per share (FY25: 3.7p per share). Subject to shareholder approval at the AGM on 11 December 2026, the final dividend will be paid on 30 January 2027 to shareholders on the register at the close of business on 4 January 2027 (ex-dividend date 31 December 2026). Going Concern The Directors have adopted the going concern basis in preparing these accounts after assessing the principal risks and having considered the impact of severe but plausible downside scenarios, including supply chain issues and demand led falls in revenue due to inflation and rises in interest rates. The Directors have considered a number of impacts on sales, profits and cash flows, taking into account experiences learnt from previous business interruptions. The Directors have considered the resilience of the Group in severe but plausible scenarios, taking account of its current position and prospects, the principal risks facing the business, how these are managed and the impact that they would have on the forecast financial position. In assessing whether the Group could withstand such negative impacts, the Board has considered cash flow, impact on debt covenants and headroom against its current borrowing facilities.
