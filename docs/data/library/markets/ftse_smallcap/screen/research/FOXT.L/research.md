## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Foxtons Group plc is a London-focused estate agency trading at a discount on conventional value metrics (P/E ~9.6×, P/B ~0.8×, dividend yield ~2.9%) after a sharp re-rating following a July 2026 profit-guidance downgrade. The quantitative screen flags the name across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families, supported by solid reported free cash flow and a multi-year revenue recovery driven by lettings and bolt-on acquisitions. The central debate is whether statutory cheapness offsets cyclical London sales weakness, rising net debt, negative tangible book value, and the earnings impact of the Renters’ Rights Act. Filing body extracts and interim RNS releases are absent from the research pack, limiting verification of management guidance, covenants and contingent liabilities before sizing a position.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Foxtons presents a classic “cyclical compounder at a trough multiple” setup. The screen passes 13 of 22 models—including Graham Enterprising, Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Magic Formula and Piotroski F-Score—with full data quality (20/20 metrics) and conviction building at 60% over three stable weeks.

The business case rests on three pillars:

1. **Recurring lettings revenue** — Management commentary (via news coverage of FY2025 results, March 2026) emphasises lettings resilience amid volatile London sales; revenue has grown from £147.1m (FY2023) to £172.5m (FY2025) per Yahoo fallback data, with EBITDA rising from £24.9m to £37.2m over the same period.

2. **Cash generation vs. headline P/E** — Reported FCF of £23.8m in FY2025 (Yahoo) against an implied market capitalisation of roughly £115–125m (derived from P/E 9.6× on £12.8m net income) suggests a high-teens FCF yield, a key screen input. Operating cash flow has exceeded £24m in each of the last two years despite acquisition spend.

3. **Balance-sheet optionality at a discount** — Equity of £145m trades below book (P/B 0.78×) despite ROE of ~9%; debt/equity of 43% is moderate for a services business, though lease-adjusted leverage is material (£40m capital lease obligations).

The screen’s buy signal is not a blind deep-value catch: quality and earnings-quality models also pass, implying the market may be over-discounting near-term earnings volatility rather than structural impairment. Insider buying (April 2026) and ongoing share repurchases (£5.5m in FY2025) add a modest alignment signal. The value case weakens if the Renters’ Rights Act permanently compresses lettings margins or if acquisition goodwill (£54.5m) requires impairment.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory — Renters’ Rights Act:** Multiple July 2026 headlines cite lettings reform as a driver of the profit-guidance downgrade. Without filing body text, the precise margin impact and compliance cost are unquantified here; this is the most material near-term earnings risk.

**Cyclical — London sales market:** FY2025 results commentary (March 2026) and January 2026 trading updates reference a weaker sales pipeline. Sales commissions are high-margin but volatile; prolonged transaction volume weakness would pressure earnings despite lettings stability.

**Balance sheet — intangible-heavy, rising leverage:** Goodwill and intangibles (£171m) exceed equity (£145m); tangible book is negative. Net debt is rising while cash is minimal. Lease obligations (£40m) are substantial. Impairment risk exists if acquired lettings portfolios underperform — not assessable without annual report narrative.

**Acquisition integration:** Cumulative M&A spend exceeds £30m over FY2023–FY2025 (Yahoo cash-flow data). Integration failures or overpayment would erode the value case; no purchase-price allocation detail is available in this pack.

**Governance / disclosure gaps:** Companies House accounts are indexed but not parsed; no RNS body text on covenants, related-party transactions or auditor emphasis-of-matter paragraphs. A June 2026 “Holding(s) in Company” RNS suggests ongoing shareholder register activity but content is not extracted.

**Pension:** No pension deficit or IAS 19 disclosure available from fallback sources — flag as unverified.

**Competitive:** London estate agency remains fragmented but consolidating (news references broader UK takeover activity, July 2026). Foxtons’ brand is differentiated but fee pressure and online disruptors are structural headwinds not captured by quantitative screens.

**Macro (colour only):** GBP/USD ~1.33 and FTSE 100 ~10,736 (macro_context.json) provide no direct veto; London property volumes correlate more with domestic rates and sentiment than index level.
