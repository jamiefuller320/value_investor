# Sabre Insurance Group plc (SBRE.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T12:27:28.097087+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Sabre Insurance Group is a UK-focused motor underwriter (private car, motorcycle, taxi) that has grown profit through a softer 2025 pricing cycle by prioritising margin over volume. FY2025 IFRS profit before tax rose 4.9% to £51.0m on lower gross written premium (£202.9m, −14.2%), with net insurance margin of 19.2% and a combined operating ratio of 82.3%. H1 2026 shows premium re-acceleration (+15.7% GWP) and management reiterates full-year profit slightly ahead of 2025, with margins expected back in the 18–22% target range as new business earns through.

The quantitative screen rates SBRE.L as a buy (8/22 models; composite 54%): cheapness (P/E 11.4, earnings yield 8.8%, FCF yield 11.1%), quality, dividend (7.6% yield, growing ordinary payout), GARP, and risk families all pass. Capital returns (13.5p total dividend for 2025; recurring £5m buybacks) and solvency coverage of 154–161% post-distribution support the valuation hook.

The central debate is whether disciplined cycle management and Ambition 2030 (direct motorcycle, differentiated pricing, AI) can sustain mid-teens ROE and dividend growth if the wider UK motor market remains competitively priced into 2026–27, and whether reported H1 margin compression (15.7%) is purely timing or signals renewed pricing pressure.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Sabre offers a cyclically resilient, capital-light insurer trading below earnings power implied by recent underwriting: screen metrics of P/E 11.4, P/B 1.7, and 7.6% dividend yield sit alongside 15.0% ROE and statutory earnings that grew in 2025 despite a shrinking top line. The screen’s passing models—Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Low P/E + High Yield, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, and Earnings Quality—align with a business that converts underwriting profit into cash and shareholder returns rather than balance-sheet leverage.

Business quality is evidenced in primary filings: motor net loss ratio improved to 50.5% in FY2025; the Group holds no external debt; Solvency II coverage remains within the stated 140–160% operating band even after dividends and buybacks; and management has repeatedly sacrificed volume (2025 GWP down 14.2%) to protect margins. FY2025 return on tangible equity was 37.2% (APM per RNS). Organic capital generation funds an ordinary dividend policy of 70–80% of profit after tax plus specials and buybacks—behaviour typical of a mature, shareholder-oriented underwriter rather than a distressed cyclical.

The buy case is reinforced by the inflection visible in 2026 trading updates and H1 results: GWP up 15.7% in H1 2026, motor vehicle policies up >15% since year-end 2025, and Sabre Direct motorcycle premium up >50%. Management targets profit before tax “slightly ahead” of 2025’s £51.0m and maintains Ambition 2030’s >£80m PBT by 2030. At current multiples, the market appears to discount cyclical mean-reversion in premiums more than Sabre’s demonstrated ability to outperform industry loss ratios through the cycle.

Screen failures (Graham Defensive, Net-Net, Piotroski F-Score 3/9, Economic Moat) largely reflect insurance-specific balance-sheet presentation (low current ratio, goodwill, leverage metrics ill-suited to insurers) and slight negative earnings growth in the screen input (−4.4%), not fundamental distress—filings show rising PBT and PAT in 2025 and confirmed 2026 guidance.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / competitive:** Management warns the UK motor market is “over-competing,” with pricing lagging claims inflation; industry profitability may fall in 2026 even if Sabre outperforms. FY2025 premium was deliberately cut 14.2%; recovery depends on market rate increases materialising. Taxi line remains structurally challenged (88.0% net loss ratio FY2025; H1 2026 taxi ratio volatile).

**Underwriting / reserving:** Motorcycle loss ratio spiked to 120.9% in H1 2026 (small book volatility); FY2025 motorcycle was 70.0%. Favourable prior-year development (5.5% FY2025; 10.8% H1 2026) boosts reported margins—sustainability of reserve releases is a monitoring point. PPO discounting and IFRS 17 mechanics add complexity.

**Regulatory:** Filings note reduced uncertainty after the Government Taskforce on Insurance (late 2025) and closure of premium-financing review; ongoing FCA focus on ancillary products, premium finance APRs, and claims management flagged as areas where Sabre claims low exposure—regulatory change remains a sector risk.

**Capital / goodwill:** Goodwill of £156.3m equals ~61% of IFRS equity; tangible equity is materially lower. Solvency coverage has drifted down (198.7% → 161.5% pre/post dividend movement) though still inside target band. Repeated buybacks and dividends reduce buffer if underwriting deteriorates.

**Operational:** Principal risks listed in H1 2026 interim include insurance, operations, IT/cyber, people, climate (EV repair costs vs frequency benefits), and geo-political supply-chain inflation—filings flag limited current impact but future cost pressure possible.

**Governance / going concern:** No going-concern qualification; PwC interim review noted no material going-concern uncertainties undisclosed. No related-party transactions of material impact in H1 2026.

**Screen-specific flags:** Piotroski F-Score 3/9; current ratio 0.55; negative screen earnings growth (−4.4%)—partly H1 timing, partly insurance working-capital structure. Graham and deep-value models fail—expected for a goodwill-heavy insurer, not necessarily a fundamental red flag.
