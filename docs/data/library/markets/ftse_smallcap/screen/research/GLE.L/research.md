# MJ Gleeson plc (GLE.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-10T07:46:20.402103+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
MJ Gleeson is a dual-division UK residential business: Gleeson Homes (affordable housebuilding in the Midlands and North) and Gleeson Land (planning promotion in the South, West and Central England). The quantitative screen rates the name a buy; filing evidence supports a asset-backed, dividend-paying small-cap housebuilder trading through a margin-compression cycle, with net assets of c.522–527p per share and modest leverage against a £135m revolving credit facility. FY2025 profit before tax fell 17% to £20.5m despite 5.9% revenue growth, as Homes margins compressed from 24.1% to 20.7% gross; FY2026 adjusted PBT is guided in line with lowered consensus of c.£10.1m after Gleeson Land sale delays and substantial exceptional charges. The central debate is whether Project Transform, partnership growth (320 completions in FY2026), and a land pipeline can restore earnings power toward management’s medium-term “3,000 homes / profit tripling” ambition, or whether cyclical headwinds, planning bottlenecks and recurring legacy-site costs cap the recovery.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Gleeson offers a familiar UK housebuilder setup: tangible net asset backing, a stated dividend policy (11.0p total for FY2025, 2.6× cover on normalised earnings), and a balance sheet that has absorbed inventory build without distress. Shareholders’ funds were £307.7m at June 2025 (527p NAV); net borrowings were only £0.8m at year-end, rising seasonally to £22.5m at December 2025 with £111m of undrawn facility remaining. Going-concern disclosure in the audited FY2025 accounts stress-tests a 20% volume fall, 5% permanent price cut and 10% land-value decline and still finds adequate liquidity and covenant headroom.

The buy case rests on three filing-supported pillars: (1) **volume and order-book momentum** — homes sold rose from 1,772 (FY2024) to 1,793 (FY2025) to 1,968 (FY2026 per July 2026 trading update), with the forward order book up 64% to 978 plots at H1 FY2026; (2) **strategic optionality in Gleeson Land** — operating profit rose from £2.2m to £7.0m in FY2025, portfolio at 77 sites / 18,401 plots, record 15 planning applications in H1 FY2026; (3) **affordable positioning** — average selling price £193,600–£198,800, 78% of FY2025 sales affordable to a couple on National Living Wage, which may prove defensive in a rate-sensitive market.

The screen’s buy signal aligns directionally with undemanding capital structure and NAV anchor, though `screening_snapshot.json` carries **no populated model metrics** (data_quality_score 0.0, models_passed 0), so conviction must come from filings rather than quantitative overlays. Business quality is improving operationally (partnerships, reservation rates) but not yet in reported margins or normalised earnings — a classic “show me” value setup rather than a quality compounder.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and margin:** Management repeatedly cites subdued consumer confidence, flat underlying selling prices and incentive dependence. FY2026 guidance assumes a strong Spring selling season; H1 commentary warns bulk/PRS investor demand has softened. Geo-political and policy uncertainty flagged for FY2027.

**Operational and governance:** Project Transform followed “issues around process and compliance with procedures” causing cost overruns (FY2025 CEO statement). Multiple restructuring phases (July 2025, January 2026, July 2026 regional consolidation) and £1.3m+ ongoing exceptional costs suggest control weaknesses being repaired, not fully resolved. Legacy site adoption provisions of £5.2–7.1m (May 2026) indicate historical build-quality / infrastructure handover gaps.

**Regulatory:** Building-safety provision £11.7–11.9m for 18 buildings; contingent liabilities noted for historic Gleeson Construction Services contracts. Future Homes Standard, employer NI and residential property developer tax cited as margin headwinds. Planning system delays constrain site openings — a structural regulatory bottleneck, not merely cyclical.

**Gleeson Land timing:** Land sales are lumpy; deferral of the largest FY2026 transaction cut consensus PBT by ~£7.5m. National housebuilders reviewing southern land-buying strategies may prolong transaction timelines.

**Liquidity and leverage:** Not distressed — going-concern confirmed under severe stress scenarios in both annual and interim accounts, covenants expected to be met, £111m+ undrawn at H1 — but net debt swung from £12.9m net cash (June 2024) to £22.5m net debt (December 2025) on inventory build. Seasonal working-capital demands remain material.

**Governance/board:** Chair transition (James Thomson out April 2025; Fiona Goldsmith appointed July 2025); audit committee chair search ongoing; related-party land purchase from NASCIT-linked Hampton Investment Properties Ltd noted in FY2025 accounts.

**Pension:** No material pension deficit flagged in available filing extracts.
