# MJ Gleeson plc (GLE.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T19:42:22.776083+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
MJ Gleeson is a northern England and Scottish Borders affordable housebuilder trading at a deep discount to net assets (P/B ~0.5, P/E ~10.8, yield ~4.1%) after several years of reported earnings compression. The quantitative screen flags a persistent buy across cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk families, supported by minimal leverage, a current ratio of 3.87 and equity that has continued to grow despite softer profits. The central debate is whether asset-backed valuation and resilient Gleeson Homes trading outweigh a clear downward trend in historical earnings (FY2022–FY2024, Yahoo fallback), June 2026 guidance that adjusted group PBT could be ~£7.5m below consensus owing to Gleeson Land slippage, and legacy-site provisions flagged in May 2026 trading commentary. Primary annual and interim report bodies are largely absent from the library; only one RNS body extract is available for direct verification.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Gleeson presents a classic asset play in a cyclical sector: the shares screen as statistically cheap while the balance sheet remains defensively structured.

**Quantitative alignment.** The screen passes 10 of 22 models across all four metric families—cheapness (Graham Defensive, Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, Earnings Yield), dividend (Low P/E + High Yield, Dividend Growth), GARP (Neff PEGY, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple) and risk (Financial Health). Headline metrics—P/E 10.8, P/B 0.52, yield 4.1%, current ratio 3.87—sit well inside typical value thresholds. The signal has been stable for five weeks with high data quality (19/20 metrics).

**Business quality vs. screen.** Gleeson operates affordable homes on owned land in lower-cost regions, distinct from premium national builders. Tangible book is heavily weighted to inventory and work-in-progress (Yahoo FY2024 fallback: inventory £345m on total assets £378m). Shareholders’ equity rose from £272m (FY2022) to £298m (FY2024) even as net income fell from £35.1m to £19.3m, suggesting land and WIP carrying values have held up while margins and volumes normalise post-cycle. Lease-adjusted debt is modest (~£5.1m; Yahoo FY2024), underpinning the Financial Health and Graham Defensive passes.

**Valuation hook.** At P/B 0.52 on shareholders’ equity of £297.7m (Yahoo FY2024), implied market capitalisation is roughly £155m against a net asset base approaching £300m—a substantial discount typical of distressed or deeply cyclical builders, but here paired with positive FY2024 free cash flow (£15.9m; Yahoo) and ongoing dividends (£7.6m cash paid in FY2024).

**Caveat.** Reported ROE is only 4.8% (screen), reflecting earnings decline rather than balance-sheet weakness. The screen’s trailing FCF metric is negative (£-5.8m), which conflicts with Yahoo’s FY2024 FCF of +£15.9m and signals lumpy near-term cash generation; this is a quality overlay, not a screen veto.

### Primary filing: Gleeson Land update (RNS body, 9 June 2026)

From the filing body extract:

- A major land sale representing ~50% of FY2026 forecast plot sales is unlikely to complete in the current financial year; completion is now anticipated in H1 FY2027.
- Two smaller transactions originally expected in FY2026 are also deferred to H1 FY2027, citing national housebuilders reviewing land-buying strategies; one further site sale is still expected in June 2026.
- Gleeson Homes trading is **in line with management’s full-year expectations**.
- The deferral is expected to leave **adjusted group PBT ~£7.5m lower** than company-compiled consensus of **£17.8m** (range £16.4m–£18.5m) for the year ending 30 June 2026—implying adjusted PBT of roughly **£10.3m** if no other offsets.
- Management expects demand to remain solid for high-quality sites, though transactions will take longer.

No going-concern, covenant or pension language appears in this body extract.

**Interpretation.** Gleeson’s income statement shows a three-year earnings reset: volumes and revenue retreated from the FY2022 peak, gross margins tightened (24.5% in FY2024 vs 26.2% in FY2022), and finance costs nearly quadrupled. The balance sheet nevertheless expanded—equity +£25m over the period—driven by inventory accumulation (finished goods and WIP together ~£345m), consistent with land-led affordable housing rather than aggressive write-downs. FY2023’s negative operating and free cash flow coincided with a £58m inventory build; FY2024 saw a partial unwind with positive FCF despite lower profit.

**Returns.** ROE implied from Yahoo FY2024 (NI £19.3m / equity £297.7m) is ~6.5%, consistent with the screen’s 4.8% (likely LTM-adjusted).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and operational**

- UK affordable housing is highly sensitive to mortgage rates, housing policy and regional employment. May 2026 trading commentary flags softer recent customer activity despite resilient spring sales.
- Reported earnings have fallen ~45% from FY2022 to FY2024 (Yahoo fallback), with margin and interest-cost pressure ongoing; ROE remains low for a quality compounder.
- **Gleeson Land timing:** The June 2026 RNS body flags ~£7.5m adjusted PBT shortfall versus £17.8m consensus owing to transaction deferral into FY2027; national housebuilders’ slower land-buying decisions may persist.
- **Legacy sites:** £5–7m provision flagged (May 2026 trading update, news)—suggests historical site issues not fully reserved in prior accounts.

**Balance sheet / working capital**

- Inventory is ~91% of total assets (Yahoo FY2024 fallback). In a downturn, write-down risk could erode the book-value anchor supporting the value case.
- FY2023 demonstrated that inventory build can absorb cash rapidly (FCF -£18.9m); liquidity improved in FY2024 but remains event-driven.

**Governance / market**

- Long-term share-price performance has been poor (Yahoo headline, 15 Jan 2026: investors who bought five years ago sit on a ~37% loss despite a recent bounce).
- July 2026 PDMR and HMRC share-scheme purchases (news, 8–9 Jul 2026) are modest positive alignment signals but not thesis-changing.

**Filing gap**

- Without annual or interim body extracts, contingent liabilities, related-party transactions, pension deficit and banking covenant headroom cannot be assessed from primary sources—material for a land-heavy builder.
- **Not observed (data absent):** No going-concern qualification, covenant breach or pension crisis language could be confirmed from available filing bodies; absence of extracts is not evidence of absence.

**Macro (colour only)**

- UK rates and housing policy remain backdrop variables (e.g. *UK Housebuilder Shares Facing The Biggest Rate And Policy Test Yet*, simplywall.st, 19 Jul 2026). Not used to override the screen signal.
