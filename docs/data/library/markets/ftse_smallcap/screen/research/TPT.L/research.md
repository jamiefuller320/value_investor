# Topps Tiles Plc (TPT.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-11T07:47:19.169192+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Topps Tiles is the UK’s largest specialist tile and hard-surface coverings distributor, pursuing “Mission 365” (medium-term sales of £365m and an 8–10% adjusted PBT margin). The quantitative screen flags a buy, but the snapshot carries no passed models, no composite score, and zero data-quality metrics — the signal is directional only and needs filing-based validation. Filings show a credible recovery: FY25 adjusted PBT rose 46% to £9.2m on adjusted revenue of £265.4m, with statutory return to profit and a 2.9p full-year dividend; however, H1 FY26 pro forma adjusted PBT was flat at £2.2m, and the July 2026 Q3 update cut the FY26 adjusted PBT outlook to above £6.5m (from £9.2m in FY25). The value hook is a cash-generative, market-share-gaining specialist trading at depressed earnings power in a weak RMI cycle, with self-help (23 store closures, ~£6m annualised savings) and digital/trade mix as levers. The central debate is whether near-term cyclical and margin pressure is already in the price, or whether lease-heavy balance sheet, integration risk, and governance friction limit re-rating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, the case rests on four pillars visible in UK RNS filings rather than the thin screen output.

First, relative business quality in a structurally challenged market. Management estimates the tile market remains ~20% below pre-Covid levels, yet Group revenue is ~15% above 2019; FY25 adjusted revenue grew 6.8% while the prior year fell 5.4%, and interim filings describe continued outperformance versus Barclays DIY/RMI spend data (~-2.5% over Oct 2025–Mar 2026). Trade now ~75% of sales; Pro Tiler Tools grew ~20% in H1 FY26; digital penetration reached 21.1% in FY25 and 23.3% in Q3 FY26.

Second, earnings and cash inflection, albeit from a low base. FY25 adjusted EPS was 3.43p (statutory basic 3.05p); operating cash flow was £23.7m and free cash flow £2.6m before acquisitions (filings). Adjusted net cash was £7.4m at September 2025 with £37.4m total facility headroom on a £30m RCF committed to October 2027. The dividend policy targets ~67% of adjusted EPS (flexible to 100%); FY25 payout was 2.9p (~85% of adjusted EPS).

Third, identifiable self-help and M&A optionality. CTD (£9m, August 2024) and Fired Earth (£3m, November 2025) expand B2B and premium reach; CMA clearance required disposal of four stores (largely complete). H1 FY26 interim report targets ~£3m H2 cost savings and ~£6m annualised from store closures, flexible labour, and head-office consolidation.

Fourth, screen alignment with caveats. The buy signal is unsupported by model pass detail (`models_passed: 0`, `data_quality_score: 0.0`, no key metrics in `screening_snapshot.json`). Qualitative filing evidence supports a cyclical value angle (profitable recovery, dividend, FCF, net-cash ex-leases) but not a high-conviction quantitative confirmation. Mission 365 implies ~£30m+ adjusted PBT versus ~£6.5–9m today — the gap defines both upside and execution risk.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / demand:** Heavy RMI exposure; management and Q3 update cite weak consumer confidence, housebuilder pressure, and demand shift to lower-priced products. Barclays DIY spend tracking negative through H1 FY26.

**Leverage / leases:** IFRS 16 lease liabilities ~£83–100m against net assets £3.7–7.7m; adjusted net cash positive but lease-adjusted capital employed ~£100m. Interest on leases £5.4m in FY25; finance costs rising with RCF utilisation (£19m drawn at H1 FY26).

**Integration / M&A:** CTD lost £1.7m in FY25 trading; H1 FY26 loss £0.4m — improving but not yet sustainably profitable. Fired Earth small and early. ERP migration (Dynamics 365 Business Central) flagged as principal risk in FY25 results.

**Regulatory:** CMA Phase 1 concluded April 2025 with four store disposals — largely resolved. Historic EU loss-relief tax provision £1.1m remains provided (FY25 filing note).

**Governance:** Statement Regarding Shareholder Consultation, 15 July 2026 — Resolution 13 at January 2026 AGM received only 53.16% in favour (>20% against); Board engaging shareholders. Prior July 2025 consultation on similar grounds. MS Galleon AG ~29.9% shareholder; related-party supplier Cersanit (~0.4% of COGS).

**Management:** CEO succession (Rob Parker to Alex Jensen, December 2025); CFO Caroline Browne from May 2026; interim CFO in H1 FY26.

**Accounting:** Large FY24 ROU impairments (£17.1m) and FY25 impairment reversals (£2.4m gain) affect statutory PBT; adjusted metrics exclude these — understand both lenses. CTD/Fired Earth acquisition accounting partly provisional under IFRS 3.

**Going concern / covenants:** Interim FY26 going concern confirmed; reverse stress test shows no covenant breach in modelled 10% sales decline; mitigants include dividend suspension. Facility expires October 2027 — refinancing discussions expected 2026 per H1 FY25 interim.

**Competitive:** New principal risk on heightened competition (H1 FY26 interim); AI impact on search/PPC noted.
