## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Card Factory is a UK value-oriented greeting-card and gifts retailer trading at a deep discount on headline multiples (P/E 7.8×, P/B 0.7×, dividend yield 7.1%), which drives the quantitative screen’s **Strong Buy** (12/22 models, 85% composite). The investment hook is cheap cash generation: Yahoo shows FY2026 free cash flow of £90.9m against net income of £31.2m, implying the market may be pricing earnings compression rather than insolvency. The central debate is whether H2 FY2026 margin pressure—softer UK footfall, rising net debt (£66.5m), and negative tangible book value (−£35.1m)—is cyclical or structural. Primary regulatory filings (annual report, interim results) are **not present** in the source pack, limiting verification of going-concern language, covenants, and segment detail; deep research therefore confirms cheapness but not business-quality certainty.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags Card Factory across all four value families—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—with passes including Graham Enterprising, Deep Value, FCF Yield, Low P/E + High Yield, Magic Formula, and Acquirer’s Multiple. At sector-relative composite 81% versus FTSE Small Cap peers, the name screens as statistically cheap with income support (7.1% yield, FCF £70.9m per screen / £90.9m per Yahoo FY2026).

For a value investor, the case rests on a cash-rich franchise trading below book and well below historical earnings power. Revenue has compounded from £463.4m (FY2023) to £582.7m (FY2026, Yahoo), reflecting store rollout and acquisitions (£25.7m business purchases in FY2026). Operating cash flow remained robust at £110.3m despite net profit falling 35% year-on-year to £31.2m, suggesting working-capital and non-cash items are cushioning the P&L. The screen’s failures—Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Piotroski F-Score (5/9), Financial Health, Graham Defensive—appropriately flag that this is a **deep-value income** idea, not a quality compounder: ROE is modest (8.9%), gross margin has compressed (32.4% in FY2026 vs 35.7% in FY2025), and goodwill/intangibles (£388.8m) dominate the balance sheet.

The Strong Buy signal is new (one week, conviction 42%, trend improving) and rests on price dislocation rather than earnings momentum—consistent with a contrarian retail entry if consumer demand stabilises.
