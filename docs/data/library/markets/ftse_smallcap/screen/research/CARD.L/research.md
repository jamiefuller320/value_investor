# Card Factory plc (CARD.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-10T07:44:55.966442+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Card Factory plc is the UK’s leading specialist greeting-card and celebrations retailer, pursuing a “celebrations group” strategy via store expansion, the Funky Pigeon digital acquisition, and wholesale/international partnerships. FY26 (to 31 January 2026) revenue rose 7.4% to £582.7m, but adjusted PBT fell 15.2% to £56.0m as weak H2 UK footfall hit peak Christmas trading; adjusted free cash flow improved 41.2% to £40.7m, supporting a 5.0p total dividend and a new £15m buyback. The quantitative screen flags a strong buy, though the snapshot carries no populated model metrics (data quality score 0.0), so the valuation hook rests mainly on depressed earnings, progressive cash returns, and leverage of 1.0x (excluding leases) versus a 1.5x target. The central debate is whether cash-rich resilience and strategic diversification offset cyclical UK discretionary weakness, integration risk, and a tangible book value that remains negative once goodwill and intangibles are stripped out.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, the case is a cash-generative UK retailer trading through a consumer downturn while returning surplus capital. Primary filings show FY26 adjusted free cash conversion of 98.9% of adjusted earnings, net debt (excluding leases) of £67.9m, and adjusted leverage of 1.0x—comfortably inside the 2.5x covenant—with £73.7m of undrawn RCF capacity at year-end. Management targets mid-single-digit revenue growth and mid-to-high-single-digit adjusted PBT growth over the medium term, backed by the “Simplify & Scale” programme (£21m of benefits in FY26), 27 net new stores (estate at 1,117), and Funky Pigeon synergies of at least £5m from FY28.

The strong-buy screen signal aligns directionally with a business that remains profitable, dividend-paying (5.0p DPS, +4.2% YoY), and actively repurchasing shares. However, the screening snapshot records zero models passed, zero key metrics populated, and “insufficient_data” on timing—so the quantitative overlay cannot be independently verified from this pack. Qualitative business quality is mixed: vertically integrated sourcing, >99% UK celebration participation, and Q4 physical card market share gains sit against adjusted EPS down 17.3% to 11.8p and cardfactory.co.uk LFL sales down 19.9%. The value angle is therefore “cheap on trough-ish earnings with a cash-return floor,” not “compounding quality at a fair price”—which fits a patient value allocator if FY27 consensus adjusted PBT (~£58.2m per company-compiled estimates) materialises.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Consumer cyclicality:** Management attributes H2 weakness to low consumer confidence, reduced high-street footfall, and fewer store transactions—particularly in peak Christmas trading. FY27 remains sensitive to UK discretionary spending; scenario analysis in the going-concern note assumes permanent sales/profitability reductions.

**Geopolitical and input-cost risk:** Filings explicitly cite Middle East conflict as a potential driver of container rates, energy, and fuel surcharges, plus broader inflation and sentiment uncertainty. ~50% of goods for resale are USD-denominated; FY26 included £4.7m non-cash unrealised FX derivative losses despite hedging.

**Leverage and lease burden:** Adjusted leverage 1.0x on bank debt is manageable (covenant 2.5x), but IFRS 16 lease liabilities total £123.2m; net debt including leases is £191.1m. RCF was upsized to £160m to fund Funky Pigeon, increasing gross bank debt to £86.7m.

**Acquisition and integration:** Funky Pigeon (£25.7m) plus prior Garven/Garlanna deals expand digital and wholesale exposure; digital platform impairment of £3.2m and cardfactory.co.uk LFL -19.9% highlight execution risk before £5m synergies from FY28.

**Governance / capital allocation:** December 2025 AGM consultation recorded >20% votes against share-allotment resolutions; feedback cited dilution from employee share awards (partially addressed via buybacks). One large holder preferred core investment over acquisitions—a tension with the current M&A-led growth path.

**Balance sheet quality:** Goodwill and intangibles of £388.8m exceed tangible equity; Yahoo reports tangible book value of -£35.1m at FY26. Less than 2% of stores are loss-making, but £1.5m net store lease impairment was booked.

**Competitive / structural:** Physical card market share gains in Q4 notwithstanding, online competition and channel shift remain structural headwinds; closure of Getting Personal signals portfolio rationalisation.

No pension deficit is flagged in available filings. Going concern language is supportive; reverse stress testing described covenant/liquidity breach scenarios as “remote” without mitigating actions.
