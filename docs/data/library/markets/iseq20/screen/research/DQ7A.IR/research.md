## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Donegal Investment Group plc presents as a post-divestiture Irish holding company trading below stated book value (P/B ~0.78) with a modest dividend yield (~3%) and a large pool of assets held for sale (~€13.6m of €18.4m total assets per Yahoo FY2025). The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness, dividend, and risk families, driven chiefly by deep value metrics (P/B, EV/EBITDA ~1.8) and an elevated screen-reported FCF yield (~35%). The central debate is whether remaining net asset value will be realised and returned to shareholders, or eroded by continuing operating losses (~€1.2m SG&A on zero reported revenue) and opaque wind-down mechanics. Primary regulatory filings are entirely absent from the research pack, leaving material uncertainty on timing, covenants, and governance.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, the mechanical case is straightforward: the stock screens as statistically cheap against book and enterprise value, passes five of 22 models (Deep Value, FCF Yield, Dividend Growth, Composite Value, Earnings Quality), and sits at an 82% composite score versus 71% sector-relative. Balance-sheet markers support a “liquidation option” framing—FY2025 Yahoo data show zero reported debt, €3.96m cash, and a current ratio above 3x—while the group repurchased ~€5.0m of stock in FY2025, signalling capital-return intent.

Business quality, however, is poor on a continuing-operations basis. Operating revenue fell to zero in FY2024–FY2025 (Yahoo), continuing operations posted losses of €671k (FY2024) and €1.81m (FY2025), and ROE is deeply negative (-18.4% per screen). Profits have been driven by discontinued operations (€2.47m in FY2024; €1.98m in FY2025), consistent with an active portfolio unwind rather than an enduring franchise. The screen’s buy signal therefore rests on asset-backed cheapness and historical cash generation, not on operating earnings power or moat quality—models for Graham quality, Buffett quality, economic moat, Piotroski F-Score (5/9), and financial health all fail. This is a capital-structure / realisation story, not a compounder.
