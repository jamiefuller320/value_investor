## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Glenveagh Properties PLC (GVR.IR) is an Irish residential homebuilder that the quantitative screen rates as a **buy** (7/22 models, 61% composite), driven by cheapness, balance-sheet strength, and risk metrics rather than dividend or deep-value criteria. At roughly 12.4× trailing earnings and 1.6× book, the market prices in cyclical caution despite record FY2025 revenue and a rebound in free cash flow. ## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy signal rests on a coherent value case: Glenveagh passes Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, Quality Value, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Piotroski F-Score (8/9), and Financial Health. Key metrics align with a disciplined cyclical operator trading below growth multiples — P/E 12.4, P/B 1.6, ROE 13.9%, debt/equity 31%, and current ratio 5.9 — while the company fails dividend-oriented and deep-value screens (no dividend, P/B above 1.0, FCF yield 4.4% below the 5% hurdle).

For a value investor, the appeal is a profitable, asset-backed franchise in a structurally undersupplied housing market, with improving scale: Yahoo-sourced revenue rose from €608m (2023) to €926m (2025), and net income from €47m to €108m over the same period. Management has returned capital via buybacks (€75m in FY2025 per Yahoo cash-flow data), shrinking the share count from ~638m (2022) to ~520m (2025), which supports per-share value accretion. Balance-sheet metrics screen as conservative for a developer — net debt of ~€164m against equity of ~€793m — and the Piotroski score signals operational improvement across leverage, liquidity, margins, and asset turnover. The screen’s earnings-growth failure (-10.6% trailing) and weak cash-conversion flag are partly explained by FY2024’s working-capital drag; FY2025’s operating cash flow of €100m and free cash flow of €88m (Yahoo) suggest normalisation, though cyclicality means this is not yet a durable quality compounder by Buffett/Moat criteria.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and market risk:** Glenveagh is a pure-play Irish residential developer. Revenue, margins, and cash flow are highly sensitive to mortgage rates, affordability, planning timelines, and macro sentiment. FY2024’s cash-flow collapse demonstrates how quickly inventory and receivables can absorb reported earnings in a downturn or slowdown.

**Inventory and liquidity:** €838m of inventory on the balance sheet represents the bulk of asset value. Mark-to-market, impairment, or slower sell-through would pressure equity and covenant headroom; primary filings are unavailable to assess impairment policies or site-level exposure.

**Leverage and interest:** Net debt of ~€164m and interest expense of ~€19m are manageable against EBITDA of ~€147m (Yahoo 2025), but refinancing risk rises if volumes or prices weaken. Debt/equity of 31% screens as modest; absolute debt roughly doubled from 2022–2024 before stabilising.

**Governance and capital allocation:** Aggressive buybacks (€146m in 2022, €63m in 2023, €30m in 2024, €75m in 2025 per Yahoo) reduced the share count but consumed cash during periods of negative FCF (2024). Without filing disclosures, buyback timing relative to cycle peaks cannot be assessed.

**Regulatory and policy:** Irish housing policy (planning reform, Help-to-Buy, rent controls, infrastructure levies) can alter demand, margins, and delivery timelines. No RNS or annual-report extracts were available to cite specific regulatory contingencies.

**Screen-identified weaknesses:** No dividend; FCF yield below threshold; earnings growth negative on trailing basis; failed Economic Moat and Earnings Quality models due to ROE below 18% and weak free-cash conversion history.

**Data gap:** Absence of annual reports, half-year results, or trading updates in the filing index prevents verification of segment mix, forward sold units, landbank acreage, or management guidance — a material red flag for deep research, not for the quantitative signal itself.
