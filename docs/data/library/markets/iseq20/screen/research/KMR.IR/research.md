# Kenmare Resources plc (KMR.IR) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T13:02:39.914096+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Kenmare Resources plc operates the long-life Moma titanium minerals mine in Mozambique and ranks among the world’s largest ilmenite and zircon producers. The quantitative screen flags a **buy** on extreme asset cheapness (P/B 0.3, D/E 25%) and balance-sheet resilience, but earnings quality is poor: FY2025 reported a $325m loss after a $301.3m impairment, adjusted loss after tax of $23.7m, and the final dividend was suspended. The investment case rests on a trough-cycle asset backing discount, completion of the $341m WCP A upgrade, a 2026 pivot to “value over volume” (shipments guided above 1.1m tonnes via inventory drawdown), and eventual mineral-sands price recovery. The central debate is whether book value and operating cash flow (~$102m in 2025) support the equity through a prolonged pricing downturn, or whether Mozambique Implementation Agreement (IA) uncertainty, elevated net debt ($158.8m at YE2025; $175.7m at H1 2026), and weak ilmenite markets erode the deep-value thesis.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
Kenmare fits a contrarian value screen rather than a quality compounder. The screen passes four of 22 models—Schloss Low P/B, Deep Value, Composite Value, and Financial Health—driven by P/B 0.30 versus tangible book of ~$815m (Yahoo fallback for balance-sheet detail; filings confirm the impairment-driven equity write-down). Debt remains moderate at 25% D/E with a current ratio of 3.2x, and operating cash flow stayed positive at $102m in 2025 despite a cyclical revenue collapse.

Business quality is asset-led, not earnings-led. Moma supplies ~6% of global titanium feedstocks, holds nine billion tonnes of mineral resources with Nataka representing ~70% of reserves, and has absorbed over $1.5bn of lifetime capital. The WCP A upgrade is substantially complete (~82% of $341m spent by YE2025), with development capex falling to ~$30m in 2026 versus ~$156m in 2025. Management’s 2026 plan—shipments above 1.1m tonnes while producing less than 800,000 tonnes of ilmenite—targets working-capital release from 344,000 tonnes of finished-goods inventory at YE2025; H1 2026 shipments of 555,600 tonnes are on pace for that goal.

For a value investor, the screen’s buy signal aligns with a cyclical trough entry: the market prices the equity below impaired net assets after a non-cash $301.3m write-down tied to weaker long-term price assumptions and IA renewal terms. Recovery optionality comes from zircon price stabilisation (higher Q2 2026 prices per Q1 production report), global supply curtailments tightening ilmenite, and ZrTi by-product monetisation. The screen correctly identifies cheapness and financial headroom; it underweights earnings, FCF (TTM FCF −$85m per screen), and Mozambique fiscal risk—hence a accumulate rather than high-conviction buy overlay.

## FINANCIAL REVIEW
**Annual (FY2025 — primary filing: Publication of 2025 Annual Report, 10 April 2026)**

Revenue and profitability deteriorated sharply in a weak pricing environment. Mineral product revenue was **$312.1m**, down **20%** year-on-year, reflecting a **13%** fall in shipments and a **6%** decline in average realised price to **$338/t** (excluding ZrTi by-product). Reported results were dominated by a **$301.3m impairment** (inclusive of **$100.3m** recognised at H1), driven by lower future revenue projections, uncertain pricing outlook, and updated IA renewal assumptions. **Adjusted EBITDA** (excluding impairment) was **$58.0m**, down **63%**, at a **19%** margin. **Adjusted loss after tax** was **$23.7m** versus **profit after tax of $64.9m** in 2024. The **2025 final dividend was suspended**; interim dividends totalling **$24.1m** were paid during the year.

Operations: HMC production **1,233,300t** (−15%), ilmenite **842,300t** (−17%), shipments **947,900t** (−13%). Total cash operating costs were **$242.7m** (flat YoY), but unit costs rose **11%** to **$242/t** on lower volumes. An additional **~$15m** inventory write-down to net realisable value was flagged for preliminary results.

Balance sheet and cash (annual report for net debt; Yahoo fallback for full balance-sheet line items where filing bodies lack detail): **Net debt $158.8m** at 31 December 2025 versus **$25.0m** at YE2024, driven by **~$156m** WCP A capex and dividend payments. Yahoo reports total debt **$205.6m**, cash **$48.6m**, shareholders’ equity **$814.8m** (down from **$1,161m** in 2024), and reported net loss **$325.0m** (Basic EPS **−$3.64**). Operating cash flow was **$102.0m** (Yahoo; consistent with screen input) versus **$159.8m** in 2024; free cash flow **−$103.1m** after **$205m** capex. Kenmare drew **$120m** on its revolving credit facility and is in discussions with lenders on covenant amendments.

Historical context (Yahoo fallback): revenue peaked at **$526m** (2022) with net income **$206m**; 2024 revenue **$415m** and net income **$64.9m** confirm the cyclical down-leg preceding 2025.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Mozambique IA renewal (regulatory):** The IA governing processing and export fiscal terms expired 21 December 2024; legacy terms remain in force pending renewal. Filings repeatedly warn that proposed renewal terms could raise royalties and VAT working-capital requirements, and that impairment assumptions embed unratified IA amendments. A February 2026 government meeting targeted agreement by 20 March 2026 but missed; Kenmare reserves rights up to **arbitration**.

**Cyclical pricing and impairment (cyclical / accounting):** Mineral sands markets remain oversupplied; FY2025 prices fell throughout the year. The **$301.3m** impairment and **~$15m** inventory NRV adjustment signal management and auditors see carrying values above recoverable amounts under current price decks. ROE **−32.9%** and Piotroski F-Score **3/9** reflect trough earnings quality.

**Leverage and liquidity (leverage / liquidity):** Net debt rose from **$25m** to **$159m** (YE2025) and **$176m** (H1 2026). The company relies on its **$230m RCF** (post-upsize, June 2026 per filing index headline) and covenant resets (net debt/EBITDA reset to **3.0x** for FY2025 per Q4 2025 report). Dividend suspension and negative FCF underscore limited distribution capacity near-term.

**Customer concentration / receivables (customer_concentration / liquidity):** A distressed customer left **$9.3m** unpaid and **65,000t** of contracted volume at risk. Partial recovery progressed in Q1 2026 (Spanish invoice settled; Malaysian shipment pending resale).

**Operational execution (other):** WCP A commissioning ran slower than planned through H1 2026 (average **2,800 tph** vs **3,500 tph** design), constraining HMC and ilmenite output. A fatal security incident at Moma (September 2025) highlights site-risk exposure.

**Governance / M&A overhang (governance):** June 2025 offer discussions with Oryx Global Partners terminated after revised pricing “substantially below” an initial **530p** proposal—evidence of private-market disagreement on fair value, but also potential bid interest at higher levels.

**Going concern:** No going-concern qualification appears in available filing bodies; management states impairment is non-cash and does not affect operations, creditor payment, or covenant compliance—but covenant amendments and RCF upsizing indicate lender engagement on stress scenarios.
