# ANTA Sports Products Limited (2020.HK) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T12:35:05.659170+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ANTA Sports Products is China’s leading multi-brand sportswear group (Anta, Fila, Amer Sports, and a pending ~29% Puma stake), and the quantitative screen rates it a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families at roughly 12.9× P/E, 2.6× P/B, 22.5% ROE, and 3.0% yield. The value hook is a dominant domestic franchise with strong operating cash flow and FCF, trading at a modest multiple versus global peers, while the market discounts China consumer softness and integration risk from aggressive global M&A. The central debate is whether 2025 earnings softness (Yahoo: net income down ~13% YoY) is cyclical and transitory, or the start of margin pressure and balance-sheet strain ahead of the US$1.8bn Puma transaction. Primary HKEX annual and interim filings were not available in the research pack; conviction rests on secondary Yahoo data and news flow, not verified regulatory extracts.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, ANTA offers a rare combination of scale, brand portfolio depth, and cash generative capacity at a sub-15× earnings multiple with a covered dividend. The screen passes 11 of 22 models—including Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Earnings Quality, and Financial Health—reflecting a business that converts revenue into cash (screen TTM FCF ~US$13.8bn; Yahoo FY2025 FCF RMB 18.3bn) while maintaining moderate leverage (D/E ~44%, current ratio ~1.8×).

Revenue has compounded solidly on Yahoo data (RMB 53.7bn in 2022 → RMB 80.2bn in 2025, +~49% over three years), supported by multi-brand diversification that reduces single-brand cyclicality versus pure-play domestic peers. ROE above 20% and a 3% yield suggest the market is not fully pricing franchise durability. The screen fails on deep-value and momentum criteria (P/B above 1.0, Piotroski F-Score 5/9, flat statutory earnings growth), which is consistent with a **quality-at-a-reasonable-price** rather than deep-value entry—appropriate for a platform acquirer building global reach. Sector-relative composite (59% vs 56% absolute) indicates modest cheapness within Hang Seng consumer cyclicals.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**M&A and leverage.** The agreed ~29% Puma stake for US$1.8bn (news, January 2026) will further concentrate capital in equity-method investments and may increase gross debt; integration and board-influence strategy add execution risk without full operational control.

**China consumer cyclicality.** ANTA is consumer cyclical with heavy domestic exposure; macro softness in mainland consumption could pressure same-store growth and inventory turns—2025 earnings decline on Yahoo data may be an early signal.

**Governance and key-person risk.** Anta Brand CEO Xu Yang stepped down in July 2026; Lai Shixian (co-CEO of ANTA Sports) appointed interim brand CEO. Leadership transitions during a global expansion phase warrant scrutiny; no filing-body language on succession or related-party transactions was available.

**Earnings quality.** Piotroski F-Score 5/9 (screen): ROA, leverage, current ratio, and gross margin trends flagged as not improving. Statutory earnings growth is flat to slightly negative (-0.15% per screen inputs).

**Competitive intensity.** Domestic rivalry (Li Ning, etc.) and global players (Nike, Adidas, New Balance, Hoka) pressure Puma and core brands; ANTA’s multi-brand strategy is a response but increases complexity.

**Data and disclosure gap.** Zero indexed HKEX/SEC filing bodies means going-concern, litigation, pension, and contingency disclosures could not be reviewed; this is a material limitation for verify-before-trade.
