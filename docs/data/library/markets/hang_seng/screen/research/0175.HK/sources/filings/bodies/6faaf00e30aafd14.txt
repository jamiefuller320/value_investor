Interim results released the same day showed first-half revenue of 173.6 billion yuan (approximately $25.8 billion), up 15% year-on-year, with core net profit attributable to shareholders of 9.68 billion yuan (approximately $1.4 billion), up 46%, and gross margin rising to 17.9%. Key Elements Li Shufu An Conghui Geely Automobile Gan Jiayue Gui Shengyue Zeekr Zhejiang Geely Holding Group Li Donghui On August 17, Geely Automobile Holdings Limited (0175.HK) announced a seismic management shakeup alongside its 2026 interim results: founder Li Shufu resigned as chairman and executive director effective August 18, receiving the title of lifetime honorary chairman. An Conghui, who has spent nearly 30 years within the Geely system, succeeds him as chairman, while Gan Jiayue takes over as CEO. The move officially marks Geely Automobile's transition from a founder-driven enterprise to a mature corporate governance model led by professional managers. Sweeping Management Overhaul, Major Governance Upgrade According to the company announcement, the reshuffle spans multiple core positions. Beyond Li Shufu stepping down as chairman, Li Donghui resigned as vice chairman but remains an executive director; Gui Shengyue stepped down as CEO, transitioning to vice chairman while retaining his executive director role; and Gan Jiayue was appointed CEO, responsible for day-to-day operations. Speaking via video at the earnings briefing, Li Shufu said An Conghui is an outstanding professional cultivated within the Geely system — a person of both integrity and capability with unwavering commitment — and expressed full confidence in his performance as the new chairman. He emphasized that the automotive industry is a marathon with no finish line, and that corporate succession and value orientation determine a company's capacity for sustainable development. Gui Shengyue characterized the personnel changes as a strategic declaration of organizational evolution for Geely Automobile. He noted that most Chinese private enterprises are now confronting succession challenges, and this adjustment represents Geely Auto Group's manifesto for de-familialization. The company has moved from a founder-driven startup to a mature entity powered by institutional and team-driven governance, with greater transparency, more professional decision-making, and more scientific management going forward. An Conghui, 56, joined Geely Holding Group in 1996. He led the critical integration work following Geely's acquisition of Volvo, propelled Geely Automobile past the one-million-unit annual sales milestone in 2017, and built Zeekr from scratch into a representative brand of China's premium new energy vehicle segment. Following the merger of Zeekr into Geely Automobile late last year, An returned to Geely Holding Group as CEO, overseeing group-wide operations. Notably, Li Shufu has not fully exited the Geely system. He remains chairman of Zhejiang Geely Holding Group and, as the listed company's controlling shareholder, retains influence over major strategic decisions. First-Half Results: Profit Growth Far Outpacing Revenue, Driven by Premiumization and Overseas Expansion Released alongside the management transition was a half-year report that stood out amid industry headwinds. In the first half of 2026, Geely Automobile's total sales reached 1.423 million units, up a modest 1% year-on-year. Total revenue hit 173.6 billion yuan (approximately $25.8 billion), up 15% and a record high, marking six consecutive years of positive growth. Core net profit attributable to shareholders came in at 9.68 billion yuan (approximately $1.4 billion), up 46% — more than triple the revenue growth rate. Gross margin improved 1.6 percentage points to 17.9%, while core net profit per vehicle rose 45% to 6,806 yuan. Zeekr sold 178,000 units in the first half, up 97% year-on-year. Despite accounting for only 12.5% of total volume, Zeekr contributed 31.7% of revenue, with an average transaction price of approximately 350,000 yuan (approximately $51,940) — which the company says now exceeds the transaction prices of comparable BBA (BMW, Benz, Audi) models. Lynk & Co sold 144,000 units, with new energy vehicles rising to 65% of its mix. Geely Galaxy sales approached 520,000 units, ranking among the top three new energy brands globally. The China Star fuel-vehicle lineup sold over 580,000 units, securing the title of China's best-selling domestic-brand fuel passenger car for the tenth consecutive year. The expense side also reflected the realization of economies of scale. Administrative expense ratio fell to 1.7%, and R&D expense ratio dropped to 5.2% — yet absolute R&D spending actually grew 8% to 9.06 billion yuan (approximately $1.3 billion). This means Geely improved efficiency without cutting technology investment. It should be noted that under IFRS accounting standards, profit attributable to owners of the parent was 9.091 billion yuan (approximately $1.3 billion), a slight 2% decline from 9.256 billion yuan (approximately $1.4 billion) in the same period last year. The discrepancy stems primarily from sharp swings in foreign exchange gains and losses: the first half produced a net FX loss of 550 million yuan (approximately $81.6 million), versus a net FX gain of 2.64 billion yuan (approximately $391.8 million) a year earlier. Excluding such non-operating items, core operating profit growth remained robust. Overseas Business Becomes the Biggest Growth Engine If domestic growth was within expectations, the overseas business exploded far beyond forecasts. Geely's overseas sales in the first half reached 474,000 units, up 158% year-on-year, already surpassing the full-year 2025 export total of 420,000 units. Of this, new energy vehicle exports totaled 277,200 units, surging 585% and accounting for 58.5% of total exports. Monthly overseas sales exceeded 100,000 units in both June and July, lifting Geely to third place in China's automaker export rankings. Based on this momentum, Geely raised its full-year 2026 overseas sales target from 640,000 units at the start of the year to 920,000 units, with a stretch goal of one million. On its overseas model, Geely adheres to an asset-light approach, pursuing industrial collaboration with partners including Volvo, Proton, Renault, and Ford. At the earnings briefing, An Conghui detailed the localized production layout: Volvo's European plants will take on production of premium luxury models across multiple Geely-system brands, with production slated to begin in 2028; Proton is expected to achieve 200,000 units in sales this year, with its factory undergoing technical upgrades to expand capacity to 500,000 units; the Spain plant, a joint venture with Ford, also has 500,000 units of capacity; and the Brazil project in partnership with Renault has at least 300,000 units of capacity. As of the first half, Geely had 12 overseas manufacturing plants in operation, with overseas capacity exceeding 650,000 units, planned to rise above 840,000 units by year-end. Its distribution network covers 114 core overseas markets with more than 2,000 offline stores. An Conghui stated unequivocally that Geely's long-term strategy is to generate two-thirds of sales from overseas markets — and localized production is the only path to achieving that goal. Capital Markets Respond Positively, Shareholder Returns Continue to Rise The secondary market gave a positive reception to the leadership change and earnings release. On August 17, Geely Automobile shares rose as much as 6% intraday before closing at HK$18.67, up 4.77%, with a total market capitalization of HK$201.36 billion (approximately $25.7 billion). Among Chinese passenger vehicle stocks listed on A-shares and H-shares, Geely was the only company to achieve positive share price growth in the first half of this year. Forty-six institutions increased or initiated positions, with Citi, Morgan Stanley, and JPMorgan all issuing positive ratings. On shareholder returns, Geely repurchased HK$1.89 billion (approximately $240.3 million) worth of shares in the first half. A dividend of HK$0.5 per share was paid at the end of July, up 51.5% year-on-year, totaling HK$5.39 billion (approximately $687.0 million) — marking five consecutive years with a core profit payout ratio above 30%. As of end-June, the company's cash reserves stood at 69.56 billion yuan (approximately $10.3 billion), a historical high. Gui Shengyue candidly acknowledged at the earnings briefing that domestic total sales are currently declining by double digits year-on-year, chip and bulk commodity prices are soaring, and competition among automakers is exceptionally brutal. While the results are impressive, he said, they are not yet stunning. However, he emphasized that backed by the system capabilities of the "One Geely" strategy, these results are sustainable over the long term with significant room for improvement. For the new management team, the core tests in the second half are clear: whether the 920,000-unit overseas target can be delivered, how the Zeekr 9X Guanghui and other premium models perform post-launch, and how AI technology investment translates into per-vehicle profit. An Conghui and Gan Jiayue inherit a Geely that is financially healthy, brand-focused, and expanding rapidly overseas. But with the founder no longer at the daily helm, whether this multi-million-unit automotive group can sustain its growth momentum remains a question only time will answer.
