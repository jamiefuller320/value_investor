Corporate Profile
ʮ̡ᔊʧ
Sunny Optical Technology (Group) Company Limited (the “Company ” 
or “Sunny Optical Technology ”, together with its subsidiaries, the 
“Group”) (Stock Code: 2382.HK) is a global leading manufacturer of 
integrated optical products with large-scale and a globally competitive 
provider of intelligent optical system solutions (whole device). The 
Group is engaged in the design, research and development ( “R&D”), 
production and sales of integrated optical products and intelligent 
optical system solutions (whole device). By deeply integrating cutting-
edge technologies such as optics, electronics, algorithms, software, 
mechanics, and artificial intelligence ( “AI”), the Group empowers global 
clients to achieve innovative breakthroughs and value growth across 
application fields including smartphones, vehicles, extended reality 
(“XR”), pan Internet of things ( “Pan-IoT ”) and precision optics.

MARKET REVIEW
In 2025, the global economy progressed with twists and turns amid 
the multiple factors of trade policy uncertainty, reshaping of the 
geopolitical landscape, and technological revolutions such as AI. 
International observations point out that global economic growth 
is likely to remain moderate in the near term. Facing this complex 
macro environment, building resilience in advance and focusing on 
endogenous drivers will be key to seizing future cycles. Throughout 
this process, AI-driven investment in upgrading entire industrial chains, 
coupled with the structural recovery of demand in key sectors such 
as consumer electronics, has become an essential force supporting 
resilience. At the same time, China ’s economy has demonstrated 
strong buffering capacity, with policy guidance and technological 
innovation continuously infusing new momentum into the real 
economy, thereby laying a solid foundation for the development of the 
high-end manufacturing sector in which the Group operates.
In 2025, the global smartphone market experienced moderate 
growth, with annual shipments reaching approximately 1.26 billion 
units, representing a year-on-year increase of about 2.0% (source: 
International Data Corporation ( “IDC”)). Despite the sluggish overall 
market growth, a pronounced trend of internal structural upgrades was 
evident. Various brands continued to push into the premium segment, 
and innovations in form-factors such as foldable screens continued to 
grow. In 2025, the global sales volume of smartphones priced above 
United States dollars ( “USD”) 600 recorded a year-on-year increase of 
approximately 8.8% (source: IDC). This trend of “stable total volume 
with structural upgrades ” has driven industry-chain resources to 
concentrate further towards leading companies with technological 
advantages.

The steady expansion of the global automobile market has created 
a vast demand base for vehicle camera solutions, and China, as the 
core hub of the global automobile industry, serves as a key engine 
driving this growth. In recent years, global vehicle sales have climbed 
steadily, reaching approximately 95.6 million units in 2025. Within 
this total, new energy vehicles emerged as the primary growth driver, 
with sales exceeding 23.37 million units and the market penetration 
rising steadily (source: Frost & Sullivan). In 2025, the automobile 
market officially entered the era of intelligent competition, marked by 
deep integration of intelligent perception and smart cockpits, driving 
the transformation of vehicle camera solutions from “basic quantity 
coverage ” towards “advanced performance enhancement ”. In the 
intelligent perception segment, the certification for China ’s first batch 
of level 3 autonomous driving models was officially implemented 
and issued. This milestone event further marks the substantive 
development of intelligent connected vehicles transitioning from the 
assisted-driving phase into the stage of high-level autonomous driving. 
Against this backdrop of significantly increasing perception demands 
for high-level autonomy, vehicle cameras are rapidly evolving towards 
higher resolution, wider dynamic range, and improved low-light 
performance to support more stable algorithm input. Moreover, high-
level autonomous driving systems place higher demands for long-
range detection, 3D contour reconstruction, and complex operating 
conditions. Consequently, the industry has progressively adopted 
multi-sensor fusion solutions combining “vision and light detection and 
ranging ( “LiDAR ”)” to improve safety redundancy in nighttime, backlit, 
and dense mixed-traffic scenarios. With the implementation of L3 
and above autonomous driving, LiDAR will become an indispensable 
perception unit for high-precision environmental sensing and is 
expected to evolve from an emerging feature into a core standard 
component of intelligent driving systems. In the smart cockpit 
segment, the popularization of driver monitoring systems ( “DMS”) 
and occupant monitoring systems ( “OMS”) has further expanded the 
application scope of in-cabin vision technology, extending from basic 
monitoring to multifunctional capabilities such as identity recognition, 
attention and status detection, as well as health and safety alerts. 
This progression drives holistic optimization of the cabin interaction 
experience across both safety and comfort dimensions.

In 2025, the XR market exhibited significant internal segmentation. 
Mixed reality ( “MR”) device sales continued to decline due to a 
lack of new content and users, with global shipments amounting to 
approximately 4.97 million units, representing a year-on-year decline 
of 34.3% (source: Wellsenn XR). In sharp contrast, empowered by 
large AI models, smart glasses evolved from simple display tools 
into personal intelligent assistants, significantly boosting market 
vitality. The global shipments amounted to approximately 7.66 million 
units, representing a year-on-year increase of approximately 400.7% 
(source: Wellsenn XR). In addition, the pursuit of display quality, 
light-weight design, and intelligent interaction has driven advanced 
optical solutions such as optical waveguide technology to become 
the directions in R&D and application for augmented reality ( “AR”) 
glasses. Global technology giants and innovative enterprises continue 
to increase investment in R&D and production capacity. This not

3.1 Basis of preparation of consolidated financial

The consolidated financial statements have been prepared 
in accordance with HKFRS Accounting Standards as issued 
by the HKICPA. For the purpose of preparation of the 
consolidated financial statements, information is considered 
material if such information is reasonably expected to influence 
decisions made by primary users. In addition, the consolidated 
financial statements include applicable disclosures required 
by the Rules Governing the Listing of Securities on The Stock 
Exchange of Hong Kong Limited ( “Listing Rules ”) and by the 
Hong Kong Companies Ordinance.
The directors of the Company have, at the time of approving 
the consolidated financial statements, a reasonable 
expectation that the Group has adequate resources to 
continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. 
Thus they continue to adopt the going concern basis of 
accounting in preparing the consolidated financial statements.
3.2 Material accounting policy information
Basis of consolidation
The consolidated financial statements incorporate the financial 
statements of the Company and entities controlled by the 
Company and its subsidiaries. Control is achieved when the 
Company:
• has power over the investee;
• is exposed, or has rights, to variable returns from its 
involvement with the investee; and
• has the ability to use its power to affect its returns.

Cash Flow Interest Rate Risk
The Group ’s cash flow interest rate risk is primarily related to variable 
rates applicable to bank borrowings. The Management will review the 
proportion of borrowings in fixed and variable rates and ensure they 
are within reasonable range. Therefore, any future variations in interest 
rates will not have any significant impact on the results of the Group.
Liquidity Risk
The Group manages liquidity risk by maintaining an adequate level 
of cash and cash equivalents through continuously monitoring the 
forecast and actual cash flows and matching them with the maturity 
profiles of financial assets and liabilities.

For the year ended 31 December 2025, other than the transfer of 
Shanghai OmniLight ’s entire equity interests in return for equity 
interests of Goertek Optical and further subscription for equity 
interests in Goertek Optical, there were no other material acquisitions 
and disposals of subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures of the

FINAL DIVIDEND
For the year ended 31 December 2025, the Board proposed a final 
dividend of HKD120.60 cents per Share (equivalent to approximately 
RMB105.90 cents per Share), with a dividend rate of approximately 
25.0% of the profit for the year attributable to owners of the Company. 
The Board confirms that this final dividend reflects an increase in 
the target payout ratio from 20.0% to 25.0% of profit for the year 
attributable to owners of the Company (as compared to the previous 
corresponding period), primarily to improve Shareholder returns. The 
final dividend payable on 26 June 2026 is subject to the approval of 
the Shareholders at the forthcoming AGM of the Company to be held 
on 27 May 2026.
DIVIDEND POLICY
The Directors consider sustainable returns to the Shareholders to be 
one of the main objectives pursued by the Group. The basic policy 
is to pay final dividends in each financial year. Retained profits are 
effectively used by the Board to fund the future development of the 
Group.
According to the dividend policy of the Company (the “Dividend 
Policy ”), the Directors currently intend to recommend a distribution 
to all the Shareholders in an amount representing approximately 
20% to 30% of the profit for the year attributable to owners of the 
Company in respect of each financial year by way of dividends. 
The recommendation for dividends is subject to the discretion of 
the Board. According to the Dividend Policy, the Board takes into 
account the following factors when considering the declaration, 
recommendation and payment of dividends:
• the Company ’s cash position and distributable reserves;

Investments in associates (Continued)
An investment in an associate is accounted for using the 
equity method from the date on which the investee becomes 
an associate. On acquisition of the investment in an associate, 
any excess of the cost of the investment over the Group ’s 
share of the net fair value of the identifiable assets and 
liabilities of the investee is recognised as goodwill, which 
is included within the carrying amount of the investment. 
Any excess of the Group ’s share of the net fair value of 
the identifiable assets and liabilities over the cost of the 
investment, after reassessment, is recognised immediately in 
profit or loss in the period in which the investment is acquired.
The Group assesses whether there is an objective evidence 
that the interest in an associate may be impaired. When any 
objective evidence exists, the entire carrying amount of the 
investment (including goodwill) is tested for impairment in 
accordance with HKAS 36 as a single asset by comparing 
its recoverable amount (higher of value in use and fair 
value less costs of disposal) with its carrying amount. Any 
impairment loss recognised is not allocated to any asset, 
including goodwill, that forms part of the carrying amount 
of the investment. Any reversal of that impairment loss is 
recognised in accordance with HKAS 36 to the extent that the 
recoverable amount of the investment subsequently increases.

enters into the lease is different to that of the Group and 
whether the lease benefit from a guarantee from the Group.
