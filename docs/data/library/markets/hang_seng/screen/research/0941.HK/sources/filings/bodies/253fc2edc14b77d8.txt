Key Elements China Mobile computing services intelligent services AIDC Jiutian model token operations Mobile Cloud 5G base stations AI computing services MoMA platform On August 13, China Mobile Limited (0941.HK) released its 2026 interim results. Operating revenue for the first half came in at 538 billion yuan (approximately $79.8 billion), down 1.1% year-on-year, while profit attributable to shareholders fell 6.3% to 78.9 billion yuan (approximately $11.7 billion). After excluding external factors such as VAT category policy adjustments, revenue achieved positive growth on a like-for-like basis. Behind the earnings pressure lies a clear picture of accelerating transition from legacy to new growth engines. Telecommunications services — the company's core business — generated revenue of 350.42 billion yuan (approximately $52.0 billion) in the first half, down 5.7% year-on-year. Meanwhile, computing services and intelligent services combined accounted for 22.6% of core revenue, up 2.2 percentage points year-on-year, crossing the 20% threshold for the first time. The subscriber base remained resilient. As of end-June, mobile customers reached 1.011 billion, with a net addition of 5.88 million; 5G network customers totaled 687 million, up 44.74 million in the half; broadband customers reached 337 million, up 7.7 million; and IoT connections stood at 1.511 billion, up 29.58 million. Mobile data traffic grew 16.0% year-on-year, a notable acceleration from prior periods. Computing segment becomes core growth engine Computing services revenue reached 52.9 billion yuan (approximately $7.8 billion) in the first half, up 14.0% year-on-year, lifting its share of core revenue to 11.7%. Within this segment, data center revenue was 19 billion yuan (approximately $2.8 billion), up 13.2%; AIDC (AI data center) revenue surged 486.1% year-on-year, with cumulative contracted capacity reaching 7GW and 5GW of new contracts signed in the half. AI computing services revenue totaled 5.3 billion yuan (approximately $786 million), growing 130.1% and contributing 46.1% of the incremental computing services revenue. Mobile Cloud's public cloud revenue entered the top three in China's domestic market for the first time, marking a historic breakthrough. Intelligent services revenue was 49.34 billion yuan (approximately $7.3 billion) in the first half, up 0.9% year-on-year, representing 10.9% of core revenue. Jiutian model revenue grew 12.8%, with over 50 industry-specific large models launched. Fintech revenue surged 116.6% year-on-year. Digital culture revenue rose 12.5%, with Migu Video's AI-powered smart viewing features recording 360 million cumulative uses during the World Cup period. Capex tilts toward computing and intelligence To support its transformation, China Mobile optimized its capital expenditure structure and adopted a flexible capex mechanism. First-half capex was 61 billion yuan (approximately $9.0 billion), up 4.5% year-on-year. Telecom network spending fell 9.8%, while computing network spending rose 71.3% and intelligent network spending jumped 85.2%, with resources clearly tilting toward computing and AI. On network infrastructure, China Mobile had deployed 2.94 million 5G base stations by end-June, with gigabit network coverage reaching 560 million households. Average uplink and downlink speeds on dedicated high-speed rail networks improved by over 15%, and the autonomous network achieved L4 capability across 21 high-value scenarios. The company also co-released the industry's first batch of 5G-A passive IoT solutions, and its 6G international standard proposals rank among the top tier of global operators. International markets emerged as a new growth driver. First-half international business revenue reached 18.2 billion yuan (approximately $2.7 billion), up 30.1% year-on-year. Points of presence (POPs) totaled 463, with international transmission capacity at 437Tbps. Inbound roaming traffic grew 59.9%, and the number of Chinese enterprises served overseas increased 19.6% from end-2025. Token-based operating strategy in full swing In 2026, China Mobile is advancing a token-based operating strategy as the core operational unit of the AI era. The initiative spans consumer and enterprise scenarios, leveraging Mobile Cloud and AIDC computing infrastructure to build an efficient "token factory," with the MoMA platform providing unified computing resource scheduling to ensure inference performance. The company has established a token office to coordinate end-to-end token innovation. Responding to investor questions, China Mobile said first-half performance met expectations and demonstrated strong resilience and vitality. The company highlighted three positive developments: mobile customer net additions of 5.88 million, mobile data customer net additions of 11.45 million, and individual customer net additions of 1.14 million, with customer churn continuing to decline; mobile traffic growth of 16% — a doubling of the growth rate — with AI emerging as a significant new source of mobile network traffic; and computing services revenue growth of 14%, including AIDC revenue up more than fourfold and AI computing services achieving triple-digit growth. Notably, despite the short-term decline in book profit, cash flow performance was exceptional: first-half operating cash inflow reached 114.9 billion yuan (approximately $17.0 billion), up 37.0% year-on-year, while free cash flow hit 53.9 billion yuan (approximately $8.0 billion), up 111.6%, providing ample funding for future technology R&D, network construction, and business expansion. Industry analysts view the interim report as reflecting "short-term adjustment, long-term improvement." The phased pressure on traditional telecom services is fundamentally an inevitable stage in operators' transition from "demographic dividend" to "digital dividend." China Mobile stated that the ICT industry has moved from the mobile communications era and mobile internet era into the mobile intelligence era, and the company will accelerate its upgrade from a communications operator to a technology services enterprise, advancing the integrated deployment of telecom, computing, and intelligent networks while enhancing the converged innovation of all three service categories.
