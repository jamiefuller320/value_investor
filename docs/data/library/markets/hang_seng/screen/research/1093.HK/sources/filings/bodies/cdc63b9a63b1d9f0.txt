CSPC Pharmaceutical Group reported strong interim results for the six months ended 30 June 2026, driven mainly by robust growth in its finished drugs segment. Group revenue rose 40.1% year-on-year to RMB18.59 billion, while reported profit attributable to shareholders surged 139.2% to RMB6.09 billion, and underlying profit jumped 165.8%, reflecting improved operational efficiency and a more profitable product mix. Basic earnings per share more than doubled to RMB53.45 cents, enabling the board to raise the interim dividend by 28.6% to HK18 cents per share. The company also deployed HK$321 million to buy back over 46 million shares under its share award scheme, signalling confidence in its outlook and using equity incentives to align employees with its innovation-led, international growth strategy. The performance highlights the growing contribution of higher-margin finished drugs, which offset declines in bulk products and functional food sales. Management’s continued emphasis on R&D and talent investment under its dual-engine strategy reinforces CSPC’s competitive position in the pharmaceutical sector and suggests further strengthening of its earnings profile and shareholder returns over the medium term. More about CSPC Pharmaceutical Group CSPC Pharmaceutical Group is a Hong Kong–incorporated, comprehensive pharmaceutical enterprise that integrates research and development, manufacturing, and sales. With a strategic focus on innovation and internationalisation, the group targets unmet clinical needs and aims to advance clinical treatment through technology-driven drug development, positioning itself as a leading innovator in China’s pharmaceutical industry. The company emphasises a dual-engine strategy to strengthen core competitiveness, continually increasing R&D investment and building specialist teams. Its portfolio spans finished drugs, bulk pharmaceutical products, and functional foods, with innovation-oriented products serving as a key growth driver and underpinning its long-term sustainable development ambitions in global healthcare markets.
