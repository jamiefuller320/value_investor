CNOOC Limited, the subsidiary listed in Hong Kong and Shanghai of Chinese state oil group China National Offshore Oil Corporation, published interim results on August 26, 2026 showing record net profit and output for the first half of 2026. The performance comes as oil prices rose sharply during the period, a rally several media outlets have linked to the escalation of the conflict in the Middle East and disruptions to shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. The company itself, however, only refers to volatile oil markets, without confirming this causal link picked up by the press. Advertisement A result driven by prices and volumes Net profit attributable to shareholders of CNOOC Limited rose sharply year on year, according to the company, driven both by a higher realized selling price for oil and by growth in net hydrocarbon production, in China as well as internationally. Revenue from oil and gas sales reached a record level for a first half, the company said. A broader total revenue figure was also cited by one media outlet but was not found in the company's own communications reviewed for this report — a distinction between hydrocarbon sales and consolidated revenue that should be kept in mind. Rising costs, unchanged targets The all-in production cost rose over the course of the half, an upward trajectory the company itself documents quarter after quarter while describing it as competitive against peers. This increase comes as CNOOC Limited kept its annual production target and its capital expenditure envelope unchanged. The group also confirmed a capital spending budget committed as early as the first quarter, suggesting an acceleration in spending over the remainder of 2026. Record dividend and governance The board of directors, chaired by Zhang Chuanjiang, approved an interim dividend described as a record for the total amount distributed over the period, with a payout ratio disclosed by the company itself. The dividend, paid in Hong Kong dollars, is described as "tax inclusive," meaning the announced amount is gross of any withholding tax. The characterization of the per-share dividend as the highest since the company's initial public offering, echoed by part of the press, does not exactly match the wording of the company's statement, which attaches the "record" label to the total amount distributed rather than to the per-share figure. Huang Yongzhang has served as chief executive officer and executive president of CNOOC Limited since at least the first quarter of 2026, with no governance change identified over the period. Exploration activity continues in China On the exploration front, CNOOC Limited announced several discoveries and appraisals of oil and gas structures in China during the half. These announcements form part of the group's reserve-replacement strategy, as net hydrocarbon production continued to grow over the period, both in China and internationally. No volume or precise location was disclosed for these discoveries in the documents reviewed.
