# Las Vegas Sands Corp. (LVS) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:03:14.729255+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Las Vegas Sands is a post-Vegas-sale pure play on premium integrated resorts in Macao and Singapore, with Marina Bay Sands delivering exceptional cash generation while Macao faces intensifying competition and margin pressure despite volume recovery. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on FCF yield (7.2%), quality/dividend metrics, and a Piotroski F-Score of 8/9, though reported ROE (1.2%) and P/B (25.5x) are distorted by aggressive treasury-stock accumulation. The central debate is whether Singapore strength, concession-driven asset upgrades, and heavy buybacks ($1.54bn in H1 2026 alone) justify accumulation after a Q2 2026 earnings miss driven by low VIP rolling-chip hold and higher Macao reinvestment costs, against an ~$8bn Singapore expansion and ~$4.5bn Macao investment plan that will absorb capital through 2030–2032.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, LVS offers exposure to two of Asia’s most defensible gaming/resort franchises at a mid-teens P/E (17.9x) with a 2.6% dividend yield and 7.2% FCF yield — metrics that pass FCF Yield, Neff PEGY (0.07), Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Piotroski F-Score, and Earnings Quality screens. Business quality is underpinned by scale assets on the Cotai Strip and Marina Bay Sands, a mass-market-focused model with higher-margin non-gaming revenue (rooms, malls, MICE), and a 10-year Macao concession through December 2032 plus Singapore duopoly protection until at least 2031.

Filings confirm the operational split: in FY2025, Macao contributed 44% and Singapore 56% of consolidated adjusted property EBITDA (10-K filed 6 Feb 2026). Singapore H1 2026 adjusted property EBITDA rose 7.6% on 11.7% net gaming revenue growth; Macao H1 revenues rose 11.1% but adjusted property EBITDA fell 3.5% as patron reinvestment and payroll costs rose in a competitive market (Q2 2026 10-Q). Capital return is a deliberate part of the thesis — H1 2026 saw $1.54bn of share repurchases (28.1m shares) and $400m of dividends, with a $0.30 quarterly dividend declared for August 2026 — while management cites $3.38bn unrestricted cash and $4.26bn of undrawn credit capacity (Q2 2026 10-Q).

The screen’s buy signal is directionally supported by cash-generative assets and shareholder returns, but fails on traditional deep-value and moat screens (Graham Defensive, Deep Value, Economic Moat among 14 failed models), and the headline -19.8% growth metric reflects recent earnings volatility rather than structural decline. Conviction is moderate: the asset base is high quality, but near-term earnings are hold-sensitive and capex-heavy.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and licence:** Macao concession expires 31 Dec 2032; gaming assets revert to the government without compensation on expiry or termination (FY2025 10-K). Macao requires ~$4.47bn of concession-mandated investment by Dec 2032, subject to annual government approval of execution plans. Singapore Marina Bay Sands expansion (~$8.0bn total; ~$2.5bn incurred at 31 Dec 2025) must complete by Jul 2029 (government approval needed for extensions). Marina Bay Sands casino licence expires April 2028; renewal not assured (FY2024 10-K Note 9). Macao shareholder dividend tax agreement (substituting for 12% dividend tax) was requested for extension through 2027 — “no assurance such agreement will be granted” (Q2 2026 10-Q).

**China/Macau geopolitical:** FY2025 10-K flags material dependence on Macao operations and risk that evolving PRC oversight of businesses in Macao/Hong Kong could adversely affect operations and securities value.

**Competitive and cyclical:** Macao faces six concessionaires with significant new Cotai capacity; H1 2026 filings cite a “competitive operating environment” with rising payroll and patron reinvestment despite volume growth. Gaming revenues remain sensitive to travel disruption, VIP hold volatility (evident in Q2 2026), and discretionary spending cycles.

**Leverage and capex:** Gross debt ~$13.7bn at 30 Jun 2026 against stockholders’ equity of $581m; simultaneous $8bn Singapore build, Macao concession capex, and aggressive buybacks create refinancing and free-cash-flow allocation risk. Singapore term facilities begin elevated amortisation from H2 2025 (FY2024 10-K Note 12).

**Governance:** FY2025 10-K lists principal stockholder interests potentially diverging from minority holders; several directors also serve on Sands China Ltd., creating conflict risk.

**Contingencies:** Ongoing Nassau Coliseum lease litigation in New York after abandoning the casino-licence pursuit (April 2025, FY2025 10-K); $1.26bn seller-financing loan receivable from former Las Vegas operations (collected in H1 2026 per balance sheet movement). Credit-loss provision on casino receivables rose to $237m at 30 Jun 2026 from $225m at year-end.

**Pension:** No material defined-benefit pension exposure identified in available filing extracts.
