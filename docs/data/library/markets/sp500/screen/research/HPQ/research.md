# HP Inc. (HPQ) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:54:12.393011+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
HP Inc. (HPQ) screens as a strong buy on deep value, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (13/22 models; composite 98%), anchored by a sub-10x P/E, ~4.9% yield and roughly $3.5bn of trailing free cash flow against a cyclical PC and print franchise that still generates substantial cash. The investment case rests on capital return (dividends plus an active buyback programme with ~$8.0bn authorisation remaining), a Personal Systems recovery led by commercial PCs and AI-enabled devices, and a Printing supplies engine that remains highly profitable despite secular headwinds. The central debate is whether near-term margin pressure from rising memory and storage costs, ongoing print decline and heavy restructuring charges (Fiscal 2026 Plan) are transient, or mark a lower structural earnings base than the screen implies. Filing coverage is incomplete for annual statement bodies (truncated 10-K extracts), so FY2025/FY2024 annual comparisons rely partly on Yahoo fallback data.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags HPQ across all four passed families — cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk — with a 98% composite score, sector-relative 99% ranking, P/E of 9.1, earnings yield of ~11%, EV/EBITDA of 6.5, dividend yield of 4.9% and FCF of ~$3.45bn. That profile fits a classic value setup: a mature, cash-generative hardware name trading at a discount to earnings power while returning cash to shareholders.

Business quality is mixed but adequate for a value holder. Personal Systems (~71% of H1 FY2026 revenue) is benefiting from commercial refresh and AI PC positioning; Printing (~29%) still delivers high segment margins (~18% earnings-before-tax margin in H1 FY2026) but faces well-documented secular pressure on hardware and supplies. HP’s scale, channel reach and supplies annuity partially offset commoditisation risk. Capital allocation supports the screen: FY2025 dividends of ~$1.09bn and buybacks of ~$850m (Yahoo fallback), with H1 FY2026 cash dividends of ~$1.10 per share and 18.3m shares repurchased in the six months to April 2026 (Q2 FY2026 10-Q).

The screen’s negative P/B (-164) reflects negative book equity from cumulative repurchases, not operational insolvency — a known accounting distortion in heavily bought-back names. For a value investor, the hook is cash yield and earnings power at a cyclical trough-plus-recovery inflection, not asset backing. Conviction is tempered by earnings downgrades tied to component inflation and print mix, but the quantitative signal is directionally consistent with a business that still converts revenue to cash through the cycle.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and cost risks (10-K/10-Q risk-factor language)**
- Personal Systems faces “significant inflationary trends in memory and storage costs” and potential inability to pass through component inflation, explicitly cited in FY2025 10-K risk factors.
- Competitive pricing pressure in PCs and print, channel inventory distortion and uneven quarter-end sales patterns complicate forecasting.

**Secular print decline**
- FY2025 10-K cites decreased demand for printing from digitisation, hybrid work, generic/refill supplies and shift to big-tank printers (lower lifetime supplies profitability). Supplies revenue was essentially flat YoY in H1 FY2026 ($5,553m vs $5,557m).

**Restructuring and pension**
- Fiscal 2026 Plan restructuring: $491m charges in H1 FY2026; $0.5bn cumulative plan cost to date (Q2 FY2026 10-Q note).
- Enhanced Early Retirement programme triggered Special Termination Benefit charges of $280m (pension) plus $60m (healthcare) in Q2 FY2026 — a non-recurring but real cash/pension-plan draw.

**Governance and litigation**
- Ongoing shareholder derivative actions (Franklin, Lovoi) related to historical print supplies disclosures remain stayed; legacy securities litigation referenced in Q2 FY2026 10-Q note.
- Controller transition: 8-K (25 Jun 2025) records Stephanie Liebman’s departure and appointment of Manpreet S. Grewal as Chief Accounting Officer — routine but worth monitoring during a restructuring cycle.
- Gross unrecognized tax benefits of $822m as of 30 Apr 2026 ($650m would affect effective rate); IRS audit of 2018–2019 returns ongoing.

**Balance sheet / capital structure**
- Persistent stockholders’ deficit from aggressive buybacks — not a covenant breach (HP reported compliance with revolving credit facility covenants as of 30 Apr 2026), but limits P/B-based metrics and raises questions if FCF compresses further.
- Total debt ~$8.9bn against ~$3.7bn cash; net debt manageable but buybacks compete with component-cost headwinds.
- Credit-rating maintenance explicitly flagged as a risk factor in filings.

**Competitive / strategic**
- AI PC investment may cannibalise traditional PC margins before returns materialise; AI regulatory and product-risk language is extensive in FY2025 10-K.
- Supplies competition from imitation cartridges and third-party sellers remains a structural threat to the highest-margin business line.
