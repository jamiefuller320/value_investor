# Everest Group, Ltd. (EG) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:33:31.217384+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Everest Group, Ltd. (NYSE: EG) is a Bermuda-domiciled global property-and-casualty reinsurer and insurer undergoing a deliberate pivot toward reinsurance treaty and global wholesale/specialty, while divesting commercial retail books. The quantitative screen rates EG a **strong buy** (13/22 models, 89% composite): P/E 7.6, P/B 1.0, yield 2.1%, ROE 13.8%, and debt/equity 23%, with passes across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families. Filings show a strengthened balance sheet—FY2025 shareholders’ equity of **$15.5bn** and total assets of **$62.5bn** (10-K)—and a sharp Q1 CY2026 earnings inflection (**net income $653m**, diluted EPS **$16.21** vs $210m / $4.90 a year earlier; 10-Q). The valuation hook is a franchise-rated A+ trading near book with double-digit ROE and active capital return, against a market that appears to discount catastrophe, reserve, and rating-agency uncertainty. The central debate is whether the low multiple reflects a durable underwriting/investment compounder after portfolio simplification, or a cyclical peak in investment income and one-off divestiture gains masking prior-year loss development and event volatility.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, EG aligns unusually well with classical screens and with observable business quality in the primary filings. The screen passes Graham Enterprising, Schloss Low P/B, earnings yield, FCF yield, acquirer’s multiple, and composite value—consistent with a name trading at **~1.0× book** and **~7.6× earnings** despite **13.8% ROE** and a **2.1% dividend**. That is rare in financials without obvious distress; the 10-K affirms scale (FY2025 gross written premiums **$17.7bn**, ~72% reinsurance), A+ financial strength ratings across operating subsidiaries, and no single customer above **3.6%** of GWP.

Business quality is improving on disclosed metrics, not just on multiples. Q1 CY2026 (10-Q) delivered **underwriting gain $316m** versus an **$(104)m** underwriting loss in Q1 CY2025, with a combined ratio of roughly **91%** vs **~103%** in the prior-year quarter (derived from premiums earned and incurred losses/commissions/expenses in the filing). Catastrophe losses fell to **$130m** (Middle East conflict, winter storms) from **$534m** (Southern California wildfires **$512m**). **Net investment income** rose to **$567m** from **$491m**, supporting the screen’s earnings-quality and FCF-yield passes. Capital allocation reinforces the value case: Q1 CY2026 treasury share purchases of **$330m** and a **$2.00** quarterly dividend; FY2025 buybacks of **$797m** per Yahoo `financials_annual.json` (filing bodies for the 10-K income/cash-flow statements were truncated and did not contain those tables).

The strategic repositioning—AIG renewal-rights sale (~**$2bn** estimated retail GWP), adverse development cover (ADC) up to **$1.2bn**, and a new three-segment structure from 1 January 2026—targets a cleaner, reinsurance-weighted earnings stream better matched to EG’s historical edge. If underwriting discipline holds and investment income normalises rather than collapses, the screen’s strong-buy signal looks fundamentally supported rather than purely statistical.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Catastrophe and reserve risk (primary filing language).** Safe-harbour and risk-factor text across 10-K/10-Q filings emphasises that cat losses may exceed projections, reserves may prove insufficient (including social inflation), and prior-year adverse development may recur—with explicit caution that such development could materially affect net income. Q1 CY2026 incurred **$58m** from Middle East conflict exposure; filings note ongoing uncertainty. The October 2025 ADC (up to **$1.2bn** on North American insurance/other reserves of **~$5.4bn** at inception) is a tangible signal that management and reinsurers saw meaningful tail risk in legacy books.

**Ratings.** All major operating subsidiaries remain **A+**, but A.M. Best, S&P, and Moody’s moved outlooks to **negative** in late 2025 (FY2025 10-K)—a red flag for a reinsurer whose franchise value depends on ratings.

**Strategic/execution risk.** The AIG renewal-rights transaction includes earn-back provisions (up to **$70m** reimbursement if renewal retention falls below 80% of 2025 premiums). EU closing completed December 2025; Canada sale to Wawanesa (**C$410m**, anticipated H2 2026) adds further transition complexity. Q1 CY2026 recognised **$81m** net transaction expenses related to the AIG deal.

**Concentration and governance.** Top two brokers (Marsh **~22%**, Aon **~19%** of GWP; FY2025 10-K) create distribution concentration. Bermuda **Insurance Amendment Act 2025** (effective January 2026) may impose new group supervision, capital, and approval requirements on the holding company—impact still being evaluated per 10-K.

**Legacy liabilities.** Asbestos/environmental reserves are small (**0.6%** of total reserves; FY2025 10-K) but inherently uncertain; retrocession cover on Mt. McKinley liabilities caps at **$450m**.

**No going-concern flag** appears in available extracts; filings instead stress liquidity, dividend upstream dependence on subsidiaries, and market-value sensitivity of the **~$45bn** investment portfolio.
