# CDW Corporation (CDW) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:16:02.337586+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
CDW is a leading US-centric IT solutions reseller (Fortune 500, S&P 500) serving 250,000+ business, government, education, and healthcare customers across the US, UK, and Canada. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cheapness, dividend growth, GARP, and risk metrics (P/E ~15.8x, FCF yield ~5.2%, dividend yield ~1.9%, ROE ~44%), suggesting the market prices a cyclical distributor at a discount to cash-generative quality. FY2025 net sales re-accelerated to $22.4bn (+6.8% YoY per 10-K), with software and services mix rising, but Q1 2026 showed a YoY step-down and the shares fell sharply despite a sales beat — highlighting the central debate: whether AI-driven infrastructure demand and recurring software/services can offset enterprise IT budget caution and margin pressure. Leverage (~$5.8bn total debt at end-2024), vendor concentration, and an active DOJ False Claims Act inquiry into E-Rate bidding add medium regulatory and cyclical overhangs not fully captured by value screens.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, CDW offers a rare combination in IT distribution: durable scale, high returns on capital, and shareholder-friendly capital return at a mid-teens earnings multiple.

**Business quality.** CDW sits as a trusted adviser in a fragmented $1tn+ addressable market, vendor-agnostic across hybrid infrastructure, security, digital experience, and services. Scale supports 10,500+ customer-facing coworkers, deep vendor certifications, and national logistics — competitive advantages screens cannot fully encode but that underpin recurring wallet share. US net sales represent ~90% of revenue; five dedicated US channels each exceeded $1.7bn in FY2025 (10-K), providing diversification within cyclicality.

**Quantitative alignment.** The screen passes six models across four families — cheapness (FCF Yield, Composite Value, Magic Formula), GARP (Neff PEGY ~0.08 on ~7.7% growth), dividend (Dividend Growth), and risk (Earnings Quality) — with full data quality (20/20 metrics). That profile fits a cash-compounding reseller trading below intrinsic estimates cited in recent commentary, not a distressed operator. Persistent buy signal (4 weeks, conviction ~59%) suggests the discount is stable rather than a one-off dislocation.

**Capital allocation.** Management returns substantial FCF via dividends and buybacks (board expanded repurchase authorisation by $750m in February 2025; ~$484m remained under the programme at 31 March 2026 per Q1 2026 10-Q body). Negative reported retained earnings reflect aggressive repurchases — consistent with a mature, FCF-rich franchise rather than balance-sheet distress.

**Valuation hook.** At ~15.8x P/E and ~5.2% FCF yield versus mid-single-digit net margins and ~22% gross margins, the market appears to price CDW as a low-growth cyclical. If AI infrastructure, security, and managed services sustain mid-single-digit top-line growth with stable margins, the screen’s GARP and FCF-yield passes look structurally supported.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and legal**
- **DOJ False Claims Act investigation (FY2024 10-K body):** Civil Investigative Demand issued 11 June 2024 relating to bids for contracts funded by the Schools and Libraries (E-Rate) Programme. Outcome and reserve status not disclosed in available extracts — a material unresolved overhang for the Public segment (~government, education, healthcare).
- Public-sector contract non-compliance risks include suspension, debarment, and termination (10-K risk factors).

**Cyclical and demand**
- Filings explicitly flag decreases or delays in enterprise and government IT spending, federal budget uncertainty, procurement changes, and shutdown risk.
- Q1 2026 YoY decline (Yahoo) confirms near-term demand softness despite longer-term AI tailwinds.

**Competitive and structural**
- Disintermediation risk from vendor direct sales, hyperscaler marketplaces (AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft), and “as-a-service” models that bypass traditional resellers.
- Vendor concentration: significant sales from Apple, Cisco, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft; >25% of purchases via Ingram Micro and TD SYNNEX (10-K). Loss or terms change with any key partner is a material risk.

**Operational**
- **ERP transition:** New ERP implemented in 2025 with incremental releases planned for 2026 (FY2025 10-K); Q3 2025 10-Q notes control changes. Execution risk on financial close and customer service.
- **Supply chain:** Filings cite memory/storage tightness as AI datacentre demand prioritisation lengthens lead times and raises pricing on commercial devices and servers.
- Cybersecurity, data breach, and AI governance risks extensively documented; no material breach reported to date.

**Financial structure**
- ~$5.8bn debt stack (FY2024 10-K) with inventory-financing dependence; rising rates increase interest burden.
- Accounts receivable and inventory risks elevated in downturns; FY2025 receivables growth absorbed FCF (Yahoo).
- Goodwill/intangibles exceed tangible equity — impairment risk if growth assumptions weaken.

**Governance / management**
- Sona Chawla (Chief Growth and Innovation Officer) retiring effective 31 December 2025 (8-K, 28 October 2025); several C-suite role changes in 2025 (CFO Albert Miralles promoted; new Chief Services and Solutions Officer from Slalom, August 2025 per FY2025 10-K).
- No going-concern qualification in available extracts; controls reported effective in recent 10-Q certifications.
