# L3Harris Technologies, Inc. (LHX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-13T06:47:40.298028+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
L3Harris Technologies is a US aerospace and defence contractor whose quantitative screen reads **buy** (composite ~65%, four of 22 models; quality, dividend, GARP and risk families). Filings show a strengthening demand picture—contractual backlog rose from **$34.2bn** (funded **$23.3bn**) at 3 January 2025 to **$38.7bn** (funded **$26.9bn**) at 2 January 2026 and **$42.0bn** at 3 July 2026—with H1 FY2026 revenue and earnings running ahead of the prior year. The value hook is not classic deep discount (screen P/E **24.8**, P/B **2.4**) but a combination of balance-sheet repair, rising operating cash flow and dividend continuity against a large, funded order book. The central debate is whether backlog conversion and missile/space programme wins outweigh rich headline multiples, **~$11bn** debt, lumpy quarterly cash flow, and leadership turnover after the August 2025 CEO change.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented process, LHX fits the **quality + GARP + financial health** bucket more than pure cheapness: the screen passes Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Piotroski F-Score (**8/9**) and Financial Health, while failing Graham-style, deep-value and earnings-yield screens. That pattern is consistent with a post-merger, intangible-heavy defence platform trading at a full multiple but delivering improving fundamentals—FY2025 net income **$1,606m** (10-K, year ended 2 January 2026) versus **$1,512m** in FY2024 and **$1,198m** in FY2023, and operating cash flow **$3,106m** versus **$2,559m** (FY2024 10-K cash flow statement, cited in FY2025 10-K body). Business quality anchors include diversified defence electronics, space, communications and propulsion (Aerojet Rocketdyne), with **~$42bn** contractual backlog at mid-2026 providing multi-year revenue visibility; management expects roughly **40%** of backlog to convert to revenue within twelve months (Q2 FY2026 10-Q). Dividend yield ~**2.0%** (screen) with no interim cut flag supports the income leg of the thesis. The buy signal is therefore best read as **accumulate on quality and cash-flow compounding**, not as a net-net or contrarian P/E play—conviction on the screen is still low (**~30%**, new signal) until price and timing data firm up.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Customer and budget concentration:** Defence exposure implies sensitivity to US appropriations, contract recompetes and stop-work risk; backlog is not all funded, and IDIQ/options are excluded from reported backlog (10-K language).

**Leverage and capital structure:** **~$11.1bn** total debt against **~$19.6bn** equity (FY2025 10-K); screen debt/equity **~34.6**. The Q2 FY2026 10-Q references covenants tied to a Department of War investment agreement and Aerojet Rocketdyne subsidiary preferred/warrant structures—complexity and potential redemption triggers merit diligence beyond the screen.

**Pension and retirement benefits:** Significant defined-benefit footprint; net periodic benefit **income** boosts pre-tax results in recent quarters (e.g. FAS pension income **$185m** in Q2 FY2026 per MD&A table). A turn in discount rates or asset returns could reverse this tailwind.

**Governance / key person:** News flow highlights **CEO removal in August 2025** with guidance reaffirmed but share price weakness, plus planned **officer stock sales** (e.g. Kubasik, September 2026 headlines)—not proof of operational failure but a sentiment and alignment flag.

**Cyclical / market:** Sector has underperformed broader indices in recent months (news); defence spending optimism vs budget caps remains cyclical.

**Accounting / programmes:** Rising EAC-driven revenue adjustments and percentage-of-completion exposure; Q4 FY2025 diluted EPS was seasonally low (**$1.59** in Yahoo quarterly income—filing corroboration via 8-K exhibit not in body extract).

**Liquidity:** FY2025 year-end cash improved, but **H1 FY2026 operating cash flow ($784m)** is well below half of FY2025’s **$3,106m**—seasonality likely, not yet verified for full-year conversion.

**Legal / regulatory:** 10-Q tables reference Item 1 legal proceedings and Item 1A risk factors, but **no proceeding detail** appears in the stored body extracts; no **going concern** language found in available bodies. Item 3 “Defaults Upon Senior Securities” is listed in 10-Q headers without accompanying default disclosure in extracts.
