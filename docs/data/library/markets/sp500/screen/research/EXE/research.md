# Expand Energy Corporation (EXE) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:42:24.330538+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Expand Energy is the largest independent US natural gas producer by net daily output, formed by Chesapeake’s October 2024 merger with Southwestern and rebranded as EXE. The quantitative screen flags a compelling value case—P/E 6.8, P/B 1.1, 3.5% yield, 17.6% ROE, modest leverage (D/E 26%)—supported by scale, reserve growth, and improving cash generation after integration. FY2025 filings show production of 2,622 Bcfe, proved reserves of 25.9 Tcfe (standardised measure $17.1bn), ~$1.2bn debt reduction since the merger, and ~$865m returned to shareholders; the stock joined the S&P 500 in 2025. The central debate is whether cheap headline multiples reflect genuine asset quality and LNG/data-centre gas demand, or cyclical peak earnings, derivative/marketing complexity, and recent CEO/CFO turnover. We see a confirmed value opportunity with material unresolved execution and commodity risks.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, EXE scores unusually well across all four screen families (cheapness, quality, dividend, risk): 15 of 22 models pass with a 94% composite score and full data quality (20/20 metrics). Low multiples sit on a business that filings portray as structurally stronger post-merger—2.6 Tcfe annual production, investment-grade unsecured revolver upsized to $3.5bn (September 2025), debt-to-capitalisation covenant at 65% (in compliance at each reported quarter-end), and equity of $18.6bn at 31 December 2025 per interim balance sheets.

Business quality aligns with the screen’s quality and FCF-yield passes. FY2025 10-K reports operating ~99% of production, premium Haynesville and Appalachia acreage adjacent to Gulf Coast and Northeast demand, and reserve PV-10 rising from $7.6bn (FY2024) to $19.4bn (FY2025) on higher commodity price assumptions and Southwestern integration. Interim trends reinforce the case: nine-month 2025 net income of $1.27bn and operating cash flow of $3.62bn (10-Q, September 2025); Q1 2026 net income $1.16bn and operating cash flow $2.40bn on 7.4 Bcfe/d production (10-Q, March 2026). Capital returns (dividends plus buybacks) and stated 2026 priority of debt reduction then shareholder returns fit dividend-growth and acquirer’s-multiple frameworks.

The screen’s strong-buy signal is not a pure commodity momentum play: leverage is contained relative to E&P peers, and the balance sheet has been actively managed (note redemptions/calls disclosed in Q1 2026). That said, earnings quality is heavily influenced by derivatives and marketing pass-through revenues—value investors should treat normalised cash earnings, not headline P/E alone, as the binding metric.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / commodity:** Filings repeatedly warn that natural gas, oil, and NGL price volatility can materially affect revenues, reserve values, and impairment risk. Q1 2026 MD&A cites heightened geopolitical volatility. Realised prices in Q1 2026 averaged $4.35/Mcfe (including derivatives) vs a higher NYMEX benchmark—hedging helps but caps upside.

**Derivative and marketing complexity:** Consolidated statements show large marketing revenues and derivative gains/losses (e.g. Q1 2025 unrealised gas derivative losses of $962m; Q1 2026 total revenues $4.4bn including $1.2bn marketing). Reported earnings are sensitive to mark-to-market movements; screen quality metrics may overstate through-cycle earnings power.

**Governance / leadership:** August 2025 8-K: CFO Mohit Singh departed without cause; Brittany Raiford appointed interim CFO. Q1 2026 10-Q: CEO Domenic Dell’Osso separated; Chairman Michael Wichterich named interim CEO; Marcel Teunissen appointed CFO effective 6 April 2026. Dual interim roles at the top create execution uncertainty during integration and LNG strategy rollout.

**Tax attributes:** FY2025 10-K risk summary flags an annual limitation on utilisation of tax attributes triggered by the Southwestern merger (Section 382-style), potentially increasing future cash taxes; further stock issuance could tighten limits.

**Debt and covenants:** Total debt ~$5.0bn; 65% debt-to-capitalisation covenant on the revolver. No defaults disclosed (Item 3 consistently references no defaults upon senior securities). Near-term note calls/redemptions ($875m 6.75% notes, $440m 5.875% notes per Q1 2026 notes) reduce maturity risk but consume cash.

**LNG / regulatory:** Filings highlight LNG export policy uncertainty, EU methane rules, and EPA methane regulations (with ongoing litigation and rule revisions). April 2026 Delfin FLNG SPA (~1.15 mtpa from 2031, subject to FID) replaces prior Delfin/Gunvor arrangements—long-dated, execution-heavy.

**Legacy / operational:** Chesapeake Chapter 11 history (2020) is disclosed; environmental litigation and delivery commitments (~7,800 Bcf gas over 20 years per FY2025 10-K) create contingent obligations. ~648 Bcfe of proved developed reserves were shut-in at 31 December 2025.

**Pension:** Current/post-retirement benefit plan liabilities appear on balance sheets (~$107m current, ~$688m long-term provisions at FY2025 per Yahoo); not a primary screen risk but worth monitoring.

No going-concern qualification identified in available extracts.
