## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
CRH plc is a leading North American–anchored building materials compounder—aggregates, cementitious materials, asphalt, and infrastructure products—benefiting from IIJA-style public spending and reindustrialisation demand. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy; filing evidence supports a quality operator with revenue and margin momentum, though the screen snapshot carries no populated model metrics (data quality score 0.0), so the signal rests on the headline rating rather than disclosed factor detail. Valuation debate centres on whether recent 52-week lows (news cites ~$90–94) adequately discount a pending ~$8.5bn Arcosa acquisition and higher leverage, against H1 2026 revenue growth of 7% and Adjusted EBITDA up 9%. Key debate: cyclical construction exposure versus durable pricing power and portfolio reshaping (divest non-core, buy aggregates/infrastructure).

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, CRH offers a rare combination: scale leadership in localised, hard-to-replicate quarry networks; a proven M&A engine (1,250+ deals); and capital returns (dividends growing ~5% annually, buybacks when balance sheet permits). The screen’s buy signal aligns with business quality visible in filings—FY2025 Total revenues of $37.4bn, net income $3.8bn, and Adjusted EBITDA $7.7bn (2025 Form 10-K)—and continued organic momentum: H1 2026 Total revenues $18.1bn (+7% YoY), Adjusted EBITDA $3.2bn (+9%), Q2 diluted EPS $2.21 (+14% YoY on net income of $1.5bn). Americas Materials Solutions is the engine (Q2 2026 segment revenues +10% YoY; aggregates volumes +14% in Q1 2026 per MD&A), while infrastructure (40% of FY2025 revenue) and repair/remodel (40%) diversify cyclicality versus pure new-build housing. Portfolio actions—Eco Material ($2.1bn, Sep 2025), Axius Water ($0.7bn), proposed Arcosa ($8.5bn EV)—sharpen the U.S. aggregates and infrastructure footprint; divestitures of lawn & garden ($1.1bn) and construction accessories ($0.7bn) recycle capital. The stock’s weakness into 52-week lows despite Q2 2026 earnings beats (30 Jul 2026) suggests the market is penalising deal financing risk more than operating performance—a classic value setup if integration and deleveraging execute. Caveat: screen model detail is absent; conviction rests on filings and news, not factor-level confirmation.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical:** Construction demand is inherently seasonal and macro-sensitive; Q1 losses and winter weather volatility are normal but can mask trend (Q1 2026 net loss $180m). Risk factors emphasise sensitivity to interest rates, housing slowdown, and government funding delays.

**Leverage / M&A:** Net debt rose from ~$5.3bn (FY2023, Yahoo) to ~$13.6bn (FY2025) before Arcosa adds ~$8.5bn EV. Bridge/term-loan commitments increase execution and refinancing risk; share buyback **paused** post-Arcosa announcement (Jun 2026). Integration of 16+ deals per half-year raises goodwill/intangible load ($13.2bn goodwill at Jun 2026).

**Regulatory / environmental:** Extensive climate, emissions, quarry permitting, and mine reclamation obligations; CRH operates in Ukraine (physical risk flagged in 10-K). LSE delisting (completed 20 Apr 2026) simplifies listing but removes European retail liquidity.

**Pension:** Non-current pension liability **$214m** at Jun 2026—modest relative to scale; actuarial losses flow through OCI.

**Governance / structure:** Redeemable noncontrolling interests ($435m) with EBITDA-linked put/call options create future cash obligations. Philippines VIE structure adds complexity.

**Competitive:** Commodity-like products in fragmented markets; pricing power varies by region (Q1 2026 Americas aggregates pricing -1% despite volume +14%).

**Accounting:** Q1 2026 **$48m goodwill impairment** on held-for-sale construction accessories; divestiture gains ($266m in Q2) inflate reported net income—adjust for underlying run-rate.

No material litigation disclosed; no covenant breach indicators in filings.
