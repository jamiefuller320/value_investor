# United Parcel Service, Inc. (UPS) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:30:22.425151+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
UPS is a global integrated parcel and logistics franchise undergoing a deliberate network reset: management is shedding low-yield volume from its largest customer, consolidating facilities, and targeting billions in annual cost savings while pivoting toward healthcare logistics and small-and-medium business (SMB) mix. The quantitative screen flags the name as a strong buy on cheapness, dividend yield, and earnings-quality metrics (P/E 18.6, yield 5.8%, ROE 33.4%), but reported earnings and volumes have been falling for three consecutive years. The central debate is whether Network Reconfiguration savings and revenue-per-piece gains can stabilise margins and protect a dividend that already exceeds free cash flow, or whether structural share loss to FedEx and Amazon freight services caps the recovery. We see a credible value-and-income case, tempered by execution and payout risk.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal rests on five passing factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with particular strength in Neff PEGY (PEGY 0.03), High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Quality Value, Magic Formula, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality. At roughly 19× trailing earnings and a near-6% yield, UPS trades at a discount to its own pre-2023 earnings power and well below peak-cycle multiples, while still generating high reported ROE and positive operating cash flow.

Business quality remains intact in filings: UPS holds a dense global ground-and-air network, a unionised but manageable cost base under the Teamsters master agreement, and growing healthcare logistics exposure (global healthcare portfolio exceeded $11 billion in revenue in 2025, per the FY2025 10-K). Management delivered approximately $3.5 billion of Network Reconfiguration and Efficiency Reimagined savings in 2025 and guides to roughly $3 billion in 2026, having closed 93 U.S. buildings and reduced operational headcount by approximately 48,000 positions in 2025.

For a value investor, the screen correctly identifies a cyclically depressed, capital-light cash generator trading on yield and normalised-earnings optics rather than near-term growth (screen growth metric −27.2%). The mismatch is that “cheap” reflects real volume decline and strategic shrinkage, not a passive macro trough—so the thesis requires belief that adjusted EPS ($7.16 in 2025 vs. $6.56 GAAP, per the 10-K) and cost take-out can bridge the gap before dividend coverage becomes a binding constraint.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Strategic / cyclical**
- Deliberate volume reduction from the largest customer (target 50% below 2024 levels by June 2026) will keep reported volumes and revenue under pressure through the transition; Q1 2026 filings warn of further building closures and possible accelerated depreciation.
- International margins compressed in 2025 as trade-policy changes shifted lanes away from higher-margin U.S. trade routes (10-K).
- Competitive intensity rising: Amazon’s opening of freight services to third parties triggered sector weakness in July 2026 news flow.

**Labour and pension**
- 10-K risk factors flag material pension, post-retirement healthcare, and multiemployer plan exposure; future Teamsters negotiations could require significantly higher contributions.
- Workers’ compensation and auto-liability costs rose in 2025 with adverse prior-year development (10-K).

**Financial / governance**
- Dividend payout above free cash flow for two years; no buybacks planned for 2026—limited flexibility if savings lag.
- Net debt rose to $18.2 billion in 2025 (Yahoo); interest expense increased 17% to $1,017m (10-K).
- SEC settlement (Nov-2024) over impairment-control practices related to the 2021 UPS Freight divestiture—remedial actions required, no admission of violation (FY2024 10-K).
- Asset impairment risk remains elevated as network plans evolve; $182m charges in Q4 2025 from MD-11 retirement.

**Regulatory / legal**
- Filings cite tariff uncertainty, climate regulation (ReFuelEU, CORSIA), and ongoing litigation exposure; no material going-concern or covenant breach disclosed.
