# Invitation Homes Inc. (INVH) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T11:10:20.537159+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Invitation Homes is the largest US institutional single-family rental platform, owning ~86,000 wholly leased homes across 16 core Sun Belt and coastal markets. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cash-flow cheapness (FCF yield ~5.8%), dividend yield (~4.1%), earnings quality, and Piotroski quality, despite modest reported earnings growth (-3.7%). Primary filings show accelerating rental revenue through 2025 and into Q1 2026, funded by rent growth and portfolio scale, while net income is increasingly shaped by property gains, higher interest expense, and new homebuilding activity following the ResiBuilt acquisition. The valuation hook is a high-quality, bond-like income stream trading below intrinsic value on FCF and dividend metrics, not on headline P/E (~31×). The key debate is whether political and regulatory risk around institutional home ownership—and a ~$8.9bn debt stack with a 2028 maturity wall—are adequately priced against resilient occupancy and rent fundamentals.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, INVH offers a rare combination of **visible cash generation, dividend income, and operational scale** in a supply-constrained US housing sub-sector. The screen passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with high data completeness (20/20 metrics), suggesting the signal is not driven by missing inputs.

Business quality supports the screen:

- **Platform moat:** Vertically integrated ProCare operations across 16 dense core markets (~5,000 homes per market on average), with homebuilder partnerships, a May 2025 developer-lending programme, and the January 2026 ResiBuilt acquisition adding internal build capacity.
- **Portfolio:** 86,192 wholly owned homes and 8,006 joint-venture homes at 31 December 2025 (FY2025 10-K body); 85,970 wholly owned at 31 March 2026 (Q1 2026 10-Q).
- **Income orientation:** Dividends of $0.87/share declared over the first nine months of 2025 (Q3 2025 10-Q); $0.30 quarterly dividend signalled in news flow (June 2026). Screen yield 4.1% and FCF yield 5.8% align with a REIT valued on distributable cash, not GAAP net income.
- **Capital return:** Q1 2026 repurchase of 17.1m shares for ~$439m (Q1 2026 10-Q), completing a programme referenced positively in sell-side commentary.

The screen’s Neff PEGY (0.08) and Composite Value passes imply the market is discounting growth fears—linked to 2025–26 policy rhetoric on institutional landlords—more than the operating trajectory warrants. Filings describe demand as resilient, with leasing seasonality and rent resets providing inflation pass-through, albeit with a lag.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory / political (elevated):** The FY2025 10-K devotes extensive risk language to federal and state proposals to restrict institutional home ownership, rent control, eviction limits, and tenant-rights expansion. News flow (January 2026) linked share weakness to Trump-era housing policy proposals; WSJ coverage (July 2026) flagged messy implementation risk for large landlords. This is the central non-screen risk and is explicitly cited in filings as potentially affecting acquisition ability, rental income, and valuation.

**Leverage and refinancing:** ~$8.9bn total contractual debt (Q1 2026 10-Q). Unsecured notes carry financial covenants including maximum total leverage, secured/unencumbered leverage, fixed-charge coverage, and unsecured interest coverage. Secured debt requires reserve accounts under servicer control. **No defaults disclosed**, but ~43% of debt matures in 2028—a refinancing cliff in a ~4.7% 10-year rate environment (macro context marker, colour only).

**Interest expense:** Q1 2026 interest rose 13% YoY despite prior-year rate cuts; revolver draw increased. Floating-rate term loans (SOFR + margin ~4.5%) remain sensitive to rate volatility.

**Cost inflation:** Filings flag property taxes, insurance, and HOA fees as largely fixed and not fully pass-through. Q1 2026 property O&M rose 5.9% YoY.

**Earnings quality:** GAAP net income is boosted by recurring gain-on-sale of properties ($87m in Q1 2026 alone) and securities gains in 2025 (Yahoo). Screen passes Earnings Quality, but filing composition suggests normalised earnings power is lower than headline EPS.

**Execution / M&A:** ResiBuilt integration ($91m cash plus $8.5m contingent consideration in Q1 2026) and the developer-lending programme add construction-cycle and credit risk not present in the legacy lease-only model.

**Governance:** Chief Legal Officer Mark Solls announced retirement effective end-FY2026 (8-K, 31 December 2025); not a disagreement, but succession timing matters given regulatory intensity.

**Pension:** Not material; no defined-benefit exposure flagged in available filings.
