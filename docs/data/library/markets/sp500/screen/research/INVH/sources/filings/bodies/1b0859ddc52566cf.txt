Allowance for Credit Losses on Construction and Development Loan Receivables The amortized cost of loan receivables is reduced by the allowance for credit losses. We estimate our allowance for credit losses using a probability of default, loss given default, and exposure at default methodology applied to our construction and development loan portfolio. Expected credit losses are measured over the contractual life of each loan and incorporate historical loss data, current portfolio conditions, and macroeconomic forecasts over a 12-month reasonable and supportable forecast period. Following this forecast period, the loss rate assumptions revert to historical expectations using a method. We assess credit quality primarily through our internal risk rating system for each loan. Loans are assigned a risk rating on a scale of 1 to 5, with 1 representing the lowest level of credit risk and 5 representing the highest level of credit risk. Ratings are determined using a structured framework that considers several factors, including ratios, sponsor strength and experience, construction progress, general contractor experience, occupancy and leasing conditions, market dynamics, and payment performance. The risk rating drives the loss rate assumptions used in the reserve calculation. Our allowance includes reserves for both funded loan balances, which are recorded as a to the related loan receivables, and unfunded loan commitments, which are recorded as a liability. interest is added to a loan’s principal balance and is incorporated into the allowance for credit loss model.
