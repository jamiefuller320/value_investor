# Copart, Inc. (CPRT) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-09T07:40:34.076664+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Copart operates the leading global online salvage-vehicle auction platform, earning fee-based service revenue from insurance companies (~81% of vehicles processed) and a growing international footprint. FY2025 revenue reached $4.6 billion with $1.7 billion operating income (FY2025 10-K), yet the share price has fallen roughly 30–40% over the past year amid US volume softness and cyclical insurance-industry headwinds. The quantitative screen flags a strong buy on what appears to be a high-quality, net-cash compounder trading at a depressed multiple. The central debate is whether recent US assignment declines are cyclical/temporary or structural, and whether leadership change, a potential CCC Intelligent Solutions acquisition, and an ongoing DOJ money-laundering inquiry introduce execution or regulatory risk that the screen cannot capture.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
Copart fits a value-oriented quality screen: dominant network economics, asset-light US agent model, rising international mix, and exceptional cash conversion with minimal leverage.

**Business quality.** Copart is the global leader in online vehicle remarketing via its VB3 platform, with operations across the US, UK, Europe, Canada, Brazil, and the Middle East. The FY2025 10-K describes a wide moat built on geographic yard density, proprietary technology (VB3, Co.ai, Copart 360), catastrophe-response capability, and a ~1 million-member buyer base. No single customer exceeds 10% of consolidated revenue, though insurance sellers supply ~81% of vehicle volume — a structural but diversified concentration.

**Financial profile.** Annual revenue grew from $4.2 billion (FY2024 10-K) to $4.6 billion (FY2025 10-K), with operating income from $1.6 billion to $1.7 billion. Operating margins remain high (~37% in the nine months to April 2026 per the Q3 FY2026 10-Q). The balance sheet is fortress-like: $3.35 billion cash and restricted cash plus $846 million held-to-maturity Treasuries at 30 April 2026, total liabilities of $858 million, and an undrawn $1.25 billion unsecured revolver (8-K, 23 January 2026). Capital returns are aggressive — 43.4 million shares repurchased for $1.63 billion in the first nine months of FY2026 (Q3 10-Q).

**Screen linkage.** The quantitative screen rates CPRT **strong_buy**. While the screening snapshot carries sparse underlying metrics (zero models passed, data quality score 0.0), the fundamental profile — consistent earnings, high FCF, net cash, and share-price dislocation versus business quality — aligns with a value screen identifying mispriced compounders. Interim results show flat nine-month net income (~$1.15 billion) despite US volume pressure, suggesting earnings resilience rather than deterioration.

**Valuation hook.** News coverage repeatedly frames the stock as undervalued after a 32–42% decline (e.g. Simply Wall St, 9 September 2026; Yahoo Finance, 23 July 2026), with JPMorgan upgrading to Overweight and a $40 target (Yahoo Finance, 3 September 2026). The screen’s strong-buy signal likely reflects depressed multiples against durable cash generation rather than near-term growth acceleration.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / US volume.** The Q3 FY2026 10-Q MD&A explicitly cites US volume declines. Insurance industry cost discipline and lower accident frequency (discussed in sell-side and news coverage) can reduce total-loss assignments — Copart's core supply. Hurricane-related revenue in FY2025 creates difficult comps. This cyclicality is the most plausible explanation for the share-price decline despite intact profitability.

**Customer concentration.** ~81% of vehicles processed come from insurance companies (FY2025 10-K). No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but the sector concentration leaves Copart exposed to insurer remarketing strategy shifts, including direct sales or competitor contracts (e.g. RB Global/IAA).

**Competitive.** The FY2025 10-K names RB Global (Insurance Auto Auctions), Carvana, Openlane, Manheim, ACV Auctions, and LKQ as competitors; LKQ and dismantler groups can bypass remarketers by sourcing salvage directly from insurers. Seeking Alpha coverage (24 August 2026) frames an ongoing battle for "salvage king" status with IAA.

**Regulatory — DOJ investigation.** The Q3 FY2026 10-Q (Note 9) discloses an ongoing US Department of Justice investigation into potential money-laundering violations related to auction-platform member practices. A DOJ letter in October 2023 indicated possible exposure. The company states it cannot predict duration, scope, or financial impact — a material unresolved regulatory overhang.

**Governance / key person.** Executive Chairman Jay Adair returns as CEO on 31 July 2026, replacing Jeff Liaw (8-K, 29 June 2026). Jane Pocock was appointed President effective 1 August 2026 (8-K, 8 July 2026). The "reverse transition" to a founder-CEO raises questions about strategy continuity versus a fresh growth push; Liaw's departure was not due to financial reporting disagreements. Director Daniel Englander sold ~$2.2 million in shares (news, 15 July 2026).

**M&A execution.** Bloomberg/Yahoo reports (19 August 2026) that Copart is among suitors for CCC Intelligent Solutions, competing with private-equity firms. A large acquisition would shift the capital-allocation story from buybacks to integration risk.

**Environmental / litigation.** FY2025 10-K notes environmental liabilities at yard facilities and ordinary-course litigation; no material pending proceedings beyond the DOJ matter, though the company cautions unpredictable legal outcomes could be material.

**Pension.** No material pension deficit flagged; current pension-related balance-sheet items are modest (~$67 million current, FY2025 Yahoo).
