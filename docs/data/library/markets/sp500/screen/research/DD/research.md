# DuPont de Nemours, Inc. (DD) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T13:21:26.532981+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
DuPont de Nemours has completed a multi-year portfolio reset—spinning Qnity Electronics (November 2025), divesting Aramids (April 2026), and refocusing on Healthcare & Water Technologies and Diversified Industrials. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cash-flow yield, GARP, dividend growth, and balance-sheet health, but fails nearly all quality and earnings-multiple tests (P/E 62.8, ROE 1.8%). The investment case rests on a simplified ~$7bn continuing revenue base, improving continuing-operations earnings, and aggressive capital return—not on reported statutory earnings, which remain distorted by discontinued operations and legacy charges. The central debate is whether post-transformation free cash flow justifies the screen’s cheapness signal, or whether PFAS liabilities, spin-related indemnities, and non-recurring divestiture cash flows inflate the picture.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen rates DD a buy (5/22 models, composite 59%, conviction 29%, signal improving). Four factor families pass—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—with standout metrics of ~9.3% FCF yield, PEG 0.42, and 149.6% earnings growth (likely from a depressed base). Failures on Graham, quality, moat, low P/E, and earnings quality (negative net income flagged) indicate the signal is driven by cash-flow and growth optics rather than reported profitability.

Business quality, on a continuing basis, is reasonable: two focused segments serving healthcare packaging, water filtration, construction materials, and industrial adhesives/lubricants, with established brands (Tyvek, Filmtec, Corian, Vespel). H1 2026 continuing net sales rose 4% to $3.5bn; continuing net income was $341m versus $104m prior year; H1 operating cash flow from continuing operations was $632m versus $151m. Management completed a $775m share repurchase (per news coverage), holds a $2bn buyback authorisation (FY2025 10-K body), and raised FY2026 net sales guidance to $7.16–7.19bn (news; not present in available filing extracts).

For a value investor, the hook is a post-spin industrial compounder trading near book (P/B 1.4) with improving operating momentum and capital return, screened cheap on normalised cash-flow metrics. The screen’s buy case is partially supported by continuing-operations trends but undermined by low reported ROE, high headline P/E, and FCF figures that may include divestiture proceeds rather than recurring operations alone.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**PFAS and legacy litigation.** FY2025 10-K and interim filings emphasise ongoing PFAS/PFOA litigation, remediation obligations, and the January 2021 MOU cost-sharing arrangement with Corteva and Chemours. Water District Settlement ($400m DuPont contribution in 2024, per FY2024 10-K) illustrates scale. Outcomes remain uncertain and may exceed accruals.

**Spin and indemnification complexity.** DuPont retains 56% of legacy liabilities post-Qnity split and shares PFAS stray liabilities with Corteva. Contingent tax liabilities from prior Dow/Corteva/N&B separations persist. Third-party indemnitors (Qnity, Dow, Corteva) may lack adequate resources when obligations fall due—DuPont may bear losses temporarily.

**Covenant constraint.** Minimum EBITDA under the Corteva Letter Agreement reset to $1.4bn (8-K, Dec 2025), limiting future divestitures without meeting conditions.

**Earnings quality and accounting noise.** FY2025 statutory loss; screen fails earnings quality and Piotroski (6/9). Goodwill of $7.8bn on a $21bn asset base creates impairment risk; $768m Aramids impairment already taken. FCF yield screen metric (~$1.84bn TTM) materially exceeds Yahoo-reported annual FCF ($172m), suggesting non-recurring divestiture cash inflates the cheapness signal.

**Operational/cyclical.** Diversified Industrials exposes DD to construction (seasonal H2 demand), automotive, and aerospace cycles. Raw-material and tariff risks flagged in risk factors.

**Leverage and liquidity.** Net debt ~$1.4bn post-Aramids cash (Jun 2026: $3.1bn debt, $1.7bn cash)— manageable, but debt exchange offer for 4.725% 2028 notes (news, Aug 2026) signals active liability management. No going-concern language identified; Item 3 "Defaults Upon Senior Securities" present as standard 10-Q heading without disclosed defaults in extracts.

**Governance/structure.** Repeated portfolio reshaping (Dow, Corteva, N&B, M&M, Delrin, Electronics, Aramids) creates stranded costs and comparability challenges. Reverse stock split (Jun 2026) may signal capital-structure tidying but can reduce retail liquidity.
