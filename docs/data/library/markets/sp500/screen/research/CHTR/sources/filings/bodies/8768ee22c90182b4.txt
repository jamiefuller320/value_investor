Business Combination Accounting The operating results of Cox Communications have been included in the Company's consolidated statements of operations for the period from the date of acquisition through September 30, 2026. Because these costs cannot be attributed to Cox Communications without assumptions and allocations that would not provide a meaningful measure of its results, it is impracticable to determine its earnings since the acquisition date. Charter applied business combination accounting to the Cox Transactions. The purchase price was allocated to the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values, with the excess recorded as goodwill. Fair values were preliminarily determined using income, cost and market approaches, primarily based on valuations using management’s assumptions, including expected future cash flows. These inputs are largely unobservable (Level 3), except for assumed debt and the Charter Holdings common units, which were valued using quoted market prices (Level 1). Property, plant and equipment was valued using the cost approach, which considers replacement cost adjusted for physical depreciation and functional and economic obsolescence. Franchise rights and customer relationships were valued using a excess earnings method, a discounted cash flow approach that isolates the cash flows attributable to each intangible asset. The weighted average life of customer relationships acquired is 7 years. Deferred tax assets and liabilities were recorded for acquisition-date differences between book basis and tax basis, subject to the transaction structure. The Contribution was treated as a nontaxable partnership contribution, and no deferred taxes were recorded in purchase accounting as the basis difference in the partnership from the net assets contributed was associated with Cox Equity Holdings. Franchises and Goodwill During the third quarter of 2026, the Company determined that the sustained decline in Charter’s stock price was a triggering event requiring an interim quantitative impairment analysis of its franchises and goodwill as of July 31, 2026. Franchises represent the future economic benefits of the right to solicit and service potential new customers, and the right to deploy and market new services to those potential customers. For purposes of the franchise impairment analysis, the Company's franchises are aggregated into two essentially inseparable units of accounting, which generally represent geographical clustering of the Company's cable systems into groups by which such systems are managed. The Company estimated the fair value of its franchises using a discounted cash flow income approach called excess earnings method. Under this method, the Company isolates discrete cash flows attributable to franchises from Charter’s enterprise cash flows, after deducting charges representing the required returns on contributory assets, and discounts the resulting cash flows to present value using current market participant assumptions. Significant assumptions used in the analysis include projected enterprise cash flows, the portion of those cash flows attributable to Charter’s franchises based upon customer growth and attrition, and discount rates. The determination of fair value also required significant judgment regarding the fair value of property, plant and equipment and the associated contributory asset charges. Changes in these assumptions could materially impact the amount of excess earnings attributed to the franchises and the resulting estimate of fair value. 9 CHARTER COMMUNICATIONS, INC. Overview With the closing of the Cox Transactions, we added approximately 5.4 million residential customers becoming the nation’s leading Internet and video provider, passing more than 70 million homes and businesses across the United States. In September 2026, we began offering the full suite of Spectrum residential and small business products across the Cox Communications footprint. The competitive environment continued to challenge our Internet customer growth in the third quarter of 2026 with legacy Charter losing 156,000 Internet customers. Legacy Charter mobile lines grew by 440,000, and video customer losses improved compared to the prior year period, reflecting improvements to our video product offering. In November 2026, we announced a new operating model focused on returning to growth, principally by winning more Internet customers, and becoming a simpler and more efficient organization. To return to growth, we are reformulating our consumer proposition and approach to increase Internet connects through simpler, more transparent offers, modified sales channel capabilities and the layering of mobile and video into our Internet offerings. We also intend to reduce churn through reconfigured promotional and retail offers and a more disciplined focus on the drivers of customer service calls, including continued investment in technology. We seek to maintain or increase average revenue per customer at connect and over the course of the customer relationship while returning to customer growth. To become more efficient, we are redesigning how we operate our business to reduce complexity and focusing resources on activities that contribute most directly to growth. In addition to the more than $1 billion of annualized operating expense synergies we anticipate from the Cox Transactions, we are targeting an additional $2 billion of annualized 21 operating expense savings over the next 18 months. We expect the savings to come primarily from eliminating projects that do not contribute to Internet growth, reducing management layers and obtaining better speed, quality and pricing from a more focused and narrowed set of vendors. We cannot provide assurance that we will achieve these synergies or savings in the amounts or timing we expect, or at all.
