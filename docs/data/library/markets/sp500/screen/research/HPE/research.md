# Hewlett Packard Enterprise Company (HPE) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-09T07:19:31.405288+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) is a leveraged AI-infrastructure and networking compounder: the quantitative screen rates it a buy (6/22 models; composite 66%) on cash-flow yield, growth-adjusted valuation, and dividend metrics, but not on balance-sheet quality or classic deep-value screens. The investment case rests on surging AI server and Juniper-enhanced networking demand, with fiscal Q3 2026 revenue of $12.2bn (+34% YoY per the 10-Q) and raised FY2026 guidance, against a backdrop of post-Juniper leverage (~$20.2bn total borrowings at 31 July 2026), elevated inventory, and statutory earnings distorted by acquisition-related charges. The central debate is whether normalised free cash flow (screen TTM ~$4.7bn; filing nine-month operating cash flow $4.2bn) justifies the re-rating, or whether integration risk, goodwill sensitivity, and cyclical AI capex leave upside largely priced in after a strong multi-year run.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy signal is driven primarily by cash-flow and growth families, not asset-based or quality screens. HPE passes FCF Yield, Lynch PEG (0.07), Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality; it fails Graham-style, deep-value, Buffett quality, Piotroski F-Score (4/9), and Financial Health (current ratio ~1.01; debt/equity ~76%). That profile fits a GARP/cash-flow value investor rather than a net-net or quality compounder.

Business quality is improving operationally, if not on the balance sheet. Post-Juniper, HPE spans AI servers (Cloud & AI segment: $9.0bn revenue in Q3 FY2026; server sub-line $6.8bn), networking ($2.9bn, with data-centre networking up sharply), and GreenLake hybrid cloud. Segment operating profit for the nine months to 31 July 2026 was $4.9bn on consolidated revenue of $32.2bn (10-Q), versus a consolidated operating loss in the prior-year comparable period after a $1.4bn goodwill impairment. Management cites AI backlog, Oracle networking partnership, and supply constraints limiting fulfilment—demand appears real, but working-capital intensity is rising.

The valuation hook is cash generation at a mid-cycle growth inflection, not low P/E: screen metrics show P/E 28.9, P/B 2.9, FCF yield ~6.4%, and ROE 10.9%. Statutory EPS is not a reliable anchor (FY2025 net income $57m per Yahoo fallback; Q3 FY2026 benefited from a $444m H3C divestiture gain per the 10-Q). For a value investor, the screen correctly identifies underappreciated cash-flow power and earnings recovery potential, but the quality-family failure and leverage argue for sizing discipline rather than a deep-value conviction bet.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Integration and leverage:** Juniper closed for ~$13.4bn cash (10-Q Note 7). Total borrowings ~$20.2bn at 31 July 2026; FY2025 10-K narrative (business section, body extract) states HPE is “prioritising and remain committed to retaining our credit rating” while carrying elevated post-acquisition debt. Debt/equity ~76% on the screen; Piotroski leverage component failed.

**Goodwill and impairment:** $1.4bn goodwill impairment in Q2 FY2025 (nine-month comparative in Q3 10-Q). Cloud & AI reporting unit remains sensitive—10% fair-value headroom per Note 8. Tangible book is deeply negative (Yahoo FY2025).

**Working capital / inventory:** Inventory nearly doubled to $11.8bn; nine-month inventory cash outflow $5.8bn (10-Q cash flow statement). Filing language cites GPU supply unevenness, elevated backlog, and AI inventory buffering. Excess/obsolescence provision $440m (9M FY2026).

**Cyclical and competitive:** AI server demand is lumpy; competitors include Dell, Super Micro, Cisco, and Arista. 10-K body warns of uneven AI/GPU supply, tariff/geopolitical logistics risk, and intense industry competition. Screen fails Economic Moat and Acquirer’s Multiple (EV/EBIT 53.9).

**Customer concentration:** One distributor represented ~10% of FY2025 net revenue (FY2025 10-K business section, body extract).

**Governance / activism:** Elliott Cooperation Agreement (8-K, 1 June 2026) caps board size at 14 directors through 2027 annual meeting.

**Litigation and regulatory:** Q3 10-Q references legal proceedings relating to Juniper acquisition; IRS audits for fiscal 2020–2022 with $318m reserve increase (Note 4); unrecognized tax benefits $1.1bn. News cites NLST ITC patent action against HPE (5 September 2026). Oracle equipment deal included warrant for 4.2m shares at $0.01 each (Forbes, 3 September 2026)—dilution/overhang risk.

**Liquidity:** Current ratio ~1.01 (screen); Financial Health model failed. Cash $6.2bn against near-term obligations is adequate but tight relative to inventory and payables expansion.

**Pension:** Net periodic pension benefit credit (~$67m for 9M FY2026 per Note 3); not a primary red flag but present ($930m non-current employee benefits per Yahoo FY2025).

No going-concern language identified in available filing bodies.
