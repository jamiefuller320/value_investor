# Domino's Pizza, Inc. (DPZ) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:17:59.832432+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Domino's Pizza is a high-quality, asset-light global franchisor (~99% franchised; 22,100+ stores per FY2025 10-K business section) whose economics are driven by royalty and supply-chain cash flows rather than company-operated restaurant margins. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (P/E 18.3, PEGY 0.07, 2.5% yield, strong free cash flow), yet the share price has de-rated sharply over the past year amid sluggish U.S. same-store sales and elevated leverage. The investment case rests on durable franchise cash generation, net store growth and capital returns (buybacks/dividends) at a mid-teens earnings multiple, against a debate over whether flat comps, ~$4.9bn securitised debt and negative book equity limit re-rating potential. Filings confirm operational resilience in H1 2026 but do not yet resolve the comp-growth question.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, DPZ offers a rare combination of brand leadership, predictable cash conversion and shareholder-return discipline within consumer cyclicals. The screen passes six distinct models — Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Dreman Contrarian, Piotroski F-Score and Earnings Quality — spanning five factor families with 18/20 data quality and a persistent four-week buy signal. Business quality aligns with these screens: the FY2025 10-K describes U.S. QSR pizza market-share leadership (~23.3% total share, up from 22.5%), >85% of U.S. retail sales via digital channels, and a capital-light model where franchisees fund store growth while the parent captures royalties (5.5% U.S.) and supply-chain margin. Net store growth remains robust (209 global net openings in Q2 2026 per the 20 Jul 2026 10-Q), supporting revenue even when comps are muted. Capital allocation reinforces the value case: H1 2026 operating cash flow of $352.6m funded $234.6m of buybacks and $68.2m of dividends, with a further $1.0bn added to the repurchase authorisation in Apr 2026. At ~18x trailing earnings with mid-single-digit EPS growth and a growing dividend, the screen's "cheap quality compounder" profile is credible — provided comps stabilise and leverage remains serviceable.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and capital structure.** ~$4.88bn of securitised fixed-rate debt (Q2 2026 10-Q) against ~$1.76bn of total assets and negative stockholders' equity of ~$3.98bn. Weighted-average borrowing rate 3.9%. While leverage-ratio tests were met and no going-concern language appears in available extracts, the 2027 maturity wall (~$1.34bn) and dependence on capital markets/refinancing represent structural risk not fully captured by earnings-based screens.

**Same-store sales deceleration.** U.S. comps +0.1% in Q2 2026 and international comps −0.1% (ex-FX) mark a sharp slowdown from prior-year growth (+3.4% U.S. in Q2 2025). The FY2025 10-K warns that comparable-store sales are critical, fortressing can cannibalise existing units, and failure to grow comps could adversely affect royalty streams and stock price.

**Competitive and cost pressures.** Filing language highlights intensifying competition from Pizza Hut, Papa John's, Little Caesars, delivery aggregators (Uber Eats, DoorDash), meal kits and QSR broadly. Cheese (~25% of store food basket), labour and minimum-wage increases (notably California) are ongoing margin risks for franchisees and, indirectly, for royalty growth.

**Concentration and China.** Largest international master franchisee (Domino's Pizza Enterprises) operates 3,524 stores (~24% of international count) per FY2025 10-K. Equity stake in DPC Dash (China master franchisee) produced $18.4m of unrealised losses in H1 2026 as the HK-listed share price fell from HK$71.90 to HK$35.58.

**Supply chain single-source exposure.** U.S. pizza cheese from a single supplier (contract to Dec 2029); meat toppings largely single-source (to Dec 2027). Disruption or unfavourable renegotiation could affect franchisee economics.

**Governance / insider activity.** No material governance red flags in filings, but news flow records planned insider sales by CEO Russell Weiner (10,850 shares, ~$3.6m, Jul 2026) and EVP Kelly Garcia under Rule 10b5-1 plans — routine but worth noting alongside flat comps.

**Pension.** Current pension/post-retirement liabilities appear in balance sheet (~$53m current per Yahoo FY2025); no material pension deficit flagged in available filing extracts. Contingencies and legal proceedings are referenced in 10-Q headers but bodies for most 8-K filings are unavailable.
