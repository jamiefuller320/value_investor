# Fair Isaac Corporation (FICO) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:33:38.457294+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Fair Isaac Corporation (FICO) is a high-quality analytics franchise centred on the FICO Score and decision-management software, with interim filings showing accelerating momentum: first-half FY2026 revenue of $1.20bn (+28% y/y) and net income of $422.8m (+34% y/y), driven by a Scores segment that nearly doubled in the March 2026 quarter. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness, quality, GARP and risk (PEG 0.57, Piotroski pass, ROIC rank 100%), but headline P/E of 39.3x and negative book equity reflect aggressive buybacks funded with debt rather than weak economics. The central debate is whether mortgage-score pricing power, FICO Platform adoption and sustained double-digit EPS growth can justify the multiple, or whether regulatory pressure, VantageScore competition and financial leverage cap upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, FICO offers a rare combination of wide-moat scoring economics and compounding earnings that the screen captures well. Four of four factor families pass — cheapness (Lynch PEG 0.57 against ~69% earnings growth), quality (Piotroski F-Score, earnings quality), GARP and risk — with a persistent buy signal over four weeks and sector-relative composite score of 59%. The business is not optically cheap on trailing P/E (39.3x), but growth-adjusted metrics and cash conversion argue the market underprices the durability of revenue.

Filings describe a two-segment model: Scores (B2B via Experian, TransUnion and Equifax; B2C via myFICO.com) and Software (FICO Platform, fraud, decisioning). FY2025 10-K language confirms concentration — the three US CRAs accounted for 51%, 45% and 41% of total revenues in fiscal 2025, 2024 and 2023 respectively — yet this same channel dependency has supported pricing power as Scores revenue surged. Interim data reinforce quality: H1 FY2026 operating margin of 52.9% ($636.5m on $1,203.6m revenue) and six-month operating cash flow of $397.4m, with low capex intensity. FICO Platform ARR reached $263.6m at 30 September 2025 (35% of software ARR per the 10-K), supporting a land-and-expand software narrative alongside the scoring cash engine.

The buy case rests on earnings compounding (diluted EPS from $14.18 in FY2022 to $26.54 in FY2025 per Yahoo fallback) while the share count falls — a capital-allocation model the screen’s GARP and quality filters reward, even if absolute valuation remains demanding.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Customer and end-market concentration. FY2025 10-K states Experian, TransUnion and Equifax collectively accounted for 51%, 45% and 41% of total revenues in fiscal 2025–2023, and 92% of revenue came from banking. Consolidation among CRAs or banks, or renegotiation of score-pricing terms, would disproportionately affect FICO.

Competitive and regulatory pressure. The 10-K identifies VantageScore (owned by the three CRAs) as a direct competitor, alongside AI-enabled alternative data providers. News coverage references mortgage score pricing at $0.99 (TIKR.com, 17 July 2026), highlighting political and regulatory scrutiny of score economics even as FICO pursues FICO Score 10T adoption.

Financial leverage and negative equity. Total debt rose to $3.07bn at FY2025 year-end (Yahoo) and $3.64bn long-term debt at 31 March 2026 (10-Q), while stockholders’ equity is deeply negative. H1 FY2026 repurchases of $776.6m plus new senior-note issuance indicate continued reliance on debt markets; interest expense is rising ($86.6m in H1 FY2026 vs $60.9m prior year per 10-Q income statements).

Software transition risk. Risk factors warn the FICO Platform strategy may cause revenue volatility from mix shifts between licence and SaaS recognition, and that market acceptance is uncertain.

Macro and cyclicality. FY2025 10-K risk language cites banking-industry stress, rate volatility and mortgage origination cycles as potential revenue headwinds; slow US bank account growth could limit new-logo opportunity.

Governance. An 8-K (5 March 2026) records charter amendments allowing officer exculpation and eliminating a supermajority voting provision. James Wehmann, President Scores, retired effective 5 September 2025 (8-K, 28 August 2025); CEO William Lansing assumed direct Scores oversight — a leadership transition in the core franchise.

Legal proceedings. 10-Q cross-references Item 1A risk factors for legal matters; no material litigation detail appears in available body extracts. No going-concern qualification found.
