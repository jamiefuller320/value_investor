# UnitedHealth Group Incorporated (UNH) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T13:15:40.380841+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
UnitedHealth Group remains the dominant US integrated payer–provider platform, combining UnitedHealthcare insurance with Optum’s pharmacy, data and care-delivery businesses. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy on cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (P/E 25.8, FCF yield 6.7%, PEG 0.42, yield 2.3%), though deep-value and quality screens fail on leverage and thin reported margins. FY2025 was a trough year—consolidated net earnings fell to $12.1bn (10-K parent schedule) on Brazil/South America exit losses, Optum Health operating losses and Medicare medical-cost pressure—yet H1 2026 filings show a sharp interim recovery, with net earnings of $12.2bn (+21% YoY) and medical care ratio (MCR) improving to 85.3% from 87.1%. The central debate is whether margin repair and portfolio reshaping (Medicare Advantage exits, South America divestiture, Optum realignment) can restore earnings power fast enough to justify a premium multiple, against heavy CMS exposure (44% of premium revenue), ongoing DOJ/IRS actions and reputational fallout from the December 2024 killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, UNH offers a scaled franchise trading below its historical earnings peak but with identifiable trough dynamics and screen-supported cash-return attributes. The buy signal rests on four pillars visible in the source pack.

**Scale and integration.** Per the FY2025 10-K segment table, consolidated revenues reached $447.6bn (+12% YoY from $400.3bn), with UnitedHealthcare premiums of $352.2bn and Optum revenues of $270.6bn (before eliminations). CMS premium revenue rose to 44% of consolidated premiums, underscoring government-program scale but also concentration risk.

**Trough-to-recovery earnings arc.** Reported net earnings declined from $22.4bn (2023) to $14.4bn (2024) to $12.1bn (2025), driven by an $8.3bn Brazil disposal loss in 2024, $950m of 2025 held-for-sale charges, and UnitedHealthcare segment earnings from operations collapsing from $15.6bn to $9.4bn. Optum Health swung from $7.8bn operating profit (2024) to a $278m loss (2025). Interim filings indicate stabilisation: H1 2026 net earnings of $12.2bn versus $10.0bn in H1 2025; Q2 2026 diluted EPS of $6.04 versus $3.74; Q2 MCR of 86.7% versus 89.4%.

**Cash generation and shareholder returns.** Screen FCF of ~$24.3bn (TTM) and filing-confirmed capital return discipline support the dividend and GARP model passes. FY2025 share repurchases totalled $5.5bn (12m shares at $454.82 average); dividends were raised to an $8.84 annual rate. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $20.0bn per the Q2 2026 10-Q.

**Valuation hook.** At P/E 25.8 and PEG 0.42, the market prices a partial recovery rather than a full re-rating to pre-2024 peaks (diluted EPS $23.86 in 2023). If H1 2026 run-rate earnings persist, the screen’s growth input (61.5%) may reflect rebound arithmetic rather than structural growth—a point requiring verification, but consistent with a GARP entry rather than deep value.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and reimbursement.** CMS premium concentration at 44% creates direct exposure to Medicare Advantage rate cuts, RADV audit clawbacks, and coding compliance. The 10-K and Q2 2026 10-Q disclose an active DOJ False Claims Act case on risk-adjustment submissions; a Special Master recommended summary judgment for UNH in March 2025, but the DOJ has asked the court to reject that report—outcome cannot be reasonably estimated. Broad government investigations across CMS, OIG, DOJ, SEC and IRS are routine but ongoing.

**Medical cost and membership.** Q2 2026 MD&A confirms continued Medicare Advantage membership contraction and Optum Health care-arrangement shrinkage through 2026. Elevated Medicaid behavioural, pharmacy and home-health utilisation creates a timing mismatch with state rate updates. MCR improvement in H1 2026 is encouraging but may reflect mix shift from exits as much as underlying trend moderation.

**Tax.** On 6 Mar 2026 the company received IRS Notices of Proposed Adjustment for 2017–2020 intercompany transfer pricing, with potential extension to later years (Q2 2026 10-Q). Reserves are deemed adequate, but the dispute could be material.

**Leverage and liquidity.** Screen current ratio 0.79 and debt/equity 69% fail multiple financial-health models. Credit ratings cited in Q2 2026 MD&A show negative outlooks on senior unsecured debt (A2/A+/A-). Put/call obligations on unconsolidated businesses estimated at $4.8bn (10-K Note 12). Negative tangible book value (~$36.9bn per Yahoo) reflects goodwill-heavy balance sheet.

**Governance and reputational.** CEO transition: Stephen Hemsley returned as Chair and CEO (10-K signed March 2026); Wayne DeVeydt appointed CFO (effective September 2025 per 10-K exhibits). The December 2024 killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson and subsequent Luigi Mangione guilty plea (August 2026 news) sustain elevated public scrutiny of insurer practices.

**Portfolio execution.** Optum Health’s FY2025 operating loss and South America exit losses highlight execution risk in portfolio reshaping. A $1.7bn gain from deconsolidation of a business in Q4 2025 (10-K Note 14) adds complexity to earnings quality assessment—partially captured by the screen’s Earnings Quality model pass, but Piotroski F-Score of 6/9 failed.
