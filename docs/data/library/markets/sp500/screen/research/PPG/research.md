# PPG Industries, Inc. (PPG) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T11:26:42.078753+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
PPG is a global coatings franchise trading at a mid-teens P/E with a 2.7% dividend yield, passing the quantitative screen on quality, dividend, GARP, and risk metrics rather than deep-value criteria. FY2025 continuing-operations earnings rebounded sharply (diluted EPS $6.94 vs $5.75 in FY2024 per the 10-K), supported by portfolio simplification—divestiture of US/Canada architectural coatings and the silicas business—while H1 2026 shows accelerating top-line momentum (+6.9% net sales) but persistent margin compression. The central debate is whether organic growth and capital returns (dividend hike, buybacks) can offset raw-material and mix headwinds that caused a Q2 2026 GAAP earnings miss despite a revenue beat. For a value investor, the case rests on durable cash generation and a reasonable absolute multiple, not asset-based bargain pricing.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags PPG as a buy via eight models spanning cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families—consistent with a “quality at a fair price” profile rather than net-net or deep-value territory. Key screen metrics (P/E 15.9, P/B 3.0, yield 2.7%, ROE 19.3%) align with passes on Neff PEGY, Buffett Quality, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, and Earnings Quality; failures on Graham Defensive, Deep Value, Schloss Low P/B, and FCF Yield (4.5% vs 5% threshold) confirm the stock is not statistically cheap on book or asset bases.

Business quality remains the anchor. PPG operates two reportable segments—Performance Coatings (automotive refinish, aerospace, protective & marine, traffic solutions) and Industrial Coatings (automotive OEM, general industrial, packaging)—with global scale, R&D intensity (~$420M+ annually per filings), and recurring refinish/aerospace demand. FY2025 income from continuing operations rose to $1,587M from $1,377M (10-K), and operating cash flow from continuing operations reached $1,936M against capital expenditure of $778M, implying roughly $1.2B of free cash flow. Management returned capital aggressively: $790M of treasury stock repurchases and $628M of dividends in FY2025 (10-K equity and cash-flow disclosures).

The value hook is a de-rated quality compounder: the screen’s PEGY of 0.06 reflects depressed trailing growth (-1.5%) against a normalised earnings step-up post-portfolio cleanup, while the shares have underperformed peers around recent results (news flow, July 2026). H1 2026 adjusted diluted EPS of $4.06 vs $3.93 (+3.3%, Q2 2026 10-Q) and six consecutive quarters of organic sales growth (news, July 2026) suggest the underlying franchise is intact even as margins lag. Dividend growth (board approved $0.74/quarter, July 2026 news) and ongoing buybacks ($175M H1 2026 per 10-Q) reinforce shareholder-yield appeal that the screen’s dividend-family passes capture.

INVESTMENT THESIS (continued): This is a buy for value investors who accept moderate leverage and cyclical end-market exposure in exchange for a proven coatings platform at ~16× earnings—not for investors requiring P/B below book or NCAV support.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and margin:** Coatings demand ties to automotive production, industrial activity, and construction/refinish cycles. Q2 2026 cost of sales growth (+10.5%) exceeded revenue growth (+7.2%), and news coverage flagged automotive refinish volume weakness and margin pressure overshadowing sales gains (e.g., “PPG Slides as Margin Pressure Overshadows Solid Q2 Sales Growth,” 29 Jul 2026).

**Leverage and covenants:** Debt-to-equity of ~87% (screen input) and screen failure on Financial Health are material. PPG’s credit agreements require total indebtedness to total capitalization of 60% or less (Q2 2026 10-Q); the company reported full compliance as of 30 Jun 2026. Term Loan maturity extended to January 2029 (10-Q). Rising interest expense ($132M H1 2026 vs $118M H1 2025) is a headwind.

**Pension and OPEB:** Accrued pension liabilities of $549M and other postretirement benefits of $386M at Jun 2026 (10-Q balance sheet). Net periodic pension expense guided at ~$20M and OPEB ~$20M for 2026 (10-Q footnote).

**Environmental and legal contingencies:** FY2025 10-K notes unreserved environmental losses of $100M–$200M as reasonably possible, beyond accrued reserves. Q2 2026 10-Q references ~400 plaintiffs in Cook County, Illinois litigation and legacy site remediation (Jersey City chrome, glass/chemical sites). Management states resolutions are not expected to materially affect financial position or liquidity, but timing and magnitude remain uncertain.

**Restructuring:** Restructuring reserve of $192M at 30 Jun 2026 (10-Q); majority of actions expected to complete 2026–2027. Ongoing charges flow through “Other charges, net.”

**Intangibles and goodwill:** Goodwill of $6,163M at Jun 2026 (~27% of total assets) creates impairment risk in a prolonged downturn; screen Piotroski score of 6/9 reflects leverage not declining and gross margin/asset turnover not improving.

**FX:** Large translation gains/losses ($950M OCI gain FY2025 vs -$916M FY2024 per 10-K) underscore earnings sensitivity to dollar moves; ~55%+ of pretax income from foreign operations (10-K disclosure pattern).

**Governance:** No adverse going-concern or control deficiencies noted in indexed filings. CEO Tim Knavis referenced in Jul 2026 earnings-call coverage; no management turnover flagged in news manifest.
