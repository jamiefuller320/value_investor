# Arch Capital Group Ltd. (ACGL) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T11:24:37.570354+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Arch Capital is a Bermuda-domiciled S&P 500 specialty insurer, reinsurer and mortgage insurer trading on a value screen that flags **strong buy** (11/22 models, 86% composite; P/E 7.7, P/B 1.6, ROE 21.3%, debt/equity 11%). Filings show sustained earnings power: FY2025 net income to common of **$4.4bn** on **$16.5bn** net premiums written, with book value per share rising to **$65.11** from **$53.11** a year earlier (2025 Form 10-K). Capital return has accelerated sharply—**~$1.9bn** of buybacks in 2025 versus **~$24m** in 2024—supporting per-share value creation even as absolute growth moderates. The central debate is whether disciplined underwriting across insurance, reinsurance and mortgage can hold combined ratios and ROE as reinsurance pricing softens and catastrophe volatility persists. Deep research broadly confirms the quantitative case but flags cycle timing and reserve/cat tail risk as reasons to accumulate rather than treat the screen as a blind trigger.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen passes all four factor families—**cheapness, quality, GARP and risk**—with high data coverage (18/20 metrics). That alignment is unusual for a financial and maps to a business that has compound book value while earning mid-teens to low-20s returns on equity through the hard-market cycle.

**Why it fits a value investor:**

1. **Valuation vs. quality mismatch.** At P/E ~7.7 and P/B ~1.6, the market prices ACGL closer to a cyclical peak insurer than a franchise generating **$4.4bn** of common net income and **$65.11** book value per share (FY2025 10-K). Models passed include Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat and Acquirer's Multiple—consistent with an underpriced compounder rather than a deep cyclical trap.

2. **Diversified, specialty-focused platform.** Three segments—insurance, reinsurance and mortgage—provide revenue diversification across P&C cycles and housing credit. The August 2024 Allianz U.S. middle-market and entertainment acquisition (MCE) adds scale and "ballast" to insurance (2025 10-K). Mortgage remains a structurally attractive fee-like credit business when housing is stable.

3. **Capital discipline and shareholder alignment.** FY2025 repurchases of **~$1.9bn** (vs. **~$24m** in FY2024) and **$1.1bn** remaining authorisation signal management's view that intrinsic value exceeds market price. Q1 2026 repurchased **8.3m shares for ~$783m** (Q1 2026 10-Q)—continuing that posture.

4. **Balance-sheet strength.** Capital of **~$26.9bn** at year-end 2025, investable assets of **$47.4bn**, and low financial leverage (screen D/E 11%; long-term debt **~$2.7bn** per Yahoo balance sheet—used only for leverage context) support the "risk" family pass.

5. **Screen stability.** Signal stable for three weeks with "building" conviction (53%)—early in the holding period but not flickering.

The screen's strong buy is therefore grounded in real fundamentals: high ROE, strong FCF generation (Yahoo: operating cash flow **$6.17bn**, FCF **$6.13bn** in 2025—fallback for cash metrics not extracted from filing bodies), and a valuation that embeds little credit for continued execution.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical / underwriting**

- Filings explicitly warn of ability to execute in **"soft" as well as hard markets** and of **competition on pricing, capacity and terms** (2025 10-K cautionary note).
- Q1 2025 combined ratio of **100.1%** shows earnings are not linear; catastrophe and large-loss frequency can overwhelm a quarter.
- News and sell-side commentary flag **reinsurance pricing softening** ("Arch Capital: The Real Test Begins As Reinsurance Pricing Softens," Seeking Alpha, 17 July 2026)—a direct challenge to the reinsurance segment that contributed **$441m** of Q1 2026 underwriting income.

**Reserves and recoverables**

- Loss reserves are **"inherently subject to uncertainty"**; ultimate liability may exceed estimates (2025 10-K). Unpaid/paid recoverables **~$9.5bn** at year-end 2025 create **credit risk** if reinsurers fail.
- Filings cite risk of **adverse prior-year development** and **greater-than-expected loss ratios**—not fully quantified in available extracts.

**Regulatory / tax**

- **Bermuda corporate income tax** and **OECD Pillar I/II** implementation cited as material tax/regulatory risks (2025 10-K risk factors).
- Multi-jurisdiction regulation across Bermuda, U.S., U.K., EU and Australia adds compliance complexity.

**Operational**

- **Cyber/AI** risks explicitly flagged; AI is used in underwriting with a governance framework.
- **Climate change** affects cat-exposed insurance/reinsurance and mortgage housing markets.
- MCE integration and Allianz runoff reinsurance add operational and reserve-transfer risk not yet fully visible in ratios.

**Governance / related parties**

- Strategic stakes in Greysbridge (Somers), Premia and Coface create related-party and consolidation complexity; not a red flag per se but adds opacity for minority shareholders.

**Pension:** No material pension deficit flagged in available extracts (insurer balance sheet dominated by technical reserves, not DB pension).

**Screen blind spots:** Quantitative models may underweight forward reinsurance rate declines, cat clustering, and the step-down in nine-month 2025 earnings vs 2024 (**$3,131m** vs **$3,347m**).
