# Healthpeak Properties, Inc. (DOC) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:24:27.269976+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Healthpeak Properties is a large-cap US healthcare REIT (outpatient medical, life-science lab, senior housing) that screens as a buy on cash generation, dividend yield, and growth-at-a-reasonable-price metrics rather than headline GAAP earnings. The investment case rests on a scaled, hospital-affiliated outpatient platform (post–Physicians Realty Trust merger), durable lab exposure in San Francisco/Boston/San Diego, and capital-recycling initiatives—notably the Janus Living senior-housing IPO (completed March 2026) and a $2.1bn Brookfield outpatient joint venture—that aim to unlock value and de-risk the balance sheet. Valuation support comes from a ~5.5% dividend yield, ~7.2% FCF yield, and low screen PEG (0.19), though the reported P/E (69.6x) and ROE (2.8%) are distorted by REIT accounting and one-off items. The central debate is whether rising interest expense, lab-tenant cyclicality, and complex corporate actions (Janus Living, JVs) mask or enhance underlying NOI growth; filing coverage is strong on interim quarters but annual income-statement detail in 10-K body extracts is incomplete.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags DOC across all four value families—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—with 7 of 22 models passing, 20/20 data quality, and a persistent buy signal. For a value investor, the screen’s FCF Yield and High Dividend Yield passes align with a REIT whose operating cash flow (Yahoo fallback: $1.25bn FCF in 2025) comfortably covers dividends, while Lynch/Neff PEG passes capture earnings-growth optionality from portfolio repositioning rather than pure cyclical recovery.

Business quality is reasonable for a healthcare landlord: 689 properties at year-end 2025 (507 outpatient medical, 145 lab, 34 senior housing), with outpatient buildings 96% hospital-affiliated and lab space 89% leased (FY2025 10-K body). Adjusted NOI rose across core segments in 2025—outpatient medical to $796m, lab $567m, senior housing $177m (FY2025 10-K)—reflecting scale from the March 2024 Physicians Realty merger and organic leasing. Q1 2026 revenue of $753m (+7% YoY per Q1 2026 10-Q) and nine-month 2025 revenue of $2.10bn (+5% YoY per Q3 2025 10-Q) confirm top-line momentum despite flat Q2 2025 quarterly revenue.

The screen’s “risk” family pass is notable given ~$10.4bn gross debt (Q1 2026 10-Q: commercial paper $1.75bn, term loans $1.65bn, senior notes $6.78bn, mortgage $0.25bn) and rising interest expense ($87m in Q1 2026 vs $73m in Q1 2025). Management reports covenant compliance (Enterprise Total Indebtedness to Enterprise Gross Asset Value ≤60%) at March 2026. Cash of $1.17bn at quarter-end reflects Janus Living IPO proceeds, providing liquidity buffer. Capital recycling—$72m gains on 2025 dispositions and ongoing asset sales—supports the dividend-growth narrative without requiring aggressive external equity issuance.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Sector and tenant cyclicality.** Filings emphasise concentration in healthcare real estate and lab tenants’ dependence on venture funding, NIH grants, and FDA approval timelines. Policy shifts affecting NIH indirect-cost reimbursements, drug pricing, and Medicaid/Affordable Care Act subsidies (FY2025 10-K risk factors) could impair lab demand and hospital-affiliated outpatient volumes.

**Lab segment softness.** Adjusted lab NOI fell 3.9% in 2025 to $567m despite portfolio scale, and lab occupancy was 89% (vs outpatient 72% leased). Repositioning lab space is capital-intensive relative to other property types (10-K risk language).

**Leverage and interest-rate sensitivity.** Gross debt exceeds $10bn; interest expense rose across all reported interim periods. Filings warn that increased rates could constrain refinancing, dispositions, and dividend coverage. Covenant breach risk is disclosed though the company reported compliance at March, June, and September 2025 and March 2026.

**RIDEA / senior-housing operational risk.** Senior housing (including life-plan communities) is operated through taxable REIT subsidiaries with third-party managers; Healthpeak bears operational liabilities including staffing, regulatory compliance, and resident-care claims with limited control (10-K). Janus Living IPO adds structural complexity: Healthpeak retains ~81% economic/voting interest, consolidates Janus Living, and faces conflicts-of-interest and mark-to-market exposure on the listed subsidiary.

**Joint-venture and earnings volatility.** Nine-month 2025 equity losses of $177m from unconsolidated JVs highlight earnings unpredictability from partnership structures. The SWF JV buyout and Brookfield outpatient JV (announced July 2026, ~$1.025bn proceeds cited in news) should simplify the structure but execution risk remains.

**Tenant concentration.** HCA at 7% of total revenues creates single-tenant exposure within an otherwise diversified outpatient book.

**Regulatory and reimbursement.** Extensive Medicare/Medicaid fraud-and-abuse, licensure, and CMS disclosure requirements apply to operators; reimbursement cuts could flow through to rent-paying capacity.

**No going-concern flag** appears in available filing extracts. Commitments and contingencies are referenced (Note 10/11) but detailed litigation/contingency amounts are not in truncated bodies.
