# Dell Technologies Inc. (DELL) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T12:32:27.322730+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Dell Technologies presents a GARP-oriented value case: the quantitative screen rates it a buy on growth-adjusted metrics (Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Magic Formula, Earnings Quality, Dividend Growth), whilst traditional deep-value and quality screens fail on leverage, negative book equity, and a 32x P/E. FY2026 (ended 30 January 2026) showed a sharp earnings and free-cash-flow inflection, and Q1 FY2027 revenue of $43.8bn (+87% year-on-year) confirms AI infrastructure and commercial PC demand as the growth engine. The valuation hook is rapid earnings compounding at a screen-implied low PEG, offset by a structurally levered balance sheet (negative stockholders’ equity, ~$31bn debt) and cyclical AI-server margin risk. The central debate is whether AI order momentum and capital return can sustain EPS growth, or whether memory shortages, customer concentration, and mix-driven margin compression will re-rate the stock.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Dell qualifies as a **growth-at-a-reasonable-price compounder** rather than a classic asset-based or net-net candidate. The screen passes four of four factor families (cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk) via growth-adjusted valuation models, not absolute cheapness: Graham, Deep Value, FCF Yield, and Buffett Quality all fail. That profile fits a business transitioning from PC-centric cyclicality toward AI server infrastructure, with improving earnings quality (Piotroski F-Score 6/9; positive net income, OCF > net income, ROA improving).

Business quality anchors include: (1) **ISG/CSG franchise** — Infrastructure Solutions Group and Client Solutions Group are the reportable segments, with ISG goodwill of $15.3bn and CSG goodwill of $4.2bn at 30 January 2026 (Q1 FY2027 10-Q); (2) **backlog visibility** — remaining performance obligations of approximately **$97bn** at 1 May 2026, with ~80% expected to convert within twelve months (Q1 FY2027 10-Q); (3) **capital return** — FY2026 share repurchases of $6.4bn and dividends of $1.5bn (Yahoo fallback); Q1 FY2027 repurchases of $1.6bn and dividends of $464m (Q1 10-Q). The screen’s flagged 282.5% earnings growth and 62.0% dividend yield appear distorted (likely special-dividend or base-effect artefacts in the screening inputs); treat headline yield with caution and rely on filing-confirmed cash dividends ($0.630 per share declared in Q1 FY2027).

The buy case ties screen output to fundamentals: FY2026 diluted EPS rose to **$8.68** from **$6.38** (Yahoo fallback — 10-K body extracts contain balance-sheet notes only, not the income statement), and Q1 FY2027 diluted EPS of **$5.24** versus **$1.37** prior year (Q1 10-Q) validates the growth narrative underpinning Lynch/Neff model passes. This is a **cash-generative, shareholder-return story at a premium headline multiple**, not a margin-of-safety deep-value entry.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Balance sheet and liquidity**
- **Negative stockholders’ equity** and **current ratio 0.91** (screen inputs) — current liabilities exceed current assets; working capital deficit of $5.7bn at 30 January 2026 (Yahoo). This is typical for Dell’s deferred-revenue model but limits traditional financial-health screen passes.
- **Total debt ~$31bn**; net debt ~$20bn (Yahoo). Interest expense $1.56bn in FY2026 (10-K notes). Q1 10-Q confirms revolving credit facility covenant compliance and **interest coverage ratio** tested quarterly — company was in compliance at 1 May 2026. Indentures impose lien and sale-leaseback limitations; no going-concern language in available extracts.
- **DFS securitisation facilities** contain performance triggers (credit losses, delinquencies); criteria were met at 1 May 2026, but failure would delay receivable funding (Q1 10-Q, Note 4).

**Tax and legal**
- **IRS examination** of fiscal years 2018–2019 with “significant adjustments” under protest; appeals expected to extend beyond twelve months. FY2020–2022 audit ongoing. Company states unfavourable outcomes **could materially affect** results (Q1 10-Q, Note 11). Net unrecognized tax benefits: **$1.1bn**.
- **ERISA class action** (*Lowbruck et al. v. Dell Technologies*, filed 28 January 2026) alleging fiduciary breaches in the Dell 401(k) plan — damages unspecified; company intends to defend (Q1 10-Q, Note 10).
- Company discloses no reasonable possibility of material loss exceeding accrued amounts across proceedings as of 1 May 2026, but notes outcomes could be material individually.

**Operational and cyclical**
- **Margin compression** evident in Q1 FY2027 gross margin (17.8% vs 21.1% prior-year quarter) despite revenue surge — aligns with news on AI-server margin concerns and memory shortages.
- **Customer concentration** in AI servers flagged in recent press (TechStock², 28 July 2026); not quantified in available filing extracts.
- **Purchase obligations** of **$16.9bn** for remainder of FY2027 (Q1 10-Q, Note 10) — component supply commitments create execution risk if demand softens.
- **Severance/restructuring:** $569m charges in FY2026; $136m liability remaining (10-K notes).
- **Pension:** actuarial net losses in accumulated OCI ($24m at 1 May 2026) — immaterial relative to scale.

**Governance**
- Michael S. Dell (Chair/CEO) and Silver Lake maintain control via multi-class structure and stockholder agreements. CFO transition: David Kennedy appointed permanent CFO 24 November 2025 (8-K/A, 25 November 2025). Yvonne McGill separation September 2025 (referenced in Q3 10-Q exhibits index).
- Executive 10b5-1 sales plans disclosed in FY2026 10-K (Jeffrey Clarke, Richard Sharp).
