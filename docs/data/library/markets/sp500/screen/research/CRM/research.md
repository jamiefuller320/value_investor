# Salesforce, Inc. (CRM) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:49:02.541123+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Salesforce remains the scale leader in enterprise CRM, pivoting aggressively toward Agentforce AI and data-platform monetisation (Data 360, Informatica). The quantitative screen flags a rare combination of quality, cash generation, and relative cheapness (P/E ~18, FCF yield ~12%, PEG ~0.35) against a franchise with recurring revenue and rising profitability. Valuation appeal rests on cash compounding and margin expansion offsetting mid-single-digit organic growth, not on a re-rating to historical SaaS multiples. The central debate is whether Agentforce and public-sector wins (e.g. the $1.6bn VA contract) re-accelerate growth, or whether AI-native competitors and sceptical sell-side commentary keep the stock in a “show me” penalty box despite solid fundamentals.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy rests on five passing factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with standout metrics including FCF yield (12.3%), Lynch/Neff PEG (~0.35), ROE (16.9%), and models such as FCF Yield, Buffett Quality, Piotroski F-Score, and Earnings Quality. That profile fits a value investor seeking **cash-rich compounders trading below growth-implied multiples**, not deep cyclical bargains.

Business quality remains high: subscription-led revenue, no customer >10% of sales (10-K risk disclosures), and contracted backlog (RPO) well above reported revenue. Interim filings show sustained mid/high-single-digit revenue growth with operating leverage—Q1 FY26 revenue $11.1bn (+13% y/y), Q3 FY26 revenue $10.3bn (+9% y/y), and nine-month FY26 revenue $30.3bn (+9% y/y)—while net income and operating cash flow are tracking ahead of prior year. Capital returns (dividend ~1.1% yield plus large buybacks) add a shareholder-yield dimension rarely seen in classic growth SaaS.

The screen’s “growth=52.2%” headline likely reflects normalised or forward-looking inputs; **filed revenue growth is ~9–10%**, which is still respectable for a $40bn+ revenue platform. The value case is therefore **quality + cash + reasonable multiple**, with optional upside if AI/agent monetisation converts scepticism into renewed growth premium.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**AI disruption and Agentforce execution.** Filings warn that generative/agentic AI startups may bypass traditional workflows, and that Agentforce/Data 360 monetisation remains “relatively new and uncertain.” News flow mirrors this: *“Salesforce Downgraded On Views 'Agentforce, As A Product, Just Isn't There'”* (Investor's Business Daily, 9 Jul 2025) vs management claims of **$1bn Agentforce run-rate** (*Stocktwits*, 24 Jul 2026). The market is pricing failure until proven otherwise.

**Integration and leverage from Informatica.** Completion announced 18 Nov 2025 (8-K). FY26 10-K cites **$6.0bn** borrowing under Informatica credit agreements; Q1 FY26 shows a much larger debt/repurchase spike. Acquisition integration, goodwill impairment risk (goodwill **$57.9bn** at FY26 YE), and amortisation drag are material.

**Balance-sheet quality.** Goodwill plus intangibles ~**$65bn**; negative tangible equity. Deferred revenue drives negative working capital (**–$8.9bn** per Yahoo FY26)—normal for SaaS but magnifies perceived risk if growth slows.

**Competitive/regulatory.** 10-K risk factors emphasise cybersecurity breaches, privacy regulation, international/trade restrictions, and AI ethics/regulatory scrutiny. No pension-related flags surfaced in extracts.

**Governance / capital allocation.** Heavy SBC (~**$3.5bn** FY26 per Yahoo cash-flow) partially offsets buyback economics. Filings note ERP implementation in Q2 FY26 with potential reporting disruption risk.

**Cyclical / macro.** Enterprise software budget scrutiny; sector-wide AI-displacement fear (*“How Snowflake Stock Is Keeping Cool While Other Software Stocks Melt”*, Investors.com, 24 Jul 2026, referencing Salesforce weakness).

**Sell-side sentiment.** *“Morgan Stanley Slashes Salesforce Price Target By 35%”* (Yahoo Finance, 22 Jul 2025); second downgrade citing balanced risk/reward without growth inflection (TradingView/Yahoo, 21 Jul 2025). These do not appear in the screen but weigh on timing.
