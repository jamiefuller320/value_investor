# Loews Corporation (L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:19:32.031013+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Loews Corporation is a Tisch-family-controlled conglomerate whose value rests on a sum-of-parts discount to CNA Financial (~92% owned, ~81% of revenue), Boardwalk Pipeline Partners, Loews Hotels, and a 53% unconsolidated stake in Altium Packaging. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness (P/E ~15×, P/B ~1.3×), moderate leverage (D/E ~46%), and strong reported free cash flow (~$2.1bn), though it fails most quality screens (Piotroski 6/9; no Graham Defensive, Buffett Quality, or Economic Moat pass). FY2025 was strong (net income to Loews shareholders rose materially year on year per Yahoo fallback data), but the most recent interim filing shows Q1 2026 earnings down 9% year on year, driven mainly by weaker CNA underwriting. The central debate is whether a holding-company discount and aggressive share repurchases outweigh cyclical insurance headwinds, heavy Boardwalk growth capex (~$3.3bn through 2030), and the screen’s anomalous 21% “yield” figure, which does not reconcile with filings.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Loews offers a classic conglomerate arbitrage: a well-capitalised insurance anchor, contracted midstream growth, and a recovering hotels platform, trading below the implied value of its largest parts. The screen’s buy signal (6/22 models; composite 57%; families: cheapness, dividend, risk) aligns with moderate absolute multiples and a multi-year pattern of shrinking the share count—diluted shares fell from ~221m in Q1 2025 to ~206m in Q1 2026 per the 10-Q, with $806m of parent repurchases in FY2025 (Yahoo fallback). Passed models including Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Acquirer’s Multiple, and Dreman Contrarian fit a contrarian, asset-backed profile rather than a high-quality compounder. CNA’s underlying combined ratio (~97% in Q1 2026) suggests the core franchise remains acceptable, while Boardwalk and Hotels contributed offsetting growth in the latest quarter. The holding-company structure also embeds optionality (Altium Packaging equity method; further buybacks) that screens do not fully capture. Offsetting this, the screen’s 21% yield and “dividend” family pass are not corroborated by filings—cash dividends paid were only ~$52m in FY2025 (Yahoo)—so the income angle is weak; the case is really repurchase-driven value, not yield.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Insurance and CNA (~81% of revenue): Filings emphasise reserve adequacy risk—“CNA may need to increase its insurance reserves if it determines that its recorded reserves are insufficient”—along with social inflation, catastrophe volatility, asbestos/environmental pollution legacy exposure (LPT with NICO capped at $4.0bn; $3.7bn ceded as of the FY2024 10-K narrative), mass-tort/reviver-statute risk, and long-tail care-policy reserve sensitivity. Q1 2026 showed reported combined ratio above 100% and unfavourable prior-year development at CNA per the 10-Q MD&A. Allowance for credit losses on investments rose to $69m at 31 Mar 2026 vs $45m a year earlier.

Boardwalk Pipeline: FY2025 10-K cites ~$3.3bn of growth projects through 2030 (4.2 Bcf/d incremental capacity), contingent on FERC approvals, permitting, and construction risk; filings flag FERC rate regulation, pipeline safety rules, environmental/permitting uncertainty (NEPA, WOTUS), customer concentration, debt covenants on the revolving credit facility, and climate-related reputational and operational risks. Substantial capex could constrain upstream dividends to the parent.

Holding-company structure: The parent “derives substantially all of its income and cash flow from its subsidiaries”; insurance regulatory limits on CNA dividends and Boardwalk capital needs create a structural cash upstream constraint. Filings state there are “no Parent Company guarantees” of subsidiary debt—creditors have priority at subsidiary level.

Governance: Continued Tisch family influence (Benjamin J. Tisch became CEO in January 2025; multiple family relationships among officers/directors per FY2025 10-K). Not necessarily value-destructive, but limits external governance pressure.

Screen data quality: The 21.0% “yield” in `screening_snapshot.json` is inconsistent with ~$52m annual dividends on ~206m shares (<0.5% cash yield). Treat screen dividend metrics with caution.

No going-concern language was found in available filing extracts; liquidity discussions reference subsidiary distributions and parent cash/investments without covenant breach disclosure in the Q1 2026 extract.
