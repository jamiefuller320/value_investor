# FedEx Corporation (FDX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:57:38.820624+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
FedEx offers a classic value entry into a globally scaled logistics franchise undergoing a deliberate simplification: Network 2.0, the FedEx Freight spin-off (completed 1 June 2026), and portfolio pruning (Supply Chain sale, InPost stake) aim to raise returns on a structurally lower-cost express network. The quantitative screen flags cheapness (P/E ~17, FCF yield ~7.6%), dividend quality, GARP, and risk metrics across four factor families, with full data quality (20/20). The valuation hook is cash-generative express economics at a mid-teens multiple after a sharp re-rating, tempered by freight weakness pre-spin, spin-related accounting noise, and an unsettled trade/tariff regime. The central debate is whether post-spin FedEx is a cleaner, higher-FCF parcel compounder—or a cyclical name still absorbing transformation costs while Amazon and UPS compete aggressively on price and capacity.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s Strong Buy signal rests on inexpensive cash returns from a business with durable network advantages. FedEx’s integrated air–ground system, brand, and scale (220+ countries; ~700 aircraft in the pre-spin structure) are difficult to replicate; management is explicitly pivoting from a portfolio of separate operations to “one FedEx” with Network 2.0 and digital/AI tooling. Quantitatively, FDX passes FCF Yield, Neff PEGY (0.11), Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality—covering cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk families with sector-relative composite 82% versus 87% absolute.

For a value investor, the case is not heroic growth but **normalised earnings power at a discount**: ROE ~14.8%, dividend yield ~1.6% with declared quarterly growth ($1.45/share in February 2026 vs $1.38 prior year), and FCF yield ~7.6% on screening inputs. Business quality is supported by improving express margins in interim filings—Federal Express segment operating income rose to $4.3bn in the first nine months of FY2026 from $3.3bn a year earlier—while transformation charges (business optimisation, separation) are identifiable and largely non-recurring in nature. The screen’s flagged “growth = -4.3%” sits awkwardly against filing-reported revenue acceleration; treat that metric as a screen artefact pending harmonised post-spin reporting rather than a confirmed top-line decline.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Structural / spin-off:** The Freight distribution (80.1% to holders on 1 June 2026; FedEx retains 19.9%) reshapes segments, comparability, and leverage at the pro forma express entity. The 8-K (13 May 2026) warns the separation may not deliver intended benefits and creates transaction uncertainty. Restricted cash and $3.7bn Freight Holding notes add balance-sheet complexity pre- and post-spin.

**Cyclicality:** The Q3 FY2026 MD&A cites weakened transport conditions, declining U.S. consumer imports since late 2022, and **lower FedEx Freight shipments** directly hurting results—classic cyclical exposure not fully captured by static value screens.

**Trade and tariffs:** Global trade policy shifts (de minimis removal, IEEPA tariff invalidation by the U.S. Supreme Court on 20 February 2026) create **unquantified** receivable/refund risk. FedEx states it **cannot reasonably estimate** financial impact; a material outcome is **reasonably possible**. Related litigation (FedEx suit for IEEPA refunds; five customer class actions) adds legal overhang.

**Competition:** FY2026 10-K narrative flags UPS, DHL, regional carriers, and **Amazon’s expanding delivery and LTL capabilities** as direct competitive threats—particularly price-sensitive e-commerce volumes.

**Labour and pension:** Pilot collective bargaining was unresolved in the February 10-Q; a new CBA was subsequently ratified per FY2026 10-K business disclosure (effective June 2026, amendable December 2030)—wage inflation is a forward margin risk. U.S. defined-benefit pension is ERISA fully funded (no mandatory 2026 contribution); voluntary **$275m** contribution in 9M FY2026. Pension/post-retirement obligations ~$1.7bn on the February balance sheet remain a long-tail liability.

**Operational:** Boeing 737 MAX grounding directive (November 2025) affected operations in Q3 FY2026. Network 2.0 and international workforce reductions ($225–325m estimated pre-tax over time) carry execution risk.

**Governance / legal:** Contingency note: aggregate litigation liability “will not have a material adverse effect” on position, results, or cash flows **except** where tariff/refund outcomes remain unbooked. **No going-concern language** identified in available filing bodies; standard forward-looking caution applies throughout.

**Environmental/regulatory:** CORSIA, EU ETS, SAF mandates, and export-control strict liability (penalties up to $1m criminal / $378k civil per violation cited in 10-K) are ongoing cost and compliance risks.
