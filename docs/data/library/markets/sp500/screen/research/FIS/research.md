# Fidelity National Information Services, Inc. (FIS) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:12:34.066774+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Fidelity National Information Services (FIS) screens as a strong buy on classic value metrics — trailing P/E ~7.8x, P/B ~1.3x, dividend yield ~4.2%, and ROE ~17% — against a recurring-revenue banking and capital-markets technology franchise. The investment case rests on a simplified post-Worldpay model, the January 2026 acquisition of Global Payments’ Issuer Solutions business, and mid-single-digit organic revenue growth with improving Banking margins. The central debate is whether headline cheapness survives materially higher leverage (~$16.9bn total debt post-deal), heavy goodwill/intangibles, and integration plus interest-cost drag. We see a credible value entry for patient investors, but earnings quality is currently distorted by one-offs and the balance sheet is no longer conservative.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags FIS with 13 of 22 models passing (composite score 94%), spanning cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families — unusually broad for a technology name. That aligns with a business whose revenue is predominantly recurring (82% of Q1 2026 revenue per the March 2026 10-Q) and contractually underpinned (~$25.5bn of remaining performance obligations as of 31 March 2026).

For a value investor, the hook is a de-risked strategic pivot: FIS sold 55% of Worldpay in January 2024, exited its remaining 45% stake on 9 January 2026, and redeployed proceeds plus ~$7.7bn of new debt into Issuer Solutions — repositioning as a core banking, payments-issuer, and capital-markets platform provider rather than a merchant-acquiring conglomerate. Annual revenue from continuing operations rose from $9.83bn (2023) to $10.13bn (2024) and $10.68bn (2025) per the FY2025 10-K segment table — roughly 4% CAGR with Banking Solutions growing from $6.74bn to $7.29bn.

Interim filings confirm operational momentum into the deal close: nine-month 2025 revenue reached $7.87bn (+4% YoY), Banking Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 45.8% in Q3 2025 (from 45.2%), and Q1 2026 revenue of $3.30bn (+30% reported; +7% pro forma at $3.35bn vs $3.13bn) reflects Issuer Solutions contribution. The screen’s FCF yield and dividend-growth passes are supported by historically strong cash conversion — though 2025 free cash flow and full-year cash-flow statement detail are not available in the truncated 10-K body extracts and are cited from Yahoo below.

Business quality markers include Fortune 500/S&P 500 membership, FFIEC-supervised bank-technology provider status, and sticky multi-year processing contracts. At ~7x earnings and ~4% yield, the market appears to price execution risk from the Worldpay era and balance-sheet concerns rather than a broken franchise.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Leverage and interest burden: Total debt rose ~63% in Q1 2026 to fund Issuer Solutions. The 10-Q states management expects “future cash paid for interest to increase from historic levels.” With ~$4.2bn in commercial paper and $17bn total debt, refinancing and rate sensitivity are material; covenant detail is not in the available 10-K extract.

Integration and goodwill: The Issuer Solutions purchase price was ~$13.5bn (enterprise value), adding ~$6.5bn goodwill and ~$3.6bn intangibles. Goodwill impairment was not triggered at 31 March 2026 (qualitative assessment), but $16m Corporate goodwill impairment was recorded in Q1. Purchase-price allocation remains provisional.

Worldpay legacy exposures: Through 31 December 2026, FIS maintains parental guarantees on Worldpay standby letters of credit ($288m capacity) and a CVR liability ($378m due October 2027). The company considers loss “remote” but indemnification complexity persists.

Litigation and governance: Securities class action over former Merchant Solutions disclosures reached settlement (expected substantially insurance-funded; preliminary approval February 2026; final hearing July 2026 per Q1 10-Q). Shareholder derivative demands and related actions (Hialeah, McCollum) remain active; the board rejected demands in November 2025 and moved to dismiss. News reports a $210m settlement approval (10 July 2026).

Regulatory intensity: FIS is an FFIEC-examined bank service provider, faces CFPB/SEC/FINRA oversight in multiple subsidiaries, and was designated a DORA Critical Provider in November 2025 — adding direct ESA supervision for EU financial-institution clients. April 2025 news flagged regulatory scrutiny of the name.

Earnings quality: Reported Q1 2026 EPS is not representative of run-rate economics. Equity-method Worldpay swings ($526m loss in FY2025 per Yahoo; $2.2bn gain in Q1 2026 per 10-Q) distort P/E-based screens.

Operational/transformation costs: Q1 2026 M&A integration ($55m) and enterprise transformation ($93m) totalled $148m in Corporate & Other; severance continues through 2026.

Competitive/cyclical: Filings warn of intense competition from global banks’ in-house tech, fintech disruptors, and pricing pressure on contract renewals; transaction volumes are economically sensitive.

Pension/employee benefits: Yahoo balance sheet shows non-current employee benefit/pension obligations (~$491m combined); detailed pension funding status is not in available 10-K extracts.
