# Kenvue Inc. (KVUE) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T12:28:07.304044+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Kenvue is a global consumer health business (Tylenol, Neutrogena, Listerine, Zyrtec, Aveeno) that the quantitative screen flags as a buy on cash generation, dividend yield, and earnings-recovery metrics rather than classic deep-value multiples. FY2025 filings show net income rebounding to $1.47bn and operating cash flow rising to $2.20bn, but net sales slipped 2% to $15.1bn and the balance sheet remains leverage-heavy with negative working capital. The central debate is whether ~4.4% dividend yield and improving margins justify holding through Tylenol litigation and a pending Kimberly-Clark merger (0.14625 KMB shares plus $3.50 cash per KVUE share), which caps standalone upside but offers a defined exit path. We view this as an income-plus-event name, not a Graham-style bargain.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s buy signal rests on five passing factor families—cheapness (FCF yield ~5%), quality (Buffett Quality, Piotroski 7/9), dividend (4.4% yield, dividend growth), GARP (PEG ~0.49, though earnings growth is flattered by the FY2024 impairment trough), and risk (earnings quality pass). That profile fits a post-spin consumer franchise stabilising after a difficult FY2024, not a net-net or low-P/B deep-value candidate.

Business quality is genuine: iconic OTC and personal-care brands, global distribution, and durable cash conversion. FY2025 10-K operating cash flow of $2,197m and capital expenditure of $475m imply free cash flow of roughly $1.7bn (operating cash flow less capex), up from $1,769m / $434m ($1.3bn FCF) in FY2024. Net income rose 43% to $1,470m ($0.76 diluted EPS) as FY2024’s $578m impairment (chiefly Dr. J® in China) did not repeat; FY2025 impairments were only $23m. Interim Q1 FY2026 (10-Q, period ended 29 March 2026) showed net sales of $3,909m (+4.5% y/y) and net income of $474m (+47% y/y), suggesting operational momentum into the merger window.

For a value investor, the hook is not asset cheapness (P/B 3.5x; tangible book deeply negative due to $18.2bn goodwill and intangibles) but cash-backed income at a reasonable earnings multiple (P/E ~23x) with a credible capital-return programme ($1,581m dividends in FY2025; quarterly $0.21 declared per news flow). The Kimberly-Clark transaction, shareholder-approved on 29 January 2026 with US antitrust clearance on 4 February 2026, adds a merger-arbitrage overlay: holders receive stock plus cash rather than a pure re-rating story.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Merger / strategic.** The 8-K merger agreement (2 November 2025) offers 0.14625 Kimberly-Clark shares plus $3.50 cash per KVUE share, with an outside date of 2 November 2026 (extendable to 3 May 2027 for regulatory approvals). Termination fee: $1.136bn. Shareholders approved the deal on 29 January 2026; US HSR waiting period expired 4 February 2026, but foreign regulatory approvals remain. Failure to close, a superior bid, or adverse terms revision would hit the share price; upside as a standalone value re-rating is limited while the deal is live.

**Litigation / regulatory.** Filings repeatedly cite pending litigation as an input to goodwill and intangible impairment testing. News flow (e.g. “KVUE Stock Dips After Court Revives Tylenol Autism Lawsuits,” 13 July 2025) highlights acetaminophen product-liability exposure. The 10-K accrues product-liability claims when probable and estimable but does not quantify Tylenol-specific reserves in the available extract—material legal exposure remains unquantified in this pack.

**Operational / competitive.** FY2024’s $488m Dr. J® impairment reflects weak China consumer sentiment and shifting retail patterns in Skin Health and Beauty. FY2025 ORSL® trade-name impairment ($23m) followed regulatory changes in India. Inflation, tariffs, and supply-chain cost pressure are flagged in MD&A; management partially offset via pricing, but margin protection is not guaranteed.

**Balance sheet / governance.** ~$8.5bn gross debt (current plus long-term per FY2025 10-K) against $1.1bn cash; screen debt/equity ~83%. Negative working capital and failed Financial Health / Economic Moat screens (ROE 15.7% below 18% threshold; high leverage) warrant caution. Separation-era J&J indemnities (tax, product liability) add counterparty dependency. CEO Kirk Perry was appointed permanently on 2 November 2025 after an interim period from July 2025; related-party Circana data purchases (~$7.6m in 2024) disclosed in the 8-K.

**Pension.** Non-current employee benefit obligations of $340m (FY2025 10-K); not a primary red flag but a fixed cash claim on earnings.
