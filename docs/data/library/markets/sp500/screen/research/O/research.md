# Realty Income Corporation (O) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T13:31:59.686087+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Realty Income Corporation (NYSE: O) is the largest net-lease REIT by scale, branded as “The Monthly Dividend Company,” with 15,500+ properties across the US, UK and continental Europe. The quantitative screen flags a **buy** on dividend yield (5.2%), dividend growth, Lynch/Neff PEG metrics, and earnings quality — not on deep value or balance-sheet quality. Filings show accelerating revenue and earnings through 2025 and H1 2026, a 99%-leased portfolio, and $5.3bn annualised base rent, but also rising interest expense, a larger credit book, and ongoing real-estate impairments. The central debate is whether dependable monthly income and acquisition-led AFFO growth justify a rich headline P/E (~46×) and leveraged, rate-sensitive capital structure in a ~4.7% 10-year yield environment.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented income investor, O offers a rare combination of scale, contractual rent growth, and 31+ consecutive years of dividend increases (133 raises since 1994 IPO). The screen passes five of 22 models across three families — **dividend**, **GARP**, and **risk** — with composite score 58% (sector-relative 52%), full data quality (20/20 metrics), and an improving one-week signal. Lynch PEG (0.66) and Neff PEGY (0.08) reflect strong reported earnings growth (69.2%) against a 5.2% yield, aligning with a business that grows via accretive acquisitions rather than deep discount.

Business quality supports the screen’s income bias. The FY2025 10-K (filed 25 Feb 2026) describes a portfolio of 15,511 properties that was 99% leased, generating $5.31bn annualised base rent, with 32.2% from investment-grade tenants and the top 20 clients at 35.8% of rent. Diversification is improving: UK/Europe reached 19% of base rent (from 14% in 2024), with industrial at 15.4% and gaming at 3.1%. Management is expanding beyond pure equity ownership into loans/preferred equity ($3.1bn at year-end 2025, per 10-K) and private-capital vehicles (U.S. Core Plus Fund, Apollo JV), broadening the investment pipeline while retaining fee income.

What the screen does **not** capture is equally important. O fails 17 models, including Graham, Schloss, Deep Value, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, and Financial Health (high net debt/EBITDA, weak interest coverage). P/B of 1.5×, ROE of 3.2% (typical for depreciating-asset REITs), FCF yield of 2.7%, and Piotroski F-Score of 5/9 all signal this is an **income compounder at fair-to-full price**, not a classic deep-value REIT. The buy case rests on dividend durability and growth-at-a-reasonable-peg, not margin of safety on asset values.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** Total debt rose to $29.3bn at year-end 2025 (Yahoo fallback) and gross interest-bearing obligations exceeded $30bn by mid-2026 (10-Q). Interest expense grew faster than revenue in 2024–2025 and again in H1 2026. The screen flags high net debt/EBITDA and weak interest coverage; with the 10-year Treasury near 4.7% (macro context, colour only), refinancing and spread risk remain material despite A-range market access cited in news.

**Acquisition dependence and integration.** The FY2025 10-K risk factors emphasise that growth requires continuous property acquisition in competitive markets, with no assurance of accretive returns. H1 2026 alone deployed $3.6bn; failure to maintain investment spreads above the cost of capital would impair dividend growth.

**Tenant and sector concentration.** Top-20 clients represent 35.8% of annualised base rent (10-K). Dollar General, Walgreens, Family Dollar, and 7-Eleven feature prominently — exposure to discount retail and pharmacy headwinds. Filing language on client bankruptcy, lease rejection, and rent recapture is extensive; impairments of **$144m** (H1 2026) and credit-loss provisions of **$46m** on the loan book confirm ongoing asset-level stress, albeit lower than H1 2025.

**Strategic complexity.** Expansion into Europe (19% of rent), gaming (Bellagio JV), data centres, and a **$4.9bn** credit portfolio introduces underwriting, regulatory (gaming laws per 10-K), and foreign-exchange risks. The Apollo JV ($1.0bn third-party capital, March 2026) and U.S. Core Plus Fund ($1.7bn commitments) add consolidation and non-controlling-interest complexity.

**Capital markets and dilution.** Equity issuance remains central: $830m net share issuance in H1 2026 (10-Q), a new 150m-share ATM programme (8-K), and convertible notes create potential dilution despite capped-call hedging (news). Share count rose from 892m (end-2024) to 946m (Jun 2026).

**Valuation.** The screen’s own failures — earnings yield 2.2%, FCF yield 2.7%, EV/EBIT 35.7× — indicate limited traditional value margin. News commentary (“Realty Income Stock Looks Fully Priced,” 15 Aug 2026) aligns with this.
