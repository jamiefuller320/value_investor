# The Cooper Companies, Inc. (COO) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-13T06:48:09.451312+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Cooper Companies (COO) is a dual-segment medtech franchise—CooperVision (contact lenses, including MiSight myopia management) and CooperSurgical (women’s health and fertility)—that the quantitative screen rates as a buy on cheapness, GARP-style metrics, and balance-sheet-oriented risk models (composite score ~76%, FCF yield ~5.3%, P/E ~18.5, P/B ~1.2). The valuation case assumes durable cash generation and a moderate multiple after a sharp post-earnings de-rating, not deep “quality compounder” credentials (ROE ~6.8%; most quality/moat screens fail). The central debate is whether CooperVision can normalise after U.S. channel destocking and Asia softness while CooperSurgical funds growth and absorbs litigation and strategic-review costs, versus a market that has already punished the name on revenue misses and lowered forward expectations.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented allocator, COO offers a cash-backed healthcare business at a screen-friendly price: five of twenty-two models pass, spanning FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Composite Value, Earnings Quality, and Financial Health, with high input completeness (19/20 metrics) and sector-relative composite strength. The economic engine is recurring consumables in vision (silicone hydrogel daily/monthly franchises, toric/multifocal mix) plus a diversified surgical/fertility portfolio; FY2025 scale is roughly $4.1bn revenue with operating cash flow of $796m and free cash flow of $434m per Yahoo annual data (the 10-K body extracts in this pack do not include Item 8 statement tables, so those annual totals are taken from `financials_annual.json`, not from filing line items). That profile supports the screen’s emphasis on FCF yield and financial health rather than dividend yield or classic Graham deep value (P/B is only ~1.2, not below book; no dividend gate pass). The mismatch with “Buffett/moat” families is instructive: reported ROE is modest, amortisation and acquisition intangibles weigh on book returns, and near-term operating momentum has softened—nine-month fiscal 2026 operating income is down versus the prior year in the Q3 10-Q, even as revenue grinds higher. The buy signal is therefore better read as “moderately priced cash generator with operational noise,” not “hidden quality at a deep discount.”

At July 31, 2026, total debt was **$2,544.2m** (up from $2,505.3m at October 2025), with **$316.5m accrued litigation liability**; the company reported compliance with debt covenants. Working capital fell to **$414.9m** from $993.6m year-end, driven mainly by current debt classification. Share repurchases accelerated (nine months ~$447m at ~$72 average).

**Prior interim reference (10-Q April 30, 2026):** Q2 included the bulk of the litigation accrual (~$271.6m net), producing a GAAP operating loss at consolidated level for the quarter.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Regulatory and product risk remain elevated: EU MDR/IVDR recertification, FDA device and pharma rules (including Paragard), and fertility lab/genomic compliance are recurring themes in the 10-K risk narrative. **Product recall litigation** (CooperSurgical LifeGlobal embryo culture media, December 2023) crystallised in a large FY2026 accrual and settlement payments; reputational and tail litigation risk in fertility is material. **Tax/contingent liability:** HMRC payroll tax dispute on the 2014 Sauflon acquisition—FTT decision largely against the company (March 2026); appeal ongoing; reasonably possible loss up to **£71.7m plus interest**, no balance sheet accrual yet. **Operational/cyclical:** CooperVision U.S. destocking and Asia Pacific softness; customer consolidation increases concentration risk (though no single customer >10% of revenue in FY2025 per 10-K narrative). **Leverage and liquidity:** ~$2.5bn debt, meaningful revolver utilisation, and reduced working capital cushion; variable rates and swap hedges add earnings sensitivity. **Governance/strategy:** Management completed a strategic review and **retained CooperSurgical** (per news flow around September 2026 earnings), limiting near-term portfolio simplification. **Accounting:** Low reported ROE, heavy intangibles (~$5.4bn goodwill and intangibles at October 2025 per Yahoo), and volatile effective tax rates driven by discrete items—screen “earnings quality” pass merits sceptical cross-check. Plaintiff-firm “investigation” headlines appear in the news feed and may be noise rather than new facts.
