# Jack Henry & Associates, Inc. (JKHY) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T11:09:34.420970+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Jack Henry & Associates (JKHY) is a high-quality US core-banking and payments technology vendor serving roughly 7,400 community banks and credit unions, with a recurring, cloud-oriented revenue model and excellent client retention. The quantitative screen rates the name a buy (9/22 models; composite 67%; P/E 20.7, ROE 24.9%, PEGY 0.12), supported by accelerating organic growth, expanding margins, and strong cash conversion in recent interim filings. The valuation hook is quality-at-a-reasonable-price: mid-teens to low-20s earnings multiple against double-digit EPS growth and disciplined capital return. The central debate is whether AI-driven fintech disruption and competition from FIS/Fiserv will compress the franchise multiple, or whether JKHY’s embedded payments, private-cloud core, and switching-cost moat justify continued premium pricing.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, JKHY offers a rare combination of defensible franchise economics and visible growth at a modest absolute multiple. The screen passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—including Economic Moat, Buffett Quality, Piotroski F-Score, and Dividend Growth, which aligns with a business whose revenue is largely contract-based processing and support rather than cyclical lending exposure.

Filings describe a sticky installed base (950+ bank cores, ~715 credit union cores) with cross-sell across Core, Payments, and Complementary segments. FY2025 interim and FY2026 year-to-date SEC data show sustained organic momentum: nine-month revenue to March 2026 rose 8.0% to $1,900m (10-Q, filed 7 May 2026), with adjusted organic growth of 7.3% in Q3 after stripping deconversion noise. Operating leverage is evident—nine-month net income reached $391.5m ($5.41 diluted EPS) versus $328.1m ($4.49) a year earlier—while nine-month operating cash flow improved sharply to $459.3m from $314.4m.

Balance-sheet conservatism supports the risk screen: stockholders’ equity of $2.13bn at March 2026 (10-Q) against total assets of $3.05bn, with the company in compliance with credit-agreement covenants. Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly—$284m of treasury share purchases in the first nine months of FY2026 versus $35m prior year, plus dividends declared of $1.77 per share year to date—without impairing reinvestment (capitalised software and developed technology remain substantial). The screen’s low PEGY and 12.5% growth estimate look credible against filing trends rather than speculative re-rating alone.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Competitive and structural:** The FY2025 10-K names FIS, Fiserv, Corelation, and Finastra as core competitors, noting industry consolidation and rivals with larger balance sheets. Failure to achieve “high renewal rates upon favorable terms” would directly hit revenue and margins—a material explicit risk in filing language.

**Cyber and operational:** Risk factors emphasise data-security breaches, third-party failures, and prolonged processing outages (including natural disaster/climate exposure). Payment-transaction failures could cause financial loss. These are first-order risks for a bank-tech processor, not fully captured by accounting screens.

**AI and narrative risk:** News flow (see below) highlights investor concern that AI could disrupt traditional core-processing vendors; this is not quantified in filings but is a live sentiment overhang.

**Financial/governance:** Goodwill of ~$828m (~27% of assets) creates impairment sensitivity if growth slows—the 10-K warns impairment could affect net income and cash flow. No going-concern language appears in available extracts. Pension is not a prominent risk in indexed bodies. Legal proceedings are referenced as an Item 1 topic in 10-Q filings; no material contingent liability quantification appears in the truncated extracts reviewed.

**Capital structure:** Leverage remains modest, but FY2026 repurchase acceleration plus revolver usage ($90m at Q3) bears monitoring if growth capex rises or deconversion revenue fades.
