# McCormick & Company, Incorporated (MKC) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T17:53:10.684117+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
McCormick & Company (MKC) is a global flavour and branded condiments business rated **strong buy** on the quantitative screen (13/22 models; composite 73%), driven chiefly by a headline P/E of 8.3, P/B of 1.9, 3.8% dividend yield, and 24.7% ROE. The valuation hook is a de-rated Dividend King in consumer staples that screens cheap on earnings yield and dividend metrics after a sharp share-price drawdown, against a backdrop of margin recovery and accretive Latin America consolidation. The central debate is whether reported earnings and balance-sheet quality genuinely support that cheapness, or whether the screen is flattered by a $866.8 million Q1 FY2026 remeasurement gain on McCormick de Mexico and by pending transformational leverage from the Unilever Foods combination ($15.7 billion cash consideration, up to $15.7 billion bridge facility). Deep research largely confirms the income and franchise attributes underlying the screen, but materially weakens confidence in the headline multiple until adjusted earnings and deal financing are clearer.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, MKC combines durable category leadership (spices, seasonings, condiments across Consumer and Flavor Solutions segments), long dividend history ($0.48 quarterly dividend affirmed in filings; $483 million paid in FY2025 per the 10-K), and screen-passing cheapness/quality metrics: Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Low P/E + High Yield, Buffett Quality, Magic Formula, and Composite Value all pass, spanning cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk families. Business quality is evident in filing trends: FY2025 net sales rose to $6,840.3 million from $6,723.7 million (FY2024), operating income to $1,070.8 million from $1,060.3 million, and diluted EPS held at $2.93 versus $2.92, with operating cash flow of $962.2 million and net debt manageable relative to cash generation (debt/equity 65% on screen). FY2026 interim momentum is solid on an underlying basis: Q2 net sales were $1,936.6 million (+16.7% YoY per the 10-Q), with adjusted diluted EPS of $0.80 versus $0.69 after special charges, driven by McCormick de Mexico consolidation, pricing, CCI savings, and an IEEPA tariff refund. The stock fits a quality-at-a-reasonable-price mandate more convincingly on dividend yield, FCF yield, and moderate P/B than on raw trailing P/E, which is distorted by non-recurring accounting gains. Failed screen models (Graham Defensive, Deep Value, Lynch PEG, Economic Moat, Piotroski F-Score 6/9, Financial Health) appropriately flag leverage, intangible-heavy balance sheet, and earnings-quality nuance rather than outright distress.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Transformation and financing risk.** The pending Unilever Foods merger (Merger Agreement dated 31 March 2026, per 10-Q) requires a $15.7 billion cash payment to Unilever, would leave current MKC shareholders with ~35% of the combined entity, and is backed by a bridge facility of up to $15.7 billion plus a $2.0 billion term loan at closing. A termination fee of up to $420 million may be payable under specified circumstances. Even with permanent financing plans, pro forma leverage and integration execution risk are material and not captured by trailing screen metrics. Receipt of financing is not a closing condition — balance-sheet risk sits with McCormick shareholders.

**Earnings quality / screen distortion.** The $866.8 million Q1 remeasurement gain inflates trailing net income, ROE, and depresses headline P/E (~8.3 on screen). Without normalisation, value metrics overstate near-term earnings power. Six-month consolidated operating income was flat to down YoY despite sales growth.

**Intangible concentration and impairment.** Goodwill and intangibles total ~$8.9 billion on FY2025 balance sheet (~67% of assets); brand names alone ~$3.0 billion. The auditor flagged intangible valuation as a critical audit matter. Tangible book value is negative (Yahoo: -$3.07 billion at FY2025 — cited only as secondary cross-check).

**Operational and macro.** Filings cite commodity cost inflation, Middle East conflict-related freight costs, global trade policy uncertainty, and contested IEEPA tariff refunds ($30.8 million receivable recorded as probable but appeal risk noted). Customer concentration in consolidated retail channels and private-label competition remain structural pressures (10-K revenue recognition and concentration disclosures).

**Governance / personnel.** 8-K (12 March 2026): VP & Controller and Principal Accounting Officer Gregory Repas to retire; Julie Giese appointed effective 1 April 2026 — orderly transition, but finance leadership change coincides with the largest transaction in company history.

**Pension and covenants.** Non-current pension/post-retirement liability ~$118 million (FY2025 10-K); plans largely frozen. Revolving credit facilities require minimum interest coverage; company states compliance and no foreseeable covenant constraint — but covenants will matter post-Unilever close.

**Screen failures as red flags.** Piotroski 6/9 (ROA and gross margin not improving; current ratio not improving). Failed Financial Health and Economic Moat models align with rising deal-related debt and limited moat scoring despite strong brands.

No going-concern language identified in available filing bodies.
