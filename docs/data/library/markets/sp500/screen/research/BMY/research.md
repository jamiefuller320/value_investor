# Bristol-Myers Squibb Company (BMY) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:44:19.895261+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Bristol-Myers Squibb presents a classic large-cap pharma value setup: mid-teens earnings multiple, ~4% dividend yield, and ~8% free-cash-flow yield on a business that still generates nearly $48bn of annual revenue. The quantitative screen flags strong buy across cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families — metrics that align with filing-backed cash generation rather than headline GAAP noise.

The central debate is whether normalized earnings and pipeline launches can offset the Legacy Portfolio patent cliff (Revlimid, Pomalyst, eventual Eliquis erosion) and Medicare pricing pressure. FY2025 GAAP earnings recovered sharply after the 2024 Karuna-related acquired IPR&D charge, but revenue is essentially flat and interim 2025 trends show only modest top-line growth.

For a value investor, the case is attractive on cash returns and screen breadth, but conviction should be tempered by leverage, intangible-heavy balance sheet, and pipeline execution risk — including the pending departure of the Chief Medical Officer.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s strong-buy signal rests on unusually strong value metrics for a mega-cap pharma name: P/E 17.4, P/B 6.3, dividend yield 4.1%, ROE 38.7%, FCF yield 7.8%, and Neff PEGY 0.04 on 9.2% growth. Twelve of twenty-two models pass, spanning FCF Yield, Buffett Quality, Magic Formula, Piotroski F-Score, High Dividend Yield, and Earnings Quality — a breadth that suggests the signal is not driven by a single distressed metric.

Business quality, as disclosed in the FY2025 10-K (filed 11 February 2026), remains anchored in a dual portfolio: a **Growth Portfolio** (Opdivo, Opdualag, Reblozyl, Breyanzi, Camzyos, Sotyktu, Krazati and others) and a **Legacy Portfolio** (Eliquis, Revlimid, Pomalyst/Imnovid, Sprycel). Total revenues were $48.2bn in 2025, essentially flat versus $48.3bn in 2024 (10-K revenue table). R&D commitment remains heavy at $10.0bn in 2025 versus $11.2bn in 2024, with acquired IPR&D normalising to $3.7bn from $13.4bn (Karuna-driven in 2024).

For a value investor, the hook is the gap between market scepticism around the patent cliff and the company’s demonstrated ability to fund a ~$5bn annual dividend while generating double-digit billions in operating cash flow (nine-month operating cash flow of $12.2bn in 2025 per the Q3 10-Q). The screen’s dividend and FCF families capture this disconnect; the quality and GARP passes suggest the market may be pricing in cliff risk that is partly already reflected, while underweighting pipeline optionality (mezigdomide, cell therapy expansion, AI-accelerated discovery).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Patent cliff and generic competition (filing language).** The FY2025 10-K patent table flags Pomalyst generic entry expected in the US in Q1 2026; Revlimid faces ongoing generic lenalidomide entry under settlement licences (licences noted as no longer exclusive as of 31 January 2026); Eliquis faces generic challenges in EU markets. Yervoy EU exclusivity estimated 2026. This is the primary earnings headwind not fully captured by backward-looking screen metrics.

**Medicare / IRA pricing.** Filings reference Inflation Reduction Act drug-price negotiation and a voluntary pricing agreement covering multiple products at steep discounts (~80% off list for named brands). News coverage (30 June 2026: *"Is Bristol-Myers Squibb Company (BMY) Stock at Risk From Proposed Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Updates?"*) reinforces this as an active overhang.

**Balance sheet and intangibles.** Total debt ~$44–47bn; tangible book value negative (Yahoo: −$22.6bn at FY2025). Goodwill and intangibles ~$41bn. Deleveraging is progressing (long-term debt down ~$4.7bn YoY per Yahoo), but the capital structure remains a constraint if pipeline setbacks emerge.

**M&A and R&D volatility.** Acquired IPR&D swung from $13.4bn (2024) to $3.7bn (2025); Karuna integration and ongoing deal activity (Orbital Therapeutics acquisition, BioNTech collaboration, Philochem licence per 10-K) create earnings volatility and integration risk.

**Restructuring.** Q1 2025 and Q3 2025 filings show restructuring and M&A-related charges (e.g. $2.1bn restructuring in Q1 2025 per income statement). Workforce and cost programmes are ongoing.

**Management turnover.** CMO departure (8-K, July 2025) during a heavy launch cycle (mezigdomide, cell therapies) adds execution uncertainty.

**Legal contingencies.** Pending legal proceedings referenced for Eliquis EU generics and other matters; quantified exposure not available in extracts.

**Pension.** Minimum pension liabilities and defined-benefit plans disclosed on balance sheet (~$330m non-current employee benefits at Q1 2026); not a primary red flag but present.

**Screen limitations.** High ROE is inflated by low/negative tangible equity. P/B 6.3x reflects intangibles, not hard asset backing. Dividend sustainability is debated in recent press (*"Is Bristol Myers Squibb's Dividend Too Good to Be True?"*, 24 July 2026) even as filings show continued dividend payments (~$1.28bn in Q1 2026).
