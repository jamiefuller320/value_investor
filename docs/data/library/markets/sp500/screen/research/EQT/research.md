# EQT Corporation (EQT) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:52:25.259127+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
EQT Corporation is the largest vertically integrated Appalachian natural gas producer, combining upstream production with gathering and transmission assets following the July 2024 Equitrans Midstream merger. The quantitative screen flags a Strong Buy on cheapness (P/E 12.4, P/B 1.3), earnings/FCF yield, dividend growth, quality (Piotroski pass), and a modest leverage profile (Debt/Equity ~20% on screen inputs). FY2025 marked a step-change in scale and profitability—total operating revenues rose to $8.64 billion and net income attributable to EQT reached $2.04 billion—while H1 2026 showed continued volume growth and aggressive debt reduction, with total debt falling to $5.66 billion by 30 June 2026. The valuation hook is a cyclically depressed energy name trading below historical earnings power, with midstream fee income and MVP pipeline equity providing partial insulation from spot gas volatility. The central debate is whether structurally rising US gas demand (power, LNG, data centres) and balance-sheet repair outweigh commodity-price sensitivity, derivative mark-to-market noise, and ongoing capital demands from acquisitions and pipeline expansion.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The Strong Buy screen signal aligns with a business that has materially improved in quality over the past 18 months, not merely rerated on sentiment.

**Valuation and screen fit.** EQT passes ten of 22 quantitative models across five factor families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk). Key screen metrics—P/E 12.4, P/B 1.32, dividend yield 1.2%, ROE 11.1%—place the stock in classic value territory for a scaled producer with investment-grade credit (Moody's Baa3, S&P BBB–, Fitch BBB as of June 2026). Passes on Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Magic Formula, and Piotroski F-Score suggest the market is underpricing normalized cash generation relative to asset base and balance-sheet improvement.

**Business quality.** Post-Equitrans, EQT operates three segments—Upstream, Gathering, and Transmission—with long-dated firm contracts (gathering ~10–13 years weighted average remaining term; transmission ~10–13 years per Q2 2026 10-Q). This integrated model is designed to produce durable free cash flow in low-price environments via midstream reservation fees and MVC-backed revenues, while retaining upside in higher-price periods through reduced hedging intensity. Proved reserves stood at 26.3 Tcfe at 31 December 2024 (FY2024 10-K), with a multi-decade drilling inventory. MVP Mainline is in service (2.0 Bcf/d capacity); MVP Southgate and MVP Boost are advancing under FERC review.

**Capital allocation.** Management targets debt reduction to ~$5.0 billion (updated Debt Retirement Plan, subject to commodity conditions), down from $7.5 billion at end-2024. H1 2026 alone saw ~$2.1 billion of debt repaid/redemption. A $2.0 billion share repurchase programme remains authorised ($622.1 million executed through end-2025; news reports a completed buyback tranche in July 2026). The Olympus Energy acquisition (July 2025, ~$1.9 billion consideration) and Blackline Midstream acquisition (July 2026, ~$77 million) extend footprint but add integration execution risk.

**Why value investors should care.** Normalised earnings power (FY2025 diluted EPS $3.31) sits well above the trailing screen-implied multiple, while the balance sheet is de-levering faster than the 2024 10-K outlook suggested. For a value allocator, EQT offers scale, contracted midstream cash flows, and a credible debt-reduction path at a mid-cycle multiple—provided one accepts commodity and derivative accounting volatility.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Commodity and derivative volatility.** Filings repeatedly warn that revenues, earnings, and liquidity are substantially dependent on natural gas, NGL, and oil prices, and that the company does not designate derivatives as cash flow hedges—meaning mark-to-market swings flow through operating revenues. Q2 2026 illustrates the distortion: upstream operating income fell 89% YoY despite 12% volume growth, largely due to derivative comparatives. Lower prices may also trigger impairments; the FY2024 10-K cautions that future commodity declines or strategy changes could result in material asset impairments and reserve write-downs.

**Cyclical and concentration risk.** Operations are concentrated in the Appalachian Basin; gathering/transmission customers and assets share geographic exposure. The business remains a price-taker on upstream sales despite midstream contract buffers.

**Leverage and capital intensity.** Although debt has fallen sharply, total assets exceed $41 billion and H1 2026 capex ran at $1.27 billion. MVP Southgate ($370–430 million project cost) and MVP Boost ($400–540 million) require further EQT capital contributions and performance guarantees ($38 million guarantee issued for Southgate in April 2026). Failure to achieve commodity conditions could slow progress toward the $5.0 billion debt target.

**Regulatory and legal.** Extensive FERC, EPA, and state environmental regulation applies to production and interstate pipelines. The securities class action drove legal reserves in 2025 (settlement-related reserve in Q2 2025 per 10-Q). IRS audit of EQM for tax year 2023 commenced in 2025; uncertain tax positions reserve was $60 million at 31 December 2025. No going-concern qualification is present in available extracts.

**Governance and structure complexity.** The Midstream Joint Venture with Blackstone Credit & Insurance ($3.5 billion invested December 2024) creates a noncontrolling interest with a ~$3.4 billion remaining Base Return hurdle; $238 million was distributed to Blackstone in H1 2026. This structure improves liquidity but dilutes midstream cash flow to EQT until hurdles are met.

**Competitive and contract risk.** ~99% of transmission firm capacity is under negotiated-rate agreements that may not adjust for cost inflation; FERC rate challenges remain a stated risk in the FY2024 10-K.

**Screen limitations.** The quantitative Strong Buy signal does not capture derivative accounting volatility, reserve revision risk at SEC pricing ($1.47/Mcf gas price for 2024 reserve PV vs strip-based PV of ~$22 billion disclosed sensitised), or event-driven M&A integration risk.
