## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
First Solar, Inc. (FSLR) is the leading US manufacturer of thin-film cadmium-telluride solar modules, with a manufacturing footprint in the United States, Malaysia, Vietnam, and India and more than 93 GW of modules sold globally (FY2025 10-K). The quantitative screen rates the name **strong_buy**, though the screening snapshot carries sparse model detail (zero models/families passed with a 0.0 data-quality score), so the signal rests on the label rather than populated factor metrics. Filings show a business inflecting from loss-making in 2022 to record profitability, supported by US domestic manufacturing, IRA Section 45X credits (~17 cents per watt), and a large deferred-revenue backlog. The valuation debate centres on whether tariff and IRA tailwinds are fully priced versus policy rollback (OBBBA amendments), trade-law volatility, and episodic negative operating cash flow during capacity build-out. H1 2026 earnings momentum remains strong, but working-capital absorption makes near-term free cash flow uneven.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The **strong_buy** screen aligns with a business that screens well on classical value dimensions even where automated metrics are thin: net-cash-adjacent balance sheet, rising returns on invested capital, and earnings power that has scaled sharply since 2022.

**Business quality.** First Solar occupies a differentiated niche—US-made thin-film modules benefiting from trade barriers against crystalline-silicon imports and from Section 45X advanced manufacturing credits. The FY2025 10-K describes cumulative R&D above $2 billion and Series 7 production scaling across multiple geographies. Q2 2026 MD&A states the company expects to qualify for refundable/transferable Section 45X credits through 2032 (phase-down from 2030), providing a structural margin and liquidity tailwind not available to most peers.

**Quantitative alignment.** Where the screen snapshot lacks populated Piotroski, FCF, or composite scores, filing-derived metrics fill the gap: FY2025 net sales of **$5.22bn** and net income of **$1.53bn** (Yahoo fallback—see Financial Review), diluted EPS **$14.21**, gross margin ~41%, and operating margin ~31%. Stockholders’ equity reached **$9.88bn** at 31 March 2026 (Q1 10-Q) against total liabilities of **$3.47bn**; cash and equivalents were **$2.36bn** plus **$64m** marketable securities, with only ~**$426m** of combined current and long-term debt. Deferred revenue (current plus non-current) totalled roughly **$1.79bn** at 31 March 2026, signalling contracted forward demand. TTM free cash flow per Yahoo cached quarterly data is **~$1.50bn**, recovering from negative FCF in 2024 when capex peaked.

**Why a value investor might accumulate.** The combination of net-cash balance sheet, policy-moated US manufacturing, double-digit EPS growth, and contracted revenue visibility resembles a quality compounder trading at a cyclical-multiple discount—provided one accepts solar policy and tariff risk. News flow (August–September 2026) highlights domestic-manufacturing tailwinds post-Section 232 action, offset by debate over whether tariff benefits are already in the price.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and policy.** The Q2 2026 10-Q risk-factor update is extensive: IRA/OBBBA amendments (including accelerated termination of certain energy tax credits under the “One Big Beautiful Bill,” signed 4 July 2025), Section 45X phase-down from 2030, and trade-law volatility (IEEPA tariffs ruled unlawful February 2026; Section 301 actions; Section 232 investigations on polysilicon and critical minerals). Management explicitly warns that subsidy reduction could “negatively impact demand and/or price levels” and impair foreign manufacturing assets. This is the central bear case.

**Cyclical and demand.** Solar remains policy- and tariff-sensitive; Q2 2026 MD&A links net sales variability to “availability and size of government subsidies.” Global clean-energy investment headlines (September 2026) cite a 17% H1 spending decline—context only, not a screen veto.

**Customer concentration / contract dispute.** September 2025 complaint against BP Solar/Lightsource seeks **$323.6m** remaining termination payments (total claim **$384.6m**); Defendants counterclaim **$175m**. Filing states First Solar does not believe counterclaims have legal merit. This is material single-customer exposure.

**Litigation.** Active TOPCon patent enforcement against JinkoSolar, Canadian Solar, Trina Solar, Mundra/Adani, and a USITC Section 337 investigation (Investigation No. 337-TA-1494; hearing scheduled February 2027). Outcome uncertain; adverse rulings could impair IP moat or trigger counterclaims. News reports (July 2025) of securities class actions over tariff and utilisation disclosures are **not** corroborated in the filing body extracts reviewed—treat as unverified headline risk.

**Liquidity and leverage.** No going-concern warning. February 2026: new **$1.5bn** unsecured revolving credit facility (Net Leverage covenant **3.50×**; Interest Coverage **3.00×**); prior secured revolver terminated. May 2026: India credit facility prepaid **$328.2m**. H1 2026 operating cash outflow and cash decline ($2.9bn → $1.7bn) warrant monitoring despite ample headroom.

**Governance.** Routine insider 10b5-1 sales (CTO, supply-chain chief, GC) in Q2 2026; CEO sold shares in May 2025 per news. Not alarming at current scale but worth tracking.

**Competitive.** Crystalline-silicon cost curve, Chinese oversupply, and peer technology shifts (Enphase/SolarEdge data-centre power narratives) create relative-performance risk even when First Solar wins on US policy.

**Accounting.** Malaysia tax holiday expires 2027; Vietnam incentive expires 2036—both conditional on employment/R&D thresholds. Section 45X credit recognition and tax-credit transfer agreements ($600m fixed + up to $175m variable, October 2025 8-K) add complexity.
