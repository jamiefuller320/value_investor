# McKesson Corporation (MCK) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:17:16.418774+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
McKesson is the leading US pharmaceutical distributor pivoting toward higher-margin oncology and specialty services, with FY2026 revenue of $403.4bn and diluted EPS of $38.38 — up sharply from $25.72 in FY2025. The quantitative screen flags a buy on FCF yield (~5.7%), earnings growth, and GARP metrics, though classic quality and balance-sheet screens fail (Piotroski 6/9, negative book equity). The investment case rests on durable scale, rising operating margins, and aggressive capital return ($4.8bn buybacks in FY2026), offset by $5.7bn accrued opioid liabilities, extreme customer concentration (CVS ~24% of revenue), and a low-margin distribution model where revenue growth does not automatically translate to per-share value.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
McKesson fits a value-oriented GARP profile rather than deep value or quality compounder. The screen passes six of 22 models across cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk families (composite 61%, sector-relative 64%), driven by FCF yield, Lynch/Neff PEG metrics, dividend growth, Magic Formula, and earnings quality. Business quality is anchored in irreplaceable US distribution infrastructure: FY2026 operating income reached $6.2bn on $403.4bn revenue (operating margin ~1.5%, up from 1.2% in FY2025), with operating cash flow stable at $6.2bn.

Management is reshaping the portfolio toward structurally higher-return assets. FY2026 acquisitions included Core Ventures ($2.5bn, 70% of Florida Cancer Specialists’ administrative platform) and PRISM Vision ($875m, ophthalmology), both in the Oncology & Multispecialty segment, while divesting Canadian retail (Dec 2024) and Norway (Jan 2026). Diluted share count fell from 134.1m (FY2024) to 124.1m (FY2026), amplifying EPS growth; net income attributable to McKesson rose 44% to $4.76bn. The dividend was raised from $0.71 to $0.82 per quarter in July 2025, with FY2026 cash dividends of $381m ($3.17/share). For a value investor, the hook is strong cash conversion and capital allocation at a mid-teens P/E (screen: 21.9x) despite multi-year earnings compounding — not asset-based cheapness, given negative common equity from cumulative buybacks.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Opioid litigation** — The dominant balance-sheet overhang. Accrued liability $5.7bn at 31 March 2026, payable in instalments through 2038; the company cannot reasonably estimate the upper bound of ultimate losses. Residual cases include Baltimore (judgment under appeal; company has not adjusted reserves), Cabell County/Huntington (Fourth Circuit remand, Oct 2025), and non-settling subdivisions. An adverse judgment or renegotiated settlement could materially affect results and liquidity.

**Customer concentration** — FY2026 10-K: top ten customers ~73% of consolidated revenue; CVS alone ~24%. Loss or repricing of a major account would be material.

**Regulatory and reimbursement** — Distribution margins depend on manufacturer pricing, generic substitution, and government programme rules. Branded deflation benefited FY2026 via LIFO but can reverse.

**Leverage and negative equity** — Debt rose to $6.5bn; debt-to-capital 128%. Piotroski leverage and current-ratio components failed. Screen P/B of -46.5 reflects negative book value, not hidden asset value.

**M&A integration** — $3.4bn FY2026 acquisition spend added $2.6bn+ intangible assets and $943m redeemable noncontrolling interests (Core Ventures put option). Goodwill impairment testing passed, but earn-out and integration risk remain.

**Restructuring** — Technology modernisation programme: estimated $650–700m total charges through fiscal 2028; $146m charged in 9M FY2026.

**Governance** — News flow references CEO share sales and a new strategy chief (Jul 2026); not independently verified in filing bodies but worth monitoring alongside continued insider activity.

**Tax uncertainty** — Unrecognised tax benefits ~$1.2bn at 31 March 2026; opioid-related tax positions flagged as a critical audit matter.
