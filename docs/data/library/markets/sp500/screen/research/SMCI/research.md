# Super Micro Computer, Inc. (SMCI) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-09-09T07:16:02.706776+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Super Micro is a high-growth AI server and storage systems integrator trading at a modest headline multiple (P/E ~12.3, P/B ~2.4) after revenue roughly doubled in fiscal 2026. The quantitative screen flags cheapness, quality, GARP, and risk families, but fails on free-cash-flow yield, Piotroski F-Score (2/9), earnings quality, and moat models — a split that reflects the core debate.

The investment case rests on AI infrastructure demand, NVIDIA-aligned product breadth, and reported net income of $2.2bn in FY2026. The counter-case is severe working-capital strain: TTM operating cash flow of roughly −$6.8bn against reported earnings, heavy inventory and receivables build, persistent material weaknesses in internal controls, and fresh export-compliance legal exposure.

For a value investor, SMCI offers growth at a discount on reported earnings, but cash conversion, governance, and cyclicality remain unresolved. Deep research weakens blind reliance on the screen’s buy signal pending evidence that margins and cash flows normalise.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen rates SMCI a **buy** with 9 of 22 models passing across cheapness, quality, GARP, and risk (composite 69%, sector-relative 64%, data quality 19/20). Key passes include Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, Lynch PEG, Quality Value, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value, and Financial Health. Headline metrics — P/E 12.3, P/B 2.4, ROE 21.5%, debt/equity 64% — look inexpensive for a business scaling AI server deployments.

Business quality, read through filings, is operationally strong but financially messy. The FY2026 10-K narrative cites net income of **$2,230.5m** (vs $1,048.9m in FY2025 and $1,152.7m in FY2024), NVIDIA Blackwell/Rubin platform alignment, and expansion of direct liquid-cooled rack-scale offerings. Interim 10-Q filings show accelerating top-line: Q3 FY2026 net sales of **$10.2bn** (vs $4.6bn YoY) and nine-month net sales of **$27.9bn** (vs $16.2bn).

However, the same filings reveal a working-capital-intensive model ill-suited to classic value screens on cash: nine-month operating cash flow to March 2026 was **−$7.6bn**, driven by **−$6.2bn** in receivables and **−$6.7bn** in inventory changes, despite **$1.05bn** of net income over the period. The screen’s canonical FCF of **−$8.2bn** (FCF yield −31%) and Piotroski score of 2/9 are consistent with this divergence.

For a value investor, SMCI is a **conditional** rather than **classic** buy: the market may be discounting governance overhang, earnings-quality risk, and the possibility that peak AI-server margins are temporary (management and sell-side commentary flagged in recent news). The screen correctly identifies statistical cheapness on earnings; deep research suggests sizing and patience until cash conversion improves.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Accounting and controls.** FY2026 and prior 10-K risk factors state the company **previously identified material weaknesses** in internal control over financial reporting that, if not remediated, could impair timely and accurate reporting. The screen’s earnings-quality and Piotroski failures (weak cash conversion, high accruals, negative operating cash flow) align with this risk. Inventory valuation adjustments of **$239m** (nine months to Mar 2026) warrant monitoring.

**Regulatory and export compliance.** An 8-K dated 20 March 2026 reports the US Attorney’s Office (SDNY) unsealed an indictment of three individuals associated with SMCI for alleged export violations. The company is not a defendant; a board member resigned and employees were placed on leave. News coverage (Aug 2026) references completion of an export-compliance review with adopted recommendations, but Taiwan staff detentions linked to illegal NVIDIA chip exports (3 Sep 2026) keep regulatory risk live.

**Liquidity and leverage.** Rapid growth has consumed cash: nine-month OCF **−$7.6bn** to Mar 2026 despite **$1.05bn** net income. The company entered a **$1.25bn ATM programme** (Jun 2026), issued **7.00% mandatory convertible preferred stock** (~$2bn gross, Jun 2026), and maintains **$2.0bn** revolver capacity. Debt/equity of 64% and rising interest expense (Q3 FY2026: **$115m** nine-month vs **$37m** prior year) add financial risk if AI demand slows.

**Customer concentration and cyclicality.** Three 10%+ customers in FY2026; revenue is tied to AI capex cycles and NVIDIA platform transitions. Management and analysts (news, Aug–Sep 2026) caution that recent gross-margin strength may be **temporary**, with guidance implying normalisation.

**Governance and key-person risk.** Founder-CEO Charles Liang remains central; multiple insider sales under 10b5-1 plans reported Sep 2026 (CEO, director Sara Liu). Senior VP Worldwide Sales Don Clegg announced retirement (May 2026). Related-party transactions appear in 10-Q footnotes but are small relative to revenue.

**Competitive and litigation.** Competes with Dell, HPE, and ODMs in AI servers; no durable moat signal in screens (ROA, margins). Named in Netlist DDR5 ITC complaint (Aug 2026 news). Prior Hindenburg-related scrutiny and delayed filing history (context from news, not re-litigated here) remain an overhang for some investors.

**No going-concern flag** identified in available filing extracts; liquidity risk is operational/cyclical rather than immediate insolvency.
