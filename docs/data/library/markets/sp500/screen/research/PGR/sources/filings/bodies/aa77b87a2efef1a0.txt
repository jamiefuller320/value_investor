MANAGEMENT’S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS OF FINANCIAL CONDITION AND RESULTS OF OPERATIONS. I. OVERVIEW The Progressive Corporation’s insurance subsidiaries continued to generate underwriting profitability above our 4% companywide underwriting profit goal during the third quarter 2026, producing a companywide underwriting profit margin of 11.3%. Both our Personal Lines and Commercial Lines operating segments generated strong underwriting profitability during the third quarter 2026. We also maintained growth in both premiums and policies in force, albeit at a more moderate pace than in the third quarter last year, despite the continued increase in competition in the marketplace. Companywide net premiums written were $22.3 billion, an increase of $0.9 billion, or 4%, compared to the third quarter last year, while net premiums earned increased 5%. Policies in force increased 6%, or by 2.4 million policies, compared to September 30, 2025; with policies in force increasing by 0.4 million in the third quarter 2026. For the third quarter 2026, we experienced a $73 million decrease in net income, compared to the third quarter 2025, with total comprehensive income decreasing $2,254 million. The decrease in total comprehensive income was primarily driven by net unrealized losses on our securities in the current period, compared to net unrealized gains during the third quarter last year. At September 30, 2026, total capital (debt plus shareholders’ equity) was $42.9 billion, an increase of $5.7 billion from 2025. The increase was primarily driven by $5.8 billion of comprehensive income earned during the first nine months of 2026 and the issuance of $1.5 billion of senior notes during the first quarter 2026. These increases were partially offset by the repurchase of 7.7 million of our common shares at a total cost of $1.6 billion. A. Insurance Operations Our companywide underwriting profit margin for the third quarter 2026 was 0.8 points higher than in the same period last year, reflecting a 4.5 point decrease in the underwriting expense ratio, partially offset by a 3.7 point increase in the loss and loss adjustment expense (LAE) ratio. The decrease in the underwriting expense ratio primarily reflected Florida personal auto policyholder credit expense (Florida policyholder credits) recognized in the third quarter 2025 (see our 2025 Annual Report to Shareholders for further discussion of the Florida policyholder credits). This expense added 4.6 points to the third quarter 2025 underwriting expense ratio. The increase in the loss and LAE ratio was primarily due to higher severity. Personal Lines represented 89% of companywide net premiums written during the third quarter 2026 and is comprised of our personal vehicle and property products. Personal vehicle products include personal auto and special lines products (e.g., recreational vehicles, such as motorcycles, RVs, and watercraft), and personal property products include homeowners, renters, and umbrella products. During the third quarter 2026, we continued to focus on selectively increasing the availability of our personal property products. Beginning late in 2025, we took actions in certain markets to generate new business growth at the state level based on our concentration risks, product segmentation, rate adequacy, cost sharing, geographical diversification, and the regulatory and market conditions. Some of these actions include expanding independent agency relationships, reopening new business in certain agency and direct channel markets, and lifting targeted underwriting restrictions on older roofs, medium- to homes, and homeowners products, where we intend to bundle in the future, in certain markets. Certain restrictions remain in place in markets where we continue to focus on improving profitability and reducing exposure in more volatile markets. Personal Lines generated an underwriting profit margin of 12.0% for the third quarter 2026, compared to 10.5% for the same period last year, with personal vehicle and personal property products reporting strong underwriting profit margins of 11.3% and 27.2%, respectively. Profitability in our special lines products, which typically experience higher losses during warmer weather months due to the seasonal nature of these products, had a 0.6 point unfavorable impact on the personal vehicle combined ratio during the third quarter 2026. The strong underwriting profit margin in personal property was primarily driven by a low level of incurred catastrophe losses, prior period rate increases, and lower loss frequency, and we believe was influenced by our sustained efforts to reduce exposure and improve profitability. For the third quarter 2026, Personal Lines net premiums written increased 4%, with personal vehicle business increases of 2% in agency and 5% in direct, and a 5% increase in personal property, each compared to the same period last year. The current period net premiums written growth was primarily driven by policies in force growth in our personal auto products, which were up 7%, compared to September 30, 2025. The effect of the growth in policies in force was, in part, offset by a decrease in average written premiums per policy, due to aggregate rate decreases taken over the last 12 months in response to increased 27 competition in the marketplace. Changes in net premiums written are a function of new business applications (i.e., policies sold), retention, business mix, and premium per policy. Personal vehicles experienced a decrease in new business applications of 1% and an increase in renewal business applications of 9% during the third quarter 2026, compared to the same period in the prior year. Despite continued increased competition in the marketplace, our personal vehicle business continued to generate total application growth, in relation to the growth during the same period last year. Personal property experienced a 3% increase in both new and renewal business applications during the third quarter 2026, compared to the same period last year. New business applications in our homeowners product (which excludes our renters and umbrella products) increased 22%, compared to the period, and was flat in our renters product. On a countrywide basis, during the third quarter 2026, personal auto rates were flat and personal property rates decreased less than 1%, in the aggregate. We believe a key element in improving the accuracy of our personal auto rating is Snapshot ® , our insurance offering. During the third quarter 2026, Snapshot adoption rates relative to total new business personal auto customers increased 8% in agency and decreased 4% in direct, compared to the same period last year. Approximately half of direct new business customers elected Snapshot in both the third quarter 2025 and 2026. The increase in the agency adoption rate was primarily due to the expansion of Snapshot eligibility in the second half of 2025, which increased the number of agents able to write Snapshot policies. Snapshot is available in all states, other than California, and our latest segmentation model was available in states representing 85% of countrywide personal auto net premiums written (excluding California) on a trailing 12-month basis at quarter end. We continue to invest in our mobile application, with the majority of new enrollments choosing mobile devices for Snapshot monitoring. The Commercial Lines segment includes our core commercial auto products, transportation network company (TNC) business, Progressive Fleet & Specialty Programs (FSP) products, and business owners’ policy (BOP) product. Total Commercial Lines generated an underwriting profit margin of 7.4%, compared to 10.8% in the same period last year, with a net premiums written increase of 9% and a policies in force increase of 5% for the third quarter 2026, compared to the third quarter 2025. This growth was driven by an increase in our core commercial auto products, due to an increase in both new and renewal applications, primarily resulting from targeted rate decreases in certain states and business market targets (BMT), and increased advertising and agent incentive spend. Core commercial auto products experienced an increase in new business applications of 11% and an increase in renewal business applications of 8% during the third quarter 2026, compared to the same period last year. New business applications increased in all BMTs and renewal business applications increased in all BMTs, except transportation. In aggregate, we increased core commercial auto rates 1% on a countrywide basis during the third quarter 2026. We believe we are currently adequately priced in our personal auto, personal property, and core commercial auto products in most states through the remainder of the year. However, we regularly monitor the factors that could impact our loss costs, which may include loss severity and frequency, inflation, new and used car prices, miles driven, driving patterns, weather events, building materials, construction costs, tariffs, and other factors, on a basis. We continue to closely monitor our expenses, including acquisition expenses and expenses, which we view as important measures of operational efficiency as we seek to offer consumers our most competitive rates. During the third quarter 2026, advertising expense was $1.6 billion, or 18% higher than the third quarter last year. The effect of the higher advertising spend on the current period expense ratio was partially offset by growth in net premiums earned, contributing an additional 0.8 points to the underwriting expense ratio in the third quarter 2026, compared to the same period last year. We will continue to invest in advertising to maximize growth as long as the spend remains efficient and we remain on track to achieve our profitability goal. For the third quarter 2026, on a basis, average written premium per policy decreased 2% in personal auto, 1% in personal property products, and 3% in core commercial auto products. In aggregate, personal auto rate decreases on a countrywide basis over the previous 12 months contributed to the decrease in average written premium per policy. The decrease in core commercial auto average written premium per policy was primarily due to a shift in the mix of business, including a shift to a higher percentage of 6-month policies, which have about half of the amount of net premiums written as 12-month term policies. Given that our personal property and commercial auto policies are predominantly written for 12-month terms, rate and actions take longer to earn into premium for these products. We realize that to grow policies in force, it is critical that we retain our customers for longer periods. Consequently, increasing retention continues to be one of our most important priorities. Increasing our share of Progressive auto and personal property bundled households (i.e., Robinsons) remains a key initiative, and we plan to continue investing in the customer experience in order to support that goal. Policy life expectancy, our actuarial estimate of the average length of time a newly written 28 policy remains in force before cancellation or lapse in coverage, is our primary measure of customer retention in both Personal Lines and Commercial Lines. In personal auto, we evaluate retention using a trailing 12-month and a trailing 3-month policy life expectancy. Although the latter can reflect more volatility and is more sensitive to seasonality, we believe this measure is more responsive to current experience and may be an indicator for the future trend of our 12-month measure. For the third quarter 2026, both the trailing 3-month and 12-month total personal auto policy life expectancy decreased 9% year over year. We believe these decreases were primarily due to an increase in shopping and competition in the marketplace and, to a lesser extent, a shift in our mix of business. Our trailing 12-month policy life expectancy was down 7% for our personal property products year over year for the third quarter 2026. We believe the retention decrease was primarily driven by rate increases in previous years, increased competition in the marketplace, and, to a lesser extent, a continued shift in the mix of business to more renters policies. For core commercial auto products, trailing 12-month policy life expectancy increased 3%, compared to the same period in the prior year. We believe the increase reflected a shift to the business auto and contractor BMTs, which historically have higher policy life expectancies, moderation in our rate increases, and various initiatives, including payment and renewal reminders. B. Investments The fair value of our investment portfolio was $99.2 billion at September 30, 2026, compared to $97.4 billion at December 31, 2025. The increase from 2025 primarily reflected significant positive cash flows from insurance operations and proceeds from the senior note issuances in March 2026, partially offset by the payment of our annual variable common share dividend, valuation declines across sectors, repurchases of our common shares, and the Florida policyholder credits paid in the first quarter 2026. Our asset allocation strategy is to maintain 0%-25% of our portfolio in Group I securities and 75%-100% in Group II securities as defined below under Results of Operations – Investments . At September 30, 2026, 7% of our portfolio was allocated to Group I securities, compared to 6% at December 31, 2025, with the remainder allocated to Group II securities. Our recurring investment income generated a pretax book yield of 4.3% in the third quarter 2026, compared to 4.2% in the same period last year. The investment portfolio produced a fully taxable equivalent (FTE) total return of -1.5% in the third quarter 2026, compared to 1.7% in the same period last year. For the third quarter 2026, the and common stock portfolios generated FTE total returns of -1.7% and 2.2%, respectively, compared to 1.5% and 8.1%, in the same period last year. The decrease in the portfolio’s FTE total return primarily reflected movements in U.S. Treasury yields. The portfolio maintained a weighted average credit quality of AA- at September 30, 2026 and 2025, and December 31, 2025. The portfolio duration was 3.6 years at September 30, 2026, compared to 3.4 years at both September 30, 2025, and December 31, 2025.
