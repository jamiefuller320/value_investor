# Crown Castle Inc. (CCI) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:11:35.820442+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Crown Castle Inc. (CCI) is transitioning into the largest pure-play US tower REIT following the May 2026 sale of its fibre and small-cell businesses for approximately $8.4–8.5 billion. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness, dividend yield (~5.7%), GARP and risk metrics, but with low timing conviction (30%) and a middling Piotroski score (5/9). The investment case rests on contractual, inflation-linked tower cash flows, capital return from fibre proceeds (debt paydown, $1 billion buyback), and a simplified operating model; the central debate is whether organic growth and cost savings can offset Sprint/T-Mobile churn, the DISH default (~$220 million annual revenue drag), and ~$24–30 billion of gross debt in a higher-rate environment. Negative book equity and extreme tenant concentration (T-Mobile, AT&T and Verizon ~90% of site rental revenues per the FY2025 10-K) limit deep-value certainty despite the screen signal.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, CCI offers a high-quality infrastructure franchise at a depressed multiple after a multi-year strategic reset. The screen passes four model families (cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk) with a 67% composite score and high data quality (18/20 metrics), driven chiefly by a 5.7% dividend yield, low Neff PEGY (0.06), and earnings-quality pass—while failing classic deep-value screens (Graham, Schloss, FCF yield, Piotroski). That profile fits a “quality income at a reasonable price” bucket rather than a net-net or asset-play.

Business quality remains strong at the asset level: 40,000+ US towers, ~2.4 tenants per tower, 98–99% annual retention, and 95% of net revenues from recurring site rentals (FY2025 10-K). Post-fibre, management is refocusing capital on tower organic growth, land purchases under sites, and shareholder returns. The $8.5 billion Strategic Fiber Transaction (closed 1 May 2026 per Q1 2026 10-Q) funds ~$2.1 billion of additional 2026 debt repayment, a $1.0 billion authorised buyback, and lower forward interest expense—directly supporting AFFO and dividend coverage. Restructuring (2026 plan: ~$65 million annualised opex savings, ~$30 million one-off charges) adds operating leverage.

The screen’s buy signal is directionally supported by: (1) de-risked pure-play tower exposure, (2) meaningful FCF generation (Yahoo: $2.9 billion FCF in 2025, used as fallback—see Financial Review), and (3) contractual revenue backlog of $23.7 billion excluding DISH (FY2025 10-K). It is not fully supported by balance-sheet quality (negative equity, leverage not declining fast enough per Piotroski) or by growth (site rental revenues down 5–7% YoY in recent quarters).

## FINANCIAL REVIEW

Annual trend (10-K)

FY2025 10-K (filed 23 February 2026) describes continuing operations as towers-only after fibre was reclassified to discontinued operations. Site rental revenues represented 95% of 2025 net revenues. Contracted future cash inflows totalled $23.7 billion (six-year weighted remaining term), down from $35.9 billion at FY2024—reflecting the fibre exit and DISH exclusion. Top-three carrier concentration was ~90% of site rental revenues. Sprint/T-Mobile consolidation drove ~$200 million of tower churn in 2025, with further annual churn of 1–2% of tower site rental revenues expected through 2034.

FY2024 10-K (filed 14 March 2025) covered consolidated towers plus fibre: site rental revenues were 97% of net revenues (67% towers, 33% fibre). The company recorded a goodwill impairment on fibre and mutually cancelled ~7,000 small-cell nodes ($106 million charge in Q4 2024 per risk disclosures). The planned fibre sale was announced in March 2025 for $8.5 billion aggregate consideration.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Tenant concentration and credit: ~90% of site rental revenues from three carriers (FY2025 10-K). DISH default and lease termination (8-K, 12 January 2026) triggered a claim exceeding $3.5 billion; balance-sheet exposure was ~$165 million net (FY2025 10-K), with 2026 outlook excluding any DISH revenue (~$220 million headwind). Recovery litigation adds uncertainty.

Structural churn: T-Mobile/Sprint consolidation drove ~$200 million of 2025 tower churn; further 1–2% annual tower revenue erosion expected through 2034. This explains the 5–7% YoY site rental declines in 2025 interim quarters.

Leverage and negative equity: ~$24.3–29.6 billion gross debt; net debt ~$24 billion. Stockholders’ deficit at 31 March 2026: $(1.92) billion (Q1 2026 10-Q). Piotroski components failed on leverage decline, current ratio, gross margin and asset turnover—consistent with a leveraged REIT in transition, not a deteriorating operating business, but still a red flag for conservative value screens.

Competitive and technological: Risk disclosures cite carrier self-build, network sharing, satellite/low-earth-orbit alternatives, and pricing pressure on renewals. Pure-play status removes fibre diversification but also fibre execution risk.

Governance and restructuring: February 2026 restructuring cuts ~20% of tower headcount (~$30 million charges). Management turnover is flagged as a risk factor. Activist shareholder risk noted in filings.

Macro (colour only): 10-year Treasury ~4.68% and short rate ~3.81% (macro_context.json) sustain elevated refinancing costs despite planned debt paydown from fibre proceeds.
