# Camden Property Trust (CPT) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:07:04.447262+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Camden Property Trust is a large, investment-grade Sunbelt multifamily REIT (~60,000 units across 175 properties) whose screen appeal rests less on headline P/E cheapness than on cash generation, dividend quality, and growth-at-a-reasonable-price metrics. The quantitative signal flags FCF yield (~6.4%), Neff PEGY (0.08), dividend growth, Piotroski F-Score (7/9), and earnings quality—while traditional deep-value screens fail.

Operating fundamentals are recovering only gradually: same-store revenue growth slowed to 0.8% in FY2025 (from 1.3% in FY2024), and same-store NOI rose just $2.4m. GAAP net income is heavily distorted by property gains ($260.9m in FY2025). The central debate is whether moderating new supply and active capital recycling (dispositions, $600m buyback authorisation, 3.7% yield) justify a premium multiple, or whether rent growth and leverage normalisation will disappoint.

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
**Why the screen says buy.** CPT passes five factor families—cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk—with particular strength in cash-based and growth-adjusted metrics rather than Graham-style absolute cheapness. A 6.4% FCF yield, 3.7% dividend yield, PEGY of 0.08, and 12.1% growth score sit alongside full data quality (20/20 metrics) and an improving one-week signal trend. Piotroski 7/9 confirms operational health (positive earnings, OCF > net income, no dilution), though leverage is not declining.

**Business quality.** Camden is a scaled operator in high-growth Sunbelt and select coastal markets (Houston, D.C. Metro, Dallas, Charlotte, Florida, etc.), with ~95% stabilised occupancy and average rent of $2,006/home (FY2025 10-K). Management emphasises capital recycling—four acquisitions (~$423m) and five dispositions (~$375m) in 2025—and maintains investment-grade unsecured ratings (Fitch A-/stable, Moody’s A3/stable, S&P A-/stable). The culture franchise (18 consecutive years on Fortune’s “100 Best Companies to Work For”) supports retention in a labour-intensive model.

**Value investor angle.** For a value investor, CPT is a **quality compounder at a reasonable cash yield**, not a distressed asset play. Failed screens (Graham, Schloss, deep value, low P/E + high yield) reflect P/E 31.8 and P/B 2.9—expensive on earnings, cheaper on FCF. The buy case ties to: (1) durable Sunbelt demand as supply peaks; (2) shareholder-friendly capital return ($270.7m repurchased in 2025; new $600m plan authorised February 2026; dividend raised to $1.06/quarter, ~$4.24 annualised); (3) property-level NOI of $1.01bn (FY2025) with modest but positive same-store momentum. Conviction is moderate: screen breadth is narrow (6/22), stability is “new,” and operating growth is thin relative to the growth metrics embedded in PEGY.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Operating/cyclical**
- **Thin same-store growth:** Revenue +0.8% (FY2025) and NOI essentially flat; Q1 2026 same-store revenue +0.2%. Short ~14-month leases amplify rent-downside risk if Sunbelt supply or employment weakens.
- **Interest expense:** Up 6.5% FY2025; Q1 2026 interest +11% YoY. Variable-rate exposure (term loan, revolver, commercial paper, $500m interest-rate swap) remains a headwind if rates stay elevated.
- **Leverage:** Debt rose from ~$3.5bn to ~$3.9bn (FY2024→FY2025); unsecured notes +$361m in Q1 2026 alone. Piotroski “leverage declining” failed.

**Development/impairment**
- Land impairments: **$12.9m** (FY2025) and **$41.0m** (FY2024). Management warns further impairments are possible if land fair values fall.
- Active development (~$492m estimated cost on three projects; ~$213.8m remaining) adds execution and capital risk.

**Regulatory/litigation**
- **RealPage antitrust litigation:** 43 consolidated private cases. Q1 2026 10-Q discloses a **binding term sheet (7 Apr 2026) to pay $53.0m** in two instalments, subject to court approval—material but manageable relative to ~$1bn NOI.
- Separate actions by D.C. and Arizona attorneys general over revenue-management software; rent-control expansion risk explicitly flagged in 10-K risk factors.
- **No going-concern** flag; litigation reserves/settlement status should be monitored through court approval.

**Governance/capital allocation**
- Executive chairman **sold $3.37m in shares** (June 2025, per news)—insider sale worth noting alongside aggressive buybacks.
- Chief Accounting Officer transition announced June 2025.
- Buybacks at ~$107–110/share while stock traded near 52-week highs (~$118, July 2026)—returns depend on sustained NOI growth.

**Screen blind spots**
- Failed financial health, economic moat, and deep-value models suggest the name is **not** statistically cheap on earnings or book.
- Macro context (10y UST ~4.68%, short rate ~3.81%) is a structural headwind for cap rates; not used to override the screen, but relevant to REIT multiples.
