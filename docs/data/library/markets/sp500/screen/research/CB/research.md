# Chubb Limited (CB) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T12:27:09.086711+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Chubb Limited is a globally diversified property-and-casualty and life insurer with a disciplined underwriting culture, a $272bn asset base (FY2025 10-K), and a track record of premium growth and capital return. The quantitative screen rates CB as a buy on cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk factors (9/22 models; composite ~60%; P/E 12.4, P/B 1.8, ROE 14.8%, D/E 31%). Filings show sustained earnings momentum into 2026, with H1 2026 net income attributable to Chubb of $5.17bn versus $4.30bn in H1 2025 (Q2 2026 10-Q). The central debate is whether modest headline multiples adequately compensate for catastrophe and legacy-liability tail risk after a strong share-price run, and whether ROE in the mid-teens is sufficient for “quality compounder” status versus deep-value entry.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, Chubb offers a rare combination of scale, underwriting discipline, and shareholder-friendly capital allocation at a discount to many financial peers. The screen passes Graham Enterprising, Earnings Yield, FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Quality Value, Dividend Growth, Acquirer’s Multiple, Dreman Contrarian, and Earnings Quality — a breadth that suggests the market is not fully pricing durable cash generation rather than a single-factor anomaly.

Business quality is supported by primary filings. The FY2025 10-K describes an underwriting-first culture across six segments, with 2025 consolidated net premiums earned (NPE) of $53.0bn (+8% versus ~$49bn NPE in FY2024 per the FY2024 10-K). Geographic and product diversification (54 countries; commercial, personal, agricultural, overseas general, reinsurance, life) reduces cyclicality relative to mono-line peers. The Huatai platform (~87% ownership in P&C at December 2025) adds Asia exposure but also China regulatory concentration.

Valuation support comes from both earnings power and capital return. Yahoo fallback data (annual filing bodies lack full income statements) shows net income rising from $9.03bn (2023) to $10.31bn (2025), with diluted EPS of $25.68 in 2025 versus $22.51 in 2024. The Q2 2026 10-Q shows H1 2026 diluted EPS of $13.17 versus $10.63 in H1 2025, implying continued growth. Share repurchases remain material: $2.12bn in H1 2026 (10-Q equity statement) and $3.69bn in FY2025 (Yahoo fallback). Debt is moderate (D/E 31% on screen; total debt ~$17.2bn at December 2025 per Yahoo versus shareholders’ equity of $73.8bn).

Screen failures are informative rather than disqualifying. Graham Defensive fails on current ratio (0.41 — normal for insurers holding invested assets, not working capital). Buffett Quality and Economic Moat fail because ROE 14.8% sits below an 18% screen threshold; for a large insurer with significant unrealised investment marks and goodwill, mid-teens ROE on a $74bn equity base is respectable but not “wide moat” by strict quant rules. Piotroski F-Score 5/9 reflects leverage and margin trends, not earnings quality per se (positive net income, positive OCF, OCF > net income all pass).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Catastrophe and climate: The FY2025 10-K emphasises climate and inflation in underwriting, with wildfire exposure reduced in certain areas and catastrophe reinsurance purchased. Q2 2026 PPD commentary references catastrophe loss development in commercial property. Unresolved severity trends in nat-cat remain a structural industry risk not captured by value screens.

Legacy liabilities: Corporate segment carries asbestos and environmental (A&E) exposures from Westchester (1998), CIGNA P&C (1999), and legacy Chubb Corp (2016) acquisitions, managed through Brandywine runoff. Adverse PPD on molestation-related claims in Corporate ($158m adverse in Q2 2026 alone per 10-Q) indicates social-inflation tail risk outside standard actuarial models.

Investment portfolio: Fixed maturities of $125.5bn show gross unrealised losses of $3.7bn (AFS) at June 2026, principally from rising rates since purchase — mark-to-market headwind to reported equity via AOCI ($5.9bn accumulated OCI deficit). A $285m tax valuation allowance on unrealised losses is noted in the 10-Q.

Variable annuity / MRB: Market risk benefits liability of $505m at June 2026 (down from $659m at December 2025) with net amount at risk $1.19bn; equity-market declines would increase expected claims. This is a run-off book (no new VA business since 2007) but retains model-sensitive exposure.

China / Huatai: ~87% ownership in Huatai Group adds growth but concentrates regulatory, FX, and geopolitical risk; noncontrolling interests of $5.5bn reflect consolidated VIE and minority structures.

Reinsurance credit: Reinsurance recoverable $20.3bn net, with $324m valuation allowance for uncollectible reinsurance — manageable but worth monitoring in stress scenarios.

Liquidity screen red flag: Current ratio 0.41 fails defensive screens; this reflects insurer balance-sheet structure (large invested assets, insurance liabilities) rather than near-term insolvency. No going-concern language appears in available filing extracts.

Governance: Long-tenured CEO Evan Greenberg (since 2004) provides strategic continuity; related-party and succession concentration is a qualitative governance consideration not scored by screens.
