# Mid-America Apartment Communities, Inc. (MAA) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-26T18:15:06.182253+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Mid-America Apartment Communities (MAA) is a large Sun Belt multifamily REIT (~302 communities, ~103,000 units) rated **buy** on the quantitative screen (4/22 models; composite 60%), chiefly on **FCF yield (5.7%)**, **dividend yield (4.6%)**, **Neff PEGY**, and **earnings quality**. The investment case is a cycle turn in Sun Belt apartments: supply pressure is easing, occupancy and lease trends are stabilising, and MAA’s investment-grade balance sheet (total debt ~30% of adjusted assets; net debt/Adjusted EBITDA ~4.3x at year-end 2025) supports a growing dividend ($6.06/share in 2025). The central debate is whether modest same-store NOI pressure and elevated P/E (~41x) leave enough margin of safety for a value buyer, or whether income plus operational recovery is sufficient despite **-31.5%** trailing earnings growth and ongoing **RealPage** antitrust exposure.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, MAA offers **scaled, diversified Sun Belt exposure** with a durable cash-return profile rather than classic deep value. The screen passes all four families—**cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk**—but fails Graham, Schloss, Buffett Quality, and Piotroski (5/9), signalling this is **not** a net-net or quality-compounder pick. What it is: a **4.6% yield** on a REIT that paid **$6.06** per share in distributions in 2025 (10-K), above REIT payout requirements, with **FCF of ~$718m** (Yahoo fallback; filing bodies lack full cash-flow tables) and screen FCF yield of **5.7%**.

Business quality rests on platform scale (39 markets, smart-home and repositioning programmes, active development pipeline of ~2,500 units), conservative leverage policy (target net debt/Adjusted EBITDA 4.5x–5.5x; actual **4.3x** at 31 Dec 2025 per 10-K), and operational tools (revenue management, expense control). The **Neff PEGY** pass is notable given screen-reported earnings decline: the market may be pricing trough fundamentals while Sun Belt supply peaks—consistent with 2026 news on improving lease traction and falling new deliveries. MAA fits a **value + income + cyclical recovery** bucket more than absolute cheapness (P/B 2.8x; P/E 40.7x).

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory / legal:** The FY2025 10-K flags **RealPage revenue-management antitrust** litigation. MAA **entered a settlement agreement** on class claims alleging conspiracy to inflate rents via RealPage software; separate actions by the **District of Columbia** and **Commonwealth of Kentucky** remain pending. Filing language warns adverse outcomes could be material and are not fully insurable.

**Cyclical / operating:** Sun Belt multifamily remains supply-sensitive. Same-store NOI declined in Q1 2025 and Q1 2026 filings despite revenue growth, reflecting expense inflation (real estate taxes, utilities, insurance—explicitly flagged in risk factors). Top-five markets represent **~41%** of units (Atlanta, Dallas, Austin, Charlotte, Orlando). Short lease terms (~one year) pass rent declines through quickly.

**Governance / management:** Significant **executive team turnover** over 2024–2025, including internal elevation to CEO effective **1 Apr 2025** (FY2024 10-K); succession risk is disclosed. News (6 Apr 2026) highlighted former CEO/Executive Chairman 2025 pay—governance scrutiny possible but not determinative from filings alone.

**Financial / covenant:** No going-concern language in available extracts. Debt covenants appear comfortably met (30.2% vs 60% limit). However, filings note periods when **external funding** supported distributions and development; sustained NOI weakness could pressure dividend coverage. Interest expense rose **~9%** YoY in 2025 (Yahoo). Piotroski score **5/9** (leverage not declining; ROA and margins not improving).

**Insurance / climate:** Property insurance premiums rising; coastal/extreme-weather exposure disclosed. No pension-related red flags identified (typical REIT structure).
