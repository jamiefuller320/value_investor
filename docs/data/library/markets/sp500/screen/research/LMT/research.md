# Lockheed Martin Corporation (LMT) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:26:56.578141+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Lockheed Martin is the largest U.S. defence prime, with a record $230.4bn backlog (Q2 2026 10-Q) and broad exposure to munitions, F-35, missile defence and space. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness (Neff PEGY), dividend quality, GARP and risk metrics, despite headline earnings growth of −11.5% and a elevated P/B of 17.9x. H1 2026 filings show a sharp recovery from Q2 2025 programme charges: sales rose 5.4% to $38.1bn and net earnings to $3.3bn ($14.38 diluted EPS) versus $2.1bn ($8.75) a year earlier. The central debate is whether record demand and raised 2026 guidance (per news flow around the 23 July 2026 earnings release) outweigh cumulative contract losses on classified programmes (~$3.3bn accrued), an active IRS dispute seeking ~$4.6bn of additional tax, and securities litigation tied to prior disclosures.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, LMT offers a franchise defence compounder trading at ~21x trailing P/E with a 2.7% yield, high ROE (67.6% per screen) and a Neff PEGY of 0.08 — signalling that the market prices low growth relative to income and cash generation. Four of four screen families pass (cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk) with full data quality (20/20 metrics), and the signal has been stable for four weeks at 48% conviction.

Business quality is underwritten by U.S. government concentration (70% of H1 2026 sales per Q2 2026 10-Q MD&A), F-35 at ~28% of consolidated sales, and international mix rising to 30%. The FAS/CAS pension operating adjustment ($843m favourable in H1 2026) inflates reported segment margins but is structurally recoverable through U.S. government contract pricing. Dividend Growth and Earnings Quality model passes align with filing evidence: dividends increased to $3.45/share quarterly in 2026 ($6.90 H1 paid per Q2 2026 10-Q), and H1 2026 operating cash flow of $3.5bn supports shareholder returns despite elevated contract-asset build.

The screen’s buy signal is not a deep-value entry on asset backing (P/B 17.9x; tangible book negative per Yahoo balance sheet) but a GARP/dividend compounder at a reasonable earnings yield with improving operational momentum after a charge-laden 2025.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Programme execution and cumulative losses (filings): Q2 2026 10-Q discloses ~$1.8bn cumulative losses on an Aeronautics classified programme, ~$1.5bn on an MFC classified programme, ~$670m on CMHP and ~$130m on TUHP, with $427m–$1.1bn still accrued in current liabilities. Additional losses “could be material” if performance deteriorates. H1 2026 also recorded unfavourable adjustments on F-16 ($125m), C-130 ($95m) and helicopter programmes.

Customer concentration: F-35 at 27–28% of sales; 72% U.S. government revenue in FY2025 (10-K business section). Budget delays, continuing resolutions or F-35 funding cuts would hit disproportionately.

Tax dispute (Q2 2025 10-Q): IRS proposed ~$4.6bn additional federal income tax for 2018–2020 related to revenue/cost recognition method change. Uncertain tax position liability rose to $512m with $129m interest; resolution timing uncertain. Unfavourable outcome could materially affect profitability and cash flows.

Litigation (Q2 2026 10-Q): Putative securities class action (Jul 2025, amended Jan 2026) alleging misstatements on classified programmes and F-35, tied to Q2 2025 loss announcements. Derivative suits filed. Company states no material adverse effect expected; outcome unresolved.

Pension and leverage: Accrued pension liabilities ~$3.9bn; accumulated other comprehensive loss ~$7.5bn. Net debt ~$17.6bn (Yahoo FY2025). FAS/CAS adjustment flatters operating profit but pension risk remains if discount rates fall.

Supply chain and rare earths (10-K risk factors): Industry-wide shortages, tariff exposure and China export controls on rare earth minerals could delay deliveries and compress margins on fixed-price work.

ULA joint venture (Q2 2026 10-Q): LMT agreed to guarantee up to $500m of ULA borrowings after Vulcan Centaur performance issues; further support or impairment possible.

M&A: Definitive agreement to acquire Ultra Maritime for $3.45bn (announced 6 Jul 2026, Q2 2026 10-Q subsequent events), funded by cash and additional debt — integration and leverage risk.

Government contract terms: New executive orders may restrict dividends, buybacks and executive pay for underperforming critical defence contractors; UCA unilateral definitisation risk remains.

No going-concern language identified in available filing extracts.
