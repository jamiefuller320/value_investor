# DaVita Inc. (DVA) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:29:28.093872+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
DaVita is the dominant US outpatient dialysis operator (~2,657 US centres; ~200,500 patients), with a growing international footprint and ancillary integrated kidney care (IKC) businesses. The quantitative screen flags a buy on cheapness, GARP and risk metrics (P/E 22.7, strong free cash flow, earnings quality), but the investment case rests on a capital-light-looking franchise that is, in practice, highly levered and policy-sensitive. Filings show revenue growth and resilient US dialysis margins, yet attributable net income fell in FY2025 as interest expense, tax rate and Mozarc-related losses rose, while aggressive repurchases drove negative common shareholders’ equity. The central debate is whether durable dialysis cash generation and buyback optionality justify the current multiple after a sharp share-price run, or whether Medicare/commercial reimbursement risk, leverage and regulatory overhang cap upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, DaVita offers a quasi-utility healthcare franchise with oligopolistic US market position, recurring demand (ESRD/ESKD), and demonstrable cash conversion. The screen’s buy signal (6/22 models; composite score 80%; families passed: cheapness, GARP, risk) aligns with filing evidence of top-line expansion and operating discipline: total revenues rose from $12.8bn (2024) to $13.6bn (2025), US dialysis adjusted operating income was $2.1bn in both years, and management-reported free cash flow was $1.0bn in 2025. Passing models including FCF Yield, Graham Enterprising, Lynch PEG and Earnings Quality are consistent with a business generating substantial operating cash ($1.9bn in 2025) despite rising debt service.

The screen’s elevated ROE (81%) and negative P/B (-20.6) require context: FY2025 year-end common shareholders’ equity was **negative** $651m after $1.8bn of repurchases and treasury-stock retirement, so book-based metrics overstate “quality” on a naive reading. The more durable value hook is enterprise-level cash generation against a heavily indebted but refinancing-capable balance sheet, plus Berkshire Hathaway’s long-standing stake (news coverage cites a holder since 2011). Treatment volumes are mature in the US but ancillary/international growth and reimbursement increases (Q1 2026 US revenue per treatment +4.4% YoY per 10-Q MD&A) support modest organic growth. For a value investor willing to underwrite policy and leverage risk, the screen’s buy rating is directionally supported—but not unreservedly—by filing fundamentals.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory / reimbursement:** Filings repeatedly flag dependence on Medicare ESRD bundled rates, Medicaid, and Medicare Advantage benchmarks; MD&A cites potential impacts from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and broader federal policy. US dialysis revenue recognition carries ~1% estimating risk—material to segment margins per 10-K critical accounting policies.

**Leverage / capital structure:** ~$10.2bn debt at FY2025; debt expense consumed ~43% of operating income. May 2025 8-K documents a **$1.0bn 6.750% senior notes due 2033** offering; proceeds partly repaid revolver borrowings. Negative common equity and $1.5bn+ NCI put obligations add balance-sheet complexity not captured by simple P/E screens.

**Legal / compliance:** KPMG critical audit matters include legal proceedings and regulatory matters; 2025 MD&A references cybersecurity remediation charges ($25m) and legal accruals ($25m international in Q4 2025). Company operates in a “highly regulated industry” with qui tam, investigations and audits (10-K language). No going-concern warning; liquidity covenant comfort is management’s assertion.

**Operational:** Q1 2026 MD&A notes union organising in California clinics; labour inflation in patient care costs (+3.1% per treatment YoY). 2025 cyber incident referenced as affecting billing/collections risk. Mozarc equity investment losses/impairments ($46m in 2025) highlight ancillary execution risk.

**Governance / capital allocation:** Aggressive buybacks at elevated prices (Q1 2026 average ~$134–152 per share per 10-Q repurchase table; subsequent repurchases cited at lower prices in 10-K) consume cash that could de-lever. ~$332m paid to noncontrolling interests in 2025.

**Screen blind spots:** Distorted ROE from negative equity; P/B pass is not economically meaningful; timing/momentum data insufficient in screen snapshot.
