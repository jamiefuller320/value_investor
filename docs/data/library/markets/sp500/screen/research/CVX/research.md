# Chevron Corporation (CVX) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-02T12:30:43.988775+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Chevron Corporation (CVX) screens as a buy on dividend yield, GARP-style valuation metrics, and financial-health checks, with a 68% composite score and 3.6% indicated yield at P/E 18.9. The investment case rests on a step-change in upstream scale after the Hess acquisition, resilient operating cash flow despite lower reported earnings, and a 38-year record of dividend growth. The central debate is whether post-Hess production growth and downstream margin recovery offset a cyclical earnings trough, materially higher leverage, and quality-screen failures on ROE, moat, and free-cash conversion. Filings support cash-return capacity; they do not yet confirm a sustained earnings re-rating.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, CVX offers an integrated major with identifiable cash-return mechanics rather than deep-value or net-net characteristics. The quantitative screen passes seven of 22 models across four families—cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk—including Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, high dividend yield, dividend growth, Dreman contrarian, composite value, and financial health. That aligns with a business that returned $12.8bn in dividends and repurchased $12.1bn of stock in FY2025 (10-K, filed 24 February 2026), while raising the annual dividend to $6.84 per share.

Business quality is mixed relative to the screen. Production reached 3.7mn boe/d in 2025 (+12% YoY), driven by Hess, Permian growth, and Tengiz, with proved reserve replacement at 158% (FY2025 10-K). Free cash flow rose to $16.6bn despite net income falling to $12.3bn, indicating the asset base still monetises through the cycle. However, the screen fails Graham, deep value, Buffett quality, economic moat, and Piotroski (5/9) tests—ROCE fell to 6.6% and ROE to 7.3% in 2025—so this is a cyclical value/dividend name, not a quality compounder at current returns.

The Hess deal (~$48bn aggregate consideration, closed July 2025) reshapes the portfolio toward Guyana, the Permian, and Bakken midstream exposure, with management targeting $1bn in initial synergies. At a mid-cycle Brent assumption, growing volumes and structural cost savings ($3–4bn run-rate by end-2026, per FY2025 10-K) support the screen’s dividend and GARP passes, even as absolute valuation (P/B 2.1, EV/EBIT 25.9) is not statistically cheap on deep-value criteria.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Cyclical commodity exposure. Upstream earnings remain the primary profit driver; FY2025 results reflect a lower price environment, and interim 2025–Q1 2026 earnings are down materially year-on-year despite higher volumes.

Leverage step-up. Total debt rose from $24.5bn to $40.8bn at FY2025, largely Hess-related; net-debt-to-CFFO rose to ~1.0x from 0.6x. Interest coverage fell to 15.1x from 36.5x. Covenants are not highlighted as binding in extracts, but refinancing and commercial-paper reliance ($4.6bn outstanding at year-end 2025) add rate sensitivity.

Contingent decommissioning liabilities. FY2025 10-K discloses reverted Gulf of America decommissioning obligations ($297m spent in 2025; $200–300m annually through 2033) and states regulators may require Chevron to assume obligations if prior owners default—additional reversion possible, not currently quantified as material.

Geopolitical and sanctions risk. Filings flag Venezuela (sanctions policy changes), CPC/Tengiz export route disruption (including drone attacks), Israel operations amid regional conflict, and Russia-adjacent sanctions as sources of operational and earnings uncertainty.

Climate and litigation. Note 16 and MD&A reference climate-change litigation and Louisiana coastal matters; GHG policy displacement of hydrocarbons and potential asset impairment in sustained low-price scenarios. Chevron also removed the 2050 net-zero upstream timeline while retaining the aspiration (FY2025 10-K)—ESG/reputational overhang.

Pension and workforce. Higher pension settlement/curtailment costs contributed to “All Other” charges ($3.5bn net charge in 2025); restructuring and workforce reshaping (news, July 2025) may carry further benefit-plan impacts flagged as not yet estimable in the FY2024 10-K.

Screen-specific gaps. Quality and moat models fail on sub-12% ROE; earnings-quality overlay flags weak free-cash conversion relative to screen thresholds despite filing-level positive FCF—suggesting metric/timing mismatch rather than insolvency, but worth verifying before sizing.
