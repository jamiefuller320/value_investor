# Constellation Brands, Inc. (STZ) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:38:49.753104+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Constellation Brands, Inc. (STZ) screens as a **strong buy** on valuation and quality: 15/22 models pass (composite 77%), with P/E 12.3, P/B 2.7, 3.2% yield, and ROE 23.7%. The investment case rests on a dominant U.S. imported-beer franchise (Modelo Especial, Corona) generating high margins and robust cash returns, against a share price that news coverage describes as depressed after a multi-year drawdown. The central debate is whether headline cheapness reflects a cyclical demand trough—beer depletions were slightly negative in Q1 FY27—or a structural slowdown in Hispanic-led beer consumption, tariff and macro headwinds, and execution risk from wine portfolio reshaping and a new CEO. Filings support cash-generative beer economics but flag soft consumer demand; near-term guidance disappointment (per news) tempers the screen signal.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen aligns with a classic value setup in consumer staples: the name passes cheapness (Graham Enterprising, earnings yield, acquirer's multiple), quality (Buffett Quality, Piotroski F-Score 8+), dividend (high yield, dividend growth), GARP (Lynch/Neff PEG), and risk families, with full data quality (20/20 metrics). That profile fits a business that, per the FY26 10-K, is the #1 U.S. imported-beer supplier with Modelo Especial as the #1 U.S. beer brand by dollar sales.

Beer remains the economic engine. In Q1 FY27 (10-Q, period ended 31 May 2026), the Beer segment delivered $2,283.5m net sales (+2%) and a 39.0% comparable operating margin. Management targets comparable net leverage and dividend payout ratios, runs a $4.0bn share-repurchase authorisation (2025 Authorization), and returned capital aggressively: $223.8m of buybacks in Q1 FY27 alone, with $2,752.1m remaining on the programme as of 26 June 2026 (10-Q).

Portfolio simplification supports a cleaner beer-centric story: mainstream wine divestiture (June 2025, $845.9m proceeds), SVEDKA sale (January 2025), and New Zealand wine divestiture (completed June 2026) shrink a structurally weaker Wine and Spirits segment. The 2025 Restructuring Initiative targets over $200m net annualised savings by FY28. For a value investor, the screen's strong-buy signal is reinforced by normalized earnings power after FY25 impairment noise, high FCF generation (Yahoo: ~$1.8bn FY26), and a shareholder-return framework—offset by visible near-term volume softness and transition risk.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Demand and category cyclicality.** The FY26 10-K flags dependence on U.S. beer brands and "potential declines in the consumption of products we sell," citing subdued consumer spending, moderation/GLP-1 trends, cannabis substitution, and immigration-policy sensitivity for Hispanic consumers—a core Modelo/Corona demographic.

**Near-term volume softness.** Q1 FY27 beer depletions at −0.3% despite positive shipments suggest inventory or sell-through weakness; MD&A cites ongoing economic uncertainty and tariff impacts on FY26 and Q1 FY27 results.

**Portfolio and impairment history.** FY25's $2.25bn goodwill impairment (Q3 10-Q) and recurring asset-impairment charges (e.g. $18.3m on New Zealand wine held-for-sale in Q1 FY27) highlight wine divestiture and valuation risk. Management warns further impairments are possible.

**Leadership transition.** Nicholas Fink became President and CEO on 13 April 2026 (FY26 10-K). The filing states transition risk explicitly: strategy shifts, operational disruption, and stakeholder confidence.

**Leverage and covenants.** Q1 FY27 total debt ~$10.2bn against cash of ~$97m. The 2025 Credit Agreement imposes a **minimum interest-coverage ratio and maximum net-leverage ratio** (10-Q Note 8). No going-concern language appears in available extracts; liquidity relies on commercial paper ($336m outstanding), a $2.25bn revolver, and capital markets access.

**Tariffs and Mexico concentration.** Tariffs under Section 232 on aluminium affected results; beer was removed from that scope in April 2026 (Q1 FY27 MD&A). Production is concentrated at two Mexican breweries plus Veracruz under construction—operational, water, and geopolitical exposure.

**Governance.** Sands Family Stockholders retain Class A influence and board nomination rights (10-K risk factors). Class-action and securities litigation risk is disclosed.

**Tax.** OB3 Act provisions are expected to **negatively affect the FY27 effective tax rate** (Q1 FY27 10-Q Note 9).
