# Corpay, Inc. (CPAY) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:21:41.252350+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Corpay is a global corporate payments platform (commercial cards, AP automation, FX/cross-border payments, fleet and lodging) that has compounded revenue and earnings through organic growth and bolt-on M&A. The quantitative screen rates CPAY as a **buy** (6/22 models, 73% composite), supported by an ~8.6% FCF yield, Lynch PEG of 0.44, 32% ROE, and passes across cheapness, quality, GARP, and risk families. Filings show accelerating momentum: Q1 2026 net revenue rose 25% year-on-year to $1.26bn, with Corporate Payments up sharply post-Alpha, though reported net income was flattered by a $121m PayByPhone disposal gain. The central debate is whether structurally high returns and buyback-supported EPS growth justify leverage (~$10.4bn total debt), intangible-heavy balance sheet, and unresolved FTC litigation—not whether the underlying payments franchise is growing.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, CPAY offers a rare combination: a durable, asset-light payments model with high margins (Q1 2025 adjusted EBITDA margin ~55%, per 10-Q MD&A), strong cash conversion over the cycle, and a screen profile that looks inexpensive on growth-adjusted metrics (PEG 0.44, P/E 21.8x against ~49% quoted growth). The business sits at the intersection of B2B digitisation and spend control—a large addressable market the company estimates at ~$145tn of annual B2B spend.

Quantitative alignment is strong: six models pass, including FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Buffett Quality, Magic Formula, Composite Value, and Earnings Quality, with 19/20 data-quality metrics present. That suggests the screen is not picking up a low-quality cyclical, but a compounder temporarily available at a reasonable multiple.

Filings corroborate quality and growth. Nine-month 2025 net revenue reached $3.28bn (+12% vs $2.94bn in 9M 2024, Q3 2025 10-Q), with net income attributable to Corpay of $805m (+6%). Q1 2026 net revenue of $1.26bn (+25%) and diluted EPS of $5.07 (+49%) extend the trend, driven by Alpha (Corporate Payments), Gringo (Vehicle Payments), and FX derivative revenue mix shift. Management redeploys capital aggressively via buybacks ($786m in Q1 2026 alone; $9.4bn cumulative under the programme) and selective M&A, consistent with a high-ROE franchise.

The buy case rests on: (1) recurring, network-like payments revenue with operating leverage; (2) EPS compounding via buybacks and earnings growth; (3) screen metrics that underprice growth and cash generation relative to peers. The screen signal is stable (four weeks, 55% conviction overlay)—deep research largely confirms directionally, with caveats on leverage, acquisition integration, and legal overhang.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory / legal**
- **FTC matter** (fuel-card marketing practices): Eleventh Circuit **affirmed liability** against Corpay and CEO Ronald Clarke (Jan 2026); en banc review denied May 2026. Company states matter is “not and will not be material” but **cannot estimate possible losses**; permanent injunction in place. This is a material unresolved legal overhang not captured by quantitative screens.
- Extensive **FX derivative book** ($148bn notional at 31 Mar 2026) creates counterparty, collateral, and mark-to-market complexity.

**Financial / capital structure**
- **Leverage elevated:** Total debt $10.36bn against LTM net income ~$1.07bn (Yahoo). Credit agreement $10.15bn; significant near-term maturities (e.g. ~$2.8bn in 2027 per Q1 2026 10-Q maturity table). Covenants currently met, but refinancing and rate risk are real.
- **Intangible-heavy balance sheet:** Goodwill $7.3bn plus intangibles $3.1bn; accumulated goodwill impairment **$90m in FY2024** (Q1 2026 10-Q). Alpha accounting still preliminary.
- **Negative tangible book** limits traditional asset-based downside protection.
- **Interest expense** rising ($110m in Q1 2026 alone; $404m FY2025 per Yahoo).

**Operational / governance**
- **ITGC material weakness** identified in FY2024 10-K; management reports **full remediation as of 31 Dec 2025**, with 2025 acquisitions (23.8% of assets) excluded from internal-control assessment.
- **AvidXchange partnership** includes a potential **minimum-return guarantee** (~1.6x invested capital) if TPG sells and Corpay does not exercise its call—off-balance-sheet commitment risk.
- Q1 2026 **equity-method losses** of $17m (AvidXchange-related) in other expense.
- Credit loss allowance **$187m** at 31 Mar 2026, growing with receivables ($4.96bn gross); provision consistent with spend growth but bears monitoring.
- Customer deposits and restricted cash ($14.1bn combined at 31 Mar 2026) reflect payments-industry mechanics but add balance-sheet complexity.

**Cyclical / macro**
- MD&A flags recession, inflation, rates, FX, sanctions/tariffs as potentially material—relevant given Brazil (17% of Q1 2026 revenue) and UK/Europe exposure post-Alpha.

No **going-concern** language identified in available filing bodies.
