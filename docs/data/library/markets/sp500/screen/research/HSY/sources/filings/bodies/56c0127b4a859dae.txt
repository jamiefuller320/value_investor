QUANTITATIVE AND QUALITATIVE DISCLOSURES ABOUT MARKET RISK Interest Rate Risk The total amount of debt, net of cash, resulted in a net cash position of $241 million and $707 million, at September 27, 2026 and December 31, 2025, respectively. A hypothetical 100 basis point increase in interest rates applied to this debt as of September 27, 2026 would have changed interest expense by approximately $3.3 million for the first nine months of 2026 and $7.7 million for 2025. We consider our current risk related to market fluctuations in interest rates on our remaining debt portfolio, excluding debt converted to variable rates with instruments, to be minimal since this debt is largely and in nature. Generally, the fair market value of debt will increase as interest rates fall and decrease as interest rates rise. A 100 basis point increase in market interest rates would decrease the fair value of our debt at September 27, 2026 and December 31, 2025 by approximately $210 million and $236 million, respectively. However, since we currently have no plans to repurchase our outstanding instruments before their maturities, the impact of market interest rate fluctuations on our debt does not affect our results of operations or financial position. Foreign Currency Exchange Rate Risk We are exposed to currency fluctuations related to manufacturing or selling products in currencies other than the U.S. dollar. We may enter into foreign currency forward exchange contracts to reduce fluctuations in our long or short currency positions relating primarily to purchase commitments or forecasted purchases for equipment, raw materials and finished goods denominated in foreign currencies. The fair value of foreign currency forward exchange contracts represents the difference between the contracted and current market foreign currency exchange rates at the end of the period. We estimate the fair value of foreign currency forward exchange contracts on a quarterly basis by obtaining market quotes of spot and forward rates for contracts with similar terms, adjusted where necessary for maturity differences. The potential decline in fair value of foreign currency forward exchange contracts resulting from a hypothetical adverse change in market rates of 10% was $31.5 million as of September 27, 2026 and $38.7 million as of December 31, 2025, generally offset by a reduction in foreign exchange associated with our transactional activities.
