Growth was , with 11 of the Company's 24 domestic markets achieving increases in comparable hotel Total RevPAR. Comparable hotel Total RevPAR in 2026 was $392.74, an increase of 5.8%. • Comparable hotel RevPAR was $219.50, an increase of 8.8% compared to the same period in 2025, reflecting rate increases of 5.7% and an increase in occupancy of 2.1 points. The growth was driven by very strong transient demand throughout the quarter, boosted by the World Cup and an increase in business transient demand, in addition to an increase in group bookings, and despite the impacts from an active hurricane season in Hawaii during the quarter. Comparable hotel RevPAR in 2026 was $237.21, an increase of 6.6%. • GAAP net income was $71 million, a 56.4% decrease compared to the third quarter of 2025, reflecting a decline in gain on asset sales, partially offset by an improvement in GAAP operating profit margin of 140 basis points to 9.0%, as higher room rates offset wage expense increases. , GAAP net income was $813 million, a 27.2% increase compared to 2025, benefiting from gains on asset sales and GAAP operating profit margin of 15.8%, an improvement of 110 basis points compared to 2025. • Comparable hotel EBITDA was $331 million, an increase of 11.4% compared to the third quarter of 2025, reflecting a comparable hotel EBITDA margin increase of 90 basis points to 25.2% due to improvements in operations, largely driven by average room rate increases, which offset increases in wage expenses over the same period in 2025. , comparable hotel EBITDA was $1,327 million, an increase of 8.2% compared to 2025, while comparable hotel EBITDA margin increased 70 basis points to 30.2%. Comparable hotel results for both periods exclude Alila Ventana Big Sur, which was removed from the comparable hotel set in the third quarter after the hotel was closed for one month during an evacuation mandate due to wildfires in the vicinity. • Adjusted EBITDA re was $347 million, an increase of 8.8% compared to the third quarter of 2025. Results benefited from improved operations and comparable hotel EBITDA margins, which more than offset declines due to the 2025 and 2026 dispositions. Adjusted EBITDA re was $1,415 million, exceeding 2025 by 6.5%. • Subsequent to quarter end, the Company acquired the leasehold interest in the 195-room 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge for a total purchase price of $202 million, which is net of the $4 million furniture, fixtures and equipment ("FF&E") reserve. The Company also acquired the fee simple interest in the 308-room Renaissance Dallas at Plano Legacy West Hotel for a total purchase price of $172 million. The 1 Hotel Brooklyn Bridge, a LEED luxury hotel that sits on Pier 1 in Brooklyn Bridge Park, overlooks the East River and the Manhattan skyline. The Renaissance anchors the Legacy West development in Plano, Texas and has approximately 35,000 square feet of meeting space. • Subsequent to quarter end, the Company amended and restated its $2.5 billion credit facility, extending the maturity of the $1.5 billion revolver to January 2031 and the maturities of the two $500 million term loans to January 2031 and January 2032, respectively, with extension options that, if exercised, would align all maturities under the facility at January 2032. Under the amended facility, the Company expanded the accordion feature to $1.25 billion and achieved total reductions of 12.5 basis points on revolver draws, 15 basis points on the term loans, and 5 basis points on the facility fee, reflecting both a reset of the pricing grid to current market levels and a new pricing benefit, while maintaining pricing incentives linked to portfolio sustainability initiatives. NAREIT FFO AND NAREIT FFO PER D ILUTED S HARE We present NAREIT FFO and NAREIT FFO per diluted share as measures of our performance in addition to our earnings per share (calculated in accordance with GAAP). We calculate NAREIT FFO per diluted share as our NAREIT FFO (defined as set forth below) for a given operating period, as adjusted for the effect of dilutive securities, divided by the number of fully diluted shares outstanding during such period, in accordance with NAREIT guidelines. As noted in NAREIT’s Funds From Operations White Paper – 2018 Restatement, NAREIT defines FFO as net income (calculated in accordance with GAAP) excluding depreciation and amortization related to certain real estate assets, gains and losses from the sale of certain real estate assets, gains and losses from change in control, impairment expense of certain real estate assets and investments and adjustments for consolidated partially owned entities and unconsolidated affiliates. Adjustments for consolidated partially owned entities and unconsolidated affiliates are calculated to reflect our pro rata share of the FFO of those entities on the same basis. We believe that NAREIT FFO per diluted share is a useful supplemental measure of our operating performance and that the presentation of NAREIT FFO per diluted share, when combined with the primary GAAP presentation of diluted earnings per share, provides beneficial information to investors. By excluding the effect of real estate depreciation, amortization, impairment expense and gains and losses from sales of depreciable real estate, all of which are based on historical cost accounting and which may be of lesser significance in evaluating current performance, we believe that such measures can facilitate comparisons of operating performance between periods and with other REITs, even though NAREIT FFO per diluted share does not represent an amount that accrues directly to holders of our common stock. Historical cost accounting for real estate assets implicitly assumes that the value of real estate assets diminishes predictably over time. As noted by NAREIT in its Funds From Operations White Paper – 2018 Restatement, the primary purpose for including FFO as a supplemental measure of operating performance of a REIT is to address the artificial nature of historical cost depreciation and amortization of real estate and real assets mandated by GAAP. For these reasons, NAREIT adopted the FFO metric in order to promote a uniform measure of REIT operating performance.
