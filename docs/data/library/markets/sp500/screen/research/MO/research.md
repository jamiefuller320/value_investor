# Altria Group, Inc. (MO) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:58:29.236917+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Altria remains a cash-generative US tobacco franchise trading at a modest earnings multiple (~15×) with a near-6% dividend yield and a screen profile that passes cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk families simultaneously. The core smokeable and oral tobacco businesses continue to deliver resilient operating income through pricing, whilst reported earnings are volatile due to NJOY-related impairments, litigation accruals and lumpy investment gains. The central debate is whether current free cash flow and dividend coverage justify accumulation after a strong share-price run, or whether structural volume decline, e-vapor setbacks and persistent litigation cap upside. Deep filing review broadly supports the screen’s value case, but headline growth metrics overstate normalised trends.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The quantitative screen flags MO as a strong buy on a composite score of 89% with 10 of 22 models passing, spanning all four metric families: cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk. Key screen inputs include P/E ~15.0, dividend yield ~5.9%, FCF yield ~7.0% and a very low Lynch PEG (~0.14), alongside passes on FCF Yield, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple and Earnings Quality.

Business quality, as evidenced in filings, aligns with this profile in the legacy franchise: smokeable products segment operating companies income (OCI) rose to $10,984m in FY2025 (from $10,821m in FY2024 and $10,670m in FY2023), demonstrating pricing power despite volume pressure. Oral tobacco OCI strengthened materially to $1,828m in FY2025 (from $1,449m in FY2024), reflecting pouch category momentum. Operating cash conversion remains robust—nine-month operating cash flow was $6,019m in the period to September 2025—and capital intensity is low (FY2025 capex $216m per 10-K segment note).

For a value investor, the hook is a high-coverage income stream (quarterly dividend raised to $1.06 per share, declared May 2026) funded by durable US nicotine cash flows, plus an unrecognised ABI stake (carrying value $8.6bn vs fair value ~$11.0bn at 31 March 2026, per Q1 2026 10-Q). Negative book equity (P/B -38.0 on the screen) reflects aggressive buybacks rather than operational insolvency; retained earnings remain positive at $35.9bn. The screen’s strong buy signal is therefore grounded in real cash economics, not accounting optics alone.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and structural:** Core US cigarette volumes continue to decline; filings acknowledge FDA user fees, potential menthol restrictions and illicit disposable e-vapor competition. The ITC issued an exclusion order effective 31 March 2025 prohibiting importation and sale of NJOY ACE, triggering $873m (Q1 2025) and $285m (Q4 2025) goodwill impairments totalling $1,158m accumulated impairment on the e-vapor unit.

**Litigation:** Filings state that damages in tobacco cases “have ranged in the billions of dollars” and that an unfavourable outcome “could materially affect” results in a given quarter. PM USA had ~48 Engle progeny cases pending (January 2026) and ~182 individual smoking-and-health cases (December 2025). MSA payments remain a ~$3bn annual cash drag. Management records no probable loss accrual for most pending cases but acknowledges reasonably possible adverse outcomes.

**Tax:** Active IRS audit (2017–2023) and a September 2025 adverse district court ruling on ABI downward-attribution rules led to a $348m reserve for 2018–2025 tax years; Altria intends to appeal. Unrecognised tax benefits were $585m at 31 December 2025.

**Governance and capital structure:** Negative common equity from $43.4bn treasury stock against accumulated deficits on the balance sheet; net debt ~$21bn (Yahoo, FY2025). High dividend commitment plus buybacks constrain balance-sheet flexibility if e-vapor or litigation costs escalate.

**Pension:** Pension overfunded, but post-retirement health care underfunded by ~$1bn—manageable but a long-run cash obligation.

**Competitive:** E-vapor segment net revenues were negative $(13)m in FY2025 (returns/charges); oral tobacco is growing but faces category migration risk. Two customers accounted for ~42% of consolidated net revenues in FY2025 (10-K)—concentration risk.
