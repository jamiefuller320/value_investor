# VeriSign, Inc. (VRSN) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:30:05.087059+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
VeriSign operates a quasi-monopoly franchise as sole registry operator for .com (contracted through November 2030) and .net, earning high-margin, recurring fees on ~179 million domain names. The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on quality, GARP, dividend growth, and earnings-quality metrics (PEGY 0.22; P/E 28.4; FCF ~$834m), consistent with a business generating ~68% operating margins and mid-single-digit organic revenue growth. Filings confirm accelerating domain-base trends in 2025–2026, supported by .com price increases and record new registrations, whilst capital returns (buybacks plus a new quarterly dividend) absorb most free cash flow. The central debate is whether ~28x earnings fairly prices a regulated toll-road asset with negative book equity, ~$2.3bn of debt, and ~31% revenue concentration in one registrar customer — not whether the underlying economics are durable.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, VRSN offers rare business quality: contracted pricing power (.com fees rising to $10.97 from November 2026), minimal capex (~$23m in FY2025 per Yahoo fallback), and operating income that scales with a largely fixed cost base. The screen’s four passing families map cleanly to the filing record:

- **Quality / Piotroski F-Score:** Operating margin held near 68% in H1 2026 (10-Q, 23 Jul 2026); FY2025 operating income was $1,121m on revenue of $1,657m (10-K, 5 Feb 2026).
- **GARP / Neff PEGY:** Revenue grew 6% in Q2 and H1 2026; FY2025 revenue rose 6.4% to $1,656.6m (2024: $1,557.4m). EPS growth (~10% FY2025 diluted EPS per Yahoo) sits against a PEGY of 0.22 on the screen.
- **Dividend Growth:** Quarterly dividend of $0.81 ($1.62 paid in H1 2026; 10-Q) represents a new capital-return leg atop an aggressive buyback programme ($427m repurchased in H1 2026; Board refreshed authorisation to $1.5bn in July 2026).
- **Risk / Earnings Quality:** Deferred revenue of ~$1.45bn (current plus non-current, 30 Jun 2026 balance sheet) provides multi-year revenue visibility; renewal rates improved (Q1 2026 final renewal rate 76.3% vs 75.5% prior year).

The screen’s negative P/B (-11.2) reflects accounting equity destroyed by cumulative buybacks ($16.2bn since inception per Q2 10-Q), not impaired assets — a common pattern in capital-light, cash-rich franchises run for per-share value rather than balance-sheet optics.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Regulatory and contract renewal.** Revenue depends on ICANN registry agreements (.com through 30 November 2030; .net through 30 June 2029) and the US Department of Commerce cooperative agreement. Price increases are capped (up to 7% annually in each of the final four years of specified .com periods). Any adverse renegotiation would be existential to the thesis, though the November 2024 .com renewal provides medium-term visibility.

**Customer concentration.** One registrar accounted for ~31% of FY2025 revenue (10-K). The company downplays transfer risk, but pricing negotiations with a dominant channel partner remain a latent margin pressure.

**Leverage and negative equity.** ~$2.3bn of senior notes against $1.0bn liquid assets and a stockholders’ deficit of $2.3bn is manageable given ~$500m+ semi-annual operating cash flow, but limits financial flexibility in a downturn. Minimum senior-note obligations total $2.1bn through 2030 plus interest (10-K FY2025 commitments table). The company issued $550m of 5.10% 2031 notes in June 2026 to refinance 2017 maturities.

**Competitive and technology disruption.** Filings flag competition from ccTLDs, alternative gTLDs, and substitutes for an online presence; Q2 2026 MD&A also notes AI tools as a positive driver of website creation — the net effect is unresolved. Renewal rates near 76% leave room for erosion if domain economics weaken.

**Governance and insider activity.** D. James Bidzos remains CEO/Executive Chairman. News flow shows routine 10b5-1 sales by the CEO (3,300 shares, 22 Jul 2026) alongside director RSU grants — not alarming in isolation but worth monitoring given long tenure.

**Legal.** .web delegation disputes were resolved on confidential, non-material terms (Q2 2026 10-Q, Item 1). Other litigation is described as immaterial individually.

**Tax.** Swiss operations, OECD Pillar 2 minimum tax, and US state exposure contribute to an effective rate above the 21% federal statutory rate (~23% in H1 2026). A $55m valuation allowance on deferred tax assets persists (10-K FY2025).

**Pension.** Only a defined-contribution 401(k) plan is disclosed; no material defined-benefit pension obligation identified in available extracts.
