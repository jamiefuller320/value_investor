# Occidental Petroleum Corporation (OXY) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-16T13:24:09.935257+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Occidental Petroleum is a large, oil-weighted upstream operator with leading Permian, DJ Basin, Gulf of America, and Middle East positions, now refocused as a pure-play hydrocarbon and midstream business following the January 2026 sale of OxyChem to Berkshire Hathaway. The quantitative screen flags the name on cheapness, dividend, GARP, and risk metrics (P/E 17.2, P/B 1.7, debt/equity 35%, FCF yield), but quality screens fail (ROE 10.6%, Piotroski 4/9, current ratio 0.94). The investment case rests on a high-quality asset base, aggressive post-divestiture deleveraging, and optionality from CO₂ EOR and low-carbon ventures—not on deep moat or earnings stability. The central debate is whether debt reduction and portfolio simplification justify a value entry, or whether cyclical earnings, legacy liabilities (Tronox tax, environmental remediation), preferred dividends, and commodity exposure leave the screen too optimistic.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, OXY offers a levered but improving balance sheet on a tier-one resource base at a modest absolute and sector-relative valuation. The screen passes seven models—including Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Lynch PEG, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, and Dreman Contrarian—across all four signal families (cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk), with composite score 59% versus sector 54%. That aligns with a business that generates substantial operating cash through the cycle but has carried Anadarko-era leverage.

Filings support a credible deleveraging narrative. The OxyChem transaction closed on 2 January 2026 for $9.7 billion cash (8-K); the Q2 2026 10-Q cites an adjusted price of $9.5 billion and a gain of approximately $3.1 billion net of tax. H1 2026 proceeds and operating cash funded roughly $8.6 billion of debt repayment; cash and equivalents stood at $4.2 billion with $4.2 billion undrawn on the revolving credit facility and no RCF borrowings at 30 June 2026. Management’s stated priority is balance-sheet strength, a growing dividend (raised 8% to $0.28/quarter in August 2026), and sustaining base production (~1.43 MMboe/d in H1 2026).

Business quality is solid at the asset level: 4,603 MMboe proved reserves and 523 MMboe sales volumes in 2025 (2025 10-K), Permian scale, international oil in Oman and the UAE, and a midstream/marketing segment that contributed materially to H1 2026 earnings through marketing optimisation. Post-OxyChem, earnings are more exposed to oil and gas cyclicality—consistent with the screen’s cheapness tilt but inconsistent with moat/quality model failures. Berkshire’s long-standing stake and OxyChem purchase reinforce strategic relevance, though that is not itself a valuation anchor.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclicality:** With OxyChem sold, OXY is more exposed to oil, NGL, and gas prices (2025 10-K risk factors). No hedges at year-end 2025; H1 2026 earnings benefited from a sharp crude rebound (WTI averaged $82.36/bbl in H1 2026 per Q2 10-Q). Screen cyclical overlay was not triggered, but earnings remain commodity-dependent.

**Leverage and capital structure:** Despite ~$8.6bn H1 2026 repayments, ~$11.4bn of debt matures from 2029 onward (Q2 2026 10-Q). Preferred stock ($8.3bn equity per Yahoo 2025) absorbs ~$170m/quarter in dividends before common holders. S&P rates BB+ (below investment grade). The 2025 10-K warns that indebtedness limits flexibility in downturns.

**Tronox tax litigation:** Anadarko’s $5.2bn Tronox settlement deduction remains disputed. If disallowed, repayment of the $881m 2016 refund plus related benefits and interest totalled approximately **$2.3bn** at 31 December 2025 (2025 10-K)—not covered by insurance. An uncertain tax position is recorded; outcome is pending in U.S. Tax Court.

**Environmental and legacy liabilities:** OxyChem sale retained legacy environmental liabilities; Occidental guaranteed indemnification to Berkshire Hathaway (2025 10-K). The 2024 10-K cited reasonably possible **additional** environmental remediation losses up to **$1.9bn** beyond recorded amounts at 158 monitored sites. Q3 2025 10-Q references ongoing remediation across 155 sites. New Mexico Environment Department proposed penalties for alleged delayed emission reporting (2025 10-K Item 3).

**Governance and management transition:** The Q2 2026 10-Q exhibits include a **Separation Agreement with CEO Vicki Hollub effective 1 June 2026**—material succession risk not fully reflected in news coverage. Richard A. Jackson became COO in October 2025 and led the Q2 2026 earnings call.

**Low-carbon execution risk:** STRATOS DAC (500,000 t CO₂/yr design capacity; initial 250,000 t from trains 1–2 expected 2026) and CCUS hubs face technology, regulatory, and market-development uncertainty per 2025 10-K risk factors.

**Operational/geopolitical:** Q2 2026 10-Q notes Middle East production disruptions from regional conflict; domestic natural gas realisations were negative in Q2 2026 ($(1.48)/Mcf U.S.) due to marketing/transport dynamics.

**Screen blind spots:** Failed models include Graham Defensive (P/E, current ratio), Quality Value/Buffett/Economic Moat (ROE), Piotroski F-Score (4/9), Financial Health (weak liquidity), and Acquirer's Multiple (EV/EBIT 20.9).
