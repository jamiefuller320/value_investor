# American Tower Corporation (AMT) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T11:26:33.103121+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
American Tower is a global tower and data-centre REIT whose core property-leasing model (97% of FY2025 revenue) generates contractually escalated cash flows across roughly 149,700 communications sites and 30 US CoreSite facilities. The quantitative screen rates the name a **buy** (7/22 models; composite 59%; sector-relative 61%), driven by FCF yield, dividend metrics, Lynch/Neff PEG-style signals, and quality screens — not classic asset cheapness (P/B 22.0). The valuation hook is a cash-return compounder trading below recent highs amid rate sensitivity, with a 4.2% dividend yield and mid-single-digit revenue growth still visible in interim filings. The central debate is whether recurring tower economics and portfolio simplification (India, Australia/New Zealand, South Africa fibre exits) outweigh elevated leverage (~$37.2bn consolidated debt per the FY2025 10-K), customer concentration, and active tenant disputes (DISH default; AT&T Mexico arbitration).

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, AMT fits the screen less as a deep balance-sheet bargain and more as a **quality infrastructure franchise** screened as attractive on cash-return and growth-adjusted metrics. The screen passes five factor families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with FCF yield 5.9%, PEG 0.35, dividend yield 4.2%, ROE 30.0%, and confirmation from Piotroski F-Score and earnings-quality models.

Business quality anchors the signal. Management discloses over **$54bn** of future tenant lease revenue (FY2025 10-K), ~3% annual US contractual escalators, high incremental margins on colocations, and churn of approximately **2%** of tenant billings in both FY2024 and FY2025. AMT is investment-grade rated with **$11.1bn** of available liquidity at year-end 2025. Data centres contributed 10% of FY2025 revenue, adding hybrid-IT optionality via CoreSite without abandoning the tower core. Portfolio pruning — India sale (~$2.2bn, September 2024), Australia/New Zealand exits, South Africa fibre divestiture (2025) — redirects capital toward developed markets, consistent with management’s stated objective of growing AFFO per share and ROIC over the long term.

The buy rating is therefore coherent: a dividend-paying infrastructure compounder with screen-favourable FCF and PEG metrics, trading at a depressed multiple versus its own recent history (news references 52-week lows near $165 and rate-driven share weakness), while screens capture leverage and tenant credit only partially via the risk-family pass.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Customer concentration and credit** (10-K FY2025): four carriers account for ~59% of total revenue. Two active disputes elevate near-term uncertainty:

- **DISH Wireless:** delivered notice in September 2025 purporting to be excused from its Strategic Collocation Agreement; in default as of January 2026; litigation pending. DISH was ~2% of property revenue in FY2025; parent EchoStar’s network decommissioning is cited as a risk factor.
- **AT&T Mexico:** arbitration over master lease rent calculation; withheld rents in 2025; partial payment agreement with escrow for disputed amounts; ~$30m reserves taken; outcomes uncertain.

**Churn and consolidation:** churn ~2%, but the FY2024 10-K flagged elevated US churn through 2025 from T-Mobile/Sprint legacy lease cancellations under the 2020 master lease agreement. Carrier consolidation and RAN sharing remain structural threats to lease-up.

**Leverage and covenants** (10-K): ~$37.2bn consolidated debt; interest expense **$1.36bn** in FY2025 (Yahoo). Filing language warns that leverage could require dedicating a substantial portion of operating cash flow to debt service, limit financing flexibility, and trigger acceleration under **financial ratio covenants** in securitisation and credit-facility agreements. Restrictive covenants may restrict dividend payments if breached — relevant for REIT qualification. **No going-concern language** appears in available extracts; liquidity ($11.1bn) and investment-grade ratings mitigate but do not eliminate refinancing risk.

**International/FX:** ~51% of revenue outside US & Canada (segment mix in 10-K). Foreign operations face currency, political, and regulatory risks; Brazil debenture listing requirements and mandatory infrastructure access rules in certain markets add complexity.

**Data-centre competition:** CoreSite faces well-capitalised competitors with lower leverage; a significant portion of data-centre leases expire annually (10-K risk language).

**Accounting/governance:** FY2025 net income volatility from security gains/losses reduces earnings quality despite the screen’s earnings-quality pass. International president Olivier Puech retired effective 2 January 2026 (8-K, 27 February 2026); not a red flag on its own but worth tracking for execution continuity.
