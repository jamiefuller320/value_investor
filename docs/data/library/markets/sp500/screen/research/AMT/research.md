# American Tower Corporation (AMT) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T11:13:02.658586+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
American Tower Corporation (AMT) is a global tower and data-centre REIT with a durable, contractually escalated leasing model: property operations generated 97% of revenue in FY2025, across roughly 149,700 communications sites plus 30 US data-centre facilities (CoreSite). The quantitative screen rates the name a buy (7/22 models; composite 59%), driven chiefly by free-cash-flow yield, dividend metrics, Lynch/Neff PEG-style growth-at-a-reasonable-price signals, and quality screens — not classic asset cheapness (P/B 22.0). The investment case rests on recurring tower cash flows, a 4.2% dividend yield with growth, and optionality from 5G densification and CoreSite interconnection demand, against a backdrop of elevated leverage (~$37.2bn consolidated debt per the FY2025 10-K), customer concentration, and active tenant disputes (DISH default; AT&T Mexico arbitration). The central debate is whether FCF and AFFO growth plus portfolio simplification (India, Australia/New Zealand, South Africa fibre divestitures) justify the REIT multiple after a ~24% share-price drawdown, or whether rate sensitivity, churn, and credit risk cap upside.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value-oriented investor, AMT qualifies less as a deep asset discount and more as a quality compounder screened as cheap on cash-return and growth-adjusted metrics. The screen passes five factor families — cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, and risk — with FCF yield 5.9%, PEG 0.35, dividend yield 4.2%, ROE 30.0%, and Piotroski F-Score / earnings-quality confirmation. That profile fits a business with long-duration tenant leases (~$54bn of future tenant lease revenue disclosed in the FY2025 10-K), ~3% US contractual escalators, and low incremental cost to add tenants — structural qualities that support dividend growth and FCF even when headline P/E (26.8x) looks full.

Business quality anchors the screen signal. AMT is the largest independent tower owner globally, investment-grade rated, with $11.1bn available liquidity at year-end 2025 (10-K). Organic revenue growth is predictable; churn was ~2% of tenant billings in both FY2024 and FY2025. Data centres (10% of FY2025 revenue) add hybrid-IT exposure via CoreSite without abandoning the tower core. Portfolio pruning — India sale (~$2.2bn, September 2024), Australia/New Zealand exits, South Africa fibre sale (2025) — redirects capital toward developed markets, aligning with management’s stated capital-allocation framework (AFFO per share and ROIC over the long term).

The screen’s buy rating is therefore coherent: this is a cash-generative, dividend-paying infrastructure franchise trading at a depressed multiple versus its own history (news flow references 52-week lows near $165 and a ~24% slide on rate fears), with growth metrics that still screen favourably on a PEG/FCF basis. The gap is balance-sheet risk and tenant credit events, which screens capture only partially via the risk family pass — not fully via leverage alone.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Customer concentration and credit (10-K FY2025): four carriers account for ~59% of total revenue (T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon, Telefónica). Any material lease cancellation or renegotiation is a first-order risk. Two active disputes elevate near-term uncertainty:

- DISH Wireless: delivered notice in September 2025 purporting to be excused from its Strategic Collocation Agreement; in default as of January 2026; litigation pending. DISH was ~2% of property revenue in FY2025; parent EchoStar’s network decommissioning is cited as a risk factor.
- AT&T Mexico: arbitration over master lease rent calculation; withheld rents in 2025; partial payment agreement with escrow for disputed amounts; ~$30m reserves taken; outcomes uncertain.

Churn and consolidation: churn ~2%, but the FY2024 10-K flagged elevated US churn through 2025 from T-Mobile/Sprint legacy lease cancellations under the 2020 master lease agreement. Carrier consolidation and RAN sharing remain structural threats to lease-up.

Leverage and covenants (10-K): ~$37.2bn consolidated debt; substantial interest burden ($1.36bn interest expense FY2025 per Yahoo). Filing language warns that leverage could require dedicating a substantial portion of operating cash flow to debt service, limit financing flexibility, and trigger acceleration under financial ratio covenants in securitisation and credit-facility agreements. Restrictive covenants may restrict dividend payments if breached — relevant for REIT qualification. No going-concern language appears in available extracts; liquidity ($11.1bn) and investment-grade ratings mitigate but do not eliminate refinancing risk in a higher-for-longer rate environment (10-year Treasury ~4.68% per macro context, colour only).

International/FX: ~51% of revenue outside US & Canada (segment mix in 10-K). Foreign operations face currency, political, and regulatory risks; Brazil debenture listing requirements and mandatory infrastructure access rules in certain markets add complexity.

Data-centre competition: CoreSite faces well-capitalised competitors with lower leverage; a significant portion of data-centre leases expire annually (10-K risk language).

Accounting/governance: FY2025 net income volatility from security gains/losses reduces earnings quality despite the screen’s earnings-quality pass — worth monitoring. International president Olivier Puech retired effective 2 January 2026 (8-K, 27 February 2026); not flagged as a red flag on its own but worth tracking for execution continuity.
