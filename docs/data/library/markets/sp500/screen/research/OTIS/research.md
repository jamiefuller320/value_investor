# Otis Worldwide Corporation (OTIS) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T10:59:19.383462+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Otis is the global leader in elevators and escalators, with a structurally attractive Service franchise (~65% of net sales, ~91% of segment operating profit per the FY2025 10-K business disclosure) supporting recurring revenue from a ~2.5 million-unit maintenance portfolio. The quantitative screen flags the name as a strong buy on cheapness, dividend, GARP and risk metrics (P/E ~19x, FCF yield ~5.4%, dividend yield ~2.4%, PEGY ~0.08), yet the share price has weakened materially amid repeated profit-guidance reductions. The central debate is whether Service-led organic growth, modernization backlog and capital return can offset cyclical New Equipment weakness—especially in China—and labour-cost pressure that is compressing margins even as revenues beat.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, OTIS offers a rare combination: a dominant installed-base business with high switching costs, durable free cash flow, and a shareholder-return policy (buybacks plus a growing dividend) that the screen captures well. Seven of 22 models pass, spanning FCF Yield, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Magic Formula, Dreman Contrarian, Composite Value and Earnings Quality—covering all four factor families (cheapness, dividend, GARP, risk). At ~19x trailing earnings and ~5.4% FCF yield, the market appears to price in slower earnings growth than the screen’s ~13% growth input implies.

Business quality aligns with the screen’s risk-family pass: Service revenue is accelerating (Q2 2026 Service net sales $2.58bn, +11% year-on-year per the Q2 2026 10-Q), remaining performance obligations stood at ~$19.7bn at 30 June 2026 (~75% expected to convert to sales within 24 months), and H1 2026 operating cash flow of $680m exceeded the prior-year $405m. The offset is New Equipment—just $40m segment operating profit on $1.28bn Q2 sales (3% margin)—which remains tied to construction cycles and China (~one-fifth of global New Equipment sales per the 10-K). Negative reported book equity (treasury-stock accounting) explains the screen’s anomalous P/B and argues for cash-flow-based rather than book-based valuation.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Cyclical and geographic:** The 10-K emphasises China as the largest global new-equipment market, with Otis New Equipment sales there representing ~20% of global segment sales and >50% of unit volume. Q2 2026 10-Q flags that new U.S. and other import tariffs “could have a material impact on our results in 2026 and future years.” New Equipment segment operating margin was just ~3% in Q2 2026.

**Margin and labour:** News flow (see below) highlights labour inflation and service-quality investment weighing on profitability despite revenue beats—consistent with Service revenue growing faster than segment profit in some periods.

**Balance sheet and capital structure:** ~$7.7–8.2bn of debt; negative shareholders’ equity (~-$5.75bn at 30 June 2026) from treasury-stock accumulation. The company reports covenant compliance (Q2 2026 10-Q, Note 6) and renewed its $1.5bn revolving credit facility to August 2030 (8-K, 8 August 2025), with a maximum consolidated leverage ratio covenant. No going-concern language appears in available filing bodies.

**Pension:** Future pension and postretirement obligations of $411m at 30 June 2026; net periodic benefit cost ~$20m in H1 2026. The 10-K warns that discount-rate and asset-return volatility can affect funding.

**Legal and regulatory:** European civil cartel overcharge claims; asbestos liabilities reserved at $11m minimum (range $11–31m through 2059); German tax litigation with ~$55m indemnity payable to RTX under the Tax Matters Agreement; Belgium tax assessment fully reserved. 64% of the U.S. workforce is unionised (10-K).

**Governance / accounting:** Q1 2026 included a $114m out-of-period noncontrolling-interest adjustment (immaterial to prior P&L/cash flow per the 10-Q). Supplier finance programme obligations of $716m sit in accounts payable.

**Competitive:** Global oligopoly (KONE, Schindler, TK Elevator) but fragmented independent service providers hold ~50% of units by count.
