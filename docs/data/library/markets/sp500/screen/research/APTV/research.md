# Aptiv PLC (APTV) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-07-25T13:34:33.652806+00:00 · Mode: initial_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Aptiv is a global automotive technology supplier spanning advanced safety/software, engineered components, and electrical distribution, with a pending spin-off of the latter as Versigent (VGNT). The quantitative screen flags the name as a buy on cash-flow and earnings-yield metrics (FCF yield ~9.1%, EV/EBIT ~9.4, P/B 1.3), offset by a depressed reported ROE (4.1%) and elevated headline P/E (33.5) after a volatile 2025 earnings year. The central debate is whether strong underlying cash generation and portfolio simplification justify accumulation, or whether a $648 million Q3 2025 goodwill impairment, spin execution risk, and OEM pricing pressure undermine the value case. Near-term catalysts include the Versigent distribution (record date 17 March 2026; trading from 1 April 2026) and continued partnership-led software growth (Kyndryl, NVIDIA).

---

## INVESTMENT THESIS
For a value investor, APTV offers a classic “headline earnings vs. cash flow” dislocation. The screen passes six models across cheapness, quality, and risk families — notably FCF Yield, Magic Formula, Acquirer's Multiple, Piotroski F-Score (pass), Composite Value, and Earnings Quality — with high data quality (19/20 metrics) and a stable buy signal held for four weeks. Cash generation has remained robust: operating cash flow of $2.2 billion and free cash flow of $1.5 billion in FY2025 (Yahoo fallback; see Financial Review), supporting a ~9% FCF yield that the screen correctly identifies despite GAAP net income collapsing to $165 million.

Business quality remains defensible. Aptiv supplies 25 of the world's largest OEMs, holds leading positions in electrification, ADAS, and vehicle software (including Wind River), and is realigning into three focused segments ahead of the Versigent separation. The planned spin of Electrical Distribution Systems — expected to trade as VGNT on a 1-for-3 basis — could unlock a sum-of-the-parts discount and sharpen the remaining Aptiv portfolio toward higher-growth intelligent systems. Q1 2026 interim results (net sales +5.4% YoY to $5.1 billion; net income to Aptiv $189 million) suggest operational momentum is intact once one-offs are stripped out.

The screen's buy signal is therefore directionally supported: the market appears to price cyclical auto exposure and 2025 impairment noise, while cash economics and relative valuation metrics remain attractive. Conviction is tempered by spin complexity, customer concentration, and the gap between reported and normalised earnings.

Revenue grew modestly in FY2025 (+3.5% YoY per Yahoo), while operating income was broadly flat. Reported net income fell sharply due to non-recurring items (goodwill impairment, separation costs, absence of the prior-year Motional gain) rather than a collapse in operating performance. The FY2025 10-K body confirms ~$178 million of separation-related costs in SG&A and plans to complete the Versigent spin by 1 April 2026. Global vehicle production rose 4% in 2025 (1% on an Aptiv-weighted basis), per the filing narrative.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
**Spin-off execution:** The Versigent separation (distribution 1 April 2026; 1 VGNT share per 3 APTV shares) introduces transaction, stranded-cost, and capital-structure uncertainty. Aptiv incurred ~$178m of separation costs in FY2025 (10-K body) and expects further expenses. 8-K filings (5–6 March 2026) confirm the distribution timetable but note customary conditions.

**Goodwill impairment:** The $648m Q3 2025 charge signals management's reassessment of reporting-unit fair values — a material red flag for acquired-software/intangible-heavy segments. Goodwill and intangibles total ~$6.6 billion (Yahoo FY2025) against tangible book of ~$2.6 billion.

**Cyclical and customer concentration:** The 10-K risk factors emphasise automotive production cyclicality, tariff/trade policy uncertainty, and inventory build from customer production volatility. Top ten customers accounted for ~56% of FY2025 net sales; largest single customer ~10%. OEM contracts typically require 1–3% annual price reductions.

**China exposure:** ~29% of sales from Asia Pacific; filings warn of aggressive local competition, domestic OEM share gains, and GM restructuring in China (2025 10-K).

**EV demand moderation:** OEM delays to electrification investment strategies could slow high-voltage content growth — a key long-term growth pillar.

**Leverage and covenants:** Net debt ~$5.7 billion (Yahoo FY2025). Credit Agreement leverage covenant of 3.5x Consolidated EBITDA — manageable if EBITDA normalises, but vulnerable in a downturn combined with spin-related dis-synergies.

**Pension:** Non-current pension and post-retirement obligations ~$430m (Yahoo FY2025).

**Governance/management:** CFO transition to Varun Laroy (November 2024) and multiple segment leadership changes in 2024–2025 add execution oversight requirements during the spin.

No going-concern language was identified in available filing extracts.
