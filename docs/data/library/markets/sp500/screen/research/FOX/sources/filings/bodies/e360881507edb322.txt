Ltd. Clarkdon The Clarkdon property consists four contiguous Multicell Mining Claims comprising a total area of 18km 2 located 50km northwest of Ignace in Northwest Ontario. It is prospective for orogenic and banded iron formation (BIF) gold, with base-metal potential. Untested linear magnetic anomalies are located in the centre of the property, positioned in close proximity to known alteration mineralogy and structural features that host auriferous mineralisation elsewhere in the region 1 . In the south of the property, untested conductive horizons 2 inferred to represent BIFs, faulting, or sulphide-rich horizons 3 lie within the South Surgeon Fault Zone, a crustal scale fault structure which has recently been identified as a major fault system and potential conduit for gold-bearing fluids; with fault structure itself, and areas in close proximity having the capacity to host significant gold mineralisation 4 . Elsewhere on the property, historical mapping has identified calcium-rich alteration and sulphide occurrences associated with a poorly mapped fault structure 1 ; a similar structure located 7 km to the north has historically yielded 6.17 g/t Au and 1,000 ppm Co 6 . Additionally, a historical drill hole intercept recorded in the north of the property returned 29 ft at 0.43% Cu and 14 g/t Ag 7 . Garden Lake The Garden Lake property comprises three Multicell Mining Claims, of which two are contiguous, with an area totalling 13.84km 2 centred approximately 135 km north-west of Thunder Bay. The property is centred on 11 km² of largely untested greenstone within the Garden Lake Greenstone Belt, inferred to be a continuation of the prolific Beardmore-Geraldton Gold Belt 7,8 . The tenure hosts over 9.6 km of untested, mafic metavolcanic rocks, with inferred banded iron formations (BIFs) 9 . This geology represents a highly prospective target for gold mineralisation. Local prospectivity is highlighted by an OMI gold occurrence located within 200 metres of the property boundary 10 , featuring a vein system that graded 1.23 oz/t (42.17 g/t) Au and 0.93 oz/t (31.9 g/t) Au with visible gold. Limited historical exploration, including legacy drillin g11 , on this tenure has identified brecciation, silicification and sulphide-bearing horizons, which are typical targets for gold mineralisation 12 , with multiple inferred faults interpreted to exert structural control. Kearns The Kearns property comprises three Multicell Mining Claims, of which two are contiguous, with an area totalling 11.85km 2 . Prospective for orogenic gold mineralisation the property is located 2.5 km south--west of the Garden Lake property and also within the Garden Lake Greenstone Belt. The property overlies circa 10 km² of underexplored metavolcanic greenstone and features over 2 km of inferred banded iron formations 13 . The inferred location of the Banded iron formations is associated with albite, an alteration mineral commonly found within the alteration halos of orogenic gold deposits 14 . Regional mapping shows multiple tuff belts cross the property, these are often highly permeable and can focus hydrothermal fluids. Tuff units can also be associated with key alteration minerals, including epidote, albite, and carbonate, all of which are characteristic of greenstone-hosted orogenic gold systems 14 . Limited historical exploration has recorded albitisation and carbonation in the northern extent of the property, alongside epidote alteration in the south. Outside of Focus Xplore’s holdings, the remainder of the Garden Lake Greenstone belt has recently been consolidated by Kenorland Minerals Ltd (TSXV: KLD). Canada Bay The Canada Bay property comprises a single Licence, comprising 87 claims, covering a total area of approximately 21.75 km 2 within the prospective Humber Zone of northeast Newfoundland. A structurally controlled gold occurrence grading 0.6 g/t Au is located within a fault-controlled alteration zone in the cliffs of the northeast property area, associated with widespread alteration 15 . The east of the property is composed of the heavily faulted and sheared Maiden Point Formation 16 ; similar geology is targeted elsewhere in the Province for gold mineralisation. The west of the property is mapped as consisting of slates, phyllites, dolomites, and calcitic geology. The dolomite is mapped as heavily fractured and structurally competent, offering an untested trap environment for sediment-hosted micron gold (Carlin-type) mineralisation along a structurally complex corridor. O'Brien The O'Brien property comprises a single Licence, comprising 84 claims, covering a total area of 21.00 km 2 within the Exploits Subzone of central Newfoundland. It is positioned within 10 km of the Queensway gold deposit held by New Found Gold Corp (with an Indicated Mineral Resource of 1.39Moz Au). The western of the property targets internal, untested structures running parallel to the Mount Peyton Trend, a regional-scale system known to yield up to 166 g/t Au locally 17 . The east of the property targets orogenic gold mineralisation associated with second- and third-order fault splays branching from the crustal-scale Dog Bay Line. In this area of the property, Geophysical data reveals weak magnetic responses associated with major local faulting, potentially indicating demagnetisation linked to hydrothermal alteration, alongside a strong magnetic response that may relate to a gabbroic/dioritie intrusion, similar geology is targeted for gold along strike 18 and elsewhere in Newfoundland. Significant gold mineralisation is found in similar structural and geological settings 19 , including the Moosehead Deposit, 35km to the west, where intersections of 69.5 g/t Au over 4.85m and 40.89 g/t over 4.3 m have been reported 20 , as well as multiple deposits in Australia, including; King of the Hills 21 (Mineral Resource of 6.2 Moz Au 22 ) and Groundrush deposits 23 . Strategy Update The Acquisition and Fundraise, as defined in the announcement of 6 October 2026, is the culmination of the review commenced in April 2026 with the new Board was appointed. The primary aims of that review were (i) to identify the value in the existing properties, (ii) secure additional properties to boost the Group’s value in order to support a fundraise, and (iii) complete a fundraise of sufficient size to settle the Group’s creditors and provide sufficient working capital to fund both a work programme to build the value in the Group’s properties and meet the Company’s ongoing running costs. Identifying value in existing properties As previously announced, the Group has reduced its Canadian holdings to three lithium properties (Pearl, Iva and Biscuit Creek) and one rare earth elements property (Bay Road). Exploration work on these properties was largely completed in 2025. Securing additional properties The Acquisition brings five properties in the Gold Portfolio and has been instrumental in supporting the Fundraise. However, the Board recognise the high risk attached to early-stage mineral exploration projects with considerable time elapsing between initial exploration, to proving a resource and then eventually mining. Thus, the Board is looking to build a pipeline of accretive, early-stage North American gold acquisitions, also noting that the selective consolidation of adjacent ground and drill-ready projects, where available, would assist in building a district-scale gold position. AI modelling capability The Board believes that Artificial intelligence (AI) computer modelling can assist in the targeting of early-stage projects and plans to adapt the previous strategy by setting up a new subsidiary to develop AI modelling tools. The intention is that the new subsidiary will become self-funding and will not be a drain on the Group’s resources. The Board also sees the potential, in the future, to license the technology to third parties. The Group is in discussions with a potential management team to lead the project. In summary, the Board’s strategy is to: Acquire early-stage gold assets in Ontario and Newfoundland, repositioning the Company as a North American gold explorer. Continue to develop the existing lithium and critical-minerals projects, and pursue a sale, joint venture or other value-realising options as appropriate. Build an AI-driven geological analysis capability through a dedicated AI Special Purpose Vehicle to sharpen targeting and capital allocation. GLOSSARY Term Definition Albitisation The process whereby a rock is altered by the addition of the mineral albite (a sodium-rich feldspar), frequently associated with the fluid pathways of orogenic gold deposits. Au Gold Auriferous Containing or yielding gold. Banded Iron Formation (BIF) Distinctive sedimentary rock formations consisting of alternating layers of iron-rich minerals and silica. They are highly prospective and often host significant gold deposits. Base metals Common and relatively inexpensive industrial metals, such as copper (Cu) or zinc, as opposed to precious metals like gold (Au) or silver (Ag). Brecciation The process by which a solid rock is shattered into angular fragments and subsequently cemented back together, often creating highly permeable traps for mineralising fluids. Carbonation The widespread alteration of a rock by fluids rich in carbon dioxide, typically resulting in the formation of carbonate minerals. Carlin-type (Sediment-hosted micron gold) A type of disseminated gold deposit where the gold particles are microscopic (micron-scale) and hosted within sedimentary rocks, such as limestone or dolomite. Co Cobalt Conductive horizons Underground geological layers that conduct electricity better than surrounding rocks, often indicating the presence of metallic sulphides or water-bearing fault structures. Cu Copper Epidote alteration The chemical alteration of rocks by hydrothermal fluids that creates epidote (a calcium aluminium iron silicate mineral), commonly found in greenstone-hosted gold systems. Felsic intrusive rock Igneous rocks that are relatively rich in elements that form feldspar and quartz (silica-rich), which cooled beneath the Earth's surface. Greenfield Early-stage exploration on previously unexplored or unmined terrain, where mineralisation is theorised but not yet commercially proven. Greenstone / Greenstone Belt Ancient, metamorphosed volcanic and sedimentary rock sequences that are highly prospective for gold and base metal mineralisation. Hydrothermal fluids Hot, naturally occurring, water-rich fluids moving through the Earth's crust, responsible for dissolving, transporting, and depositing metals into concentrated veins. Mafic metavolcanic rocks Dark-coloured, magnesium- and iron-rich rocks that were originally formed by volcanic eruptions and later subjected to heat and pressure (metamorphism). Orogenic gold Gold deposits formed during periods of mountain building (orogeny). They are typically associated with deep crustal fault structures that allow gold-bearing fluids to rise to the surface. Oz Ounce Pegmatites Very coarse-grained igneous rocks that form during the late stages of magma crystallisation. They are often the primary host rocks for lithium and rare earth elements (REE). Silicification The introduction of, or replacement by, silica (quartz) in a rock. This is a very common alteration associated with gold mineralisation. Tuff belts Sequences of rock made of consolidated volcanic ash. They can be highly permeable, acting as pathways for mineralising fluids. VMS (Volcanogenic Massive Sulphide) A type of metal sulphide ore deposit, mainly containing copper, zinc, and lead, created by volcanic-associated hydrothermal events in submarine environments.
