The accompanying unaudited condensed consolidated financial statements have been prepared in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (“U.S. GAAP”) for interim financial information and the instructions to Form 10-Q and Article 10 of Regulation S-X. Substandard – Loans assigned a “substandard” grade are inadequately protected by the current sound worth and paying capacity of the obligor or of the collateral pledged, if any. Appropriate remedial plans must be implemented, and the credit continuously monitored. Doubtful – Loans assigned a “doubtful” grade have all the weaknesses inherent in one classified substandard with the added characteristic that the weaknesses make collection or liquidation in full, on the basis of currently existing facts, conditions, and values, highly questionable and improbable. An asset is to be graded “doubtful” when significant risk exposure is evident, but the actual amount of the loss is not immediately determinable pending some future event to be resolved within a relatively short time period. Fair value estimates are based on market information and assumptions regarding economic conditions, credit risk, and other relevant factors. Because active markets do not exist for certain financial instruments, these estimates involve significant judgment and uncertainty and may differ from amounts realized in an actual transaction. securities are measured at fair value on a recurring basis, while certain other assets, including loans held for investment, may be subject to nonrecurring fair value adjustments. The Company categorizes fair value measurements within the following hierarchy based on the observability of inputs used in determining fair value: Level 1 – Inputs to the valuation methodology are quoted prices (unadjusted) for identical assets or liabilities in active markets. Level 2 – Inputs to the valuation methodology include quoted prices for similar assets and liabilities in active markets, and inputs that are observable for the asset or liability, either directly or indirectly, for substantially the full term of the financial instrument. Level 3 – Inputs to the valuation methodology are unobservable and significant to the fair value measurement. The following methods and assumptions were used to estimate the fair value of each class of financial instruments for which it is practicable to estimate that value: Cash and Due from Banks and Interest Bearing Deposits – Cash and due from banks and deposits in banks are repriced on a basis; as such, the carrying value approximates fair value. Securities, – Securities are recorded at fair value on a recurring basis. Fair value measurement is based upon quoted prices, if available. If quoted prices are not available, fair values are measured using independent pricing models or other valuation techniques such as the present value of future cash flows, adjusted for the security’s credit rating, prepayment assumptions, and other factors such as the present value of future cash flows. Level 1 securities include those traded on an active exchange, such as the New York Stock Exchange, U.S. Treasury securities that are traded by dealers or brokers in active markets, and money market funds. Level 2 securities include securities issued by government sponsored entities, municipal bonds, and corporate debt securities. In certain cases where there is limited activity or less transparency around inputs to the valuation, securities are classified within Level 3 of the valuation hierarchy. As of September 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025, the Bank does not have any Level 3 securities. Loans – The fair value for loans held for investment ("LHFI") is estimated using an exit price methodology. An exit price methodology considers expected cash flows that take into account contractual loan terms, as applicable, prepayment expectations, probability of default, loss severity in the event of default, recovery lag and, in the case of variable rate loans, expectations for future interest rate movements. These cash flows are present valued at a risk adjusted discount rate, which considers the cost of funding, liquidity, servicing costs, and other factors. Because observable quoted prices seldom exist for similar assets carried in loans held for investment, Level 3 inputs are primarily used to determine fair value exit pricing. The fair value of loans is estimated based on discounted cash flows or underlying collateral values, where applicable. Deposits – The carrying amount of demand deposits and savings deposits approximates fair value due to those products having no stated maturity. The fair value of certificates of deposit is estimated based on discounted contractual cash flows using interest rates currently being offered for certificates of similar maturities and is classified as Level 2. Short term Borrowings – The carrying amount of variable rate other borrowings approximates fair value and is classified as Level 1. The Company's chief operating decision maker ("CODM") is the Chief Executive Officer. The CODM regularly reviews the financial performance of the Company's reportable segments and makes decisions regarding the allocation of resources based primarily on total revenue and net income. In assessing segment performance, the CODM considers net interest income, noninterest income, provision for credit losses, noninterest expense, and net income, as applicable. These financial measures are used to evaluate operating results, compare performance against expectations, establish business priorities, and determine the allocation of capital and other resources among the Company's reportable segments. Significant segment expenses disclosed in the accompanying segment financial information represent expense categories that are regularly provided to the CODM and included in the reported measures of segment profit or loss. Other segment items consist of expenses including amortization of intangible assets, regulatory fees, director fees, and other miscellaneous expenses. For each reportable segment, other segment items represent the difference between segment revenue, less significant segment expenses separately disclosed, and the reported segment profit or loss measure. The Company utilizes an allocation methodology to transfer certain funding costs and benefits between its reportable segments. Effective July 1, 2026, the Company revised its allocations to better align the recognition of revenues and expenses with the respective operational responsibilities and authority of Community Banking and BM Tech. The revised methodology reflects the integration of BM Tech into the Company's banking operations and the role of Community Banking management in overseeing activities that influence BM Tech's financial performance, including deposit account operations, transaction-related revenues, processing costs, and expenses. Interest expense increased by $ 0.1 million for the Community Banking segment and decreased by $ 0.1 million for the BM Tech segment. The revised methodology and allocations had no impact on interest income for either segment. Overall, for the three months ended September 30, 2026, income (loss) before taxes decreased by $ 2.7 million for the Community Banking segment and increased by $ 2.7 million for the BM Tech segment. Taxes decreased by $ 0.5 million for the Community Banking segment and increased by $ 0.5 million for the BM Tech segment. Net income (loss) decreased by $ 2.2 million for the Community Banking segment and increased by $ 2.2 million for the BM Tech segment for the three months ended September 30, 2026.
