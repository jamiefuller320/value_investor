DEFERRED INCOME TAXES – VALUATION ALLOWANCE We assess the realizability of deferred tax assets (“DTA”) each reporting period on a jurisdiction- basis. A valuation allowance is recorded when it is not that some or all DTAs will not be realized. In forming this conclusion, we weigh all available positive and negative evidence, placing significant weight on objectively verifiable evidence, including recent financial results. Cumulative losses in recent years constitute significant negative evidence that DTAs may not be realizable, while cumulative income provides objective positive evidence of our ability to generate taxable income. Consistent with GAAP, when there is a recent history of losses, greater weight is generally placed on objectively verifiable evidence than on subjective forecasts of future taxable income in assessing DTA realizability. When relevant, we use systematic and logical scheduling to estimate the timing of reversal of temporary differences (i.e., when deferred tax liabilities will generate taxable income and when DTAs will generate deductions). These assessments require assumptions and judgments and are inherently complex and subjective.
