Variable Interest Entities The Company decides at the inception of each arrangement whether an entity in which an investment is made or in which we have other variable interests is considered a VIE. Generally, an entity is a VIE if (1) the entity does not have sufficient equity at risk to finance its activities without additional subordinated financial support from other parties, (2) the entity’s investors lack any characteristics of a controlling financial interest or (3) the entity was established with voting rights. We consolidate VIEs when we are deemed to be the primary beneficiary. The primary beneficiary of a VIE is generally the party that both: (1) has the power to make decisions that most significantly affect the economic performance of the VIE and (2) has the obligation to absorb losses or the right to receive benefits that in either case could potentially be significant to the VIE. If the Company is not deemed to be the primary beneficiary of a VIE, we account for the investment or other variable interests in a VIE in accordance with other applicable GAAP. Interests When the Company consolidates an entity, 100% of the assets, liabilities, revenues and expenses of the subsidiary are included in the consolidated financial statements. For those consolidated entities in which our ownership is less than 100%, we record a interest as a component of equity on the Consolidated Balance Sheets, which represents the ownership in the net assets of the respective consolidated subsidiary. Additionally, the portion of the net income or loss attributable to the interest is reported as net income (loss) attributable to interests on the Consolidated Statements of Income. Changes in ownership interests in an entity that do not result in deconsolidation are generally recognized within equity.
