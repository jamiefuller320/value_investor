Revenue Recognition The Company utilizes the method of to recognize revenue on the majority of its performance obligations that are satisfied over time because it best depicts the transfer of control to the customer. Under the method of , the Company measures progress based on the ratio of costs incurred to date to total estimated costs for the performance obligation. The Company recognizes changes in estimated sales or costs and the resulting profit or loss on a cumulative basis. Contract adjustments represent the cumulative effect of the changes on prior periods. Contract Assets and Contract Liabilities The timing of billing does not always match the timing of revenue recognition . In instances where the Company recognizes revenue prior to billing, the Company records a contract asset (i.e., unbilled receivables). Unbilled receivables are classified as current assets and include amounts that may be billed and collected beyond one year due to the nature of many of the Company's contracts. The Company reduces contract assets when the Company has an unconditional right to payment. The Company establishes allowances for expected credit losses associated with contract assets. The Company did no t record any losses on unbilled receivables in the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 or 2025. The Company is generally entitled to bill its customers upon satisfaction of its performance obligations, except for its long‑term contracts in the Transport segment which typically allow for billing upon the acceptance of finished goods, payments received from customers in advance of performance, payments for rights to purchase future goods and extended warranties that are billed in advance of the warranty coverage period. Customer payment terms generally do not exceed one year. Fair Value Measurement FASB ASC Topic 820, Fair Value Measurements and Disclosures , defines fair value as the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability (i.e., exit price) in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date. FASB ASC Topic 820 requires disclosures that categorize assets and liabilities measured at fair value into one of three different levels depending on the assumptions (i.e., inputs) used in the valuation. Level 1 provides the most reliable measure of fair value, while Level 3 generally requires significant management judgment. The three levels are defined as follows: Level 1: Unadjusted quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities. Level 2: Observable inputs other than quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities, such as quoted prices for similar assets or liabilities in active markets or quoted prices for identical assets or liabilities in inactive markets. Overview Consolidated sales in the third quarter of 2026 increased $153 million, or 5.7%, compared to the third quarter of 2025. The increase was primarily the result of higher Access segment sales volume and improved pricing in all three segments, offset in part by lower sales volume in the Vocational segment due to production challenges associated with municipal fire apparatus. Municipal fire apparatus production was significantly below our expectations, reflecting challenges associated with initiatives to modernize manufacturing processes at Pierce. Consolidated operating income in the third quarter of 2026 was $197 million, or 6.9% of sales, compared to $260 million, or 9.7% of sales, in the third quarter of 2025. The decrease in consolidated operating income was primarily the result of unfavorable sales mix and higher selling, general and administrative expenses, offset in part by the impact of higher gross margin associated with higher sales volume and improved price/cost dynamics. The Company continued to repurchase shares of its Common Stock, repurchasing 556,138 shares during the third quarter of 2026 for $82 million, bringing share repurchases for the first nine months of 2026 to $221 million. Share repurchases during the previous twelve months benefited earnings per share during the third quarter of 2026 by $0.08 compared to the third quarter of 2025. Orders in the Access segment in the third quarter of 2026 of $2.0 billion were robust, resulting in a ratio of 1.5. Orders in the third quarter of 2026 included receipt of 2027 annual purchase orders from select national rental companies. Orders brought Access segment backlog to $2.7 billion at September 30, 2026. The Vocational segment continued initiatives to modernize municipal fire apparatus manufacturing processes and increase production capacity to better serve customer demand and address elevated backlog. However, these initiatives resulted in manufacturing challenges during the quarter and lower production and shipment volume. The Company is actively addressing these issues by improving material tracking for internally fabricated parts and " " delivery, stabilizing the processes that support its manufacturing improvements and adding experienced manufacturing leadership to support the transformation. Once fully implemented, the Company expects the production lines to support production volumes that are 25% to 30% higher than they were in 2025.
