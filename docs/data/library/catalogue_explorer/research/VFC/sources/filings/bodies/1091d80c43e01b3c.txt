Changes in Laws and Regulations VF recognizes the financial effects of changes in laws or regulations in the period in which the Company obtains a legal right to the related asset or incurs a legal obligation for the related liability. On February 20, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Power Act (“IEEPA”) were deemed invalid. Further, on March 4, 2026, the Court of International Trade ruled that U.S. Customs and Border Protection must refund IEEPA tariffs that were collected, with interest. Accordingly, as of March 2026, VF recorded a tariff refund receivable of $ 149.7 million related to tariffs paid under IEEPA from April 2025 until February 20, 2026, which was included in the accounts receivable, net line item in the Consolidated Balance Sheet at March 2026. In addition, VF recognized $ 93.8 million as a reduction to cost of goods sold in Fiscal 2026 related to inventory sold in Fiscal 2026 on which IEEPA tariffs were paid, such that the IEEPA tariffs had no net impact on gross margin in Fiscal 2026. Finally, VF recorded an inventory reserve of $ 55.9 million as of March 2026 to offset IEEPA tariffs reflected in inventory on hand at March 2026. During Fiscal 2027, VF released this reserve as a reduction to cost of goods sold as the related inventory was sold such that the IEEPA tariffs had no net impact on gross margin in Fiscal 2027. Accordingly, $ 37.3 million of the $ 55.9 million inventory reserve recorded at March 2026 was recognized as a reduction to cost of goods sold in the three months ended June 2026 and the remaining $ 18.6 million was recognized as a reduction to cost of goods sold in the three months ended September 2026. As of September 2026, VF has received substantially all of the IEEPA tariff receivable and the related interest income and has recognized all of the impacts. NOTE 16 — FAIR VALUE MEASUREMENTS Financial assets and financial liabilities measured and reported at fair value are classified in a hierarchy that prioritizes the inputs used in the valuation process. A financial instrument’s categorization within the valuation hierarchy is based on the lowest level of any input that is significant to the fair value measurement. The hierarchy is based on the observability and objectivity of the pricing inputs, as follows: • Level 1 — Quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities. • Level 2 — Significant directly observable data (other than Level 1 quoted prices) or significant indirectly observable data through corroboration with observable market data. Inputs would normally be (i) quoted prices in active markets for similar assets or liabilities, (ii) quoted prices in inactive markets for identical or similar assets or liabilities, or (iii) information derived from or corroborated by observable market data. • Level 3 — Prices or valuation techniques that require significant unobservable data inputs. These inputs would normally be VF’s own data and judgments about assumptions that market participants would use in pricing the asset or liability. 23 VF Corporation Q2 FY27 Form 10-Q Table of Contents Recurring Fair Value Measurements The following table summarizes financial assets and financial liabilities that are measured and recorded in the consolidated financial statements at fair value on a recurring basis: Total Fair Value Fair Value Measurement Using (a) (In thousands) Level 1 Level 2 Level 3 September 2026 Financial assets: Cash equivalents: Money market funds $ 190,958 $ 190,958 $ — $ — Time deposits 46,898 46,898 — — Derivative financial instruments 51,940 — 51,940 — Deferred compensation and other 74,830 74,827 3 — Financial liabilities: Derivative financial instruments 29,642 — 29,642 — Deferred compensation 71,845 — 71,845 — Contingent consulting fees 2,057 — — 2,057 Total Fair Value Fair Value Measurement Using (a) (In thousands) Level 1 Level 2 Level 3 March 2026 Financial assets: Cash equivalents: Money market funds $ 220,135 $ 220,135 $ — $ — Time deposits 74,648 74,648 — — Derivative financial instruments 28,914 — 28,914 — Deferred compensation and other 72,814 72,814 — — Financial liabilities: Derivative financial instruments 48,723 — 48,723 — Deferred compensation 69,043 — 69,043 — Contingent consulting fees 6,534 — — 6,534 (a) There were no transfers amon g the levels within the fair value hierarchy during the six months ended September 2026 or the year ended March 2026.
