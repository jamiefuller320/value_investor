The Project is located within the Boumadine Mining Licence ( ), which is held by Boumadine Global Mining SA (BGM). BGM is owned 85% by Aya Gold & Silver Inc. The Company also holds an additional 13 Mining Licences and 19 Exploration Permits within the surrounding region. 1.3 Project geology The Boumadine Project is hosted within Neoproterozoic volcanic and rocks of the Tiddiline Formation (TTF) and comprises a structurally controlled polymetallic hydrothermal system developed within a shallow volcanic environment. Mineralization is principally controlled by steeply dipping northwest- to fault corridors that are spatially associated with felsic subvolcanic intrusions and hydrothermal breccias. Hydrothermal alteration is extensive and comprises (phyllic) alteration within the felsic volcanic sequence and propylitic alteration within the underlying mafic volcanic rocks, characteristic of a hydrothermal system that evolved during repeated episodes of fault movement and magmatism. Mineralization comprises steeply dipping massive to sulphide veins, hydrothermal breccias, replacement sulphides and pyrite stockwork developed within broad alteration halos. The mineralization is typically associated with massive sulphide veins dominated by pyrite, sphalerite, galena, arsenopyrite and chalcopyrite, with surrounding zones of disseminated sulphides and veining. Recent drilling and geological studies have demonstrated that mineralization extends into both the felsic and mafic volcanic successions and has led to the refinement of the structural interpretation of the deposit, providing increased confidence in the geological model and continuity of the principal mineralized corridors. Although the precise genetic classification of the deposit remains under investigation, the MRE is based on the observed geological, structural and mineralization characteristics and does not depend on any specific genetic model. AYA GOLD & SILVER INC. , BOUMADINE POLYMETALLIC PROJECT 1 1.4 Exploration Drilling and Sampling A total of 940 drillholes, comprising 356,323.1 m of drilling, are located within the Boumadine Mining Licence. Of these, 855 drillholes, totalling 319,464.8 m, were completed to support infill of key mineralized areas and extensions to the known mineralization, while the remaining 85 drillholes relate to prospecting within the Mining Licence and did not specifically target the known Boumadine mineralization. Drillhole spacing is variable across the Project. However, the majority of the modern drilling used in the 2026 MRE was initially completed on nominal 100-200 m-spaced inclined drill fences, with subsequent infill drilling undertaken on approximately 50 m spacing. Lead, zinc and pyrite concentrates will be recovered and sold to market. OMC used its testwork database to infer missing data and to recommend further testwork for subsequent studies. While both BWi values from testwork fell outside the database range for other similar resources in the area, the average BWi fell at the 81 st percentile of the range, aligning well with OMC’s typical strategy of designing to the 85 th percentile hardness. As such, the average of both test results was used in the comminution circuit design (13.1 kWh/t). The remainder of the comminution circuit design criteria were estimated from OMC’s database. The conditions tested during the 2022 and 2024 SGS locked cycle testing campaigns were used as the basis for the concentrate recovery circuit design, since the testing incorporated recycles at steady state and produced concentrates of acceptable grade and recovery. Using locked cycle data provides a higher design certainty since it more closely represents plant operation by the incorporation of recycles and since it validates the process conditions at steady state. The design criteria derived from the 2022 and 2024 locked cycle testing includes: • General circuit design (i.e. orientation, number of stages, implementation of regrinding). • Reagent addition. • Concentrate mass pulls, grades and recoveries. • Residence times. The mine plan combines open pit (OP) and underground (UG) mining. OP mining starts in 2029, and UG development starts two years later, in 2031, with first UG mineralized material in 2032. This phased approach generates early cash flow from the OP mine while deferring the UG development. Over the life of mine (LOM), 41.2 Mt of mineralized material will be mined at an average NSR value of US$193/t, of which OP mining contributes 22.4 Mt (54.2%) and UG mining 18.9 Mt (45.8%). The LOM production plan extends over fifteen years, including , with OP mining from 2029 to 2039 and UG mining through 2043. Production during the final four years comes exclusively from the UG mine. Mineralized material is classified into three stockpiles (Low Grade, LG Pyrite and HG Pyrite) based on NSR values, and the stockpiles are used to regulate the tonnage and grade of plant feed at the nominal throughput of 8,000 t/d. 1.7.1 OP Mining activities, including and the construction of roads, pads and waste rock storage areas, will start in 2029 during the project construction phase. The South Pit will be mined in two phases to supplement Central Pit production, and the North Pit, made up of three , will be mined from 2034 to 2036.
