Production Blanket produced 17,030 oz in Q3 2026 and 49,158 oz in the Nine Months. Production in the Quarter was adversely affected by a shortage of compressed air in certain , mining areas and by a temporary increase in gold inventory in the metallurgical plant. Mining activity during September was increasingly focused on 30 and 34 Levels, approximately 990 metres and 1,110 metres below surface, respectively. Four additional compressors had been procured to support activity at these deeper levels and the increased development undertaken following the appointment of contractors earlier in the year. Delays in the delivery and deployment of two of the compressors due to port congestion in Mozambique constrained mining activity in this important mining area. Following the delivery of the two remaining compressors, in October, the available compressed air was directed towards the areas so that planned production from these areas could be maintained. The two delayed compressors were commissioned on October 31. In September, new equipment was introduced to improve efficiency, recovery and security in the gravity gold circuit. Commissioning difficulties resulted in approximately 1,100 oz of free gold being retained in the metallurgical plant due to inefficiency and limited capacity of the elution system. The gravity circuit has been remodelled to avoid further of trapped gold. On October 16, a new 3-tonne elution vessel was commissioned. In the first instance, the increased elution capacity is being used to release the 1,100 oz of gold – as at November 6, over half of the gold at September 30 had been recovered. Once the gold has been released, the increased elution capacity will be used to process a stockpile of 61 tonnes of activated carbon which contains approximately 1,300 oz of recoverable gold. Management revised Blanket’s 2026 production guidance to 69,000-72,500 oz. Looking ahead to Q4 2026 and into 2027, management remains focused on improving production consistency across Blanket. 5 Following the increase in compressor capacity at the 30 and 34 Levels and the commissioning of the new elution vessel at the metallurgical plant, the Group will focus on the priorities below for the remainder of Q4 2026. · Recover the remaining gold trapped in the metallurgical plant and release gold from the stockpile of activated carbon. · Increase ore throughput at the Lima metallurgical plant by processing a portion of the additional ore produced under the shift system introduced in June 2026. Further upgrades are planned to the main metallurgical plant at Blanket in 2027 to increase throughput from the current level of 2,500 tpd to 2,750 tpd. Together, these initiatives are expected to support improved production performance in Q4 2026 and establish a stronger platform for more consistent production in 2027. Financial Statement Reporting & Analysis Fair value movement on derivative instruments The Group experienced contrasting fair value movements across its derivative instruments during the period as outlined below. Derivative Assets The Group’s derivative assets comprise capped call options and gold put options. The capped call options were acquired in connection with the issuance of the Convertible Senior Notes to mitigate the potential economic dilution arising from future conversions of the Notes. The capped call options are and have a cap price of approximately US$56.72 per share, representing a premium of approximately 75% to the last reported sale price of the Company’s shares on the NYSE American prior to the issue of the Notes on January 14, 2026. The Company also holds gold put options which were entered into to mitigate its exposure to adverse movements in the gold price over the periods hedged. The put options provide the Group with protection against gold prices falling below the relevant strike prices in respect of the specified number of oz subject to the hedging arrangements. The hedging instruments cover only a portion of the Group’s anticipated gold production and therefore provide partial protection against downside gold price risk, while retaining exposure to movements in the gold price on unhedged production. The arrangements do not give rise to additional funding requirements such as margin calls. During Q3 2026, the fair value of the capped call options increased by US$1.7 million, resulting in a fair value gain recognised in profit or loss. Over the same period, the fair value of the gold put options decreased by US$4.6 million, resulting in a corresponding fair value loss recognised in profit or loss. Together with the US$10.0 million fair value loss arising from the remeasurement of the embedded derivative liability associated with the conversion feature of the Convertible Senior Notes, as explained below, the Group recognised a net fair value loss of US$12.9 million on derivative financial instruments for Q3 2026. This amount is presented within “Fair value (loss)/gain on derivative financial instruments” in the consolidated statement of profit or loss. Whilst a net fair value loss of US$12.9 million was recognised in Q3 2026, the Group reported a net fair value gain of US$2.6 million on its derivative assets and derivative liability for the Nine Months. 7 Derivative liabilities The Group’s derivative liability primarily relates to the embedded derivative associated with the conversion feature of the Convertible Senior Notes. At September 30, 2026, the fair value of the embedded derivative liability was US$37.4 million, compared with US$27.4 million at June 30, 2026. The resulting US$10.0 million increase in the fair value of the liability was recognised as a fair value loss in profit or loss for Q3 2026. The embedded derivative is measured using a Monte Carlo simulation model incorporating the contractual terms of the Notes and relevant assumptions. The increase in fair value was principally attributable to the increase in the Company’s share price from US$19.10 at June 30, 2026 to US$23.54 at September 30, 2026, together with a reduction in the forecast dividend yield from 2.81% to 1.91% and changes in the USD curve. Expected volatility remained broadly unchanged at 57.32% compared with 57.38% at June 30, 2026, while the reduction in the remaining term of the conversion feature partially offset the increase. Cash and cash equivalents Net cash generated from operating activities was US$13.6 million for the Quarter, broadly consistent with US$13.7 million in the comparative quarter. Higher cash generated from operations of US$31.0 million (Q3 2025: US$26.0 million), together with increased interest receipts, was largely offset by higher tax payments of US$13.7 million (Q3 2025: US$11.7 million) and US$4.3 million of finance costs paid on the Convertible Senior Notes. Net cash used in investing activities increased to US$13.6 million (Q3 2025: US$8.3 million), principally due to higher expenditure on property, plant and equipment of US$12.2 million (Q3 2025: US$6.3 million), together with US$1.5 million invested in exploration and evaluation assets (Q3 2025: US$1.5 million). Net cash used in financing activities increased to US$9.7 million (Q3 2025: US$6.3 million), primarily reflecting higher dividend payments of US$9.2 million (Q3 2025: US$5.7 million). As a result, net cash and cash equivalents decreased by US$9.7 million during the Quarter (Q3 2025: US$0.9 million decrease). After foreign exchange movements of US$0.2 million, net cash and cash equivalents, including bank overdrafts, was US$157.9 million at Quarter end, compared with US$167.8 million at the beginning of the Quarter. The decrease was largely driven by the timing of gold sales around the Quarter end, including the related receivable outstanding at September 30, 2026 and bullion held on hand at Quarter end, which was subsequently sold in early October 2026. Effective tax rate The Group’s effective tax rate for the Quarter was elevated primarily as a result of significant unrealised fair value losses on derivative financial instruments recognised in the Company. No tax benefit was recognised in respect of these losses, irrespective of the Company’s 0% tax rate, as the losses do not give rise to a tax benefit that is available for utilisation against taxable profits generated in the Company or elsewhere in the Group. Accordingly, the fair value losses reduced the Group’s consolidated profit before tax without a corresponding reduction in the income tax expense, resulting in an increase in the Group’s effective tax rate for the Quarter.
