Reverse Stock Split On June 30, 2026, following approval by the Company’s stockholders at the 2026 Annual Meeting of Stockholders on June 10, 2026, the Company effected a reverse stock split of its issued and outstanding shares of Class A common stock and Class B common stock, together with a proportional reduction in the number of authorized shares of Class A, Class B, and preferred stock (the “Reverse Stock Split”). The Reverse Stock Split was effected by filing a Certificate of Amendment to the Company’s Amended and Restated Certificate of Incorporation. As a result of the Reverse Stock Split, every four shares of the Company’s Class A common stock or Class B common stock were automatically combined and converted into one share of the respective class of stock. The Reverse Stock Split did not change the par value per share of the Company’s common or preferred stock, nor did it alter the relative rights, preferences, or privileges of the holders of such stock. As a result, the aggregate par value of outstanding common stock was reduced proportionately, with a corresponding increase to additional capital. Total stockholders’ equity remained unchanged. No fractional shares were issued in connection with the Reverse Stock Split, and stockholders who otherwise would have been entitled to a fractional share received cash in lieu thereof. All and per share information presented in the accompanying condensed consolidated financial statements and notes thereto has been retroactively adjusted to reflect the Reverse Stock Split for all prior periods presented, as if the split had occurred at the beginning of the earliest period presented. 11 Use of Estimates The preparation of the financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make various estimates and assumptions relating to reported amounts of assets and liabilities, disclosure of contingent liabilities, and reported amounts of revenue and expenses. Recent Accounting Pronouncements In May 2025, the FASB issued Accounting Standards Update No. 2025-04, “Compensation — Stock Compensation (Topic 718) and Revenue from Contracts with Customers (Topic 606): Clarifications to Consideration Payable to a Customer” (“ASU 2025-04”). ASU 2025-04 clarifies that consideration granted to a customer is measured and classified in accordance with ASC Topic 718, and that the variable consideration constraint of ASC Topic 606 does not apply to such awards. The update also explicitly incorporates conditions based on customer purchases or contract execution into the definition of "performance conditions," requiring the Company to estimate vesting probability upfront under ASC Topic 718. Finally, the standard eliminates the accounting policy election to record forfeitures as they occur for customer awards, requiring that forfeitures be estimated. The Company early adopted ASU 2025-04 on a modified retrospective basis as of January 1, 2026. As of the adoption date, all previously issued customer common stock warrants had fully vested and none remained outstanding. Remaining capitalized warrant assets from previous vestings continue to be amortized as a reduction of net revenue over the remaining contract term. Because the previously issued warrants had already fully vested, the adoption of this standard did not have any impact on the Company’s condensed consolidated financial statements as of the adoption date. Warrants issued during the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 were assessed and accounted for in accordance with the guidance in ASU 2025-04. In November 2024, the FASB issued Accounting Standards Update No. 2024-03, “Income Statement—Reporting Comprehensive Income—Expense Disaggregation Disclosures” (“ASU 2024-03”). ASU 2024-03 is intended to improve disclosures about a public business entity's expenses and provide more detailed information to investors about the types of expenses in commonly presented expense captions. The amendments in this ASU are effective for fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2026, and interim periods within fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2027. The Company plans to adopt this pronouncement in line with these effective dates. The provision for income taxes recorded in the three and nine months ended September 30, 2025 consists of state and foreign income taxes in foreign jurisdictions in which the Company conducts business. The Company regularly assesses the need for a valuation allowance on its deferred tax assets. In performing that assessment, the Company considers both positive and negative evidence related to the likelihood of realization of the deferred tax assets to determine, based on the weight of all available evidence, whether it is that some or all of the deferred tax assets will be realized. The determination of the realizability of deferred tax assets involves significant judgment and relies on management's estimates of future taxable income. The Company had cumulative U.S. book income, adjusted for permanent differences, over the period ended September 30, 2026 and expects to continue to sustain this profitability position for the foreseeable future. This cumulative profitability represents objective and verifiable positive evidence that carries significant weight. After evaluating all available positive and negative evidence, including this cumulative profitability and anticipated future earnings, the Company concluded that it is that the U.S. federal and certain states’ deferred tax assets are realizable. As a result of this change in judgment, the Company released a significant portion of the valuation allowance previously recorded against its U.S. deferred tax assets during the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026. The majority of the released valuation allowance related to U.S. federal and state net operating losses, federal tax credit carryforwards and capitalized research and development (“R&D”) expenditures. Because the change in estimate occurred during an interim period, the portion of the valuation allowance release attributable to current year income was included in the estimated annual effective tax rate, while the release supported by projections of future taxable income is recorded as a discrete benefit. The Company released $ 106.6 million of valuation allowance as a discrete tax benefit during the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026. The Company’s judgment regarding the need for a valuation allowance may reasonably change in future reporting periods due to many factors, including changes in expectations of future profitability in the U.S. and changes in tax laws or regulations. Overview Marqeta’s mission is modernizing financial services by making the entire payment experience native and delightful. Marqeta’s modern platform empowers our customers to create customized and innovative payment card programs with configurability and flexibility. Marqeta’s open APIs provide instant access to highly scalable, payment infrastructure that enables customers to embed the payments experience into apps or websites for a personalized user experience. Customers can launch and manage their own card programs, issue cards, and authorize and settle payment transactions quickly using our platform. We also deliver robust bank, network, and card program management and value added services, allowing our customers to embed Marqeta in their offering without having to build certain complex compliance elements or customer support services. Marqeta’s innovative products are developed with deep domain expertise and a mindset to launch, scale, and manage card programs. Marqeta provides the following offerings based on a customer’s desired level of control and responsibility: • Processing: Marqeta provides all of its customers with issuer processor services as our core offering. Payment processing provides customers with access to the Marqeta dashboard via our APIs and webhooks, our JIT Funding feature, and assists with certain configuration elements that enable customers to use the platform independently. • Bank and Network Management: Marqeta provides a service option to connect customers to an Issuing Bank partner to act as the BIN sponsor for the customer’s card program, define and manage a number of the primary tasks related to launching a card program, and can provide a full range of services including configuring many of the critical resources required by a customer’s production environment and managing the applicable regulations and the Issuing Bank. In addition, Marqeta provides another service offering to manage compliance with applicable Card Network rules. • Program Management: Marqeta provides additional program management services that are required as part of a card program, including chargebacks and dispute resolution, reconciliation, and card fulfillment. • Value Added Services: Marqeta provides value added services that offer a more seamless experience for our customers, which include tokenization, decisioning and fraud management, digital banking, and other customer experience services.
