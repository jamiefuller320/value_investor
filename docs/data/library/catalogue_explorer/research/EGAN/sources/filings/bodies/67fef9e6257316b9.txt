SUMMARY OF BUSINESS AND SIGNIFICANT ACCOUNTING POLICIES ​ Organization and Nature of Business ​ eGain Corporation provides AI knowledge solutions designed to help enterprises deploy artificial intelligence with greater accuracy, reliability, and governance. Our flagship (SaaS) offering, the eGain AI Knowledge Hub, unifies content across disparate enterprise systems and enables organizations to transform that content into governed, continuously maintained knowledge that can serve as the instruction layer for AI agents, as well as a source of reliable and consistent information for customers and employees. In October 2026, we introduced AI KnowledgeOps, a set of capabilities designed to help organizations maintain the accuracy, consistency, and reliability of enterprise knowledge across its lifecycle, from identifying knowledge gaps to authoring, verifying and delivering answers. The platform supports digital , contact center operations, employee support, and other workflows. It integrates with major customer relationship management and contact center platforms, as well as AI models, enabling customers to automate workflows and deploy solutions across their operations. We are headquartered in Sunnyvale, California, USA. We also operate in the United Kingdom and India. ​ Fiscal Year ​ The Company’s fiscal year ends on June 30. SaaS revenue includes cloud delivery arrangements, term licenses, embedded original equipment manufacturer (OEM) royalties, and associated support. Professional services include consulting, implementation, training, and managed services. ​ 8 Table of Contents Significant Judgment Applied in the Determination of Revenue Recognition ​ We enter into contractual arrangements with customers that may include promises to transfer multiple services, such as subscription, support, and professional services. With respect to our business, a performance obligation is a promise to transfer a service to a customer that is distinct. Significant judgment is required to determine whether services are distinct performance obligations that should be accounted for separately or combined as one unit of accounting. Additionally, significant judgment is required to determine the timing of revenue recognition. ​ We allocate the transaction price to each performance obligation based on relative SSP. The SSP is the price at which we would sell a promised service separately to one of our customers. Judgment is required to determine the SSP for each distinct performance obligation. ​ We determine the SSP by considering our pricing objectives in relation to market demand. Consideration is placed based on our history of discounting prices, size and volume of transactions involved, customer demographics and geographic locations, price lists, contract prices and our market strategy. ​ Determination of Revenue Recognition ​ Under Topic 606, we recognize revenue upon the transfer of control of promised services to our customers in the amount that is commensurate with the consideration that we expect to receive in exchange for those services. If consideration includes a variable amount in the arrangement, such as service level credits or contingent fees, then we include an estimate of the amount that we expect to receive for the total transaction price. ​ The amount of revenue that we recognize is based on (i) identifying the contract with a customer; (ii) identifying the performance obligations in the contract; (iii) determining the transaction price; (iv) allocating the transaction price to the performance obligations in the contract on a relative SSP basis; and (v) recognizing revenue when, or as, we satisfy each performance obligation in the contract typically through delivery or when control is transferred to the customer. ​ SaaS Revenue ​ The following customer arrangements are recognized ratably over the contract term as the performance obligations are delivered: ● Cloud delivery arrangements; ● Maintenance and support arrangements; and ● Term licenses which incorporate software licenses and a subscription to substantial cloud functionalities. ​ For contracts involving distinct software licenses, the license performance obligation is satisfied at a point in time when control is transferred to the customer. We typically invoice our customers in advance upon execution of the contract or subsequent renewals with payment terms generally between 30 and 45 days . Invoiced amounts are recorded in accounts receivable, deferred revenue or revenue, depending if control transferred to our customers based on each arrangement. ​ We have a royalty agreement with a customer related to our embedded intellectual property, under which the customer pays a fee for software and support for each license sold containing our embedded software. These embedded OEM royalties are included in SaaS revenue. As the customer reports its license sales to us a quarter in arrears, we estimate royalties earned in each quarter and adjust for any differences between the estimate and the reported amount in the following quarter, when the customer’s royalty report is received. Upon a review of product development activity, we determined that the embedded software is being maintained but not further developed. Furthermore, the embedded license has limited standalone value and is interdependent with the ongoing support. Beginning in fiscal year 2027, the embedded 9 Table of Contents license and support are accounted for as a single performance obligation recognized ratably over the royalty payment term and we no longer recognize a portion of these royalties at a point in time. The impact of this change was not material. ​ Professional Services Revenue ​ Professional services revenue includes system implementation, consulting, training, and managed services. The transaction price is allocated to various performance obligations based on their SSP. Revenue allocated to each performance obligation is recognized at the earlier of satisfaction of discrete performance obligations, or as work is performed on a time and material basis. Managed services include a comprehensive set of processes and activities that range from implementation to monitoring the evolution and support of eGain solutions in a company. Our consulting and implementation service contracts are bid either on a basis or on a basis. Managed services contracts are bid on a basis. Fixed fees are generally paid upon milestone billing or customer acceptance at points in the contract. Amounts that have been invoiced are recorded in accounts receivable and in deferred revenue or revenue, depending on whether transfer of control to customers has occurred. Training revenue that meets the criteria to be accounted for separately is recognized when training is provided. Contracts with Multiple Performance Obligations ​ The Company enters into contracts that can include various combinations of subscriptions, professional services and maintenance and support, which are generally distinct and accounted for as separate performance obligations. For contracts with multiple performance obligations, the Company allocates the transaction price of the contract to each performance obligation on a relative basis using the respective SSP for each performance obligation. Costs Capitalized to Obtain Revenue Contracts, Net ​ Under Topic 606, we capitalize incremental costs of obtaining subscription and support revenue contracts. The capitalized amounts consist primarily of sales commissions paid to our direct sales force. Capitalized amounts also include (i) amounts paid to employees other than the direct sales force who earn incentive payouts under annual compensation plans that are tied to the value of contracts acquired and (ii) the associated payroll taxes and fringe benefit costs associated with the payments to our employees, including compensation. ​ Costs capitalized related to new revenue contracts are generally deferred and amortized on a basis over a period of benefit that we estimate to be five years . We determine the period of benefit by taking into consideration the historical and expected durations of our customer contracts, the expected useful lives of our technologies, and other factors. Commissions for renewal contracts relating to our arrangements are expensed when incurred, as we do not consider renewal contracts to be commensurate with initial customer contracts. Historically, any commission associated with renewals have been immaterial. Amortization of costs to obtain revenue contracts is included as a component of sales and marketing expenses in our condensed consolidated statements of operations. ​ During the three months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, we capitalized $ 41,000 and $ 97,000 of costs to obtain revenue contracts, respectively, and amortized $ 320,000 and $ 320,000 to sales and marketing expense, respectively. ​ Capitalized costs to obtain revenue contracts, net were $ 1.7 million and $ 2.0 million as of September 30, 2026 and June 30, 2026, respectively, on our condensed consolidated balance sheets. ​ Deferred Revenue Deferred revenue primarily consists of payments received in advance of revenue recognition from cloud, term and ratable licenses, and maintenance and support services and is recognized as the revenue recognition criteria are met. We generally invoice customers in annual or quarterly installments. The deferred revenue balance does not represent the total contract value of annual or , cloud or maintenance and support agreements. FAIR VALUE MEASUREMENT ​ ASC 820, Fair Value Measurement (ASC 820), defines fair value, establishes a framework for measuring fair value of assets and liabilities, and expands disclosures about fair value measurements. Fair value is defined as the exchange price that would be received for an asset or paid to transfer a liability in the principal or most advantageous market for the assets or liabilities in an orderly transaction between market participants on the measurement date. Subsequent changes in fair value of these financial assets and liabilities are recognized in earnings or other comprehensive income when they occur. ASC 820 applies whenever other statements require or permit assets or liabilities to be measured at fair value. ​ ASC 820 includes a fair value hierarchy, of which the first two are considered observable and the last unobservable, that is intended to increase the consistency and comparability in fair value measurements and related disclosures. Valuation techniques used to measure fair value must maximize the use of observable inputs and minimize the use of unobservable inputs. Observable inputs reflect assumptions market participants would use in pricing an asset or liability based on market data obtained from independent sources while unobservable inputs reflect a reporting entity’s pricing based upon their own market assumptions. ​ The fair value hierarchy consists of the following three levels: ​ Level 1 – instrument valuations are obtained from quotes for transactions in active exchange markets involving identical assets. ​ Level 2 – instrument valuations are obtained from pricing sources for comparable instruments. ​ Level 3 – instrument valuations are obtained without observable market value and require a high level of judgment to determine the fair value. ​ Our money market funds are measured at fair value on a recurring basis based on quoted market prices in active markets and are classified as level 1 within the fair value hierarchy. As of September 30, 2026 and June 30, 2026, cash equivalents classified as level 1 instruments, including money market account investments, were measured at $ 49.3 million and $ 45.2 million, respectively. 19 Table of Contents 8. For the three months ended September 30, 2026, revenue from AI customers increased 7% and represented 66% of total revenue, compared to 57% in the same period in fiscal year 2026, while revenue from legacy customers decreased 25%. ​ We expect revenue from legacy customers to continue to decline, including as a result of the planned of our legacy messaging business beginning in the second quarter of fiscal year 2027, and we expect total revenue to decline in fiscal year 2027 compared to fiscal year 2026, as the anticipated decline in legacy customer revenue is expected to exceed the growth in AI customer revenue. Over time, we expect AI customer revenue to represent an increasing share of total revenue. At the same time, we are increasing our investment in sales and marketing to convert increased market awareness into pipeline, new AI customers and expansion within our installed base, which we expect to adversely affect our operating margins in the near term. There can be no assurance that growth in AI customer revenue will offset the decline in legacy revenue within the timeframe we expect, or at all.
