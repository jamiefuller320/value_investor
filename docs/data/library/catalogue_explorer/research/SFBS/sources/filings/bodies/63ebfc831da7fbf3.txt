QUANTITATIVE AND QUALITATIVE DISCLOSURES ABOUT MARKET RISK Like all financial institutions, we are subject to market risk from changes in interest rates. Interest rate risk is inherent in the balance sheet due to the mismatch between the maturities of assets and liabilities. If rates are rising, and the level of liabilities exceeds the level of assets, the net interest margin will be negatively impacted. Conversely, if rates are falling, and the level of liabilities is greater than the level of assets, the impact on the net interest margin will be favorable. Managing interest rate risk is further complicated by the fact that all rates do not change at the same pace; in other words, rates may be rising while rates remain stable. In addition, different types of assets and liabilities react differently to changes in rates. 42 To manage interest rate risk, we must take a position on the expected future trend of interest rates. Rates may rise, fall or remain the same. Our committee develops its view of future rate trends and strives to manage rate risk within a targeted range by monitoring economic indicators, examining the views of economists and other experts, and understanding the current status of our balance sheet. Our annual budget reflects the anticipated rate environment for the next 12 months. The committee conducts a quarterly analysis of the rate sensitivity position and reports its results to our board of directors. The committee thoroughly analyzes the maturities of assets and liabilities. This analysis measures the “gap,” which is defined as the difference between the dollar amount of assets repricing during a period and the volume of liabilities repricing during the same period. The gap is also expressed as the ratio of assets divided by liabilities. If the ratio is greater than one, the dollar value of assets exceeds the dollar value of liabilities; the balance sheet is “ .” Conversely, if the value of liabilities exceeds the value of assets, the ratio is less than one and the balance sheet is “ .” Our internal policy requires management to maintain the gap such that net interest margins will not change more than 10% if interest rates change 100 basis points or more than 15% if interest rates change 200 basis points. There have been no changes to our policies or procedures for analyzing our interest rate risk since December 31, 2023, and there have been no material changes to our sensitivity to changes in interest rates since December 31, 2023, as disclosed in our Annual Report on Form 10-K.
