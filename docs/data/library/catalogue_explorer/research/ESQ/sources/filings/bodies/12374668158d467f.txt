Business Combinations Policy The Company accounts for business combinations under the acquisition method of accounting. Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed are recognized at their fair values, except for items subject to specific recognition or measurement exceptions under U.S. GAAP, including allowances for credit losses, income taxes and payment awards. Goodwill is the excess of consideration transferred over the recognized amounts of identifiable net assets acquired. For items whose initial accounting is incomplete, provisional amounts may be adjusted during the measurement period as information about facts and circumstances existing at the becomes available. The measurement period ends when the necessary information is obtained, or when the Company determines that additional information is not obtainable, and may not exceed one year from the . costs are expensed as incurred. Goodwill Policy Goodwill is not amortized but is evaluated for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that it is more likely than not that the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying amount. The Company has selected October 31 as its annual goodwill impairment testing date. The Company has determined that it has one reporting unit for purposes of evaluating goodwill for impairment. The Company may first perform a qualitative assessment to determine whether a quantitative impairment test is necessary. Factors considered include economic, industry and market conditions, overall financial performance, changes in expected operating results, and sustained declines in the Company’s stock price. If the qualitative assessment indicates that it is more likely than not that the reporting unit’s fair value is less than its carrying amount, or if the Company elects to bypass the qualitative impairment test, a quantitative test is performed. Under the quantitative impairment test, the reporting unit’s fair value is compared with its carrying amount, including goodwill. An impairment loss is recognized for the amount by which the carrying amount exceeds fair value, limited to the carrying amount of goodwill allocated to the reporting unit. 8 Table of Contents Loss Contingencies Loss contingencies, including claims and legal actions arising in the ordinary course of business, are recorded as liabilities when the likelihood of loss is probable and an amount or range of loss can be reasonably estimated. Change in Allowance Methodology During the quarter ended September 30, 2026, the Company changed the methodology used to estimate the allowance for credit losses on its legacy Esquire loan portfolio from a static pool Weighted Average Remaining Maturity (“WARM”) methodology to a Discounted Cash Flow (“DCF”) methodology, effective August 1, 2026. The change was made in conjunction with the Company's merger with Signature and management's reassessment of the estimation methodology appropriate for the combined company on a prospective basis. The DCF methodology replaces the historical loss rates used in the WARM methodology with loss history derived from FFIEC Call Report data for banks with comparable asset size and loan portfolio characteristics. Probabilities of default are developed from regression models that relate peer default rates to macroeconomic variables, principally the unemployment rate and gross domestic product, using Federal Open Market Committee forecasts. Loss given default is based on peer loss experience. The model projects contractual cash flows adjusted for prepayment and curtailment assumptions derived from industry benchmark data, probabilities of default, loss given default, and recovery timing of 24 months from (six months for consumer loans), and discounts expected cash flows at each loan's effective interest rate. Management determined that DCF better reflects the contractual characteristics of the legacy Esquire portfolio and future new loans that will be originated under the combined company. The change does not indicate that the former methodology was inappropriate when previously applied. Purchased seasoned loan pools from the Company's merger with Signature are closed pools. Loans originated after August 1, 2026 are evaluated in the DCF pools. The acquired pools are evaluated using a expected cash flow methodology that uses the same assumptions as the DCF methodology but does not discount expected cash flows. Acquired loans were recorded at fair value, which includes a purchase discount. A discounted methodology would require an effective interest rate that equates each loan's expected cash flows to its purchase price. Measuring on the unpaid principal balance is consistent with how the allowance was determined under the approach. It also keeps the remaining noncredit discount, which is recognized in interest income, separate from the allowance. Management concluded that the change reflects new information and changing risk characteristics of the loan portfolio resulting from the Company’s merger with Signature rather than a correction of an error or a change in accounting principle, which was accounted for as a change in accounting estimate, with prospective application beginning August 1, 2026. amounts were not restated or presented on a pro forma basis for this change in accounting estimate. The methodology change resulted in no material impact to the allowance for credit losses and the related provision for credit losses in the current period. Allowance for Credit Losses and Loans Held for Investment The Company's loan portfolio consists of originated loans and loans acquired in connection with the Signature Merger completed on August 1, 2026. Acquired loans include purchased seasoned loans ("PSL") and purchased credit deteriorated loans ("PCD"). For originated loans evaluated collectively, the Company estimates expected credit losses using a discounted cash flow methodology that compares amortized cost with the present value of expected cash flows. For acquired PSL loans evaluated collectively, the Company uses a expected cash flow methodology that measures expected losses without discounting. Both methodologies incorporate repayment projections, probabilities of default, assumptions, prepayments or curtailments, and reasonable and supportable economic forecasts. Qualitative adjustments address risks not sufficiently reflected in the quantitative estimates. Standards Adopted in 2026 In November 2025, the Financial Accounting Standards Board (“FASB”) issued ASU 2025-08, “Financial Instruments - Credit Losses (Topic 326): Purchased Loans”. The ASU expands the use of the approach to include purchased seasoned loans, defined as loans (excluding credit cards) acquired without significant credit deterioration and deemed to be seasoned. Acquired loans obtained through a business combination qualify as purchased seasoned loans, except for excluded asset types. For qualifying loans obtained outside a business combination or initially recognized through consolidation of a variable interest entity, the loan must be acquired more than 90 days after origination and the acquirer must not have been involved in its origination. The change is intended to reduce complexity and subjectivity in loan purchase transactions, and to reduce the risk of double counting expected credit losses that are already reflected in fair value determinations made at the time of the Merger. ASU 2025-08 is effective for annual reporting periods beginning after December 15, 2026, including interim periods within those annual periods; early adoption is permitted. The Company adopted ASU 2025-08 effective January 1, 2026 and applied it prospectively to loans acquired on or after that date. 1,758,245 ​ 19 Table of Contents Credit Quality Indicators The Company categorizes loans into risk categories based on relevant information about the ability of borrowers to service their debt such as: current financial information, historical payment experience, credit documentation, public information, and current economic trends, among other factors. The Company analyzes loans individually by classifying the loans as to credit risk. This analysis is performed whenever a credit is extended, renewed or modified, or when an observable event occurs indicating a potential decline in credit quality, and no less than annually for large balance loans. The Company uses the following definitions for risk ratings: Special Mention - Loans classified as special mention have a potential weakness that deserves management’s close attention. If left uncorrected, these potential weaknesses may result in deterioration of the repayment prospects for the loan or of the institution’s credit position at some future date. Substandard - Loans classified as substandard are inadequately protected by the current net worth and paying capacity of the obligor or of the collateral pledged, if any. Loans so classified have a weakness or weaknesses that jeopardize the liquidation of the debt. They are characterized by the distinct possibility that the institution will sustain some loss if the deficiencies are not corrected. Doubtful - Loans classified as doubtful have all the weaknesses inherent in those classified as substandard, with the added characteristic that the weaknesses make collection or liquidation in full, on the basis of currently existing facts, conditions, and values, highly questionable and improbable. Loans not meeting the criteria above that are analyzed individually as part of the above described process are considered to be pass rated loans. ​ 20 Table of Contents The following is a summary of the credit risk profile of loans, measured at amortized cost, by internally assigned grade as of the periods indicated. Under the plans, options are granted with an exercise price equal to the fair value of the Company’s stock at the date of the grant. Options granted vest over three to five years and have contractual terms. All options provide for accelerated vesting upon a change in control (as defined in the plans). The fair value of each option award is estimated on the date of grant using a closed form option valuation ( ) model that uses assumptions for expected stock price volatility, expected terms of options granted, and interest rate. Expected volatilities are based on peer volatility. The Company uses peer data to estimate option exercise and termination behavior. The expected term of options granted is based on peer data and represents the period of time that options granted are expected to be outstanding, which takes into account that the options are not transferable. The interest rate for the expected term of the option is based on the U.S. Treasury yield curve in effect at the time of the grant. Restricted shares are granted at the fair value on the date of grant and vest over three to six years with a third vesting at each of the last three anniversary dates. Restricted shares have the same voting rights as common stock and nonvested restricted shareholders do not have rights to the accrued dividends until vested. Share payouts of PSUs will be earned based on the Company’s performance with respect to two equally weighted metrics, return on average assets and diluted EPS growth. Performance is measured against financial targets over a performance period and will cliff vest on the anniversary from the date of grant. Compensation expense on PSUs is based upon the fair value of the shares on the date of the grant for the expected aggregate share payout.
