ITEM 3 GENERAL DEVELOPMENT OF THE BUSINESS 3.1 Profile We use technology and networks to simplify complex business processes. We are primarily focused on logistics and supply chain management business processes. Our solutions are focused on improving the productivity, security, and sustainability of businesses. Customers use our modular, technology solutions to route, track and help improve the safety, performance and compliance of delivery resources; plan, allocate and execute shipments; rate, audit and pay transportation invoices; access and analyze global trade data; research and perform trade tariff and duty calculations; file customs and security documents for imports and exports; and complete numerous other logistics processes by participating in a large, collaborative logistics community. Our pricing model provides our customers with flexibility in purchasing our solutions either on a subscription, transactional, or perpetual license basis. Our primary focus is on serving transportation providers (air, ocean and truck modes), logistics service providers (including logistics providers, freight forwarders and customs brokers) and distribution-intensive 5 Table of Contents ​ companies (including retailers, manufacturers, distributors, and mobile business service providers) for which logistics is either a key or a defining part of their own product or service offering, or for which our solutions can provide an opportunity to reduce costs, improve service levels, or support growth by optimizing the use of assets and information The Market Logistics is the management of the flow of resources between a point of origin and a point of destination. Supply chain management is broader than logistics and includes the sourcing, inventory management, procurement, conversion and storage of resources for consumption by an enterprise. Logistics and supply chain management have been evolving as companies are increasingly seeking automation and control of their supply chain activities. Unlike other business challenges, managing supply chain and logistics operations relies on the secure exchange and management of information with parties outside of your party’s enterprise – such as suppliers, customers, partners, governments, and logistics providers. We believe companies are looking for integrated solutions for managing inventory in transit, conveyance units, people, data, and business documents. We believe organizations are seeking to reduce operating costs, differentiate themselves, improve margins, and better serve customers. Global trade and transportation processes are often manual and complex to manage. This is a consequence of the growing number of business partners participating in companies’ global supply chains and a lack of standardized business processes. Additionally, global sourcing, logistics outsourcing, imposition of additional customs and regulatory requirements and changes in business requirements are adding to the overall complexities that companies face in planning and executing in their supply chains. Whether a shipment is delayed at the border, a customer changes an order, sanctions or tariff changes impact sourcing and selling, or a breakdown occurs on the road or shipping lanes are unusually congested, there are increasingly more issues that can significantly impact the execution of fulfillment schedules and associated costs. The rise of ecommerce has heightened these challenges for many suppliers with increasingly demanding narrower periods, lower prices and greater flexibility in scheduling and rescheduling deliveries. also want updates on delivery status, adding considerable burden to supply chain management as process efficiency is balanced with affordable service. In this market, the secure movement and sharing of data between parties involved in the logistics process is equally important to the physical movement of goods. Manual, fragmented, and distributed logistics solutions are often proving inadequate to address the needs of operators. Connecting manufacturers and suppliers to logistics providers and carriers on an individual, basis is too costly, complex and risky for organizations dealing with many trading partners. Further, many of these solutions do not provide the flexibility required to efficiently accommodate varied processes for organizations to remain competitive. We believe this presents an opportunity for logistics technology providers to unite this highly fragmented community and help customers improve their operations. As the market continues to change, we’ve been evolving to meet our customers’ needs. While the rate of adoption of newer logistics and supply chain management technologies is increasing, a large number of organizations still have manual business processes and older business systems that need integration and extension for supply chain and logistics functions. We’ve been educating our prospects and customers on the value of connecting to trading partners through our Global Logistics Network (“GLN”) and automating, as well as standardizing, business processes. TRENDS IN GLOBAL TRADE The impact of the Iran Conflict, the Russia‑Ukraine conflict, broader geopolitical and trade tensions, tariff measures between major economies, and regional conflicts disrupting key ocean trade routes continues to create uncertainty in global trade. According to UNCTAD, global economic and trade growth is expected to remain subdued through 2025 and 2026, with global GDP projected to grow at only 2.6%, reflecting a challenging external environment for both developed and developing economies. While demand for consumer goods has supported elevated trade and shipping volumes in recent years, it remains unclear whether continuing geopolitical pressures, transportation capacity constraints, inflationary pressures, port congestion, resource shortages, and reshoring/nearshoring initiatives will dampen future growth. A continuing global trend is the expanding counter‑terrorism and cargo security role of customs authorities, driven by increased automation and the implementation of advanced fiscal compliance controls. The World Customs Organization’s SAFE Framework continues to guide customs modernization initiatives worldwide. Jurisdictions including the United States, Canada, the European Union (the “EU”), the UK, Japan, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, and Mexico have advanced multi‑year programs requiring electronic submission and management of customs declarations, security filings, and related trade and transport data. These evolving mandates increase compliance duties for importers, exporters, and their logistics partners. Descartes’ customs and regulatory compliance solutions connect importers, exporters, logistics providers, and government agencies to support efficient data transmission, cargo security screening, and customs declaration processing across these increasingly complex regulatory regimes. Our business may be affected as domestic and international trade regulations are introduced, modified, or repealed. Following the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine, numerous countries—including the EU, Canada, the United States, the UK, Japan, Australia, Switzerland, and New Zealand—imposed substantial sanctions on Russia and affiliated entities. These measures expand earlier sanctions dating back to 2014 and include new additions to watchlists, enhanced export controls targeting high‑tech goods, and further restrictions on the Russian financial system. Additional primary or secondary sanctions remain possible if the conflict persists. With global sanctions policies evolving rapidly and receiving heightened public and regulatory scrutiny, businesses increasingly require effective screening tools to comply with denied‑ and restricted‑party regulations. Failure to comply may result in severe penalties, loss of trade privileges, reputational harm, and public scrutiny.
