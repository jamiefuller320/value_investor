("Company" or "Cardinal Group") is a Delaware corporation formed on June 12, 2025, for the purpose of facilitating an initial public offering (the "IPO") and related organizational transactions in order to acquire an investment in Cardinal Civil Contracting Holdings LLC ("Cardinal"). Cardinal Group is a holding company with no direct operations whose principal asset is its equity interest in Cardinal, acquired in connection with the IPO. Segment Information The Company’s chief operating decision maker ("CODM") is its Chief Executive Officer . The CODM manages the business activities on a consolidated basis and as such the Company determined it is a single reportable segment. The Company derives revenue in the United States of America from construction projects based in the Southeast. The CODM primarily assesses performance for the Company and decides how to allocate resources based on net income. Net income is reported on the condensed consolidated statements of operations. Performance is continuously monitored at the consolidated level and as necessary at the project contract level to timely identify deviations from the expected results. Resource allocation is based on the CODM and Company management's estimates of growth to expand backlog and to increase production capacity to ensure timely execution of committed sales contracts. The significant expenses reviewed by the CODM, which are used to assess performance of the Company, are not disaggregated at a level lower than the captions disclosed within the condensed consolidated statements of operations. The CODM also uses net income and related profit margins in competitive analysis by benchmarking to the Company’s competitors. The competitive analysis along with the monitoring of budgeted versus actual results are used in assessing performance of the Company and in establishing management’s compensation. The measure of segment assets is reported to the CODM on the balance sheet as total consolidated assets, with the same captions as the condensed consolidated balance sheets. All of the Company's assets are based in the United States of America. 8 Derivatives The Company does not use derivative financial instruments for trading or speculative purposes. The Company may enter into interest rate derivatives to manage exposure to interest rate risks. The Company recognizes derivative financial instruments at fair value and presents them within other assets and liabilities in the condensed consolidated balance sheets. Gains and losses from derivatives that are neither designated nor qualify as hedging instruments are recognized in earnings within other expense, net in the condensed consolidated statements of operations. For derivatives that qualify as cash flow hedges, the gain or loss is reported as a component of other comprehensive income (loss) and reclassified into earnings in the periods during which the hedged forecasted transaction affects earnings. Cash flows for derivative financial instruments are classified as cash flows from operating activities within the condensed consolidated statements of cash flows, unless there is an financing element present at inception of the derivative financial instrument. Changes in Accounting Estimates Property and Equipment Depreciation Method Effective as of January 1, 2026, the Company changed its depreciation method for property and equipment from the accelerated method of depreciation to the method of depreciation. The Company believes the change from the accelerated method to the method of depreciation is preferable under U.S. GAAP as it will result in an estimate of depreciation expense which more accurately reflects the pattern of usage and the expected benefits of such assets. Additionally, the change to the method of depreciation is consistent with the depreciation method applied by other companies within the Company's industry and improves the comparability of the Company's results to its competitors. The Company's change in the method of depreciation is considered a change in accounting estimate effected by a change in accounting principle and has been applied prospectively. The effect of the change on the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 was a decrease in depreciation expense of approximately $ 3.2 million and $ 6.2 million , respectively, and a corresponding increase in income before income taxes compared to what would have been reported under the accelerated method. The effect on net income and earnings per diluted share was approximately $ 2.9 million and $ 0.14 , respectively, for the three months ended September 30, 2026 and $ 5.6 million and $ 0.33 , respectively, for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 . The fixed assets will be depreciated over their estimated remaining useful lives as follows: Asset Group Useful Lives Machinery and equipment 3 to 20 years Vehicles and trailers 5 years Office equipment and computers 3 to 5 years Leasehold improvements 15 years Customer Contracts Accounting for customer construction contracts requires the use of estimates and assumptions to determine total contract revenue, contract costs, and the extent of progress toward completion. The Company generally recognizes revenue over time using a measure of progress, under which revenue is recognized based on costs incurred relative to estimated total costs at completion. Estimates of total contract revenue and costs incorporate assumptions about future events, including labor productivity and availability, material and subcontractor costs and availability, project duration, site conditions, scope modifications, and the nature and complexity of the work to be performed. The Company updates its contract estimates as conditions evolve and additional information becomes available. Changes in job performance, site conditions, labor productivity, subcontractor and supplier performance, project schedules, and contract scope may result in revisions to estimated contract revenue, costs, and profitability. The timing and amount of revenue and profit recognized in any period therefore depend, in part, on the reliability of these estimates. Because the Company manages a portfolio of contracts of varying size, duration, and complexity, favorable and unfavorable changes in individual contract estimates may offset one another. However, significant changes in estimates for one 9 or more contracts have had, and could in the future, have a material effect on the Company’s revenue, cost of revenues, gross profit, contract assets, and/or contract liabilities in a reporting period. Factors that may contribute to changes in contract estimates include: • The completeness and accuracy of original bids and cost estimates; • Changes in project scope and the timing and recognition of related costs and revenue; • Changes in expected labor productivity, labor availability, and wage rates; • Extended project overhead resulting from customer, permitting, weather, or other delays; • Subcontractor and supplier performance; • Changes in subcontractor, equipment, fuel, and material costs; • Site conditions that differ from original bid assumptions, including circumstances in which contractual remedies may not be available; • Material and equipment availability and proximity to project sites; • Changes in project schedules and estimated completion dates; and • The identification of additional costs necessary to complete contract obligations. These factors, together with the stage of completion and mix of contracts in progress, may cause fluctuations in gross profit between reporting periods, which may be significant. Changes in estimates of contract revenue, contract costs, or the extent of progress toward completion are recognized in the period in which the revisions are determined. When a change in an estimate affects the measure of progress for a performance obligation satisfied over time, the resulting cumulative effect on revenue and gross profit is recognized in the period of the change. Such changes occur in the ordinary course of business and are inherent in the Company’s use of estimates in accounting for construction contracts. During the three months ended September 30, 2026, as additional project information became available, the Company revised its estimate of costs to complete on certain projects, which resulted in a cumulative adjustment that reduced gross profit by $ 13.2 million . The revisions represented changes in estimates based on information available during the period in the ordinary course of business, and no material amounts were identified that should have been recognized in a prior period. 4.
