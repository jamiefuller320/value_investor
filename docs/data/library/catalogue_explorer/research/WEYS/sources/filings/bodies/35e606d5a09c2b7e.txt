Inventory is purchased from overseas manufacturers. Almost all of these purchases are denominated in U.S. dollars. We have two reportable segments, North American wholesale operations (“Wholesale”) and North American retail operations (“Retail”). In the Wholesale segment, our products are sold to leading footwear, department, and specialty stores, as well as e-commerce retailers, primarily in the United States and Canada. We also have licensing agreements with third parties who sell our branded apparel, accessories, and specialty footwear in the United States, as well as our footwear in Mexico and certain markets overseas. Licensing revenues are included in our Wholesale segment. As of September 30, 2026, our Retail segment consisted of e-commerce businesses and two retail stores in the United States. Retail sales are made directly to consumers on our websites, or by our employees in our stores. As previously announced, we made the strategic decision to close our U.S. stores at the end of their lease terms. Two stores closed in Summer 2026, and the remaining two are planned to close at the end of December 2026 and January 2027. Our “other” operations include our retail and wholesale businesses in Australia and South Africa (collectively, “Florsheim Australia”). The majority of our operations are in the United States, and our results are primarily affected by the economic conditions and the retail environment in the United States. Incremental Tariff Status In early 2025, the U.S. imposed tariffs on certain imported goods under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (“IEEPA”). During 2025 and the first quarter of 2026, we paid approximately $19.8 million in IEEPA tariffs. In February 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs, and in April 2026, U.S. Customs and Border Protection (“CBP”) commenced a phased process for accepting refund claims. In April, we submitted refund claims for our Phase 1 entries totaling $18.6 million, substantially all of which were approved during the second quarter. Accordingly, during the second quarter, we recognized: $15.3 million in tariff refunds as a reduction to cost of sales, $3.3 million as a reduction of inventory, and $0.7 million of interest income. During the third quarter, the $3.3 million previously recorded to inventory was recognized as a reduction to cost of sales ($3.2 million in the Wholesale segment and $0.1 million in the Retail segment) upon sale of the related inventory. Our Phase 3 entries, totaling $1.2 million were submitted to CBP in early October, of which $0.4 million have been approved for liquidation, while $0.8 million remain under review. The timing and amount of these recoveries remain uncertain and subject to execution by CBP. Accordingly, no refunds related to Phase 3 entries were recognized in the third quarter. Following the U.S. Supreme Court's ruling in February, the Administration imposed a 10% incremental tariff under a separate statutory authority. On July 24, 2026, the Administration increased the incremental tariff on imports from China, Dominican Republic, and Vietnam to 12.5%, which remained in effect for the duration of the third quarter. U.S. trade policies continue to evolve and remain unpredictable, creating gross margin uncertainty. We have mitigation strategies in place and will continue to adjust, as appropriate, in response to future policy developments. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW We are pleased with our overall performance in the third quarter, with net sales increasing 5% and wholesale net sales increasing 6%. In a challenging retail environment, we believe we are continuing to gain market share in the categories where we compete. While conditions at retail remain uncertain, our portfolio of brands is , and we remain confident about the future. Sales of our combined legacy business, comprised of the Florsheim, Stacy Adams, and Nunn Bush brands, increased 6% in the third quarter. Florsheim’s sales increased 9%, delivering another quarter at Wholesale, driven by higher sales in the dress shoe category. Florsheim has also been able to expand its presence in casual boots, sneakers, and hybrids. While these latter categories still represent a relatively small portion of our business, we are seeing encouraging results. Stacy Adams sales increased 9% compared to last year’s third quarter, driven by higher sales in the dress shoe category. We remain focused on developing our hybrid and offerings to capitalize on the shift toward more relaxed, wardrobes. Nunn Bush sales declined 1% for the quarter. While sales were slightly below last year's levels, we believe Nunn Bush continues to compete effectively and that our investment in meaningful product differentiation is the right strategy. BOGS sales increased 6% for the quarter. We are gaining traction with our focus on innovation, particularly our seamless construction technology, which produces footwear that is lighter and twice as durable as traditional vulcanized construction. We also continue to make progress in diversifying the product line to establish BOGS as a relevant brand 365 days a year. 16 ​ Sales in our Retail segment increased 3% for the quarter, driven by strong Florsheim e-commerce sales. We continue to invest in tools and technology for our online platforms to more effectively target customers, improve conversion, and increase the productivity of our websites. Florsheim Australia's net sales declined 3% for the quarter, or 10% in local currency. The Australian retail market has been under pressure for some time, and conditions have been further exacerbated by higher fuel costs. Our team in Australia remains focused on navigating these headwinds, improving conversion rates in an environment of reduced retail traffic, and carefully managing expenses. We expect market conditions to remain challenging in the near to medium term and will continue to align our cost structure with the business environment.
