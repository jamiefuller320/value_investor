EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW We empower businesses to build stronger customer relationships through a broad range of trusted, solutions designed to facilitate payments, drive growth, and improve operational efficiency. Our comprehensive portfolio includes merchant services solutions, marketing and data analytics, treasury management solutions, and promotional products, as well as customized checks and business forms tailored to our clients' needs. We serve a diverse customer base, including small and businesses, financial institutions, and some of the world’s leading consumer brands. In addition, we offer checks and related accessories directly to individual consumers. Our extensive reach, scale, and distribution network enable us to deliver innovative solutions and reliable support, positioning us well as a valued partner to our customers. Our Strategy A comprehensive discussion of our strategy is provided in Part I, Item 1 of the 2025 Form 10-K. During the first nine months of 2026, we continued to execute on our strategic priorities of accelerating profitable growth, enhancing operational efficiency, and disciplined capital allocation. Accelerating profitable growth – We continued to execute on our strategy of growing our payments and data businesses while optimizing our overall portfolio, delivering stronger revenue mix and profitability. In March 2026, we completed the divestiture of the Safeguard small business distributor channel within our Print segment, a strategic action enabling greater focus on our growth businesses and ongoing portfolio optimization objectives. The divestiture reduced Print segment revenue by approximately $79.3 million and Print adjusted EBITDA by approximately $7.8 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared to the first nine months of 2025. On the growth side of our portfolio, our payments and data businesses demonstrated strong momentum, collectively delivering 14.6% revenue growth and an 18.0% increase in adjusted EBITDA in the first nine months of 2026. Approximately $33.5 million of the revenue increase was driven by the acquisition of Celero Commerce ("Celero") on July 31, 2026. Celero is a financial technology company that provides payment solutions to small and businesses through a diversified distribution network. Cash consideration paid for the acquisition was $625.0 million plus certain seller transaction expenses and other adjustments. Further information regarding the acquisition can be found under the caption "Note 6: Acquisitions and Divestitures" in the Condensed Notes to Unaudited Consolidated Financial Statements located in Part I, Item 1 of this report. The transaction was financed through our amended credit agreement, via a combination of additional term loan financing and revolving credit facility borrowings. Further information regarding the amended credit agreement can be found in the Capital Resources section. Celero's platform complements our existing payments offerings and is expected to accelerate the modernization of our payments technology, expand our distribution capabilities, and strengthen our competitive position through increased scale and broader customer reach. Enhancing operational efficiency – During the first nine months of 2026, we continued to focus on cost management and operational efficiency through workforce adjustments across multiple functions and optimization of our marketing strategies. We also continued to realize the benefits of initiatives, including operational efficiencies within our lockbox processing operations. On a consolidated basis, net income decreased $2.2 million in the first nine months of 2026, primarily reflecting costs associated with the Celero acquisition and continued secular declines in our Print segment. Recent Market Conditions We continually monitor macroeconomic conditions and other external factors that may affect our business, including interest rates, inflation, small business sentiment, consumer spending trends, and global economic conditions. As of September 30, 2026, 75% of our debt had a fixed interest rate of 7.3%, which provides partial insulation from changes in market interest rates. However, future changes in interest rates could continue to affect our borrowing costs on the portion of our debt. Macroeconomic conditions, including inflation, energy price volatility, and fluctuations in logistics and certain raw material costs, continue to influence our cost structure and customer demand. In response, we implemented targeted price adjustments, particularly within our Merchant Services and Print segments, to help offset increased costs while remaining mindful of customer 29 price sensitivity. We continue to monitor the potential for further cost volatility resulting from changes in energy markets, supply chain conditions, and raw material pricing. Global economic conditions remain uncertain, reflecting ongoing geopolitical unrest and evolving trade policies, treaties, and tariffs. These developments may disrupt supply chains, increase operating costs, and affect the availability, timing, and pricing of certain goods and services. We also closely monitor trends in small business sentiment and consumer discretionary spending, as these factors influence demand across our portfolio. Our analysis incorporates data from credit card networks, the Federal Reserve, leading economic forecasters, and our proprietary analytics. Recent economic indicators suggest that while consumer sentiment remains weak, consumer spending has remained relatively resilient, although consumers continue to demonstrate price sensitivity. Small business optimism has softened modestly, with recent indicators reflecting elevated uncertainty and more cautious expansion plans. Negative shifts in consumer and business spending patterns could influence transaction volumes in our Merchant Services segment and demand for certain discretionary products within our Print segment. A sustained period of economic uncertainty or a broader slowdown in economic activity could adversely affect our financial position, results of operations, and future growth prospects. Business combinations – We account for business combinations using the acquisition method of accounting, which requires us to estimate the fair values of assets acquired and liabilities assumed as of the acquisition date. These estimates involve significant judgment, particularly in valuing acquired intangible assets. The valuation of intangible assets generally involves assumptions regarding forecasted revenue, customer attrition, EBITDA margins, discount rates, and other factors. These assumptions are based on management's expectations of future operating performance and market conditions and are inherently uncertain. Changes in key assumptions could materially affect the estimated fair values of acquired intangible assets and the amount of goodwill recognized. The fair values assigned to acquired intangible assets, along with their estimated useful lives and amortization methods, determine the amount and timing of future amortization expense. In addition, if actual operating results fall below expectations, the resulting decline in the estimated fair value of the business could increase the risk of future goodwill impairment charges.
