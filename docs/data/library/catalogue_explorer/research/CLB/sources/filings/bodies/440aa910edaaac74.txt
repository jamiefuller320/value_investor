Outlook Recent geopolitical developments, including the armed conflict in the Middle East (“Middle East Conflict”) have dramatically shifted the crude oil balance. On February 28, 2026, the hostilities commenced and since then, Iran and other military groups, including Houthi militants in Yemen, have retaliated against United States and Israeli interests, as well as Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates and other countries in the region, including merchant vessels in the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea, and certain energy infrastructure. Approximately 20% of global crude oil production passed through the Strait of Hormuz prior to the Middle East Conflict. These disruptions have resulted in a significant reduction in the supply of crude oil and derived products. Although cargo traffic of crude oil and derived products through the Strait of Hormuz has gradually improved toward the end of the third quarter of 2026, crude oil supply continues to be significantly disrupted. Additionally, the drawdown of inventories through a coordinated global release of oil from strategic petroleum reserves could also create additional volatility in future prices. This has dramatically shifted the balance of crude oil and exacerbated maritime trade flows of crude oil and derived products. While the Company believes the fundamentals for services remain stable, there is volatility in commodity prices, the movement patterns of crude oil, and client activity levels. The ongoing geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East and between Russia and Ukraine, along with associated and expanded United States, European Union, and United Kingdom sanctions on Iran and Russia, as well as secondary sanctions on buyers of Iranian and Russian crude and derived products continue to cause disruptions to traditional maritime supply chains and the trading of crude oil and derived products, such as fuels. The decline in trading and maritime transport of crude oil has directly impacted, and is expected to continue to impact, demand for the Company’s associated laboratory assay services. The Company is monitoring developments with respect to these conflicts, including the impact on global commodity prices and potential shipping and logistics disruptions, which could affect our customers and their activity levels in and outside the region. As of the date of the filing of this Quarterly Report, military activity and hostilities continue in the Middle East, and the situation throughout the region remains volatile, with the potential for continued escalation into a broader and more sustained conflict. Iran and its military proxies continue to target critical infrastructure in the region, including refining and pipeline facilities, maritime ports and civilian airports. We have no way to predict the progress or outcome of these events, and any resulting government responses are fluid and beyond our control. The Company believes that activity levels associated with , crude oil development projects will be more sensitive to a decrease and/or continued volatility of prices. As such, we expect changes in crude oil prices will marginally improve drilling and completion activity levels in the U.S. onshore market which could directly affect demand for our well completion services and products. Outside the U.S., international oil and gas projects are expected to be more resilient to the volatility of prices, and the Company anticipates client projects will continue to be executed as planned, unless directly impacted by the conflicts mentioned above.
