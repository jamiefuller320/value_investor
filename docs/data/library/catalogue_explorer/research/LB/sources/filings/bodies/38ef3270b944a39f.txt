Reverse 1031 exchange In September 2026, the Company acquired properties through a reverse exchange under Section 1031 of the Internal Revenue Code (a “reverse 1031 exchange”), in which legal title to the acquired properties is held by an Exchange Accommodation Titleholder (“EAT”) until the exchange is completed. The EAT is a VIE as it lacks sufficient equity at risk to finance its activities because we funded the acquisition cost through secured, promissory notes. As manager of the EAT, we direct its most significant activities and have the obligation to absorb losses or the right to receive benefits that could be potentially significant to us. The Company has determined it is the primary beneficiary and consolidates the EAT within its unaudited condensed consolidated financial statements. The EAT’s assets consist primarily of the acquired property, which the Company operates under a property management agreement with the titleholder entities. Because that arrangement is a management agreement rather than a lease, it is not accounted for under Accounting Standards Codification (“ASC”) Topic 842. The agreement terminates on the earliest of (i) transfer of the properties to the Company or an affiliate, (ii) transfer of the EAT ownership interests to the Company or an affiliate, or (iii) 180 days after legal title transfers to the EAT. The acquired properties are recorded at cost, and no gain or loss was recognized on consolidation. The assets of the consolidated titleholder entities may be used only to settle those entities’ obligations, and their creditors have no recourse to the general credit of the Company. The Company’s maximum exposure to loss is limited to the carrying amount of the acquired properties. As of September 30, 2026, the consolidated EAT held assets of approximately $ 243.5 million . Overview Land is a fundamental requirement for the development and production of energy and the construction and operation of critical infrastructure. As of September 30, 2026, we owned or managed more than 350,000 surface acres in the Delaware Basin and adjacent Central Basin Platform in the prolific Permian Basin, which is the most active area for oil and gas exploration and development in the United States. Access to expansive surface acreage is necessary for oil and natural gas development, solar power generation, power storage, digital infrastructure and oilfield reclamation and solid waste facilities. Further, the significant industrial economy that exists to service and support energy and infrastructure development requires access to surface acreage to support those activities. Our strategy is to actively manage our land and resources to support and encourage energy and infrastructure development and other land uses that will generate revenue and Free Cash Flow for us and returns to our shareholders. We share a legacy financial sponsor, Five Point, and our management team with WaterBridge. WaterBridge is one of the largest water midstream companies in the United States and operates a network of pipelines and other infrastructure in the Delaware Basin. These relationships provide our shared management team visibility into key areas of oil and natural gas production and trends, which we leverage to encourage and support the development of critical infrastructure on our land and generate additional revenue for us. We receive royalties for each barrel of produced water that WaterBridge handles on our land as well as surface use payments for infrastructure constructed on our land.
