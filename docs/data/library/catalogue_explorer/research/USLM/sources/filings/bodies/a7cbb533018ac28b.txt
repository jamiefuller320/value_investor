We are headquartered in Dallas, Texas and operate lime and limestone plants and distribution facilities in Arkansas, Colorado, Louisiana, Missouri, Oklahoma, and Texas through our wholly owned subsidiaries, Arkansas Lime Company, ART Quarry TRS LLC (DBA Carthage Crushed Limestone), Colorado Lime Company, Mill Creek Dolomite, LLC, Texas Lime Company, U.S. Lime Company, U.S. Lime , U.S. Lime . Clair, and U.S. Lime . In addition, through our wholly owned subsidiary, U.S. Lime & G, LLC, we have royalty and working interests in natural gas wells located in Johnson County, Texas, in the Barnett Shale Formation. Our revenues increased 4.1% and 2.9% in the third quarter and first nine months 2026, respectively, compared to the third quarter and first nine months 2025. Revenues increased in the third quarter 2026, compared to the third quarter 2025, primarily due to a 2.5% increase in the average selling prices for our lime and limestone products and a 1.6% increase in sales volumes of our lime and limestone products. Increased sales volumes in the third quarter 2026 were principally due to increased demand from our steel customers, partially offset by decreased demand from our construction customers. Revenues increased in the first nine months 2026, compared to the first nine months 2025, primarily due to a 1.7% increase in sales volumes of our lime and limestone products and a 1.3% increase in the average selling prices for our lime and limestone products. Increased sales volumes in the first nine months 2026 were principally due to increased demand from our steel customers, partially offset by decreased demand from our roof shingle customers. Although we remain optimistic regarding overall construction demand in the future, certain large construction projects have experienced delays for various reasons, including securing permits, and we are monitoring indications that Texas’ residential construction is beginning to slow down. Our gross profit decreased 2.7% and 0.7% in the third quarter and first nine months 2026, respectively, compared to the third quarter and first nine months 2025. The decreases in gross profit resulted primarily from increased transportation and fuel costs, driven principally by higher diesel costs, and production inefficiencies experienced during the integration of our new Texas kiln into our existing plant operations. Our net income was $38.0 million ($1.32 per share diluted) in the third quarter 2026, compared to net income of $38.8 million ($1.35 per share diluted) in the third quarter 2025, a decrease of $0.7 million, or 1.9%. For the first nine months 2026, our net income was $103.1 million ($3.58 per share diluted), compared to $103.7 million ($3.61 per share diluted) for the first nine months 2025, a decrease of $0.6 million, or 0.6%. In 2024, we began construction on a new vertical kiln and related equipment and infrastructure at our Texas Lime Company plant. We estimate that the construction costs of the Texas kiln project will total approximately $65 million. The new kiln began intermittently producing quicklime in the third quarter and we expect to have final commissioning completed in the fourth quarter, at which point we will begin to depreciate the new kiln and related equipment. Our costs in Texas have been impacted by production inefficiencies as we continue to integrate the new kiln into our existing plant operations. Liquidity and Capital Resources. ​ Net cash provided by operating activities was $123.3 million in the first nine months 2026, compared to $119.4 million in the first nine months 2025, an increase of $3.9 million, or 3.3%. Our net cash provided by operating activities is composed of net income, depreciation, depletion, and amortization (“DD&A”), deferred income taxes, compensation, other items included in net income, and changes in working capital. In the first nine months 2026, net cash provided by operating activities was principally composed of $103.1 million net income, $19.4 million DD&A, $13.5 million deferred income taxes, and $5.3 million stock- based compensation, partially offset by a $18.1 million decrease from changes in operating assets and liabilities. Changes in operating assets and liabilities in the first nine months 2026 included an increase of $18.1 million in trade receivables, net, due primarily to increased sales in the third quarter 2026 compared to the fourth quarter 2025, and $2.2 million in inventories, partially offset by a decrease of $0.4 million in prepaid expenses and other current assets and an increase of $1.8 million in accounts payable and accrued 12 expenses. In the first nine months 2025, net cash provided by operating activities was principally composed of $103.7 million net income, $18.5 million DD&A, and $6.2 million stock- based compensation, partially offset by $9.4 million decrease from changes in operating assets and liabilities. Changes in operating assets and liabilities in the first nine months 2025 included an increase of $15.3 million in trade receivables, net, due primarily to increased sales in the third quarter 2025 compared to the fourth quarter 2024, and $1.0 million in inventories, partially offset by a decrease of $2.3 million in prepaid expenses and other current assets and an increase of $4.5 million in accounts payable and accrued expenses. We had $50.2 million in capital expenditures in the first nine months 2026, compared to $42.8 million in the first nine months 2025. Capital expenditures in the first nine months 2026 included $24.5 million related to the Texas kiln project, compared to $20.8 million in the first nine months 2025. Net cash used in financing activities was $5.4 million in both the first nine months 2026 and 2025, consisting primarily of cash dividends paid in each period. Cash and cash equivalents increased $68.1 million to $439.2 million at September 30, 2026 from $371.1 million at December 31, 2025. We are not committed to any planned capital expenditures until actual orders are placed for equipment. As of September 30, 2026, we were committed to $2.8 million of open purchase orders related to the Texas kiln project. We did not have any other material commitments for open purchase orders. As of September 30, 2026, we had incurred a total of $61.0 million on the Texas kiln project, of which $60.4 million had been paid in cash. Our credit agreement with Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. (the “Lender”), as amended as of August 3, 2023, provides for a $75 million revolving credit facility (the “Revolving Facility”) and an incremental accordion feature to borrow up to an additional $50 million on the same terms, subject to approval by the Lender or another lender selected by us. The credit agreement also provides for a $10 million letter of credit sublimit under the Revolving Facility. The Revolving Facility and any incremental loans mature on August 3, 2028. Interest rates on the Revolving Facility are, at our option, SOFR, plus a SOFR adjustment rate of 0.10%, plus a margin of 1.000% to 2.000%, or the Lender’s Prime Rate, plus a margin of 0.000% to 1.000%, and a commitment fee range of 0.225% to 0.350% on the undrawn portion of the Revolving Facility. The Revolving Facility interest rate margins and commitment fee are determined quarterly in accordance with a pricing grid based upon our Cash Flow Leverage Ratio, defined as the ratio of our total funded senior indebtedness to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, depletion, amortization, and compensation expense (“EBITDA”) for the 12 months ended on the last day of the most recent calendar quarter, plus pro forma EBITDA from any businesses acquired during the period. Pursuant to a security agreement, dated August 25, 2004, the Revolving Facility is secured by our existing and hereafter acquired tangible assets, intangible assets, and real property. The maturity of the Revolving Facility and any incremental loans can be accelerated if any event of default, as defined under the credit agreement, occurs. Our maximum Cash Flow Leverage Ratio is 3.50 to 1. We may pay dividends so long as we remain in compliance with the provisions of our credit agreement, and we may purchase, redeem or otherwise acquire shares of our common stock so long as our pro forma Cash Flow Leverage Ratio is less than 3.00 to 1.00 and no default or event of default exists or would exist after giving effect to such stock repurchase. At September 30, 2026, we had no debt outstanding and no draws on the Revolving Facility other than $4.6 million of letters of credit, principally related to the Texas kiln project, which count as draws against the available commitment under the Revolving Facility.
