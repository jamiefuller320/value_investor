Overview We are a biotechnology company focused on discovering and developing therapies for neurodegenerative diseases with high unmet medical need, including Alzheimer’s disease and Parkinson’s disease. Our pipeline is built around the Alector Brain Carrier (ABC) platform, a proprietary barrier (BBB) technology that enables diverse therapeutic modalities, including antibodies, enzymes, proteins and siRNA, to reach genetically validated targets in the central nervous system. On September 30, 2026, we licensed our AL050 program, our glucocerebrosidase (GCase) enzyme replacement therapy, to Genentech, Inc. (Genentech). Our other programs are wholly owned. The ABC platform is designed to enhance brain exposure while optimizing both safety and efficacy. Built on the principles of versatility, translatability, and differentiated binding to a distinct region of the transferrin receptor (TfR), the platform supports efficient delivery of therapeutic cargos across the BBB. By offering a broad range of TfR binding affinities, binding kinetics, and engineered formats, the ABC platform can be tailored to the requirements of different therapeutic modalities while preserving the ability to recruit the brain’s immune system when desired. These proprietary features are intended to balance brain uptake, potency, and safety across our pipeline. Our portfolio, built on the ABC platform and distinct from our prior clinical programs, is designed to address neurodegenerative diseases through targeted mechanisms, including the removal of pathogenic proteins, replacement of deficient proteins, and restoration of normal cellular function. AL137 Program Our AL137 program combines our proprietary beta (Aβ) antibody with our proprietary ABC platform, for the treatment of Alzheimer’s disease (AD). It is designed to efficiently remove brain Aβ plaques, with the goal of minimizing adverse effects and enabling convenient subcutaneous administration. The ABC platform was specifically engineered to reduce of transferrin receptor (TfR) on reticulocytes and peripheral immune cells while preserving full engagement of immune cells at Aβ plaques. This design is intended to achieve efficient plaque clearance while minimizing the hematologic adverse effects associated with antibodies. AL137 incorporates a , fully human antibody that selectively binds pyroglutamate-3 Aβ (PyroGlu3 Aβ), a validated and pathogenic form of Aβ enriched in amyloid plaques. In preclinical studies, AL137 demonstrated robust brain penetration in primates, while a murine surrogate has shown significant reduction of brain Aβ42 levels in Alzheimer’s disease mouse models. Following comparative evaluation of AL137 and AL037, we selected AL137 as the lead development candidate and subcutaneous (SC) administration as the intended clinical route, with AL037 designated as the backup development candidate. AL137 completed studies, in which it was well tolerated in nonhuman primates (NHPs) with repeated SC administration. We submitted a registration to a Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC) in Australia in October 2026 and are targeting SC dosing in Australia by April 2027. We are also targeting submission of a U.S. Investigational New Drug (IND) application in the second quarter of 2027 to enable Phase 1b studies in AD patients. siRNA Platform We continue to advance our siRNA platform. The platform is designed for peripheral dosing, offering the potential for more convenient and scalable administration compared with traditional intrathecal delivery, as well as the potential for homogeneous drug distribution throughout the brain. Our siRNA programs, which span multiple disease mechanisms, include AL164, our tau siRNA program for AD and other tauopathies; AL062, our siRNA program for Parkinson's disease (PD) and Lewy body dementia (LBD); and ADP065-ABC, an NLRP3 siRNA program for multiple neurodegenerative conditions. Together, these programs reflect the broad applicability of the ABC 16 platform across disease mechanisms. We continue to evolve our research and development plans and timing for each of our siRNA programs. AL064/AL164 Program Our lead siRNA program, AL164, is a tau siRNA program for AD and other tauopathies. AL164 aims to reduce all forms of toxic tau by degrading tau mRNA and reducing tau protein expression and aims to slow cognitive decline in AD and other tauopathies. AL064 demonstrated robust and homogeneous tau mRNA knockdown and durable reduction of 217 in multiple NHP brain regions tested. AL164, which incorporates a chemical modification intended to further optimize AL064’s siRNA stability, is targeted for studies in the second quarter of 2028. AL062 Program AL062 is an α-synuclein siRNA program for PD and LBD that aims to silence the production of intracellular α-synuclein, the primary component of Lewy bodies and a likely driver of Parkinson's disease and Lewy Body Dementia pathology. Following SC dosing in NHPs, AL062 reduced SNCA expression by up to 94% across multiple brain regions and was well tolerated, with no AL062-related adverse findings. We are targeting studies in the second quarter of 2028. AL050 Program and Genentech License Agreement AL050 is designed to address GCase deficiency, a driver of neurodegeneration in PD that is most pronounced in, but not limited to, carriers of GBA1 mutations. There is currently no approved therapy that addresses the underlying enzyme deficiency. AL050 combines an GCase, optimized for greater enzymatic activity and a longer , with ABC, which is designed to transport therapeutics across the barrier (BBB). Delivered into the brain, the enzyme is intended to break down glucosylsphingosine and glucosylceramide, lipids that accumulate in neurons and other brain cells upon GCase deficiency and contribute to neurodegeneration, with the aim of reducing cellular dysfunction and slowing disease progression.
