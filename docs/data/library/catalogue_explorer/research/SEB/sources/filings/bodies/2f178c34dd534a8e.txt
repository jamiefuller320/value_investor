LIQUIDITY AND CAPITAL RESOURCES The primary objectives of Seaboard’s financing strategy are to effectively manage financial risks, ensure efficient liquidity for daily global operations and maintain balance sheet strength. Seaboard’s principal funding sources are cash provided by operating activities, proceeds from sales of investments and borrowings from revolving lines of credit and term loans. Seaboard’s cash requirements primarily consist of working capital, capital expenditures, strategic investments and other general corporate needs. Seaboard evaluates its overall liquidity at least quarterly, and management believes that Seaboard’s cash, together with its available liquidity and borrowing capacity, will be adequate to meet all of its and commitments. As of October 3, 2026, Seaboard had cash and investments of nearly $1.5 billion and additional net working capital of $947 million. Of the total cash and investments balances, $148 million was held by foreign subsidiaries. Cash Flows Cash provided by operating activities was $236 million for the nine months ended October 3, 2026, compared to $380 million for the same period in 2025. This change was primarily due to a $172 million increase in cash used for working capital, partially offset by a $79 million increase in dividend payments received from affiliates. The increase in cash used for working capital was attributable to increases in inventory, primarily due to the timing of sales and inventory purchases in Seaboard’s CT&M segment. The CT&M segment handles large shipments of grain, so the timing of these deliveries can result in significant working capital fluctuations across periods. During the second quarter of 2026, Seaboard sold 2025 production tax credits, which were accounted for as inventory, generating net cash proceeds of $55 million after discount and transaction fees. Cash used in investing activities was $465 million for the nine months ended October 3, 2026, compared to $401 million for the same period in 2025. This change was primarily due to a $280 million increase in net purchases of investments, partially offset by lower capital expenditures of $128 million. Cash flows from investing activities for investments are part of Seaboard’s overall liquidity management strategy. investment purchases result from the investment of excess cash, asset allocation decisions arising from the active management of the portfolio and of matured securities. During the nine months ended October 3, 2026, Seaboard invested $299 million 17 ​ in property, plant and equipment, including $119 million in the Power segment, consisting primarily of installment payments for EDM IV, a new barge currently under construction. Seaboard continues to explore strategic acquisitions and investments to further grow and diversify its operations. Cash provided by financing activities was $209 million for the nine months ended October 3, 2026, compared to $117 million for the same period in 2025. Cash flows from financing activities primarily include draws and repayments under committed and uncommitted revolving facilities held with financial institutions across multiple jurisdictions and currencies. Changes in Seaboard’s borrowing balances are primarily driven by its daily working capital needs. Seaboard did not repurchase any shares under its share repurchase program during the first nine months of 2026 compared to repurchases of $38 million during the first nine months of 2025. As of October 3, 2026, $62 million remained available for repurchase under the program. Seaboard is not obligated to repurchase a minimum number of shares under the program and Seaboard cannot predict when, or if, it will repurchase any shares or the amount of any such repurchases. See Note 6 to the condensed consolidated financial statements for more discussion of Seaboard’s share repurchase program.
