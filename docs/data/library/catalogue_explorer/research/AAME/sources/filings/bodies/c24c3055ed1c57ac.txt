Controls and Procedures Evaluation of Disclosure Controls and Procedures The Company maintains disclosure controls and procedures, as defined in Rules 13a-15(e) and 15d-15(e) under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended (the “Exchange Act”), that are designed to ensure that information required to be disclosed in reports filed or submitted under the Exchange Act is recorded, processed, summarized and reported within the time periods specified in the Securities and Exchange Commission’s rules and forms and is accumulated and communicated to management, including the Company’s principal executive officer and principal financial officer, as appropriate, to allow timely decisions regarding required disclosure. As of December 31, 2025, management, with the participation of the Company’s Chief Executive Officer and its Principal Financial Officer, evaluated the effectiveness of the Company’s disclosure controls and procedures. Based on this evaluation, management, including the Chief Executive Officer and Principal Financial Officer concluded that the Company’s disclosure controls and procedures were not effective as of December 31, 2025 because of the material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting described below. Management’s Report on Internal Control Over Financial Reporting Management is responsible for establishing and maintaining adequate internal control over financial reporting, as defined in Rules 13a-15(f) and 15d-15(f) under the Exchange Act. The Company’s internal control over financial reporting is a process designed to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for external purposes in accordance with GAAP. Because of its inherent limitations, internal control over financial reporting may not prevent or detect all misstatements. Furthermore, the application of any evaluations of effectiveness in future periods is subject to the risk that controls may become inadequate because of changes in conditions, or that the degree of compliance with the policies or procedures may deteriorate. Therefore, even those systems determined to be effective can provide only reasonable assurance with respect to financial statement preparation and presentation. Management assessed the effectiveness of the Company’s internal control over financial reporting as of December 31, 2025, using the criteria set forth by the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO) in the Internal Control – Integrated Framework (2013) . Based on this assessment, management concluded that the Company’s internal control over financial reporting was not effective as of December 31, 2025, because of the material weaknesses described below. A material weakness is a deficiency, or combination of deficiencies, in internal control over financial reporting, such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of the Company’s annual or interim financial statements will not be prevented or detected on a timely basis. Material Weakness—Group Premium Receivables and Suspense Cash Application Management identified deficiencies in the design and operating effectiveness of controls over the recording and reconciliation of premium receivables and the application of premium payments held in suspense within the Company’s group whole life and group accident and health business. The Company did not design and maintain effective controls to timely and accurately reconcile enrollment, billing and cash receipt information at the individual policyholder level and to resolve differences among enrollment data, the policy administration system and the general ledger. As a result, the Company did not maintain controls operating at a sufficient level of precision to determine whether recorded premium receivables were valid, whether amounts held in suspense should be applied to individual policyholders or otherwise recognized, and whether the related amounts were recorded in the appropriate period and were appropriately classified in the Consolidated Financial Statements. These deficiencies created a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of premium revenue, premium receivables, accounts payable and accrued expenses and related disclosures would not be prevented or detected on a timely basis. Accordingly, management concluded that these deficiencies, in the aggregate, constituted a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting as of December 31, 2025. Material Weakness— Implementation of LDTI and Valuation and Accounting for contracts 75 Table of Contents Management identified deficiencies in the design and operating effectiveness of internal controls over the adoption and implementation of Accounting Standards Update No. 2018-12, Financial Services—Insurance (Topic 944): Targeted Improvements to the Accounting for Contracts (“LDTI”). The Company lacked sufficient personnel with the specialized technical accounting, actuarial and internal control knowledge and experience necessary to design and execute an effective implementation process for LDTI and to perform an appropriate level of review over the resulting actuarial calculations, accounting entries and financial statement disclosures. Consequently, the Company did not design and maintain effective controls to ensure that significant LDTI data inputs, assumptions, model configurations and accounting conclusions were identified, validated and incorporated completely and accurately into the actuarial calculations, accounting records and financial statement disclosures in time to complete the financial reporting process. These deficiencies created a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of LFPB, related remeasurement gains or losses, DAC, AOCI, income taxes and related financial statement disclosures would not be prevented or detected on a timely basis. Accordingly, management concluded that these deficiencies, in the aggregate, constituted a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting as of December 31, 2025. Material Weakness— Income Tax Accounting Management identified deficiencies in the design and operating effectiveness of internal controls over the preparation, review and financial reporting of the Company’s income tax provision and current and deferred tax balances. The Company’s controls did not operate with sufficient precision and timeliness to ensure that the reserves, premium and LDTI transition information used in the tax provision were complete and accurate. Consequently, the income tax provision calculations were subject to revisions and required subsequent correction or reclassification. Although management’s corrective procedures supported the final reported amounts, those subsequent procedures demonstrated that the underlying controls were not operating effectively as of December 31, 2025. These deficiencies created a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of income tax related balances and related financial statement disclosures would not be prevented or detected on a timely basis. Accordingly, management concluded that these deficiencies, in the aggregate, constituted a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting as of December 31, 2025. Remediation Activities As further described below, management has initiated remediation activities intended to address the material weaknesses described above, which remain unremediated as of the date of this report. These activities include the following: Material Weakness—Group Premium Receivables and Suspense Cash Application Management’s remediation activities include or will include: • Implementing controls to validate inbound enrollment files against approved formats and business rules; • Establishing procedures to communicate exceptions to submitting partners and require correction before information is processed by the system; • Implementing documented manual validation and controls while the automated solution is being developed and tested; • Establishing recurring reconciliations among enrollment data, billing records, cash receipts, policy status, balances, suspense balances and the general ledger; and • Establishing formal aging, investigation, disposition and escalation requirements for and suspense balances; Material Weakness—Implementation of LDTI and Valuation and Accounting for contracts Management’s remediation activities include or will include: • Adding personnel or engaging qualified resources with specialized technical accounting, actuarial and internal control expertise to support the ongoing application of LDTI; • Enhancing controls over actuarial data inputs, assumptions, model configurations, actuarial calculations, as well as actuarial based journal entries, account reconciliations and financial statement disclosures; and • Establishing formal handoff and review controls designed to ensure that complete, accurate and timely information is provided and reviewed before completion of the financial statement close process. Material Weakness—Income Tax Accounting Management’s remediation activities include or will include: • Implementing controls to reconcile final tax reserves and premium to the corresponding tax attributes used in the income tax provision; and • Enhancing the independent review of current and deferred income tax expense, deferred tax assets and liabilities, and related equity effects using defined review criteria and thresholds, as well as model changes.
