This was partially offset by a higher impairment charge . Profit after tax was £ 4,835 million and earnings per share were 7.6 pence (nine months ended 30 September 2025 : £ 3,322 million and 4.8 pence respectively). Total income for the first nine months of 2026 was £ 16,084 million, an increase of 13 % on the prior period (nine months to 30 September 2025 : £ 14,252 million). Net interest income of £ 10,827 million was up 10 % (nine months to 30 September 2025 : £ 9,808 million), driven by higher average assets and a higher margin, resulting from increased structural hedge income as maturing positions were reinvested at higher interest rates, partially offset by asset margin compression across portfolios, particularly in UK mortgages, and a competitive deposit market . Other income increased by 18 % to £ 5,257 million (nine months to 30 September 2025 : £ 4,444 million), with a higher net investment return and finance result in respect of insurance and investment contracts, higher net fee and commission income, higher net trading income and higher other operating income, partially offset by a lower insurance service result. The net investment return and finance result in respect of insurance and investment contracts and net trading income increased due to market movements. Net fee and commission income increased by 21 % as a result of strengthening customer activity and the addition of Lloyds Wealth. Other operating income increased by 17 % as a result of vehicle fleet growth and higher average vehicle rental value in UK Motor Finance, a gain on the securitisation of primarily legacy mortgages and continued growth in Lloyds Living . The insurance service result reduced due to increased general insurance claims, particularly as a result of the industry- wide subsidence event. Total operating expenses of £ 8,533 million decreased by 5 % (nine months to 30 September 2025 : £ 8,955 million) reflecting a lower remediation charge, continued cost savings, a lower severance expense and plateauing investment as the current strategic cycle culminates. This was partly offset by business growth costs, inflationary pressures, costs related to Lloyds Wealth and a higher operating lease depreciation charge. The operating lease depreciation charge increased given fleet growth, the depreciation of higher value vehicles and a second quarter charge for reduced used car prices, partially offset by continued risk mitigation . A remediation charge of £ 61 million was recognised by the Group in the first nine months of 2026 (nine months to 30 September 2025: £ 912 million) across a small number of programmes. The prior year included £800 million in relation to the potential impact of motor finance commission arrangements, taken in the third quarter of 2025. There have been no further charges relating to motor finance commission arrangements. The FCA published policy statement PS26/3 in March 2026 with final rules for its motor finance redress schemes. Four challenges to the FCA’s schemes have been raised, three by lenders and one by a consumer group. The implementation of the schemes has now been delayed, as the Upper Tribunal hearing in respect of those challenges is listed for hearing in the second half of February 2027. The Group will closely monitor the development of these challenges and consider any potential impact on the existing provision. Despite these uncertainties, as well as broader implications of any legal and/or regulatory developments, the current provision continues to represent the Group’s current best estimate of the potential impact of the motor finance issue. The impairment charge was £ 926 million (nine months to 30 September 2025: £ 619 million ). The higher charge compared to prior year is driven by adverse model updates alongside balance sheet growth in Retail, and lower provision releases in Commercial Banking. The charge includes a net charge from updated multiple economic scenarios (MES) reflecting the impact of the deterioration in the economic outlook due to the Middle East conflict. This is partially offset by the release of the £50 million post model adjustment for global tariff and political disruption risks within Commercial Banking in the first quarter, now considered to be adequately captured within macroeconomic assumptions and resulting modelled provisions. Credit performance remains strong and stable across Retail and Commercial Banking with arrears low and stable in all portfolios. The Group recognised a tax expense of £ 1,790 million in the first nine months of 2026 (nine months to 30 September 2025: £ 1,356 million). UK economic assumptions Base case and MES economic assumptions The Group’s base case economic scenario has been updated to reflect events through to the balance sheet date, including ongoing geopolitical developments. The Group has included updated assumptions for energy prices and impacts in its base case conditioning assumptions. Incorporating the economic data published in the third quarter of 2026, the Group’s base case scenario is for an ongoing expansion in gross domestic product (GDP), modest gains in residential and commercial property prices alongside a small increase in the unemployment rate. Recent increases in energy prices imply increased inflationary pressures, warranting an upward adjustment in Bank Rate of 25 basis points in November 2026. However, the terminal rate assumption remains unchanged at 3.50%, reaching that level in the second half of 2028. Risks around this base case economic view lie in both directions and are largely captured by the generation of alternative economic scenarios. The Group’s approach to generating alternative economic scenarios is set out in detail in note 21 to the financial statements of the Group’s 2025 Annual Report and Accounts. The Group has taken into account the latest available information at the reporting date in defining its base case scenario and generating alternative economic scenarios. The scenarios include forecasts for key variables as of the third quarter of 2026. Actuals for this period, or restatements of past data, may have since emerged prior to publication and have not been included.
