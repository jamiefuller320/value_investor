Discontinued Operations and Assets and Liabilities of Discontinued Operations – The Company classifies a component of its business as a discontinued operation when its disposal, or anticipated disposal, represents a strategic shift that has, or will have, a major effect on the Company's operations and financial results and all of the following conditions exist: management commits to a plan to sell the business; the business is available for immediate sale in its present condition; an active program to complete the plan to sell has been initiated; the sale is probable within one year; and the business is being actively marketed at a price reasonable in relation to its fair value. A component of an acquisition that meets these criteria at the acquisition date is classified as a discontinued operation at acquisition, with the associated assets and liabilities presented separately in the consolidated balance sheets from the date of acquisition. Assets and liabilities reported as discontinued operations are measured at fair value less costs to sell as of the acquisition date in accordance with ASC 805, Business Combinations. Assets and liabilities of discontinued operations are measured for impairment upon initial classification and at each subsequent reporting date. An impairment loss is recorded for assets when the carrying amount exceeds fair value less costs to sell. Other assets and liabilities are generally measured for impairment by comparing their carrying values to their respective fair values. assets within a discontinued operation are not depreciated or amortized from the date of classification. The results of discontinued operations, including any gain or loss on disposal, are presented separately in the Consolidated Statements of Income, net of applicable income taxes, for all periods presented. Interest expense directly attributable to the assets of a discontinued operation may be allocated to discontinued operations; deposit liabilities or other funding sources that are not part of the planned disposal are not classified within liabilities of discontinued operations and remain in continuing operations. Intangible Assets Acquired Intangible Asset s – Intangible assets acquired in a business combination are recorded at fair value as of the acquisition date in accordance with ASC 805, Business Combinations. acquired intangible assets are amortized on a basis over their estimated useful lives, unless another method better reflects the pattern in which the economic benefits of the asset are consumed. Estimated useful lives are determined based on factors including contractual or legal terms, the expected period of economic benefit, competitive and economic factors, and the Company's expected use of the asset. The Company reassesses the estimated useful life of each intangible asset each reporting period. Acquired intangible assets are reviewed for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate the carrying amount may not be recoverable. An impairment loss is recognized when the carrying amount of the asset exceeds the sum of the undiscounted future cash flows expected from the use and eventual disposition of the asset, and is measured as the amount by which the carrying value exceeds the asset's fair value. Internally Developed Software – Software developed for internal use is accounted for pursuant to ASC 350-40, Intangibles, Goodwill and Other — Software, as amended by ASU 2025-06, which the Company early adopted effective January 1, 2026 using the prospective transition approach. See Recently Adopted Accounting Pronouncements below. Qualifying costs incurred to develop software are capitalized when (i) management authorizes and commits to funding the project, and (ii) it is probable that the project will be completed and the software 13 Table of Contents will be used to perform the function as intended. Capitalized costs include compensation for employees who develop software and external costs related to development and implementation. Capitalization ceases once the project is substantially complete and the software is ready for its intended use. Capitalized costs for projects that are not yet ready for their intended use are reported as software in development and are not amortized. Amortization begins when the software is ready for its intended use and is amortized on a basis over estimated useful lives of three to ten years once ready for its intended use, based on the nature and expected period of benefit of each project. Software in development is evaluated for impairment when events or changes in circumstances indicate that its carrying amount may not be recoverable, including when it is no longer probable that the project will be completed and the software used as intended, in which case the capitalized costs are written down to the lower of carrying amount or fair value, if any, less cost to sell. There is a rebuttable presumption that software no longer probable of completion has a fair value of zero. All other expenditures, including those incurred to maintain an intangible asset's current level of performance, are expensed as incurred. Recently Adopted Accounting Pronouncements In September 2025, the Financial Accounting Standards Board (“FASB”) issued ASU 2025-06, Intangibles - Goodwill and Other - Software (Subtopic 350-40): Targeted Improvements to the Accounting for Software, that amends the accounting guidance on the timing of capitalization of software costs. The update removes references to software development stages, and provides guidance on how to determine when it is probable that a project will be completed and the software will be used to perform the function as intended. The accounting standard update must be applied using one of the following transition methods: (i) a prospective transition method, (ii) a modified transition approach based on the status of the project and whether software costs were capitalized before the date of adoption, or (iii) a retrospective transition approach. The accounting standard update is effective beginning after December 15, 2027, with early adoption permitted as of the beginning of an annual reporting period. The Company early adopted the accounting standard update effective January 1, 2026, using the prospective transition approach. Under this approach, the amendments are applied to new software costs on a prospective basis. The Company did not have any material capitalized software costs for projects on the date of adoption. The Company commenced capitalization of software costs related to projects acquired in connection with the acquisition of Tallied in July 2026 (see Note 2 - Acquisition). The adoption of the accounting standard did not have a material impact on the Company’s consolidated financial statements. Although structured as an asset purchase, the transaction was accounted for as a business combination in accordance with ASC 805, Business Combinations. Under the acquisition method, the purchase price was allocated to the tangible and identifiable intangible assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values as of the acquisition date, with deferred income taxes measured in accordance with ASC 740, Income Taxes. Total consideration for the acquisition was $ 6.2 million, consisting of $ 0.3 million of cash paid at closing and $ 5.9 million representing the fair value of receivables owed by Tallied to the Bank, which were effectively settled upon completion of the acquisition. The receivables owed by Tallied to the Bank were contributed as consideration at their fair value upon 14 Table of Contents closing, resulting in no gain or loss on settlement. In addition to the cash consideration and offsetting receivables and liabilities, the Company assumed $ 11.2 million of Tallied liabilities. The assets acquired and the liabilities assumed were specifically identified in the Agreement. The assets acquired comprised substantially all of the assets of Tallied. The liabilities assumed were specifically identified in the exhibits to the Agreement. The Company also hired Tallied’s remaining employees. As part of the purchase price allocation process, the Company reconciled identifiable acquired assets and assumed liabilities to the executed transaction documents and Tallied’s closing balance sheet to confirm the completeness of the acquisition accounting. The bargain purchase gain resulted from two circumstances. First, Tallied had invested significant time and resources developing a credit card servicing platform that required additional development before it could operate as intended. With limited remaining liquidity and no additional funding available, Tallied pursued an expedited sale. Second, the Company, as the sponsor bank of the credit card program serviced on the Tallied platform, had firsthand familiarity with the platform and the technical expertise to assess the work required to complete it. The combination of Tallied’s limited liquidity and the Company’s ability to complete its evaluation enabled the Company to acquire Tallied’s assets and liabilities at a price below their estimated fair value, resulting in the recognition of a bargain purchase gain. Concurrent with the acquisition, the FinWise Board of Directors approved a plan to exit the credit card portfolio business. The program sponsor relationship, program records and cardholder rewards liability acquired from Tallied are included in the associated disposal group, which is presented as discontinued operations, and were therefore measured as of the acquisition date at fair value less cost to sell. See Note 3 – Discontinued Operations. The Company expects to finalize these amounts within one year from the acquisition date, with any measurement-period adjustment recognized in the period determined as a corresponding adjustment to the bargain purchase gain. The fair value of the net identifiable acquired assets and assumed liabilities exceeded the consideration paid, resulting in a preliminary bargain purchase gain of $ 6.5 million recorded in income. The Company reviewed its acquisition accounting methods, confirmed all assets and liabilities were properly identified, and ensured measurements reflected all consideration as of the closing date. The bargain purchase gain is not expected to be included as taxable income for tax purposes. The allowance for credit losses represents management’s estimate of lifetime credit losses inherent in loans as of the balance sheet date. The allowance for credit losses is estimated by management using relevant available information, from 24 Table of Contents both internal and external sources, relating to past events, current conditions, and reasonable and supportable forecasts. The Bank measures expected credit losses for loans on a pooled basis when similar risk characteristics exist. Generally, collectively assessed loans are grouped by Call Report code and then risk grade grouping. In addition to past due and nonaccrual status criteria, the Company also evaluates loans using a loan grading system. Internal loan grades are based on current financial information, historical payment experience, and credit documentation, among other factors. Performance-based grades are summarized below: Pass – A Pass asset is higher quality and does not fit any of the other categories described below. The likelihood of loss is believed to be remote. Watch – A Watch asset may be a larger loan or one that places a heavier reliance on collateral due to the relative financial strength of the borrower. The assets may be maintenance intensive requiring closer monitoring. The obligor is believed to have an adequate primary source of repayment. Special Mention – A Special Mention asset has potential weaknesses that may be temporary or, if left uncorrected, may result in a loss. While concerns exist, the Company believes that it is currently protected against a default and loss is considered unlikely and not imminent. Substandard – A Substandard asset is believed to be inadequately protected by the current sound worth and paying capacity of the obligor or of the collateral pledged, if any. Assets so classified have identified weaknesses and are characterized by the possibility that the Company may sustain some loss if deficiencies are not corrected. Doubtful – A doubtful asset has an existing weakness or weaknesses that make collection or liquidation in full, on the basis of currently existing facts and conditions, highly questionable and improbable. Loss – A loss asset has an existing weakness or weaknesses that render the loan uncollectible and of such little value that continuing to carry as an asset on the Company’s books is not warranted. This classification does not mean that the loan has absolutely no recovery or salvage value, but rather it is not practical nor desirable to defer writing off this basically worthless asset, even though partial recovery may be effected in the future. Not Rated – For certain Strategic Program loans, the Company does not evaluate and risk rate the loans in the same manner as other loans in the Company’s portfolio. The Not Rated loans are typically homogenous, smaller dollar balances approved using abridged underwriting methods that allow the Company to streamline the loan approval process and increase efficie ncy . Strategic Program Loans with Credit Enhancement The Company partners with certain Strategic Program service providers who offer credit enhancement on loans, indemnifying or reimbursing the Bank for credit and fraud losses. In line with GAAP, we estimate and record expected loss provisions for these loans without factoring in the credit enhancement. When these provisions are recorded, a corresponding credit enhancement asset is recognized, reflecting anticipated recoveries under the service provider’s guarantee. Reimbursements are made through a deposit reserve account, which the provider replenishes periodically. The credit enhancement asset is reduced as payments or recoveries are received from the provider or its reserve account. Where a borrower faces financial hardship, modifications may offer either temporary accommodations or permanent adjustments to the original loan terms.
