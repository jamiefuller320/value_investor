Fiscal Year 2024 Challenges During the pandemic, high demand and long lead times for glass vials created an temporary imbalance of supply and demand for glass vials, and customers stockpiled glass vials (both standard and ) to mitigate risk and secure their supply chains. As a result of increased customer inventories for glass vials, the industry is now experiencing a slowdown in demand for glass vials as market participants continue to work down their inventories. As a consequence of our customers' inventory destocking, we experienced lower volumes and revenue attributable to glass vials starting in 2023 and throughout 2024. The lower revenue from vials (both standard and ), coupled with the underutilization of vial production lines and the associated under absorption of overhead costs, adversely impacted gross profit and operating profit margins. The Group currently expects a more gradual recovery in vials and anticipates that vial demand will continue to normalize throughout 2025, with bulk vials expected to recover first. As a result, considering the high marginality of glass vials and the impact on the mix of solutions, gross profit margin, operating profit margin and EBITDA margin were unfavorably impacted throughout 2024, and the Group expects that this will extend into 2025 as vial demand continues to normalize. The Group is also experiencing temporary inefficiencies tied to the phase of its capacity expansion projects, both in Italy and in the U.S., tempering gross profit margin, operating profit margin and EBITDA margin in 2024. Such inefficiencies reflect higher costs during the initial phase and temporary under absorption of costs as volumes and revenue begin to increase during the phase. These costs include the fixed assets and infrastructure, implementation of industrial processes, hiring and training of new employees, the qualification and validation activities of new production lines, as well as the time ordinarily needed by newly validated lines to progressively increase productivity to reach target level. Moreover, as anticipated, throughout the phase depreciation of new assets has further tempered gross profit margin and operating profit margin, as the productivity of the new assets has not yet reached target level. The Group expects that as the activities progress, and are completed, those anticipated temporary inefficiencies will gradually abate. The Group advanced progress on these expansion projects throughout 2024, and in the third quarter, the Group's new facility in Latina became profitable at the gross profit level, and the new facility in Fishers generated its first commercial revenue. Lastly, in 2024, the Group launched a business optimization plan for the Engineering Segment to (i) address an existing backlog of work on delayed projects, (ii) improve the health of the business, and (iii) better position the business for future growth. The plan aims to: (i) optimize our industrial footprint in order to best align with our product 73 strategy; (ii) right size the operational structure as some activities are being relocated to Italy; and (iii) harmonize our industrial processes. The Group believes these actions will lead to an improved operational structure that can drive both cost savings and gains in productivity. The backlog of work in the Engineering Segment stems from record order intake during a period of significant supply chain constraints primarily for electronic components. The challenges are predominantly isolated to its Denmark operations where the Group has experienced increased costs on certain highly customized projects in the later stages of development. The Group is executing its business optimization plan, and in the second half of 2024, the Group delivered several complex manufacturing lines that were previously delayed. The Group expects to complete the majority of the previously delayed projects by midyear 2025. The Group expects that these ongoing initiatives will continue throughout 2025 and into 2026. Research and Development Expenses In 2024, our research and development expenses amounted to 2.9% of our revenue, compared to 3.3% in 2023. Expenses in research and new product development are a strategic enabler for our future growth and we expect to continue to make substantial investments in this area in coming years. Through continued spending in our research and development programs, we intend to drive revenue and profit growth through processes that will improve innovation and quality of our existing products, and facilitate the shift towards “ ” products, services and solutions. Our ability to leverage our recent investments in research and new product development is critical to our future performance. Our current research and development efforts are focused on the ongoing innovations in (i) advancing drug containment solutions for innovative biologic drugs, including GLP1s and peptides, applications, monoclonal antibodies, cell and gene therapies, and (ii) drug delivery systems that support the rising trend towards the of medicines. In our core drug containment solutions business, the development of new products will be targeted at maintaining the stability, potency and purity of our customers’ products prior to administration. New therapies for diabetes, obesity, cancer and autoimmune diseases are based on large, complex biological molecules that are extremely sensitive to their storage environment such as applications. In the area of drug delivery systems, we are targeting the development of , accurate, reliable systems for complex pharmaceutical and biotechnology products. We have developed a portfolio of devices for this market that can be used or tailored to the specific needs of the customer. We will continue developing new drug delivery device systems based on three main pillars: patient centricity, sustainability, and digitalization, all of which are core capabilities to meet our customers' need for connected health devices. We apply a rigorous “ stage & gate ” development process to our development projects and reduce total development costs. Development timelines for new drug delivery devices typically fall into the range of four to five years to reach the start of initial production.
