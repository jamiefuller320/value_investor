## INVESTMENT THESIS
Vistry is a Partnerships-led UK housebuilder. FY2025 completions were 74% Partner Funded (11,593 units at ASP £246k) and 26% Open Market (4,065 units at ASP £391k), with group ASP £282k (+3%). The Chair (3 March 2026) says 2025 results were in line with guidance and H2 was particularly strong despite Open Market weakness and Autumn Budget delays to Partner Funded deals. Structural hooks in the same extract include alignment with the Social and Affordable Homes Programme, a £50m top-up allocation expected to be paid from April 2026, the PlacePoint joint venture with Homes England (£150m available capital; first site transferred December 2025), and medium-term targets of about 20,000 units of divisional capacity, 12% adjusted operating margin and 40% ROCE — versus FY2025 ROCE of 13.9% (2024: 14.6%) and implied adjusted operating margin of about 8.5% on £353.8m / £4,155.3m.

A value screen could still be cheap if year-end net debt, a 71,501-plot owned and controlled land bank, and recovered reported earnings are being discounted as a post-2024 reset. That cheapness is not evidenced here: there is no screening snapshot, no Yahoo multiples or FCF, and no yield. Quality is also incomplete. Adjusted operating profit was slightly down (£353.8m vs £358.2m); volumes and sales rate (0.96 vs 1.07 per site per week) deteriorated; average daily debt rose; capital returns are a residual £29m buyback with no FY2025 dividend disclosed in available pages; and the Board’s highest 2026 priority is deleveraging to a year-end net cash position, not distributions. Combined Executive Chair and CEO, the 2024 South Division control issues referenced in the Chair’s statement, and historically material fire-safety provisions in older pack accounts (not updated in the FY2025 extract) further cap any quality overlay. The thesis is therefore “possible recovery in a partnerships franchise” — not a confirmed strong buy.
