## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ME Group International plc is a sterling-reporting instant-service vending group whose FY25 audited results (year to 31 October 2025) showed revenue £315.4m (+2.4%), operating profit £78.1m (+5.0%) and diluted EPS 14.91p (+4.5%), with Wash.ME laundry already 46.1% of group EBITDA while Photo.ME vending fell 4.0%. The screen’s headline strong_buy (15/22, composite 84%) is already overlay-adjusted to buy: H1 2026 diluted EPS fell 3.9% to 6.48p, and filing-aligned free cash flow of £25.2m covered cash dividends of about £29.8m only 0.84×. On 1 June 2026 the Board cut FY26 profit before tax to £69m–£74m after an April French-consumer shock; the 13 July interim held that revised range and did not restore or cut it again. A 7.1% yield and 7.9× P/E are real in pound terms, but they price a mix shift, a mid-year guidance cut, and a dividend that is no longer covered by net FCF. This is a new one-week signal with 54% conviction — too early to treat as an unqualified strong buy.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The bull case is a still-profitable, net-cash vending estate whose higher-margin laundry engine is taking mix: Wash.ME H1 vending revenue £54.8m (+16.3%) and 53.9% of group EBITDA, with 499 net new machines and large-site contracts (ASDA; renewed SNCF/RATP). Photo.ME remains the larger revenue line but is in structural and cyclical decline (German ID-rule change; April photobooth −17%; equipment sales −14.2% by design). Cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk families all passed on Yahoo/TTM inputs, including FCF yield and high-dividend models. Those family passes do not survive an unadjusted reading of the filings: net FCF/dividend cover is below 1.0×, H1 cash generated from operations fell 18.9%, Piotroski is 6/9 (ROA, current ratio and asset turnover not improving), current ratio is 1.54, and Lynch PEG fails on screen earnings growth of −3.9% (the H1 statutory decline). The actionable screen label is therefore buy, not strong_buy. A new buyer is underwriting (i) laundry capex converting before photobooth cash flows fade further and (ii) the Board’s willingness to trim the payout, already shown by the 6.5% interim cut to 3.60p. Until H2 delivers the £69m–£74m range and a covered final dividend, the 7.9× multiple is as consistent with a fading-core value trap as with GARP.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Dividend sustainability is the live constraint. FY25 net FCF of £25.2m did not cover cash dividends of £29.8m; the Board still declared £32.6m and launched an £18m buyback, then cut the FY26 interim 6.5% while H1 investing outflows exceeded operating inflows. Under £69m–£74m PBT versus FY25 £78.2m, a 7.1% yield requires either a further payout cut, slower capex than the £57m–£59m guide, or a sharp H2 cash recovery that is not yet evidenced. Photo.ME faces a structural German ID-rule hit (May 2025; Nov 2025 news cluster) plus cyclical French/travel weakness; if laundry installation slips, the 7.9× P/E is a fading-core trap. France is the largest and most profitable market — customer and country concentration in all but name. Liquidity is not a going-concern issue in the interim statement, but net cash fell to £7.5m and the current ratio of 1.54 failed Graham Defensive; H1 working-capital drain sits on top of growth capex. FY25 accounts were delayed (RNS 25 February and 12 March 2026: Forvis Mazars needed more time; shares suspended 2–23 March; Board said it had not been informed of material audit issues). The FY25 results RNS states the audit was complete and accounts are on a going-concern basis, and FY24 was unqualified, but the full FY25 auditor report text is not in the assigned excerpt — residual accounting-process risk, plus the FY24 cash-in-transit restatement. Governance is quieter than the December 2025 offer window but not closed: discussions terminated with no offer (5 December 2025); Montefiore sold 12,523,017 shares at 132p (January 2026); Rule 9 waiver and buyback authority passed on 26 February 2026 (independent vote 83.54% on the waiver) with the Concert Party still above 30% and below 50%. Investors’ Chronicle’s 4 June “boardroom problem” piece is a headline only in `news_manifest.json` — allegations unverified. Alternate_news is unavailable, so press claims cannot be triangulated. Watch: next trading update, FY26 final-dividend declaration, French consumer/photobooth run-rate, German ID-rule residual, and whether net FCF cover stays below 1.0×.
