# ME Group International plc (MEGP.L) — Research memo

_Version 1 · Updated 2026-08-13T10:49:20.956773+00:00 · Mode: director_worker_

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ME Group International plc operates photobooths, unattended laundry and other self-service machines, with Continental Europe still about two-thirds of revenue. The screen is a new strong buy (15/22 models; cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk; P/E 7.8, yield 6.9%, ROE 28.2%), but it is only one week old, sits just below the 200-day average, and already carries an Accumulate/Medium research overlay plus an interim-quality haircut. Filings support a still-profitable, net-cash compounder that is pivoting toward Wash.ME, not a distressed balance sheet: FY25 profit before tax was a record £78.2m, and H1 2026 laundry vending rose 16.3% to £54.8m and contributed 53.9% of group EBITDA. The live debate is whether that mix shift, the ASDA laundry rollout and a >55% of profit-after-tax dividend policy offset a structurally weaker Photo.ME franchise, a France-led April demand shock, revised FY26 profit-before-tax guidance of £69m–£74m, an already-cut interim dividend, and cash that has fallen from £77.5m to £52.8m. This memo keeps the overlay. It does not confirm strong buy.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen is mechanically consistent with a high-return, low-multiple vending operator: Graham Enterprising, Buffett Quality, Economic Moat, High Dividend Yield, Dividend Growth and Financial Health all pass, with data quality 20/20. FY25 filings confirm diversified site relationships (no customer ≥10% of revenue; no site-owner account >2% of turnover), a going-concern basis, and capital returns (8.64p total dividend, 58.0% of EPS, plus an £18m buyback). H1 2026 shows the quality case migrating into laundry: Wash.ME EBITDA £30.7m versus Photo.ME £27.2m, with 499 net new laundry machines and an ASDA partnership described as the largest laundry deal in group history (first machine June 2026; up to 700 long term; no H1 revenue quantified).

That is not enough to upgrade the overlay. The signal is new (conviction 0.638; one week). Timing is neutral (RSI 54.4; price −0.11% versus the 200-day average). H1 diluted EPS fell 3.9% to 6.48p and cash generated from operations fell 18.9% to £38.7m. The FY25 results RNS does not define free cash flow; Yahoo/screen FCF of £25.2m is not filing-grade, and the screen’s net FCF/dividend coverage of 0.84× (gross −0.90×) sits only in overlay narrative. The H1 interim was cut 6.5% to 3.60p even while the >55% of annual profit-after-tax policy was restated. Photo.ME remains the larger vending revenue line (£77.6m versus Wash.ME £54.8m) and is under German ID-photo rules plus April travel weakness. France is not broken out of Continental Europe (£215.5m, 68.3% of FY25 revenue and 80.1% of EBITDA), so cyclical exposure is confirmed in direction but not quantified. For a value investor the hook is a cheap, net-cash mix shift; the discipline is to accumulate against laundry delivery and cash conversion, not to treat a one-week strong-buy print as confirmation.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Regulatory. German ID-photo rules are a disclosed H1 Photo.ME driver; the photobooth estate shrank and May recovery was only +1.8%. The Identification franchise is pressured, not shown as fully impaired: it is still the larger vending-revenue line, and laundry plus ASDA/Aldi unit growth is the offset. Magnitude and duration of the German hit are not quantified in the news manifest.

Cyclical. April 2026 showed that both photobooths and, to a lesser extent, laundry are sensitive to French consumer confidence. Continental Europe is 80.1% of FY25 EBITDA and France is not split. Recovery to date is laundry-led. The screen’s cyclical-quantification gap remains open.

Governance and key person. Serge Crasnianski is CEO and Deputy Chairman; Vlad Crasneanschi joined the Board in June 2025 and became Deputy CEO on 2 February 2026. A June 2025 strategic review ended in December 2025 with no acceptable offer. Headline-only news flags June 2026 “boardroom problem” coverage, the January 2026 secondary placing, Form 8.3–8.5/offer-period activity, a GM/Rule 9 waiver and buyback, and duty changes; those items were not assigned to filing workers, and alternate_news bodies are unavailable, so residual overhang is unresolved rather than cleared.

Accounting and controls. FY24 cash, net cash and operating-cash figures were restated for cash-in-transit. Spot-check of the 25 February 2026 delay RNS (sources/filings/bodies/4e623d8eb77b8f49.txt): Forvis Mazars needed more time; the Company said it had not been told of material audit issues; a temporary listing suspension was expected from 2 March until publication (the annual RNS then pointed to restoration after the 23 March pack). FY25 auditor opinion wording (qualified or otherwise, emphasis of matter, control findings) is still not reproduced in the assigned results RNS. That is a live documentation gap, not evidence of a qualified audit.

Liquidity and capital returns. Gross cash £77.5m → £56.5m → £52.8m; net cash £26.5m → £7.5m. Rising machine capex, inventory build, dividends and buybacks are the drain. Bank-covenant headroom after that drawdown is not quantified. Customer concentration, by contrast, is resolved in the FY25 RNS and should not be treated as an overlay leftover.
