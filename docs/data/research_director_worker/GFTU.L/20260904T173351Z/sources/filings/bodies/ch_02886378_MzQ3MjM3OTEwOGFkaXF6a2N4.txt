STRATEGIC REPORT

Introduction

This strategic report has been prepared in accordance with the requirements of Section 414 of the Companies
Act 2006. Its purpose is to inform shareholders and help them to assess how the directors have performed their
duty to promote the success of GRAFTON GROUP (UK) PUBLIC LIMITED COMPANY (‘the Company’).

Principal activities
The Company operates as an investment holding company. The Company expects to continue to engage in this
activity for the foreseeable future.

Review of business for the year and state of affairs at 31 December 2024

The statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income for the year ended 31 December 2024 and the
balance sheet as at 31 December 2024 are set out on pages 9 and 10, respectively. The profit after tax for the
year amounted to £50,548,968 (2023: £54,628, 167). Dividend income reduced by 12% in 2024. Operating costs
reduced by 7% in the year. Property profit for 2024 amounted to £2.7m (2023: £0.5m). Impairment charges on
investments in 2024 were nil (2023: £ 6.0m).

Principal risks and uncertainties

The principal risk and uncertainty affecting the Company surrounds the collectability of intercompany receivables
and the carrying value of investments in subsidiaries. The directors have assessed that the investments in
subsidiaries are recoverable and believe in relation to the amounts due from Group companies that any expected
credit loss would not be material. These risks are monitored on an ongoing basis by the directors of the Company.

Key performance indicators

The Company is an investment holding and finance company. Given the straightforward nature of the Company,
the directors are of the opinion that analysis of KPI's is not necessary for an understanding of the development,
performance or position of the Company.

Financial risk management objectives and policies

The Company's activities expose it to a number of financial risks including cash flow risk and liquidity risk. The
Company adopts a prudent approach to liquidity management and to mitigate against cash flow and liquidity risk
continuously monitors forecasted and actual cash flows and maintains sufficient cash reserves to meet its
obligations.

Employment of disabled persons
All applications for employment from disabled persons are given full and fair consideration, due regard being
given to the aptitude and ability of the individual and the requirements of the position concerned.

Disabled persons are treated on equal terms with other employees as regards training, career development and
promotion. In the event of an existing employee becoming disabled, every effort is made to ensure continuity of
employment, and that appropriate training is given where necessary.

Section 172 statement for the reporting year ended 31 December 2024

As the company is an investment holding and finance company and a wholly owned subsidiary of Grafton Group
plc (“Grafton”; “the Group”) the key board decisions made during the year were made in line with the strategic
goals and objectives of both the Company and the Group.

The Company is fully aligned with the approach taken by the Group in respect of the interests of the Group's
employees, suppliers, customers, the impact of the Group's operations on the community and the environment,
the need to maintain high standards of business and conduct and the likely consequences of any decision in the
long term, details of which are set out in detail in the 2024 Annual Report of Grafton Group plc. As a wholly-
owned subsidiary, the Directors do not consider the factor set out in section 172(1)(f) (the need to act fairly
between the members of the company) relevant to the proper discharge of their duty under section 172.

DIRECTORS’ REPORT

The directors present herewith their annual report and financial statements of GRAFTON GROUP (UK) PUBLIC
LIMITED COMPANY (the “Company’) for the financial year ended 31 December 2024

Strategic report

The principal activities of the Company, a business review, key performance indicators, likely future
developments, the principal risks and uncertainties and financial risk management objectives and policies of the
Company have not been included in this report as they are disclosed in the strategic report.

Results for the year and state of affairs at 31 December 2024

The statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income for the year ended 31 December 2024 and the
balance sheet at 31 December 2024 are set out on pages 9 and 10, respectively. The profit after tax for the year
amounted to £50,548,968 (2023 £54,628,167).

Dividends
Dividends of £150m (2023 £167m) were declared and paid on the issued A Ordinary Shares and B Ordinary
Shares in the capital of the company registered in the name of Athina Limited.

Going Concern
The board of directors have concluded that the Company has adequate resources to continue in operational
existence for the foreseeable future.

The Company has investments in and receivables due from trading subsidiaries of Grafton Group plc. The directors
are satisfied about the underlying resilience of the operating subsidiaries in so far as it impacts these financial
statements.

At 31 December 2024 the Company had net current liabilities of £232.4 million (2023: £127.2 million) including a
net liability due to Group companies of £494.2 million (2023:£472.2 million). The company is also dependent on
dividend income from its operating subsidiaries to meet its interest obligations and expenses. The ultimate parent
company has given an undertaking to use its best endeavours to provide financial support to the Company for at
least one year from the date of approval of these financial statements to enable the Company to meet its liabilities
as they fall due.

The board is satisfied that the going concern basis is appropriate in the preparation of these financial statements.

Future developments
The directors consider both the results for the year and the Company's prospects to be satisfactory. It is the
directors’ intention to continue to develop the present activities of the Company.

Directors
The directors who held office during the year and up to the date of this report are listed on page 1. The directors
remain in office in accordance with the Articles of Association.

In preparing the financial statements, the directors are responsible for assessing the company’s ability to continue as a

Auditors’ responsibilities for the audit of the financial statements

Our objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements as a whole are free from material
misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditors’ report that includes our opinion. Reasonable
assurance is a high level of assurance, but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with ISAs (UK) will
always detect a material misstatement when it exists. Misstatements can arise from fraud or error and are considered
material if, individually or in the aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users
taken on the basis of these financial statements.

Our audit testing might include testing complete populations of certain transactions and balances, possibly using data
auditing techniques. However, it typically involves selecting a limited number of items for testing, rather than testing
complete populations. We will often seek to target particular items for testing based on their size or risk characteristics. In
other cases, we will use audit sampling to enable us to draw a conclusion about the population from which the sample is
selected.

Irregularities, including fraud, are instances of non-compliance with laws and regulations. We design procedures in line
with our responsibilities, outlined above, to detect material misstatements in respect of irregularities, including fraud. The
extent to which our procedures are capable of detecting irregularities, including fraud, is detailed below.

Based on our understanding of the company and industry, we identified that the principal risks of non-compliance with
laws and regulations related to UK tax legislation, and we considered the extent to which non-compliance might have a

that have a direct impact on the financial statements. We evaluated management's incentives and opportunities for
