## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Grafton Group plc is an Irish-domiciled, LSE-listed (CDI) European builders’ merchant and specialist distributor — Chadwicks and Woodie’s on the Island of Ireland, Selco and Leyland SDM in Great Britain, Isero/IKH in Northern Europe, Salvador Escoda and Mercaluz in Iberia. The screen prints a one-week new strong buy (11/22 models, composite 0.712, sector-relative 0.808, conviction 0.475, five families: cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk). That headline is mechanically clean and overlay-blind: `cyclical_exposure_detected`, `fcf_basis_overlay`, `cash_conversion_overlay` and `dual_leverage_display` are all false on a housing/RMI name whose three sterling FCF bases disagree and whose FCF-yield model fails at 0.1%.

Filings repair the yield and the balance sheet, and they refuse the growth story. Company-defined FY2025 free cash flow is £168.3m after finance costs, property-lease payments, replacement capex and tax; Yahoo/filing-aligned OCF−capex is £205.6m. Either figure on a ~191m-share name last printing buybacks near £10.38 is a mid-to-high single-digit FCF yield, not 0.1% — a pence-versus-pounds scale error, not a quality miss. Year-end net cash before IFRS 16 was £274.0m; lease-inclusive net debt was £123.4m (0.39×). FY2025 revenue +10.4% to £2,519.6m was mostly Salvador Escoda (Iberia £212.9m versus £29.7m). Adjusted operating margin before property profit fell 30bps to 7.3%. H1 2026 (trading update only) was group LFL +0.6% with Great Britain −5.1%, and FY2026 adjusted operating-profit guidance of £190m–£200m is flat on FY2025’s £190.2m. No statutory H1 2026 P&L or cash-flow is in this snapshot (due 3 September 2026). Accumulate on cash, cover and a broken cheapness veto; do not treat the fresh strong-buy print, or the 2030 CME compounder language, as confirmed.

## INVESTMENT THESIS
The models that passed — Graham Defensive/Enterprising, Schloss Low P/B, Earnings Yield, Neff PEGY, Dividend Growth, Dreman Contrarian, Piotroski 8/9, Composite Value, Earnings Quality, Financial Health — describe a cash-generative merchant at a mid-cycle multiple (screen P/E 12.4, P/B 1.2, dividend yield 3.7%), not a wide-moat grower. The models that failed are the right vetoes on quality and the wrong veto on cash: Buffett Quality, Economic Moat and Quality Value all fail on ROE 8.4% and thin merchanting margins; Lynch PEG 2.43 fails a 5.1% growth input; FCF Yield fails at 0.1% while the screen’s own canonical FCF is £168.3m. Quality and GARP can pass on the same 8.4% ROE that sinks Buffett/Moat — that is a scoring inconsistency, not a documented exception.

For a value investor the hook is the conservative cash identity. The Chair’s APM already deducts lease cash (£81.7m) and replacement capex; on that £168.3m, FY2025 cash dividends of £72.6m are covered 2.32× and dividends plus the £56.7m buyback are covered about 1.3×, with a residual for M&A. Net cash before leases of £274.0m and leverage of 0.39× versus a ~2.0× ceiling are investment-grade, not the screen’s 37.6% D/E taken in isolation. Cumulative buybacks of £457.6m from May 2022 to 9 July 2026 retired 52.51m shares (21.8% of the count) at an average £8.71.

It is not a strong buy on earnings recovery or on the 2030 slide. FY2025 adjusted EPS +5.1% to 75.4p tracks the screen’s growth input exactly; statutory EPS +15.4% to 70.3p is the buyback- and exceptional-inflated series (MFP disposal £8.1m). H1 2026 LFL is barely positive and Great Britain is in a second year of volume stress. Guidance of £190m–£200m, with H2 “broadly consistent” with H1, is acquisitions offsetting GB, not operating leverage. The 11 June 2026 Capital Markets Event targets >10% adjusted EPS CAGR (2025–2030, 73.8p ex-property base), cumulative FCF >£850m (2026–2030) and ROCE ~13% — a reinvestment of the cash pile into Iberian/Irish M&A (Mercaluz consideration undisclosed) that the screen is already treating as a GARP/quality compounder. Size as accumulate. The one-week trade plan at £969.14 / £924.81 sits below live late-August/1 September buyback VWAPs around £10.29–£10.38; that ladder is stale versus the print, not a valuation.

## RISKS AND RED FLAGS
Housing/RMI cyclicality is the earnings driver and the screen missed it (`cyclical_exposure_detected` false). FY2025 GB LFL was only +0.4% with distribution down; H1 2026 GB LFL was −5.1% in both quarters; May and July both say GB construction is subdued and July says no meaningful near-term improvement. Ireland and Iberia are carrying the group. A higher-for-longer UK/Irish rate path hits volume, then working capital: inventory is already £395m and receivables rose £43m in FY2025. Company FCF fell 5.6% in a year when reported profit rose. If volumes recover, the WC inflow reverses; if they do not, the 7.3% margin is the defence, not the 2030 13% ROCE.

The FY2025 “recovery” is acquisition/mix. Iberia’s first full Salvador year explains most of the +10.4% revenue. Adjusted margin −30bps. Statutory profit is still far below FY2022. FY2026 guidance of £190m–£200m versus FY2025 £190.2m is Mercaluz/Cygnum/HSS offsetting GB, which is what the May update said in plain language. Treating that as organic merchanting leverage is the error the quality/GARP passes invite.

FCF-definition and overlay failure. Three sterling FCFs (£168.3m / £205.6m / £187.7m) and a 0.1% yield should have tripped `fcf_basis_overlay` and `divergence_flagged`. They did not. Dividend cover on the APM is real (2.32× cash paid); cover on Yahoo FCF is looser and is the wrong equity test because it skips £82m of leases. Do not fund a larger book off the 3.64× gross print or off a “failed” 0.1% yield.

Buybacks inflate the per-share optics more than the enterprise ones. 21.8% fewer shares since May 2022, TVR 187.5m, live programme still buying at £10.30–£10.50 versus a 2022–26 average of £8.71. Piotroski no-dilution is earned. The 12.4× P/E and the 5.1% growth score are partly denominator. FY2025 dividends plus buybacks (£129m) fitted inside £168m APM FCF; that residual is also what pays for Mercaluz, Cygnum and the next deal. The ninth £25m programme (30 June–31 October 2026) plus the ordinary dividend is fundable on the FY2025 run-rate if H1 2026 cash conversion holds — and H1 cash is exactly what is missing.

Iberian/M&A reinvestment versus 8.4% ROE. Salvador is already in the FY2025 numbers (Iberia adj. OP £13.6m on £213m). Mercaluz is two months of H1 revenue, “in line,” no price. CME 2030 needs >10% EPS CAGR and ~13% ROCE from a 10.9% ROCE / 8.4% ROE base while GB is shrinking. That can be value-accretive if HVAC distribution earns above WACC; it can also be a cash-pile recycle the screen has already scored as quality. Goodwill £659m with limited GB headroom is the impairment path if the cycle stays long.
