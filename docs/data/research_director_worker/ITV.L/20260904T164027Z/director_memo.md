EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
ITV plc screens as a strong buy on a pre-deal consolidated group (15/22 models, composite 82%, P/E 11.6, yield 7.1%), but the listed equity is already a live Media & Entertainment carve-out: Sky/Comcast agreed on 6 July 2026 to buy ITV M&E for up to £1.6bn, with completion expected in H2 2027, a 6 July 2028 longstop, and CMA/public-interest conditions still open. The 7.1% yield and dividend-family pass are group figures on company-adjusted free cash flow of £187m against a ~£190m ordinary dividend; filing-aligned FCF is £148m (about 0.79× cash dividends paid) and the overlay that should have flagged that mismatch did not fire. After close the residual is a Studios stub plus a Board-expected ~£950m cash return whose mechanics are still “in due course,” while a failed or remixed deal leaves a cyclical broadcaster with weakening cash conversion (profit-to-cash 65% in FY25, 63% rolling at H1 2026). This is a one-week signal with 53% conviction; cheapness and the headline return are real only if the perimeter the screen scored still exists.

INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk-family passes describe today’s integrated broadcaster-producer, not the equity a buyer will own after H2 2027. FY25 Studios already supplied the larger adjusted-EBITA line (£297m versus M&E £234m) and grew external revenue faster than the ad-funded arm; a long-term content-supply agreement with a £2.1bn minimum spend in 2028–32 and the inbound Love Productions assets would give a pure-play Studios some visibility if the sale completes on stated terms. That is a different cash-flow identity from the 7.1% advertising-supported yield the models passed. Dividend cover is 1.0× only on the company’s £187m FCF definition; on statutory operating cash flow minus capex it is below 1.0×, Piotroski is 3/9, and H1 2026 Studios adjusted EBITA already fell 9% on delivery phasing. The £100m buyback started on 31 July 2026 is an early slice of a return that management still ties to deal completion; the remaining ~£850m is not cash in hand. A value investor is therefore underwriting a regulatory binary and a stub-quality question, not a stable high-yield compounder. Limit orders at the screen’s 69.05p/66.26p plan buy that binary too early while RSI is 34, the price is below the 200-day average, and sell-side fair-value commentary has already moved toward about 81p on execution caution.

RISKS AND RED FLAGS
The live risk is perimeter and completion, not a generic advertising cycle. If CMA, Ofcom or a public-interest intervention cuts price, delays close past H2 2027, or kills the deal, the listed equity remains an ad-funded broadcaster and the ~£950m return does not appear; Sky’s £80m break fee is the only contracted consolation, and it does not pay if a non-regulatory condition fails. If the deal completes, ITV is smaller and less diversified (sale-RNS risk appendix), ordinary-dividend policy is only promised unchanged until completion, and post-close cash identity depends on Studios margins (already 10.6% in H1 versus a 13–15% medium-term band), a five-year CSA that may not renew on the same minimums, and ~£25m of stranded costs. Customer concentration after close is implicit: Sky/M&E becomes the contracted buyer of the library and returning series.

Cyclical advertising is evidenced and the screen’s `cyclical_exposure_overlay` is false. FY25 TAR −5%; H1 TAR +3% and Q2 +8% are explicitly World Cup-linked; Q3 is guided −5% and nine-month TAR only flat. Digital/ITVX growth (hours +27%, digital +13%) sits inside the disposal perimeter and does not accrue to the stub. Trade-press US-distribution risk was not evidenced in `news_manifest.json` titles or the assigned filings — leave it unresolved.

Cash conversion and quality: OCF £333m→£202m, profit-to-cash 83%→65%→63% rolling, working-capital outflow £196m in FY25, net debt £431m→£566m→£592m (Q1)→£652m (H1). Piotroski 3/9 failed on ROA, OCF versus net income, leverage, current ratio, gross margin and asset turnover. That is programme-inventory and receivables timing in the FY25 cash-flow note, but it is also a deterioration the quality/GARP passes ignore. Filing FCF does not cover the ordinary dividend; the Board still intends at least 5.0p for FY26.

Other: £65m pension escrow to 2034; 2032 notes amended but residual covenant text unread; Yahoo current debt £10m→£325m unreconciled in the results extracts; Liberty Global’s October 2025 secondary (stake halved, about £135m) is title-only, so free-float dynamics after the placing and the new buyback are not measured.
