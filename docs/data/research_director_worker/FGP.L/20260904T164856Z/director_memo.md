INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP and risk-family passes describe a still-consolidated operator that includes remaining DfT TOC management-fee income. Those fees are rolling off into public ownership. What continues is a larger First Bus (FY26 revenue £1,443.6m, adjusted operating profit £102.8m) with London franchising already inside the mix, open access that is growing in journeys but margin-pressed (FY26 open-access adjusted operating profit £25.6m after Stirling mobilisation and the Lumo infrastructure charge), and an Overground concession of about £300m anticipated FY27 revenue where TfL retains revenue risk and the profit margin is not disclosed. Adjusted EPS did rise — 20.3p versus 19.4p — on a smaller weighted-average share count (553.4m versus 597.7m), and filing-adjusted net debt of £137.7m is modest once IFRS 16 rail leases and ring-fenced cash are stripped. That is an accumulate case on the earnings and leverage lines, not a value trap.

It is not a strong buy on cash. Company-defined FCF fell from £113.5m to £73.8m; H1 FY26 was a £35.6m free-cash outflow on accelerated bus electrification; and Yahoo operating cash flow / FCF also stepped down (OCF £754.2m to £615.6m, FCF £597.8m to £362.6m). The Yahoo statutory series (revenue £4,653.5m to £4,332.7m, net income £127.5m to £118.3m) looks like an earnings collapse; the FY26 RNS shows the opposite on the adjusted perimeter that management steers — adjusted revenue £1,370.0m to £1,715.7m — because statutory rail pass-through left with SWR. A value investor is therefore underwriting a policy-transition mix and an FCF definition, not a broken franchise. The embedded limit plan at £173.14 / £166.14 buys that transition at a modest discount to a 4.0% yield; that is acceptable for an accumulate book and too aggressive if the screen’s FCF-yield pass is the reason for full-size entry.
