EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Costain Group PLC is a UK civil-infrastructure contractor (transport, water, energy, defence/nuclear) that the quantitative screen rates strong buy: 13/22 models, composite 0.713, five families (cheapness, quality, dividend, GARP, risk). Conviction is only 0.442 on a one-week new signal with neutral timing; every sector overlay is false, including cyclical and cash-conversion, which is the wrong prior for a UK public-capex name just after an H1 print.

Filings confirm the mechanical quality story and refuse the mechanical growth story. FY2025 statutory revenue was £1,045.7m (FY2024: £1,251.1m) while reported operating profit rose to £44.8m and company-defined adjusted free cash flow to £63.1m, with year-end net cash £189.3m. Consultancy was 17% of FY2025 revenue (FY2024: 12%). H1 2026 revenue turned +3.4% to £543.1m and the Board still guides FY2026 “in line”, with the promised step-change deferred to FY2027. The £7.0bn “forward work position” is order book plus preferred-bidder book, and the preferred-bidder half includes probable framework volumes that are not booked revenue.

The usable cash multiple is sterling statutory FCF of about £48.5m (Yahoo / screen filing-aligned), not the USD-tagged screen TTM £49.6m and not the £63.1m adjusted figure. Restricted cash, onerous-contract notes and recoverability of the IAS 19 surplus are only partly visible. Accumulate on net cash, mix and contract discipline; do not treat the fresh strong-buy print as a fully confirmed GARP compounder.

INVESTMENT THESIS
The screen’s cheapness and quality passes describe a de-risked, higher-margin book after four years of shrinking revenue, not a wide-moat grower. Graham Enterprising, FCF Yield, Lynch/Neff PEG variants, Quality Value, Buffett Quality, Magic Formula, Acquirer’s Multiple, Piotroski 8/9, Composite Value, Earnings Quality and Financial Health all pass. Failures are also informative: Graham Defensive (P/E 15.5, current ratio 1.44), Economic Moat (ROE 15.2% and contractor margins far below the 12% hurdle), and several deep-value screens that need a cheaper P/B or P/E. Piotroski’s sole miss is asset-turnover improvement — consistent with revenue falling from Yahoo FY2022 £1,421.4m to FY2025 £1,045.7m.

For a value investor the hook is net cash, rising adjusted margin (4.5% in FY2025 highlights) and a January 2026 pension agreement that removed dividend-parity matching contributions, unlocking a 4.2p FY2025 dividend and a doubled 2.0p H1 2026 interim on a new 2.5× cover target. That is quality-at-a-fair-price with a capital-return kicker.

It is not a strong buy on growth or on the £7bn headline. Half of forward work is preferred bidder / probable framework, not a legally contracted backlog; the £700m TfL award is a seat as one of three on a framework. Adjusted basic EPS was already flat in FY2025 (14.5p vs 14.6p). Reported EPS +23% and the screen’s ~22% growth score are largely lower adjusting items plus a modest buyback, not operating leverage. H1 2026 is the first revenue inflection, but H1 adjusted free cash flow was still a £1.4m outflow and year-end net cash is guided down to about £170m. Size any book as accumulate, and treat the screen’s 215.49 / 188.65 limit ladder as a one-week mechanical plan — not a post-H1 valuation.

Cyclical and public-capex dependence. Overlays `cyclical_exposure_overlay` and `cyclical_exposure_detected` are false. HS2 Old Oak Common overrun news is sector context only; Costain-specific HS2 exposure is qualitative (FY2025 rail rescheduling, H1 “change in programme delivery strategy”).

Contractor items the cheapness models ignore. Onerous-contract provisions, claims, retentions, WIP and JV recourse are not in the assigned annual notes. Current provisions £9.9m on Yahoo is a residual, not a claims inventory. Bonding utilisation £71.2m is modest versus £295m facilities but is still contingent support. Current ratio 1.44 fails Graham Defensive for a reason: working capital is the cash engine and the cash risk.

Cash is not all free. Yahoo restricted cash £26m at FY2025 is unverified in statutory notes. H1 net cash fell £24.9m from year-end after dividends, buybacks and seasonal working capital; FY2026 year-end guide is about £170m. H1 adjusted FCF was negative. Do not treat £189.3m as deployable surplus.

Pension surplus recoverability. IAS 19 surplus ~£60–63m improves book equity and ROE. H1 contributions are nil and dividend parity is gone, which is genuine relief. A 0.25% discount-rate fall would raise the liability by about £11.4m on the H1 table; surplus-to-cash and Virgin Media residual risk remain unresolved.

Buyback-inflated scores. The 15.5× P/E and EPS-growth scores sit on a shrinking share count and on reported EPS that outpaced flat adjusted EPS. That is a few points of mechanical help, not the core of FY2025 profit growth, but it is enough that GARP should be scored on adjusted EPS and WAS, not on the screen’s 22% print.

Identity and news hygiene. Costain is a civil-infrastructure group with a Defence and Nuclear Energy line (£63.0m H1). That is not a UK defence-stock re-rating. Kalkine “UK Defence Stocks: COST…” is ticker/keyword noise and must not inform the thesis. Google News `COST` / “cost” collisions (Costco, Tesco, BT, Vodafone, ISA) are discarded.
